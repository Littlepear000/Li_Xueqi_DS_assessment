Workflow v3 - DDAT coding + applied / mentioned check

Why v3. Reviewing v2 output against one or two pages of context showed that a coded sentence can describe an approach the project does not adopt: a future phase, a government vision, another programme, a lesson cited as justification, an option weighed in design, or a system built under a parent project. The sentence-level evidence level (L1/L2/L3) does not reliably catch this because the sentence itself often reads like a plan. v3 therefore adds an explicit adoption dimension decided from the wider context.

Methodology addition (step 7, adoption)

  1. For every project × approach produced by aggregation, take the strongest coded sentence (highest evidence level, PAD before ISR).
  2. Rebuild about one page (~3,200 characters) of the source document around it, with paragraph structure restored and the sentence marked.
  3. The model answers: applied (the project finances, procures, builds, deploys, pilots, scales or requires the approach: component activity, cost line, indicator/DLI, covenant, or reported progress) or mentioned, with one reason category:
mention_forMeaningTypical wording
future_planA later phase or follow-on operation"will be considered", "may explore", "in the medium term", "Phase II"
aspiration_or_visionGovernment vision, strategy, hopes, generic benefits of the technology"the Government envisions", "digital technologies can", "is expected to transform"
other_programAnother project, donor, agency or programme does it; background"financed by the EU", "the Ministry has launched", "under the national programme"
lesson_or_referenceLessons learned or international experience cited as justification"experience from Kenya shows", "lessons from the previous project"
option_consideredAlternatives weighed in design but not financed"an alternative would be", "was considered but"
parent_or_priorBuilt under a parent or earlier project; this project does not finance its expansion"developed under the parent project", "existing system will be used"
unclearThe text does not allow a judgement—
  1. The model also re-states the evidence level after reading the context; a confidence below 0.5 for "applied" is downgraded to "mentioned / unclear".
  2. Approach status becomes validated (applied and L2/L3), mentioned_only, or referenced_only (L1 only). Headline counts use validated; mentioned approaches stay in the database with their reason so that "mention without adoption" can be reported per project, sector and theme.

Replication column (step 9, replication) - how a "repeat" is detected and verified

  1. Explicit reference. The project's documents are scanned for other in-scope project IDs (P-codes) and for other in-scope project names of three words or more. A hit within ±300 characters of replication language ("builds on", "replicates", "scaled from", "lessons from", "parent project", "additional financing", "modelled on", "second phase" …) scores 0.9 (P-code) / 0.8 (name); without such language 0.5 / 0.4. The passage is stored as the link's evidence.
  2. Similar approach. The approach text (DDAT name + coded sentences) is compared by TF-IDF cosine similarity with every approach already stored for other projects in the portfolio store; links at or above 0.30 are kept (max 5), with the matched approach's id and score. The scan is incremental: each project is compared with what is already in the store, and a final rescan refreshes all links once the portfolio is complete.
  3. Direction. Approval dates decide: the earlier project is the origin (replicated_by), the later one replicates.
  4. Reviewer verification. In the web app every link has a Verify action that shows, side by side, this project's evidence in its page context and the other project's matching evidence (or the explicit-reference passage with the P-code highlighted), with a one-click jump to the other project. The reviewer records confirmed or rejected with a note; the verdict is stored per approach (replication_verdict) and exported.

Everything else is v2

Segmentation, keyword and semantic matching, batched classification with the cached DDAT taxonomy, aggregation, Note 2 solution / pillar mapping, replication scan and Run A / Run B quality control are unchanged (see the v2 definition and CHANGELOG).

Reviewer tooling

In the web app every evidence sentence has a Review in context action that rebuilds 1-4 pages of the source document on demand with the sentence, other coded sentences and approach keywords highlighted, and with aspiration / commitment wording flagged, so the applied-vs-mentioned decision can be checked quickly and overridden per approach.
