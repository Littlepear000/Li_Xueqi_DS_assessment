Workflow v6 — innovative practices from the PAD

v5 + an innovation test, the project-level innovation screen and Data Sheet exclusion. v5 extracted any specific digital practice, innovative or not (e.g. a disbursement condition requiring a cyber-risk assessment of the MIS was accepted as an "Operational" approach); v6 adds the missing test.

1. Definitions

Approach
A specific, identifiable, innovative practice: a concrete thing the project does, named precisely enough that the same practice could be recognised in another project. It must pass three tests — specificity, digital / data / AI and innovation — and it maps to exactly one category of the working paper Innovation Patterns in the World Bank Group Portfolio: Operational, Technological, Financial or Collaborative.
Specificity test
The practice names (1) the mechanism, tool or system and (2) what it is used for / who uses it. "Use of technology", "digital transformation" or a technology name alone fail the test and are not extracted.
Innovation test (new in v6)
The practice introduces a new product, service, process, delivery arrangement or organizational practice to address a development challenge in the project's context (working paper: a distinctive method for addressing a development challenge through novel products, services, processes, or organizational practices). Routine requirements fail: fiduciary, safeguard, legal or disbursement conditions; compliance audits and assessments (including IT or cyber audits) required as conditions; standard M&E and reporting; standard IT procurement or upgrades. The extractor states an innovation_basis for every approach.
Innovation cues
Hits of the six non-technology dimensions of the innovation dictionary (working paper Appendix B: innovation, innovation cycle, results-based management, innovation types, enabling processes, public good) in a reading unit. Shown to the model next to the digital cues; they support the innovation test but are not sufficient, and their absence does not fail it.
Innovation tier
Project-level screen as in v1: distinct dictionary hits (all seven dimensions) across the project's documents — High ≥ 4, Moderate 2–3, Low 0–1. Informational by default; screening.gate: true extracts only High / Moderate projects.
Adoption
apply — this project finances, builds, deploys, requires or has implemented the practice; prospect — envisaged for the future without commitment; discuss — discussed as background, lessons or options. Only apply counts in headline figures.
Reading unit
The smallest piece of text read at once: one numbered paragraph of the PAD (the running paragraph numbers are followed), or, where numbering cannot be followed, a window of consecutive sentences of about 1,200 characters. Units never split a sentence and keep character offsets into the source file. Half a page ≈ 1,600 characters.

2. Steps

  1. Innovation screen (Python, new) — innovation tier High / Moderate / Low recorded on every project.
  2. Locate PAD (Python) — the newest PAD of the project; fallback Project Paper → Program Document → PID. No document: the project is marked failed / Unclear.
  3. Reading units (Python) — the document is segmented into sentences with offsets, then grouped into units (units.target_chars / max_chars / min_chars).
  4. Cue filter (Python) — units that carry a digital / data / AI keyword cue (DDAT keyword lists + enabling-technology terms of the innovation dictionary); boilerplate units dropped; PAD Data Sheet units (Conditions, Legal Covenants, ESCP "Sections and Description", effectiveness / disbursement conditions — the zone from the first such header to the first numbered paragraph) are marked legal_condition and never extracted; every unit gets its innovation_cues; at most max_units_per_project units, ranked by cue density.
  5. Extract practices (LLM, 01_extract) — ten units per call. For each unit the model returns zero or more practices that pass the three tests, with category, adoption, innovation_basis, a one-sentence description and 1–3 sentence ids. Sentences are resolved in Python, so evidence is verbatim and anchored by construction; an unresolvable id drops the practice.
  6. Judge / expand (LLM, 02_validate, 03_revalidate) — every practice is judged on five questions (specific? digital? right category? evidence and adoption supported? innovative or routine?) with verdict accept / revise (corrected wording, category or adoption) / expand / reject. For expand, the unit is widened by validate.expand_chars (≈ half a page) on each side and judged again, this time to a final verdict. Rejected practices are written to the exclusion log with the judge's reason and a fixed reject_code: generic · not_digital · not_innovative · legal_or_compliance_condition · background_or_other_programme · unsupported_by_evidence.
  7. Sector (LLM, 04_codes) — one sector per accepted practice, chosen from the project's own projects.csv list (assigned directly when the project has a single sector). No theme is assigned (v6.1).
  8. Group sector × category (Python + one LLM call, 05_merge) — accepted practices with the same sector and the same category form one approach; their evidence is pooled and the member practices are listed in its description. The model only names groups with several members. Aim: about three approaches per project on average. Data Sheet sentences that refer to an approach found in the body are attached to it as extra evidence (evidence_source = legal_condition), never as a new approach.
  9. Final check: digital & AI + pre-2019 (LLM, 06_novelty, one call per project) — two questions per approach: (1) is digital technology, data or AI the core of the approach? incidental or none drops it (not_digital). (2) Overall, how established was this kind of approach in development projects before 2019? rare or absent (a few early pilots at most) = innovative, kept; in use or common = dropped (existed_before_2019).
  10. Build records (Python) — one approach per group: name = practice, adoption = highest among members (apply > prospect > discuss), number_of_evidence = pooled evidence sentences; one evidence row per sentence with unit id, paragraph number, judge rounds and verdict.
  11. Replication scan (Python) — explicit P-code references and TF-IDF similarity against the portfolio store (unchanged from v2).

3. What to look at when tuning

  • reject_code counts (validate step trace, exclusion log): many not_innovative = the extractor is still too generous; many generic = the specificity rule is not applied at extraction.
  • Exclusion log (table 4 / "rejected" in the drawer): the judge's reasons show whether the extractor is too generous (many reject: generic) or the specificity rule is too strict.
  • judge_rounds on evidence rows: practices that needed the expanded context; a high share suggests larger units or a larger expand_chars.
  • Cue filter recall: units without a cue are never read; candidates.require_cue: false reads every non-boilerplate unit (more calls).
  • Merging: compare approaches before and after the merge step in the trace (merged).

4. Cost profile

Per project: ⌈candidate units / 10⌉ extraction calls + ⌈practices / 12⌉ judge calls (+ one more round for the expanded ones) + ⌈practices / 30⌉ coding calls + 1 merge call. A typical PAD (60–100 candidate units, 20–40 practices) is 20–30 calls of 3–6k input tokens; the system prompt is cached on Anthropic. Token counts and a list-price cost estimate are recorded per run (llm_usage) and printed by the CLI.
