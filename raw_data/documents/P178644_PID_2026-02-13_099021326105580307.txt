Introduction and Context Country Context 1. Cabo Verde’s development trajectory is shaped by its archipelagic dispersion, which imposes high costs on connectivity, service delivery, and economic integration. The country continues to face deficiencies in road and access infrastructure, limiting mobility and access to basic services for households and businesses alike. These challenges extend to the broader connectivity ecosystem—particularly intra-island, inter-island, and international linkages—where maritime transport remains essential but constrained by operational weaknesses within key sector actors such as Cabnave, CV Inter-ilhas, and ENAPOR. 2. At the same time, Cabo Verde is highly vulnerable to climate impacts, including flooding, erosion, and the accelerated degradation of transport and urban assets. This vulnerability was clearly confirmed and further exacerbated by the extreme rainfall events of August and November 2025, which caused widespread human impacts, significant economic losses, and extensive damage to critical infrastructure across multiple islands. These climate-related hazards reduce infrastructure reliability, increase maintenance burdens, and disproportionately affect vulnerable communities. Strengthening climate resilience in the transport and urban sectors therefore remains central to sustaining inclusive growth, improving mobility, and reinforcing territorial cohesion across the islands. Sectoral and Institutional Context 3. The transport and urban sectors in Cabo Verde are characterized by infrastructure gaps, limited service reliability, and high operating costs linked to the country’s geography and climate exposure. Roads and access routes are frequently inadequate or deteriorated, constraining movement of people and goods and limiting the reach of essential economic and social services. Maritime transport—critical for interisland integration—faces operational, investment, and governance challenges that affect service continuity and efficiency. In addition, urban infrastructure across Cabo Verde is often characterized by fragmented and insufficient planning, with many neighborhoods—particularly informal or rapidly urbanizing areas—lacking adequate drainage, resilient public spaces, and reliable access to basic services such as sanitation and safe mobility. These shortcomings, combined with historically limited integration of climate and disaster risk considerations into urban design, leave urban populations exposed to flooding, coastal erosion, and extreme weather events, exacerbating social vulnerability and increasing the cost of recovery after shocks. 4. Climate vulnerabilities aggravate these structural issues, underscoring the need for resilient design standards, improved drainage and flood management infrastructure, and risk sensitive urban systems to reduce disruptions and safeguard development gains. At the institutional level, the Government of Cabo Verde is strengthening sector governance, with the MIOTH providing policy leadership and UGPE supporting project execution. Efforts to reinforce the enabling environment for SOE and PPP investments remain crucial for sustaining infrastructure improvements and ensuring efficient service delivery. 5. Building on this context, the parent project, Improving Connectivity and Urban Infrastructure in Cabo Verde (P178644), has already made tangible progress in improving transport connectivity and preparing urban infrastructure investments to more resilient standards, while strengthening institutional capacity within the Ministry of Infrastructure, Territorial Planning, and Housing (MIOTH) and the project implementation entities. As implementation advanced and detailed designs and community consultations were completed, the project not only began to address long-standing infrastructure and climate vulnerabilities but also revealed the scale of remaining gaps—particularly the need for larger, more integrated investments that embed climate-resilient design, flood-risk management, and safe urban mobility. The Additional Financing (AF) builds on the foundation established under the parent project Improving Connectivity and Urban Infrastructure in Cabo Verde (P178644). The AF expands investments that enhance climate resilience through both flood management infrastructure and the rehabilitation of urban and transport infrastructure damaged during Tropical Storm Erin in August 2025 and the heavy rains in November 2025. Investments within the AF also aim to increase the reliability and operational performance of transport and urban infrastructure, while strengthening sector institutions. 7. Component 1 – Enhancing Resilient Urban and Community Infrastructure. The AF supports priority urban and community-level infrastructure designed to reduce climate-related vulnerabilities, improve drainage and urban functionality, and strengthen resilience, with a priority on municipalities affected by the last rainy season. Investment typologies build on ongoing interventions under the parent project and include support to national planning tools and community-based resilience mechanisms. Within the pipeline of identified interventions, Crisis Response Window (CRW) resources will finance the rehabilitation and upgrading of drainage, slope stabilization, and urban access infrastructure damaged by flooding, with designs informed by Build Back Better standards. 8. Component 2 – Enhancing Transport Connectivity and Resilience. This component scales up the rehabilitation and climate-proofing of key road sections, adding additional corridors identified as vital for intra-island mobility. It also enhances institutional capacity for road asset management through targeted training and technical support. Selected road sections financed under the Additional Financing include assets affected by the 2025 rainy season, with CRW resources contributing to recovery‑oriented rehabilitation and climate‑proofing. 9. Component 3 – Technical Assistance. Technical assistance deepens sector analytical work and operational capacity, including studies related to maritime and aviation infrastructure, enhanced diagnostics on road safety to inform future interventions, and assistance to INGT on updating the country’s cartography as the baseline for a future national cadaster update. 10. Component 4 – Project Management and Implementation Support. This component finances project coordination, fiduciary and safeguards oversight, monitoring, and reporting. Under the AF, additional activities will reinforce environmental and social supervision capacity within MIOTH and UGPE through structured capacity-building initiatives. Current Projects on International Waterways OP 7.50 No Projects in Disputed Area OP 7.60 No Summary of Screening of Environmental and Social Risks and Impacts The Additional Financing (AF) is considered Substantial. The risk level is primarily linked to civil works under Components 1 and 2. Civil works for road rehabilitation will occur along existing routes in previously modified areas. Land adjacent to roads is often used for temporary plantations by local communities. Expected environmental impacts include dust, noise, vibration, and gas emissions from machinery and vehicles; traffic congestion and emissions during excavation; off-site impacts from quarry and asphalt plant operations if poorly managed and lacking permit to operate; improper disposal of construction waste; accidental spills of fuel and lubricants; Loss of vegetation, soil erosion. These impacts are expected to be site-specific, moderate in magnitude, and unlikely to extend beyond the project's footprint. While the project is expected to deliver positive socio-economic benefits, some social risks and impacts are expected, including physical or economic displacement (temporary and permanent) due to civil works, exclusion of vulnerable groups during project implementation, and limited SEA/SH incidents. The SEA/SH risks are moderate. To address these risks, the AF will: (i) rely on the national framework to manage labor and working conditions, culture heritage and SEA/SH aspects and (ii) prepare and implement relevant Environmental and Social Assessment (s) (ESIA) and/or Environmental and Social Management Plan (s) (ESMP) and Resettlement Plans (RP) sequenced with project activities. The GM is operational across the intervention sites with focal points and multi-channels established at the municipal and central levels. While persistent challenges were identified under the parent project regarding the popularization of the Grievance Mechanism, the AF will maintain this mechanism and strengthen community awareness under the Project component 4 to enhance its effectiveness. The MIOTH established a Project Execution Unit (CEP-MIOTH) that is responsible for the implementation of the Project in collaboration with UGPE. The CEP-MIOTH will maintain an environmental specialist and a social specialist under the AF implementation to assist the unit in ensuring that all subprojects are implemented in accordance with the World Bank ESF and national legislation. CEP-MIOTH will prepare and sign MoUs with ICIEG, IGT and IPC. E. Implementation Institutional and Implementation Arrangements 12.
