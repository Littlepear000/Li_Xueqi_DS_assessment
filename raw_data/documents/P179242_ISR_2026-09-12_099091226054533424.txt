KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions The Transforming Fisheries Sector Management in South-West Indian Ocean Region and Maldives Project is a regional operation financed by the World Bank. It supports the Indian Ocean Commission and its member states in strengthening evidence-based fisheries management across the South-West Indian Ocean, while simultaneously supporting the Government of Maldives in improving governance, management capacity, and private sector competitiveness in its fisheries sector. The project was approved in May 2023 and has a closing date of December 31, 2030. The total financing stands at USD 66.83 million, comprising International Development Association credits and grants. As of August 2026, the project is approximately 39 months into its 90-month implementation period. While regional activities have been performing well, activities in the Maldives are delayed. A project restructuring that removes key implementation bottlenecks was approved by the World Bank in December 2025 and countersigned by the government in July 2026. Despite these delays, a number of concrete results have been achieved. Key regional fisheries science frameworks are operational, stock assessment and socioeconomics training programmes are underway across South-West Indian Ocean member countries, and a regional fisheries dashboard is providing consolidated data to national fisheries agencies. In the Maldives, 169 beneficiaries have received job-focused capacity building interventions—34% of whom are women, exceeding the project's 30% female target. Three biosafety and quarantine standard operating protocols have been developed, 51 Anchored Fish Aggregating Devices have been installed, contracts for the Vessel Monitoring System have been signed, and the digitization of historical catch data in the Fisheries Information System is complete. 5. The project is supporting SWIOFC's capacity to strengthen national fisheries management through three operational regional platforms (RFSP, RTMP, RNCE). To date, a regional capacity assessment has been completed, training delivered in stock assessment (1 of 6 units) and fisheries socioeconomics (2 of 4 units), and the Regional Fisheries Dashboard is providing member states with consolidated data to support evidence-based decision-making. However, no new coastal stock assessments have been finalized, the SWIOFC research index has not yet been developed, and no SWIO country has commenced formal FMP development under EAF principles. This work is building the regional methodological consensus and scientific capacity that formal, regionally standardized coastal stock assessments will require.
