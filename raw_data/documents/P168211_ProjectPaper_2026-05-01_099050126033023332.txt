PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING Program Overview 1. The Program for the Recovery of the Energy Sector of Tajikistan (PREST) is a Program-for-Results (PforR) operation supported by Grant No. D558-TJ (SDR 97.2 million) and Additional Financing Grant No. E1010-TJ (SDR 59.3 million). The Parent Program financing closes on December 31, 2027, and the Additional Financing closes on December 31, 2031. Implementation Status 2. The overall Program is rated Moderately Satisfactory1, with an overall risk rating of Substantial. As of March 30, 2026, approximately US$123.42 million (57 percent) of total Program financing (US$216 million) has been disbursed. This is the fifth Program restructuring. Following the fourth Program restructuring, there are 38 Disbursement-Linked Results (DLRs), of which 23 have been achieved. Of the remaining 15, ten are on track for delivery in calendar year 2026, with an associated value of up to US$66 million during the year, including up to US$32 million by June 2026. One additional DLR is on track for delivery in 2027. Approximately US$52 million in advances remains to be recovered from Barqi Tojik (BT)2. Key Achievements 3. The Program has supported significant reforms in the energy sector since its effectiveness. Key recent achievements include: (a) Tariff Reform. The Government has demonstrated sustained commitment to cost-reflective tariff reform. A 17% tariff increase was approved in December 2025, effective February 1, 2026, following a prior increase in March 2025. The sector is expected to achieve full cost recovery by 2027. A new tariff methodology has also been adopted, providing a more transparent and predictable framework for future adjustments. (b) Legal and Regulatory Framework. A new Energy Code has been prepared to replace the existing Energy Law. The Code aims to establish an independent regulator and introduce market mechanisms into the sector, and is expected to be finalized by May 2026. (c) Financial Discipline and Transparency. New tariff-based escrow account rules introduced in August 2025 have improved cash flow predictability for the three main utilities - BT, SIB (Shabakahoi Intiqoli Barq, the national electricity transmission company), and STB (Shabakahoi Taqsimoti Barq, the national electricity distribution company). All three utilities are now regularly publishing financial and operational data. BT significantly reduced inventory purchases, from US$276 million in 2024 to US$86 million in 2025, and improved payment rates to Sangtuda 1 and Sangtuda 2. (d) Corporate Governance. Supervisory Boards (SBs) and specialized committees have been established at BT, SIB, and STB, with procurement review times reduced from two months to ten days. Key Challenges 4. The Bank is supporting the Recipient addressing several key remaining challenges in the PforR including: (a) While tariffs have increased under Disbursement-Linked Indicator (DLI) 1, undercollection of electricity bills from two large customer groups undermines the financial performance of the sector: TALCO (the state-owned aluminum company which consumed 13 percent of the country’s electricity in 2025) paid only approximately 43 percent of its electricity bills in 2025, accumulating arrears of around US$67 million by April 2026 since a payment plan was signed in 1 Implementation Status and Result Report dated November 12 2025. 2 Including US$31.5 million from the Parent Project, and US$20.3 million from the Additional Financing. (b) Debt Service (linked to DLRs 2.10 and 11.2). BT has not honored revised amortization schedules with the Ministry of Finance (MoF). In 2025, BT paid only US$17 million out of US$107 million due (approximately 16%). For 2026, a significant gap remains between MoF requirements (US$120 million) and BT's budget for debt servicing (US$42 million). (c) Corporate Governance (linked to DLRs 8.2 and 8.3). BT's Supervisory Board met only twice in 2025, failing to meet the quarterly meeting requirement. Legislative amendments are still needed to mandate the appointment of independent board members across the three utilities. (d) Cost controls (linked to DLR 10.1): BT has started implementation of the cost control measures outlined in Decree 477, but additional action is required to achieve the cost reduction targets set in DLR 10.1. Remaining DLRs 5. With less than two years remaining before the financing for Parent Program closing date, 15 DLRs worth approximately US$139 million remain to be achieved4. Several DLRs totaling US$64 million are at risk of not being achieved by December 2027. A deeper restructuring is planned in FY27 to concentrate remaining Parent Program resources on realistically achievable DLRs and shift others to the Additional Financing. Rationale for Restructuring 6. This restructuring addresses a narrow but important technical issue: two DLRs under Disbursement-Linked Indicator 1 (DLI 1, tariff reform) were achieved after their contractual deadlines, preventing formal verification and disbursement. Specifically: (a) DLR 1.3 (2024 tariff adjustment): The required tariff increase was implemented through Government Decree No. 165, dated March 5, 2025 (confirmed through independent verification audit report). This fell after the original contractual deadline of December 31, 2024. The underlying result has been substantively achieved, but the deadline must be extended to allow the World Bank to formally recognize the achievement and process the corresponding disbursement of approximately US$12.2 million. (b) DLR 1.8 (Electricity Tariff Paper disclosure): Finalizing and publicly disclosing an updated Electricity Tariff Paper (ETP) has proven complex and politically sensitive. There were also procurement delays in mobilizing the Consultant for the ETP update, compounded further by data collection challenges when the Consultant was mobilized. The ETP update requires updated sectoral assumptions, careful calibration, and broad stakeholder engagement, with the process now underway and at an advanced stage. The Ministry of Energy and Water Resources (MEWR) requires additional time beyond the original deadline of September 30, 2025, to complete this work. 7. The Government of Tajikistan has been informed of and has confirmed agreement with the proposed deadline extensions (as recorded in the Aide Memoire of the March 2026 implementation support mission).
