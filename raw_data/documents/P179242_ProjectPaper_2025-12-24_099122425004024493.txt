PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING Background 1. The Transforming Fisheries Sector Management in South-West Indian Ocean Region and Maldives Project (TransFORM, SWIOFish5) (P179242) is a collaborative effort between the Indian Ocean Commission (IOC) Secretariat and the Maldives Ministry of Fisheries and Ocean Resources (MoFOR, split from the former Ministry of Fisheries, Marine Resources and Agriculture [MoFMRA]). The project is financed by a US$26.4 million International Development Association (IDA) credit and a US$38.4 million IDA grant. It was approved on May 16, 2023, became effective on August 9, 2023, and its closing date is December 31, 2030. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to strengthen regional, evidence-based fisheries management in the South-West Indian Ocean Region and to improve competitiveness in the fisheries sector in the Maldives. 2. The PDO’s regional outcome is supported by Component 1 (Enhance Evidence-based Management Advice to the Fisheries Sector in the South-West Indian Ocean Region), which is managed by the IOC with technical support from the United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the South-West Indian Ocean Fisheries Commission (SWIOFC). Component 1 finances regional fisheries management activities across the South-West Indian Ocean (SWIO) region, namely: (i) development and coordination of regional research with a focus on capacity development programs and initiatives; (ii) coordination and consolidation of regional cooperation for evidence-based advice on management of fisheries and other uses of marine and coastal ecosystems; and (iii) linking and coordinating fisheries initiatives and programs in the region. 3. The PDO’s national outcome is supported by Component 2 (Supporting Maldives as the Catalyst for Strengthened Regional Capacity for Fisheries Governance and Management) and Component 3 (Enhanced Competitiveness and Private Sector Participation for improving Business Climate for Fisheries in Maldives and the South-West Indian Ocean Region), and both components are implemented in the Maldives by MoFOR. Component 2 finances: (i) activities that improve the implementation, enforcement, and monitoring of fisheries management plans (FMPs), including digitalization of historical catch data, enhancement of the vessel monitoring system (VMS) through servicing existing units and procuring new vessel locating devices and software, enhancement of the Fisheries Monitoring Centre by upgrading the monitoring station and providing equipment and software subscriptions, development of FMPs, carrying out biological studies, and recruitment of fisheries observers; (ii) activities that augment the country’s quarantine and disease surveillance and management capacities through construction of quarantine facilities and training; and (iii) enhancement of skills and capacities of enterprises in the fisheries sector through overseas and national education/training programs. Component 3 finances: (i) activities in support of the decarbonisation of the fisheries sector, namely studies to identify potential engine and vessel design modifications, and the installation of fish aggregating devices (FADs) to increase catching efficiencies and reduce fuel required during fishing trips; and (ii) technical assistance (TA) and a matching grants program for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) investing in the fisheries sector’s value chains (30 percent financing of capital expenditure), aimed at increasing competitiveness and leveraging private sector investment in the fisheries sector. Project Status 4. Since project effectiveness, the clients have taken several steps to advance implementation. Under Component 1, the IOC signed a contract with FAO in July 2024 to provide technical support, and the first regional steering committee meeting took place in December 2024 where the annual budget for the regional activities was approved. Under Official Use Only The World Bank Transforming Fisheries Sector Management in South-West Indian Ocean Region and Maldives Project (TransFORM, SWIOFish5) (P179242) component 2 in the Maldives, the digitization of historical catch data is ongoing; stakeholders have been consulted on an Indian Ocean yellowfin stock rebuilding plan; the Fisheries Information System (FIS) has been enhanced through improvements to modules related to licensing and electronic logbooks; work has commenced to develop a recreational FMP; activities to enhance the VMS are ongoing; six fisheries observers have been recruited; stock assessments for data-limited fisheries are ongoing as is the development of manuals and data sheets for documenting fishing grounds and the seasonality of reef fisheries; government staff have participated in several Indian Ocean Tuna Commission (IOTC) and Marine Biodiversity of Areas Beyond National Jurisdiction (BBNJ) committee meetings; work to develop currica for foundation, diploma, and bachelor degree courses is underway; and mariculture research and training is ongoing. Under Component 3 in the Maldives, a consultancy to conduct an energy audit of the onshore fish processing sector and identify potential areas for improved efficiency has been completed; a consultant is working to assess fuel use and energy efficiency of fishing vessels; terms of reference (ToR) have been developed for the design of a new generation of low- emission fishing vessels; the procurement of echo sounders and tracking devices for anchored FADs has been launched; and the installation of FADs is complete. 5. Despite these steps, implementation has been delayed overall, particularly in the Maldives, as reflected in lower- than-planned results and disbursement rates. As of November 25, 2025, the project has disbursed US$7.55 million, representing 12 percent of total project funds, compared to the planned US$9.4 million (14.5 percent) by the end of November 2025. Of the total disbursement, the Maldives’ components have disbursed US$5 million, only 9.5 percent of its allocated funds, while the IOC has disbursed US$2.55 million, or 21 percent of its allocation. The main reason for the delays in the Maldives is the presidential and legislative elections that took place in September 2023 and April 2024, respectively, which resulted in a new government with new priorities for the sector. Additionally, procurement has been slow overall with rebidding of several packages due to low interest from potential service providers, which is a common issue in the Maldives, and there have been delays in the approval of payments to service providers by the Ministry of Finance and Planning (MoFP). Specifically, under Component 2, there has been a delay in the improvement of quarantine facilities as the government has only decided recently on the location where investments should take place, and the development of FMPs has been delayed due to a lack of interest by individuals in being part of advisory committees. Also under Component 2, project activities related to the interior furnishing of a new Ministry facility in Malé have been postponed until 2027, when the building is expected to be completed. Under Component 3, the matching grants program, which accounts for approximately 35 percent of the cost of activities in the Maldives, has not yet commenced. This delay is due to the government’s request for several design modifications, driven by concerns that private sector participation might be lower than anticipated. These concerns stem from a recent increase in commercial banks’ lending interest rates and the potential duplication of support with other investments planned in the sector. Consequently, the last Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) rated Progress towards the achievement of PDO and Overall Implementation Progress Moderately Satisfactory. There are no overdue internal audit reports or interim unaudited financial reports. Rationale for Restructuring 6. The proposed Level 2 restructuring follows a request from the Government of the Maldives (GoM) dated November 23, 2025, to make changes to the project’s national activities that would reflect the Maldives’ evolving economic context and new strategic priorities articulated in the government’s Strategic Action Plan. While the Maldives is an upper-middle income country, its current economic situation is challenging, with high levels of public debt and debt servicing costs. Total public and publicly guaranteed debt increased from 79 percent of Gross Domestic Product in 2019, to 111 percent in 2022, and 116 percent in 2024, due to high fiscal deficits caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, capital spending to finance major public investment projects, and government subsidies. This has resulted in higher commercial bank interest rates that make borrowing by SMEs more challenging, putting the success of the matching grants program at risk. Furthermore, the government is no longer interested in using project financing for tuna canneries and instead Official Use Only The World Bank Transforming Fisheries Sector Management in South-West Indian Ocean Region and Maldives Project (TransFORM, SWIOFish5) (P179242) wishes to support increased capacity in the country to produce tin cans used by tuna canneries. The restructuring therefore aims to improve the matching grants program’s attractiveness to the private sector and adjust its areas of focus. It will also reflect the reorganization of the former MoFMRA into MoFOR and the Ministry of Agriculture and Animal Welfare (MoAAW) in 2024 following the elections, and adjust the national components’ titles and results framework (RF) indicators and targets to better capture their financing scope and expected results and improve the project’s measurability overall. This will be the first restructuring of the project. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 7. Clients. The restructuring will amend the name of the implementing agency in the Maldives from the Ministry of Fisheries, Marine Resources and Agriculture to the Ministry of Fisheries and Ocean Resources. 8. Components. Under Component 3, the restructuring will increase the matching grants’ contribution from 30 percent (clause 3.2 (c) of the Financing Agreement) to 40 percent to make the program more affordable to potential private sector participants. Further, the focus of one of the matching grants target areas will change, as per MoFOR’s priorities, from supporting investments in two new tuna canning plants to investment in domestic production of tin cans in an effort to increase the competitiveness of the domestic tuna canning subsector, increase the domestic supply of tin cans, and reduce tuna canning plants’ input costs. This change also reflects the GoM’s collaboration with several bilateral donors in developing tuna canning facilities of a capacity much higher than that planned under the project, expected to be completed by the end of 2027. See Annex 1 for a revised list of areas to be supported by the matching grants program and their respective allocations. Finally, the titles of components 2 and 3 will be adjusted: Component 2 will change from “Supporting Maldives as the Catalyst for Strengthened Regional Capacity for Fisheries Governance and Management” to “Strengthened Capacity for Fisheries Governance and Management in the Maldives” to better reflect its focus on fisheries management and governance activities in the Maldives; and Component 3 will change from “Enhanced Competitiveness and Private Sector Participation for Improving Business Climate for Fisheries in Maldives and the South-West Indian Ocean Region” to “Enhanced Competitiveness and Private Sector Participation in Fisheries in the Maldives” to also reflect its national focus. 9. Results. The restructuring proposes changes to the RF, including the PDO-level Indicators, Intermediate Results Indicators, and their targets and measurement methodologies. These changes will ensure that the RF measures outcomes and results that the project directly supports, in line with government priorities, that targets are realistic and achievable within the project’s lifetime, that gender targets are clearly defined, and that indicators are better aligned with the project’s theory of change as articulated in the Project Appraisal Document (Report No. PAD5126). Implementation Schedule. The implementation schedule of several key activities will be adjusted to reflect the noted delays. Adjustments include deferring the enhancement of the Fisheries Monitoring Centre to 2028 to align with the Ministry’s relocation to the Maldives Marine Research Institute (MMRI) building, avoiding duplicate costs. Lastly, the implementation timeline of the matching grants program will be adjusted to reflect the delay in its launch. RISKS 20. The current overall risk rating is Substantial. The paragraphs below discuss the substantial risks, for which mitigation measures have been agreed: 21. Macroeconomic. As noted earlier, the current economic situation in the Maldives is challenging, with high levels of public debt and debt servicing costs. Two key resulting threats to improved competitiveness in the fisheries sector in the Maldives (the national/Maldives-focused part of the PDO) are: (i) a shortage of foreign exchange, which could impact the ability of companies to import items needed as inputs to fish catching and processing (e.g., fuel, fishing gear, engines), and (ii) increases in commercial bank interest rates, increasing interest payments for fishing sector companies and compromising their ability to borrow from banks for investment. The first risk is mitigated through further promoting value-added fish processing and export diversification to increase foreign currency inflows and reduce vulnerability to foreign exchange shortages. The Government may also consider establishing a priority foreign exchange allocation mechanism for essential fisheries imports (fuel, fishing gear, engines) to ensure continuity of operations. The second risk is mitigated through the restructuring and the increase in the matching grant contribution from 30 percent to 40 percent. 22. Technical design of Project. There is uncertainty over how the matching grants program under Component 3, and related disbursement performance, will progress even with the increase in the matching grants’ contribution to 40 percent in the absence of certainty over private sector interest and whether commercial banks will be prepared to lend to the fisheries (and agriculture) sector. Furthermore, applications for smaller grants by the private sector may be disincentivized by the administrative burden of the application process, which is similar for both small and large grant applications. These risks will be mitigated by i) government participation in the review and due diligence of proposals submitted, to provide additional confidence to the banking sector about lending to private sector entities; and ii) provision of technical support to applicants to aid with the application process. The government has agreed to both mitigation measures. Additionally, if private sector interest proves to be low in response to the first call for proposals, which is planned to be launched once the restructuring has been approved, despite these measures, the World Bank and the government will consider either scaling down the matching grants program and reallocating some Component 3 funds to Component 2, or broadening the eligible target areas to include additional fisheries management investments. Both options would support the PDO’s national outcome. 23. Institutional capacity for implementation and sustainability. Mobilizing qualified technical staff remains a significant challenge, both generally and within the fisheries sector. While technical expertise exists within MoFOR, its availability to provide consistent project support is limited. Mitigation measures include delivering targeted training to PMU staff and advertising consultant positions internationally to attract specialized expertise, while ensuring effective knowledge transfer to local staff. Both implementing agencies, IOC, and MoFOR, have previous experience with several World Bank- funded fisheries projects and received satisfactory performance ratings for FM, though their capacities remain limited. The project does not include large civil work packages but involves numerous procurement activities under components 2 and 3, managed by MoFOR. Component 3 focuses on matching grants, where procurement will be limited, but MoFOR's financial management capacity will be stretched. Several capacity-building measures have been taken as mitigation. Other mitigation measures include: (a) using qualified committees to determine the eligibility of private sector applicants for matching grants and monitoring supported activities, (b) using standard “matching grant agreements” templates, (c) linking the approval of matching grants to the availability of credit/loans from commercial banks in Maldives, and (d) disbursing matching grants pro-rated to the disbursement by commercial banks of their portion of the loan/credit for the activity.
