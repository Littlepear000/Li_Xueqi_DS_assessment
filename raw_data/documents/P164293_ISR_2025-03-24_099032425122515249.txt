KEY ISSUES & STATUS Mar 24, 2025 Page 1 of 13 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Burkina Faso Higher Education Support Project (P164293) 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions This final Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) evaluates the project’s performance over its entire implementation period across the three harmonized criteria of: (i) relevance of objectives and design; (ii) efficacy; and (iii) efficiency. A more thorough and comprehensive analysis based on most up-to-date data will be presented in the forthcoming Implementation Completion and Results Report (ICR). Relevance of Objectives and Design Relevance of Objectives. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to strengthen higher education institutions to increase access and deliver quality education in priority subject areas. The project’s objective was highly relevant as it responded to key sector challenges identified. The project strategically supported a two-pronged strategy to increase and diversify the supply of higher education opportunities; and strengthen the institutional capacity of higher education institutions to absorb the demand shocks. The objectives were also strongly aligned with the country’s policy framework for the development of higher education (Plan National d’Action de Développement de l’Enseignement Supérieur), and the education sectoral plan (Plan Sectorial de L’Education et de la Formation, 2017-2030). The project fully complemented other World Bank’s operations, carefully reviewed at the time of appraisal to build complementarities, notably the Education Access and Quality Improvement Project (P148062), which through scientific high schools, created a pipeline of priority sector students and the Burkina Faso Digital Acceleration Project (P177022), which created conditions for broadband access. Given the above, the relevance of objectives is rated High. Relevance of Project Design. The Project design was based on a sound diagnostic of higher education system in Burkina Faso and built on lessons learnt. Some of these lessons included the importance of: (i) preparedness through a HEI-level strategic plan to focus on skills for competitiveness and development; (ii) build the capacity of HEIs to design and implement subprojects; (iii) develop a sustainability plan at the start of the project implementation period, (iv) prepare architectural and engineering plans early in the implementation stage, and ensure that a dedicated full-time team is in place to follow up on implementation; and (v) RBF mechanisms from the previous and ongoing ACE projects already served as a good foundation and can be scaled up at the national level. Many activities of the project such as the establishment of UV-BF and digitalization efforts have proved instrumental to absorb the flow of students toward higher education; and improve the resilience of the sector in face of large population displacement. The alignment of activities toward improved connectivity through the university that allows students to learn and provide subsidies for many students to acquire computers. The RBF mechanism allowed to strengthen many universities by acquiring much needed pedagogical tools (such as projectors), create additional seats through rehabilitation, and improve the equity of higher education by providing much needed daycare options for students with children. Based on the above, the relevance of project design is rated High. Efficacy The following analysis provides an overview of outputs and outcomes achieved under the Project. The ICR analysis will build on this preliminary assessment and report on the level of achievement on each of the performance indicators. The project has achieved extensive success, making strong progress towards the achievement of the PDO, despite delays encountered following the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic and the institutional instability in the country. Three out of four PDO-level key performance indicators (KPIs) have either achieved or exceeded their projected end-of-project Mar 24, 2025 Page 2 of 13 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Burkina Faso Higher Education Support Project (P164293) targets, with the exception of increase in the percentage of students who have successfully completed the first-year program at the HEIs financed by the project, which was partially achieved at 61.4. In addition, all but one intermediate results indicator (percentage of female undergraduate higher education students enrolled in programs funded by the Project) have been met or exceeded. The project’s Efficacy is therefore judged to be Substantial. Project components. The Project’s components correspond to the three sub-objectives under the PDO. The PDO would be achieved by: (a) offering multiple delivery methods for higher education in selected priority subject areas, (b) enhancing training for university teachers, and (c) increasing the management capacity of existing institutions to use a wider range of delivery models to increase access to quality education. Below is a summary of the status of each component: Component 1. Establishment of University Virtual: This component supports the diversification of higher education delivery models and transform access to quality and innovative higher education. Under this component, UV-BF was established, and enrollment surpassed 23,000 students during the project. Additionally, the project supported computer acquisition for 36,549 surpassing the initial target of 25,000. Broadband internet connections were installed in seven higher education institutions – providing connectivity to over 307,000 students. Two open learning spaces were constructed and equipped. The rehabilitation and extension of the main library, along with the provision of over 16,000 books, provided structural learning solutions for students. Component 2. Strengthening Higher Education Institutions and Supporting Innovations: This component supports activities to improve the quality and relevance of priority programs by supporting the implementation of a set of improved labor market-oriented academic programs. Sub-Component 2.1 Support a Competitive Grants Program. The project supported a result-based financing (RBF) agreement for eight higher education institutions. The RBF supported two types of reforms: (a) Improvement of the institutional governance; (b) Improvement of the quality and relevance of the training programs. At the end of the program, 84 percent of institutions achieve annual milestones specified in performance contract against 80 percent planned. Funds secured under the RBF allows higher education institutions to: (a) revise existing curricula; (b) modernize the ecosystem of training and learning, including laboratories; (c) introduce innovative pedagogical approaches; (d) develop faculty capacity and broaden their range of expertise; (e) foster academic partnerships and (f) rehabilitate facilities. Sub-Component 2.2 Support Faculty Development in HEIs. The project would also finance selected HEIs in each priority cluster to (a) receive professional development opportunities; (b) promote a visiting scholars program; (c) foster innovative approaches to faculty exchanges nationally, intra-regionally, and internationally; this would include the dissemination of new content drawing on locally trained university faculty and expatriates (recruiting faculty internationally); and (d) receive specific training in new pedagogical methods in mathematics and science. A total of 1,620 faculty members receive training, and a total of 300 PhD students received scholarship matching the target. The project targets were matched. Sub-Component 2.3 Strengthening Quality Assurance in HEIs. The project financed the establishment of Internal Quality Assurance Units (IQAUs) for seven existing Higher Education Institutions (HEIs). However, the project exceeded its target by establishing eight IQAUs instead of the initially planned seven. These units are responsible for ensuring the quality of educational processes, including student assessment, achievements, and attendance. The IQAUs work within the Mar 24, 2025 Page 3 of 13 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Burkina Faso Higher Education Support Project (P164293) structure of the Academic Affairs Division and collaborate with other bodies such as deans' offices and academic affairs offices to conduct internal quality assurance activities in a more holistic and efficient manner. Quality assurance is a key mechanism to improve both internal and external efficiency of higher education services. Sub-Component 2.4 Strengthening the Management Capacity of HEIs. The project supported the increased management capacity of Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) to improve their external revenue generation. As a result, HEIs were able to generate a substantial amount of external revenue, significantly surpassing the initial target. Compared to the targeted US$1.25 million, HEIs successfully generated US$40.021 million. This remarkable achievement highlights the effectiveness of the project in enhancing the financial sustainability and operational capacity of HEIs. Component 3: Project Coordination, Management, and Implementation Activities: This component supports two main types of activities: (a) Strengthening the governance and management of higher education through improving institutional capacity in the system overall to better monitor systemic changes, develop a management information system (MIS), address gender-based violence incidents, monitor climate co-benefits of the Project; (b) Strengthening the capacity of the Permanent Secretariat of the PNADES to implement the project; • The component established the PCU with highly engaged staff who satisfactorily managed the project, including through holding regular meetings to closely monitor implementation progress. Relevant technical departments both the central and decentralized levels have benefitted from institutional strengthening training programs throughout the project. • The implementation of the integrated higher education platform CampusFaso has addressed the challenges faced by IESR in academic and pedagogical management. With the developed modules, the integrated CampusFaso platform serves as the main governance tool for IESR. It supports transparent and efficient management of IESR. Efficiency The project costs have been within the estimated cost at appraisal of US$70 million. A significant amount of SDR financing was lost due to the exchange rate fluctuations. The project had implementation delays in the early stages, caused by the global COVID-19 pandemic, the complexity of the design, the fluid security situation and the country's regime change, the PCU has worked well to make up for lost time, putting in place a schedule to complete all activities by the new close of the project. By the mid-term review (MTR) in June 2022, the disbursement rate was around 69 percent of the total IDA funding. A quantitative approach to assess project efficiency shows significant efficiency gains around the higher education sector because of project activities – in terms of internal efficiency (decrease in repetition, drop-out rates), and notably in external efficiency (higher alignment between labor market needs and graduates from priority sectors). Additionally, the project was instrumental in solving the problem of overlapping academic years – leading to a decrease in the average duration to complete a bachelor’s degree. The ICR will offer an opportunity to explore deeper these gains. Efficiency rating gains are Substantial. 5.
