KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions Feb 23, 2026 Page 1 of 22 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Improving Access to and Value from Health Services in PNG: Financing the Frontlines (P167184) The Project, effective since December 2020, reflects the Government of Papua New Guinea’s commitment to enhancing health service delivery, with the National Department of Health prioritizing alignment with the national health plan. Positive progress is being made towards the Project Development Objective (PDO) of increasing quality essential health service utilization in East New Britain, Simbu, Central, and East Sepik. Disbursements have reached US$11.19 million (38% of total funding) as of January 19, 2025, with an additional US$10.1 million projected by June 2026 raising the rate to 71%. Considerable progress has been made toward achieving the PDO. The PDO indicators are on track to being achieved with service delivery interventions advancing at scale in all four target provinces. Of the 156 health facilities assessed, 153 (98 percent) have demonstrated substantial improvement in quality scores compared to the baseline, with 85% now meeting the minimum quality threshold. Moreover, quality scorecard results have continued to improve despite increasingly stringent Disbursement Linked Indicator (DLIs) targets set for Year 4. Significant improvements in the number of antenatal care forth visits and children under 1 receiving pentavalent 3rd dose were observed , with 10–13 Provincial Health Authorities improving by 1–38% over baselines following the introduction of competitive DLIs in year 3 . This initiative also provided other non-target provinces the opportunity to participate in improving key service delivery indices. Additionally, there was an 81% increase in outreach clinics (16,541 ) up from the baseline of 9158. The percentage of Level 2 to Level 4 health facilities in selected Project Provinces equipped or rehabilitated according to National Health Service Standards is expected to be fully achieved by the end of the project. Performance based funding. East New Britain Provincial Health Authority, the early adopter province, effectively utilized performance-based funds for outreach services (health services delivered in the community), training, and equipment purchase. Competitive DLIs have also seen positive results with 21 of 22 of Provincial Health Authorities signed a Memorandum of Understanding on hand participating and half of the PHAs showing improvements over their baseline for number of antenatal care 4 visits and number of children under one immunized with Pentavalent 3rd dose. Project restructuring and extension. The Project was restructured in January 2026 to address implementation delays and limited progress by reallocating funds, cancelling performance based targets that could not disburse, and updating targets and indicators to better support essential health services in selected provinces. The restructure included a no-cost project extension from June 30, 2026 to May 31, 2028 to allow sufficient time for two large value contracts (US$14.2 million) to be implemented. Activities advancing under these two contracts include health facility audits, equipment delivery, rehabilitation of health facilities and the upskilling of health workers to boost capacity. The next technical mission is set for May 2026. 5.
