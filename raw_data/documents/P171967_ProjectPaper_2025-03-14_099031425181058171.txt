PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Project status 1. The Regional Infrastructure Financing Facility (RIFF) Project was approved by World Bank’s Board of Executive Directors on June 30, 2020, and it became effective on October 5, 2020. The Project Development Objective is to expand long-term finance to private firms in selected infrastructure sectors in Eastern and Southern Africa. 2. The project was restructured in FY24 primarily to move US$50 million from component 1 to component 2 due to the growing demand for financing under component 2. 3. The project has recorded disbursements totaling US$93.32 million against a total of US$425 million. No disbursements have been made from component 1. Component 2 of the project has disbursed $75.56 million so far. Expected disbursements for the 6-month period ending June 30, 2025, is $50 million. Disbursement for the Technical Assistance is at US$12,166,000 representing 81% of the allocated amount of US$15 million. 4. As of February 28, 2025, the pipeline for component 1 had 15 projects with expected RIFF financing totaling $950 million. The pipeline represents 345% of the total available financing of $275 million under component 1, exceeding the target pipeline size of at least 300% of the available financing, to allow for a comfortable margin and higher probability of exhausting the loan amount prior to project close. TDB has also been active in building back the pipeline for component 2. This component now includes 4 projects totaling $12M. 5. The Technical Assistance component of the loan continues to support (i) institutional strengthening (ii) capacity building (iii) project development and (iv) the implementation of environment and social standards within TDB and in the sub-projects. 6. Implementation progress for the COMESA component is rated as satisfactory. Overall progress in delivery has improved with several deliverables in advance stages of completion. 7. Environmental and Social Risk Management. The overall environmental and social risk management performance is rated as Satisfactory. B. Rationale for restructuring 8. Expected private capital mobilization (PCM) amounts should be recorded, ideally at the time of first approval, in the costs and financing tables of projects which expect to leverage private financing. The RIFF has a size of US$400 million and is expected to raise private co-financing from sponsor equity as well as debt, apart from financing from TDB. However, the PCM for the project was not appropriately recorded at the time of approval in 2020, leading to unaccounted PCM, leading to the need to restructure. The only purpose of this simple restructuring, therefore, is to adequately record expected PCM II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 9. As mentioned above, the only proposed change relates to clarifying/ amending the costs and financing table to include the PCM. The project is expected to mobilize US$ 975 million in private capital from components 1 and 2 per the results indicator “Private finance mobilized (US$)”. The description of the indicator in the Results Framework of the project is as follows: “Measures total private sector finance (debt and equity) mobilized from sponsors (including Development Finance Institutions) in the sub-projects to be financed as part of the RIFF. This figure does not include the IDA credit line. The target assumes private mobilization of three times the amount of component 1.
