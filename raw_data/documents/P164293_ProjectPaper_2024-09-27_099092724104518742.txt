PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. Purpose: This paper seeks your decision on a proposed restructuring of the Burkina Faso Higher Education Support Project, which entails a six-month extension of the closing date for grant D357-BF. This is the second extension of the closing date, bringing the total extension period to 18 months. Progress towards achievement of the Project Development Objective (PDO) is rated satisfactory and the PDO remains achievable, and implementation progress is also rated satisfactory. No changes are proposed to implementation, fiduciary or safeguards arrangements/oversight, and there are no outstanding audits. PROJECT STATUS 2. Background: The project is financed by an International Development Agency (IDA) grant in the amount of Special Drawing Rights (SDR) 48.7 million (US$70 million equivalent). The Project was approved by the World Bank’s Board of Executive Directors on July 10, 2018, and became effective on December 19, 2018. It constituted the first World Bank support to the higher education subsector in Burkina Faso, and sought to address key constraints pertaining to access, quality, and relevance of higher education programs. The PDO is to strengthen higher education institutions to increase access and deliver quality education in priority subject areas. The project closing date was extended by 12 months with a new closing date of September 30, 2024. 3. Progress towards achievement of the PDO. Despite the challenging implementation environment, particularly following the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic and the political instability in Burkina Faso, the government has been able to continue implementing most project-supported activities. Three out of four PDO key performance indicators (KPI) have exceeded their expected end-of-project targets: (a) the number of undergraduate students enrolled in priority subject areas funded by the project reached 23,000 students, exceeding the projected end target of 3,891 students (of whom, 33 percent represent female students, also exceeding the projected end target of 25 percent); (b) the number of teachers1 recruited or trained (CRI) has reached 2,343 teachers trained, exceeding the projected end target of 1,620 teachers, while the number of teachers recruited has reached 969 teachers, also exceeding the projected end target of 75 teachers; and (c) the number of students benefiting from direct interventions to enhance learning has also reached 220,128 students, far exceeding the projected end target of 140,000 students (of whom 87,666 are female students, also exceeding the projected end target of 47,600 female beneficiaries. The exception is the increase in the percentage of students who have successfully completed the first-year program at the higher education institutions (HEIs) supported by the project, which currently stands at 55 percent against a target of 75 percent. 4. The project is comprised of three components. Below is a summary of each component: 5. Component 1. Enhancing Access to Higher Education: Establishment of the Virtual University of Burkina Faso (UV-BF). This component supports a cohesive set of interventions that leverage the use of technology to address limited capacity of current higher education institutes to accommodate the high demand for tertiary education. While the component has supported key activities that have shown promising results, component 1 is assessed as moderately satisfactory based on progress to date. 6. To diversify and expand access, the project supported the establishment of the Virtual University of Burkina Faso (VUBF) which, to date, offers virtual training courses to about 7,518 students among whom 42 percent are female students against the expected enrollment of 10,000 students in the 2023/2024 academic year. To this end, the government is constructing two open digital spaces (ODS) to facilitate increased enrollment. However, construction activities have been delayed and will not be completed by the current closing date. Lastly, the component has supported the provision of connectivity to the beneficiary universities. 7. The project has supported the establishment of an integrated virtual platform (CampusFaso) which is fully functional. The platform supports improved information and data management and carries out administrative functions (student enrolment, orientation, filling administrative forms, archiving of pedagogical materials among others). The project also supports an innovative program (One student/One computer) which provided one computer to each of the 27,692 students as of July 2024. This has been a critical shift to reach and support a larger number of students and maximize impact. In addition, digital equipment and resources including more than 4,000 e-books were acquired for selected university libraries. 8. Component 2. Strengthening higher education institutions and supporting innovations. This component aims to improve the quality and increase the relevance of programs provided in higher education institutions (HEIs) through four subcomponents: (a) the support of a performance grants program; (b) support faculty development in HEIs; (c) strengthen quality assurance in HEIs; and (d) strengthen management capacity in HEIs. Based on progress to date, component 2 is assessed as satisfactory. 9. Under subcomponent 2.1, the following achievements have been made to date: (i) ten performance based contracts (PBC) are currently under implementation for an improvement in the quality of teaching. The latest independent verification report has reported that 84 percent of higher education institutions have reached the set annual target compated to the expected 80 percent, thus fully achieving the target. Under subcomponent 2.2, the project supports research activities by providing scholarships to doctoral students, with 300 doctoral students having benefitted from this intervention to date. Under subcomponent 2.3, eight internal quality assurance units (above the target of seven) are well established and the development of the national quality assurance strategy and the establishment of the national higher education qualifications framework have been finalized. 10. Component 3: Project coordination, management, and implementation activities. This component supported the coordination and management of project activities, including those related to monitoring and evaluation (M&E), procurement, and financial management (FM), as well as monitoring of activities at the decentralized level. Based on progress to date, component 3 is assessed as satisfactory. The Project Implementation Unit (PIU) is composed of qualified staff who provided the necessary support to carry out these activities. The PIU has been proactive in building its capacity and actively engaging consultants to support project activities and evaluations as needed. RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 11. The proposed extension of the closing date is necessary to allow the client to complete a set of key activities, which are necessary to fully achieve the project’s objectives and further strengthen higher education opportunities. These include: (a) the ongoing construction of two ODS in Ouagadougou; (b) the ongoing rehabilitation of the central library of the University of Joseph ki Zerbo (UJKZ); and (c) the planned construction of a nursery (crèche) at UJKZ. This extension is also seized as an opportunity to improve the performance of the project and to have an impact on higher education in Burkina Faso, particularly through the addressing the continued challenge of access to quality higher education programs. 12. Recipient performance remains satisfactory. The Bank and government teams will also continue to closely monitor progress of these key activities through regular meetings and follow-up implementation support missions. II.
