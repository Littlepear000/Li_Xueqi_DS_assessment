Introduction and Context Country Context 1. Guinea is a resource-rich, lower-middle-income country on the West Coast of Africa, with a population of approximately 13.9 million and a GDP per capita of around US$900. Guinea occupies a unique position in West Africa: it is one of the continent's most naturally endowed countries, yet also one of its most environmentally vulnerable. Its forests, highlands, and river basins support extraordinary biodiversity, including more than 60 percent of the global population of the critically endangered western chimpanzee, and serve as the headwaters for major river systems on which millions of people across the region depend for water, food, and livelihoods. Internationally, Guinea's ecological significance is recognized through four UNESCO Biosphere Reserves, 16 Ramsar Wetland Sites, and the Mount Nimba transboundary World Heritage Site, among others. The country also holds roughly one-third of the world's known bauxite reserves and the largest untapped high-grade iron-ore deposit on the planet, at Simandou. 2. This natural wealth is under growing pressure. Guinea's population of 13.9 million, 65 percent of whom are under 25, is growing at nearly 3 percent per year, driving expansion of subsistence agriculture into forested areas. The mining sector, which accounts for over 90 percent of export earnings, brings infrastructure, displacement, and ecosystem fragmentation in its wake. These issues extend beyond national borders, as the degradation of the Fouta Djallon's watersheds nay undermine water security and ecosystem services in downstream countries, exacerbating regional vulnerabilities to climate variability and food insecurity. Climate change compounds these stresses: temperatures are projected to rise by up to 3°C by 2060 and rainfall is becoming less predictable. Guinea ranks among the world's most climate-vulnerable nations. 3. Guinea is at a critical juncture in managing its exceptional but threatened natural wealth. Two developments since the parent project’s approval in 2021 have made integrated natural resource management both more urgent and more achievable. The launch of the Simandou iron-ore project in 2025, one of the largest mining investments in sub-Saharan African history, sharply increases pressure on forests and communities but also creates new opportunities to integrate biodiversity protection into infrastructure planning and to channel mining revenues toward conservation. At the same time, Guinea has strengthened its environmental governance, establishing a national parks agency, updating its forestry and protected area regulations, and committing to an expanded protected areas network through a collaborative roadmap with the mining sector launched in April 2026. This project supports Guinea in turning these commitments into lasting results. Sectoral and Institutional Context Page 3 Official Use Only The World Bank Guinea Natural Resources, Mining and Environmental Management Project (P168613) – Additional Financing Request 1 4. The Guinea Natural Resources, Mining and Environmental Management Project (PGRNME, P168613) has built strong institutional foundations since becoming effective in 2021. It has supported reforms in environmental and mining governance, strengthened the National Parks Agency (OGPNRF), expanded environmental monitoring of large infrastructure projects, and improved management of protected areas across four priority landscapes. In the mining sector, the project has supported training and participation by small and medium enterprises in mining value chains and improved the management of the national mining cadastre and geological survey. 5. Despite this progress, several structural gaps remain. Long-term conservation financing is fragile and overwhelmingly dependent on short-term donor cycles. Guinea has made international commitments — including under the Global Biodiversity Framework and its Nationally Determined Contributions — that require dedicated, predictable funding mechanisms to be credibly fulfilled. The emerging carbon and biodiversity finance landscape offers Guinea an unprecedented opportunity to unlock results-based climate finance, but this requires institutional readiness that the parent project has only partially built. Finally, the onset of Simandou has created demand for policy instruments, such as a carbon pricing mechanism for the extractive sector, that can incentivize emissions efficiency before the industry's emissions profile becomes entrenched. 6. This Additional Financing advances three interconnected priorities to address these gaps: (i) establishing a dedicated Conservation Trust Fund to provide long-term, sustainable financing for biodiversity protection; (ii) developing a Forest Emission Reduction Program that generates carbon revenues to reduce deforestation and forest degradation; and (iii) piloting a Carbon Pricing Instrument to incentivize emissions efficiency in the mining sector. Together, these instruments are expected to enable up to US$100 million in additional private capital for Guinea's green economy. C. The PGRNME and its additionnal financing support Guinea to strengthen the institutions, policies, and tools needed to manage its mineral and natural resources in a coordinated and sustainable manner. The project remain organized around four components, aimed respectively at: strenghtening environmental and mining institutions, regulations, and coordination (Component A); improving mining-sector governance, transparency, and local economic participation(Component B); strenghtening environmental management and protected-area systems (Component C); and Page 4 Official Use Only The World Bank Guinea Natural Resources, Mining and Environmental Management Project (P168613) – Additional Financing Request 1 project management, monitoring, and environmental and social oversight (Component D). The Additional Financing expand activities under Components A, C, and D. Component B, which supports mining policies, institutions, governance, and economic integration, is unchanged. 11. Under Component A: Improving the Institutional Framework for Mining and Environment, the Additional Financing will support the operationalization and capitalization of the Guinea Conservation Trust Fund. The Trust Fund will provide a long-term financing mechanism for protected-area management, biodiversity conservation, forest monitoring, and community livelihood activities. It will establish dedicated financing windows for protected areas and community livelihoods, the Fouta Djallon Highlands, and forest and ecosystem monitoring. The component will also support preparation of a land-use master plan for the Fouta Djallon Highlands. 12. Component A will additionally support pilot implementation of Guinea’s Carbon Pricing Instrument for the bauxite and iron-ore sectors. Activities will include strengthening emissions reporting and monitoring systems, building the capacity of relevant public institutions and mining companies, and supporting stakeholder consultation. Revenues generated through the instrument are expected to support a mitigation fund for investments in emissions reduction, including renewable energy, forestry, and lower-carbon mining practices. 13. Under Component C: Environmental and Natural Resources Management, the Additional Financing will help Guinea prepare a Forest Emission Reduction Program that could enable access to results-based climate finance. Support will strengthen national systems for forest monitoring, reporting, and verification; develop arrangements for fair and transparent sharing of potential carbon revenues; strengthen environmental and social management; and provide legal and technical support on carbon rights, transactions, and registries. 14. The Additional Financing will also expand support for protected-area management and community livelihoods. It will strengthen the operational capacity of the National Parks Agency (OGPNRF), including planning, monitoring, patrols, and staff capacity. In buffer and transition areas around protected areas, the project will support community-led restoration, agroforestry, sustainable forest production, non-timber forest products, and other alternative income- generating activities. These activities are intended to improve livelihoods, particularly for women and young people, while reducing pressure on forests and biodiversity. 15. The Additional Financing will expand the project’s geographic coverage from four to six priority landscapes. New areas include the Fouta Djallon Highlands and the proposed Pinselly-Soyah National Park. It will also deepen support in the project’s existing landscapes: Kounounkan Forest, the Upper Niger Basin, the Guinea Forest region, and marine protected areas. 16. Under Component D: Project Management, the project will continue to finance coordination, financial management, procurement, environmental and social oversight, monitoring and evaluation, communication, and knowledge management for the extended implementation period. 17. The Additional Financing is expected to support improved management of 5,195,097 hectares of terrestrial areas, of which 1,369,251 hectares beyond the parent project’s coverage; create or improve 5,500 jobs in and around targeted protected areas, including 3,850 for women and youth; and enable up to US$100 million in private capital. Implementation 18. Implementation agencies. The Additional Financing will be implemented through two complementary channels. Activities related to the Carbon Pricing Instrument pilot and Forest Emission Reduction Program readiness will be implemented by the existing Project Implementation Unit (PIU), which is jointly staffed by the Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development (MEDD) and the Ministry of Mining and Geology (MMG). All remaining Additional Financing activities — primarily those related to the Conservation Trust Fund, protected-area management, and community livelihoods — will be implemented directly by the Guinea Conservation Trust Fund (CTF). 19. Governance arrangements. The Additional Financing will operate under the project's existing governance structures. Strategic oversight is provided by the PGRNME Steering Committee, which meets twice yearly and is chaired by the Secretary General of the MMG, with the Secretary General of the MEDD as deputy. Day-to-day operational oversight is provided by the PGRNME Technical Monitoring Committee, which meets monthly and comprises focal points from relevant ministries, chaired by the Director General of the Strategic and Development Office (BSD) of the MEDD, with the Director General of the BSD of the MMG as deputy. 20. Guinea’s CTF structure. The CTF serves as an autonomous long term financing mechanism for biodiversity conservation, designed to operate independently of political and budgetary cycles. Resources mobilized from bilateral, multilateral partners and private corporate donors are transferred to the FCG-UK. The FCG-UK receives and invests contributions and periodically transfers funds to the FCG. FCG-UK is governed by a Board of Trustees under English charity law. The FCG is governed by a separate Board of Directors and managed by a Secretariat, which allocates and disburses grants to eligible institutions managing protected areas and biodiversity. The FCG Secretariat is responsible for implementing Board decisions, monitoring grant execution, and ensuring adherence to standards of good governance, transparency, and international norms. 21. CTF financing mechanisms. The CTF will use two financing mechanisms: a sinking fund to finance immediate implementation activities and accelerate the CTF's operationalization, and an endowment with dedicated sub- windows whose annual investment returns will finance activities on a sustainable, long-term basis. This structure is designed to insulate conservation financing from political and budgetary cycles and to demonstrate to future investors that the CTF operates with strong governance and financial discipline. 22. Fund flows. Contributions from PROGREEN, SCALE (MRV window), and GBFF will be transferred to the FCG- UK, which will make scheduled transfers to the FCG. This arrangement will be governed by a tripartite subsidiary agreement between the Government of Guinea, the FCG-UK, and the FCG, which will set out the terms for fund transfers, fiduciary obligations, and reporting requirements. The relationship between FCG-UK and FCG is goverened by a Framework Agreement. PMIF contributions for the Carbon Pricing Instrument and SCALE contributions for the Forest Emission Reduction Program will flow through standard World Bank disbursement channels to the PIU. 23.
