PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING Project status 1. The Girls Empowerment and Quality Education for All Project (PEREQT, P169222) was approved on December 11, 2020, and became effective on April 15, 2021. The original operation combined an International Development Association (IDA) grant of US$15.0 million and a Global Partnership for Education (GPE) grant of US$2.2 million to equip girls with life skills and improve student learning outcomes for all. It comprised five components: (i) empowering girls through the acquisition of life skills and the promotion of a safe school environment; (ii) tackling learning poverty; (iii) reaching the most vulnerable; (iv) project coordination, monitoring and evaluation, and capacity building; and (v) a zero-cost Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC). 2. The project has successfully adapted its design and financing to accelerate implementation of priority reforms and scale up impact. A Level 2 restructuring approved in September 2023 discontinued Component 3, reallocated resources to priority activities under Components 1 and 4, revised the Results Framework, and extended the original GPE grant closing date to October 31, 2024. In March 2025, GPE Additional Financing (AF) of US$3.656 million was approved to expand life-skills and violence-prevention interventions, rehabilitate WASH facilities in two additional schools, strengthen preschool quality, digital learning and inclusive education, operationalize teacher-management reforms, and reinforce project management and the Education Management Information System (SIGE). The related amendment extended the IDA closing date to December 31, 2026. However, the AF became effective only on September 10, 2025, leaving less than sixteen months for implementation of the expanded scope. 3. Overall project performance remains satisfactory. Progress towards the Project Development Objective (PDO) and Implementation Progress are rated Satisfactory. Component 1 is rated Highly Satisfactory, while Components 2 and 4 are rated Moderately Satisfactory. As of September 2026, IDA disbursement reached 84.79 percent and grant disbursement 64.96 percent. Most PDO and intermediate-results indicators are on track or have already achieved their end targets. 4. Component 1 has delivered particularly strong results. Girls’ and boys’ clubs are operational in all 22 target schools, with cumulative participation reaching 6,262 students, including 4,049 girls and 2,213 boys, substantially exceeding project targets. Seven school-based pilot projects are being implemented successfully, with average financial execution of approximately 70 percent. Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) and accessibility investments have expanded to 17 schools, contributing to safer and more inclusive learning environments. Rehabilitation works are nearing completion across the portfolio, with several sites expected to be completed shortly and the remaining works progressing in line with updated implementation schedules. In the Autonomous Region of Príncipe (RAP), implementation has regained momentum following the remobilization of personnel and delivery of materials, with overall execution reaching approximately 80 percent. Environmental and social activities, including asbestos management, are also well advanced and expected to be completed shortly. 5. Under Component 2, implementation has advanced across several reform areas. Preschool teacher training substantially exceeded its target, and the second round of the national learning assessments (AALEB and AALES) was successfully administered in June 2026. Progress has also been made in preschool quality reforms, inclusive education, and digital learning, while contracting for the structured teaching materials package has been completed. However, the development and rollout of the materials, procurement of assistive equipment, operationalization of The World Bank Girls Empowerment and Quality Education for All Project (P169222) Page 2 the Specialized Service Center (CAE), and implementation of teacher-management and school-leadership reforms remain ongoing and require additional time to reach full operationalization. 6. Under Component 4, considerable progress has been made in strengthening sector management and data systems. The SIGE diagnostic, operational design, technical specifications, procurement documentation, and sustainability arrangements have been completed, establishing the foundation for nationwide implementation. While supplier selection has taken longer than anticipated, the remaining activities are largely focused on equipment delivery, installation, training, and system operationalization. The CERC has not been activated. Rationale for restructuring 7. The restructuring is required to complete and operationalize a limited number of financed activities that remain critical to the achievement and sustainability of project outcomes. Overall project performance remains satisfactory, most outcome and intermediate targets are on track or already achieved, and adequate financial resources are available to complete the remaining activities. However, several key investments, including those introduced under the AF, such as structured teaching materials and teacher training, the operationalization of CAE, implementation of the Digital Learning Program (DLP), remaining WASH investments, and full deployment of SIGE, require additional time to transition from procurement and implementation into operational results. 8. The need for additional time reflects implementation constraints rather than shortcomings in project performance. The AF became effective in September 2025, leaving less than sixteen months to implement the expanded scope. In addition, several technically complex procurement processes required revisions and relaunching, while temporary constraints in the supply of construction materials and workforce availability affected the pace of selected rehabilitation works. Procurement, importation, installation, testing, and user training requirements also extended implementation timelines for key investments. Furthermore, several activities must be aligned with the academic calendar to ensure effective rollout and measurement of results. 9. The Government has formally requested a six-month extension, and the June 2026 mission confirmed that sufficient resources remain available to finance the outstanding activities and corresponding project management. Given the satisfactory implementation progress achieved to date and the clearly defined set of remaining activities, the proposed extension will enable the completion and operationalization of key investments, consolidate project results, and support an orderly project closure. This is expected to be the final extension of the project. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES Proposed changes 10. This paper seeks the approval of a Level 2 restructuring to: (i) extend the closing dates of IDA-D7340 and TF- C7516 by six months, from December 31, 2026, to June 30, 2027, with a withdrawal application deadline of October 30, 2027; and (ii) reallocate proceeds among IDA disbursement categories. No changes are proposed to the PDO, project components, results framework, implementation arrangements, or environmental and social risk classification. The proposed extension will also help mitigate implementation disruptions associated with the September 2026 electoral process. 13. The extension is supported by satisfactory project performance, adequate fiduciary arrangements, and the availability of resources to complete the remaining activities. Given the satisfactory progress achieved to date and the clearly defined implementation agenda, the proposed extension will support completion and operationalization of key investments, consolidation of project results, and an orderly project closure. This is expected to be the final extension of the project. Financing reallocation 14. A reallocation of IDA proceeds is proposed to ensure adequate financing for the completion of remaining project activities during the proposed extension period. Specifically, the restructuring seeks approval to reallocate SDR 753,370.20 of unutilized resources currently allocated to Category 5 (Refund of the Preparation Advance) to Category 1 (Goods, Works, Non-Consulting Services, Consulting Services, Training, Workshops, and Stipends). The proposed reallocation reflects lower-than-anticipated financing needs under Category 5 and increased financing requirements under Category 1 as project activities continue to move from procurement into implementation. The total IDA allocation would remain unchanged at SDR 11 million, and there would be no change to the applicable financing percentages. 15. The proposed reallocation will ensure adequate financing for the completion and operationalization of the remaining project activities during the extension period. Additional resources under Category 1 will finance ongoing works, equipment and materials, technical assistance, training, structured teaching materials, SIGE deployment, inclusive education interventions, learning assessments, and other priority investments. The reallocation does not require additional financing or involve any cancellation of resources and will support full utilization of available project funds through project closure. 16. No reallocation between project components or within either GPE grant is proposed. TF-B3318 is closed and fully utilized, while allocations under TF-C7516 remain unchanged. The proposed extension of TF-C7516 to June 30, 2027, will allow completion and operationalization of the activities financed under the AF, including life-skills interventions, WASH investments, digital learning, inclusive education, teacher support, and education system strengthening.
