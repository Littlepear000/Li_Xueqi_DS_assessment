PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Introduction 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the approval of the World Bank’s Division Director for a Level 2 Restructuring of the Unleashing the Blue Economy of the Caribbean (UBEC) project, following its mid-term review (MTR) to (i) revise description of activities under the Components 1 and 2 undertaken by each participating country and the OECS Commission, (ii) reallocate funds across components and (iii) adjust the results framework to clarify indicator definitions and calibrate endline targets. This is the fourth restructuring of the project. B. Background 2. The UBEC Series of Projects (SOP) is designed as three phases of five-year operations, implemented over a cumulative fifteen-year period, involving multi-sectoral, Blue Economy (BE) investments in Caribbean countries to capitalize their untapped coastal and marine natural resources, strengthen climate resilience and accelerate economic recovery from the impacts of the global polycrisis. Phase I of the SOP focuses on integrating BE activities in the tourism, fisheries, and waste management sectors towards a shared goal of regional development in the eastern Caribbean. 3. UBEC Phase I, approved by the Board on May 2, 2022, includes financing in the amount of US$92.6 million1. The first phase is implemented in Grenada, Saint Lucia (SLU) and Saint-Vincent and the Grenadines (SVG), with the Organization of Eastern Caribbean States (OECS) Commission playing a crucial role in advancing regional BE activities. 4. The Project Development Objective (PDO) of the UBEC SOP is “To strengthen the enabling environment for the blue economy, economic recovery, and resilience of selected coastal assets in participating countries and provide immediate and effective response to an eligible emergency”, through four components, namely: (a) Component 1- Strengthening Governance, Policies, and Capacity Building (US$9 million IDA): This component supports development of regional and national policies, strategies, and institutions necessary to support low- carbon and climate-resilient action, foster economic recovery and job creation, narrow the gender gap, and improve sustainable management of natural resources. It contributes to the integrity and long-term health of coastal and marine natural resources and the ecosystem services they provide. (b) Component 2- Scale up Access to Finance and Infrastructure Investment (US$47.6 million IDA): This component supports (1) the establishment of a regional Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) matching grants program and business development services to foster recovery and resilience of MSMEs, particularly those that are women-led or owned, create new jobs in the BE, and mitigate the socio-economic impacts of climate change and the pandemic, (2) the expanded access to the established Caribbean Ocean and Aquaculture Sustainability Facility fisheries risk insurance scheme to protect fishers’ livelihoods against extreme climatic events, and (3) the scale-up of coastal infrastructure investments to ensure they are sustainable, technologically advanced, and climate-resilient in the targeted sectors of Tourism, Fisheries and Solid Waste Management. 1 This includes an original US$56 million in IDA Credits (national and regional) and grants (regional); an additional financing of US$10 million each to Governments of Grenada, SLU and SVG, cumulating to US$30 million in IDA Credits from its Crisis Response Window (CRW)-Early Response Financing (ERF) approved by the World Bank’s Board in April 2024, to replenish the financing gap resulting from the activation of the CERC in each participating country to address the emerging food security crisis. It also includes an additional financing of US$2.60 million in IDA Credits for the rehabilitation of Fort Charlotte in SVG, which was approved in June 2025 and the PROBLUE grants of US$4 million (US$1 million for each participating country and the OECS Commission), which closed on September 30, 2025. Only OECS Commission utilized part of the PROBLUE grant with the participating countries returning the funds to the World Bank for lack of expenditures. Official Use Only Page 1 The World Bank Unleashing the Blue Economy of the Caribbean (UBEC) (P171833) (c) Component 3 - Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC) (US$30 million IDA): This component helps participating countries to rapidly respond to eligible crisis or emergency, defined as “an event that has caused, or is likely to imminently cause, a major adverse economic and/or social impact associated with natural or human-made crises or disasters.” (d) Component 4 - Project Management, Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E), and Communication (US$6 million IDA): This component supports effective and efficient implementation of the project, M&E, regional collaboration, and communications. It finances goods, equipment, consulting and non-consulting services, training, and operating costs of the Project Implementation Units (PIU) established at the OECS Commission and in each participating country for expenditures related to implementing project activities. C. Implementation Status 5. UBEC Phase I is in its fourth year of implementation. The project secured its first additional financing with a restructuring, approved by the Regional Vice President on April 16, 2024 2, the second restructuring3 was approved by the Division Director on January 6, 2025, and the second additional financing with a restructuring4, also approved by the Division Director, on June 24, 2025. 6. Following the activation of the CERC in May 2023, the implementation focus in the three participating countries had primarily been on the CERC activities to address food insecurity due to global polycrisis. The CERC activities have shown strong government commitment and robust progress with significant disbursement and clear successes within communities. Following the completion of CERC activities in March 2025, there has been small but notable progress on BE-related activities across components with advancements in procurement, and it is anticipated that implementation will further accelerate. 7. The PROBLUE recipient-executed grants of US$1 million each for Grenada, SLU, SVG and OECS Commission closed on September 30, 2025. While OECS Commission utilized US$680,433 of the grant, the grants remained unutilized for Grenada, SLU and SVG, and the amounts have been cancelled as per the closure of the grants. 8. As of the Implementation Status and Results (ISR) Report of March 2026, the progress towards achievement of PDO is rated Moderately Unsatisfactory (MU), due to continuing delays in advancing the BE activities as a result of longer than anticipated transition period from the CERC activities. Therefore, progress towards all PDO indicators and BE-related intermediate results indicators (IRIs) remains limited with no quantifiable targets reported for any except the regional level policies and the MSME program. 2 This first additional financing included US$30 million in equal parts to each of the three participating countries and included a restructuring that entailed (i) modifying the shared Results Framework (RF) to incorporate three new Intermediate Results Indicators (IRI) relevant to the food insecurity activities financed under the three country CERCs and modifying the indicator target achievement date for the original set of SOP indicators to reflect the proposed new project closing date; (ii) extending the SOP closing date for all four Recipients by 12 months to compensate for the extended time the participating countries devoted to preparing their CRW-ERF requests; (iii) reallocating funds between disbursement categories to reflect CERC activation and replenishment from the AF as well as reallocation between disbursement categories to channel the original, unused Project Preparation Advance (PPA) funds for use by each of the country projects during implementation. 3 The second restructuring entailed including and reflecting the Ministry of Mobilization, Implementation and Transformation (MIT) as the Implementing Agency for the project in Grenada with the responsibility for overall project management including monitoring and evaluation of project activities. 4 The second additional Financing with a restructuring entailed (i) a revision in the allocation of Component 2 to accommodate the additional financing of US$2.55m, (ii) a modification of the Result Framework increasing an intermediate result indicator to reflect the additional infrastructure supported, and (iii) a revision of the disbursement estimates for Saint Vincent and the Grenadines. Overall implementation progress is also rated Moderately Satisfactory (MS) on account of mixed component-wise performance, Moderately Satisfactory Fiduciary performance and Satisfactory Environmental and Social (E&S) standards’ compliance. The component-wise progress based on available data as of January 2026 is summarized below. (a) Component 1- Strengthening Governance, Policies, and Capacity Building (Moderately Satisfactory): Activities under the component have gained momentum and are expected to further accelerate in 2026. At the regional level, progress has been made with the endorsement of the OECS Common Sustainable Tourism Policy by the ministerial council in March 2025. A draft OECS Regional Fisheries Policy, Strategy, and Action Plan has also been developed in collaboration with the Caribbean Regional Fisheries Mechanism (CRFM) and will be discussed with all OECS members for future adoption. The regional strategy to reduce Illegal, Unreported and Unregulated (IUU) Fishing and the Regional Sanitary and Phyto Sanitary (SPS) Protocol is in advanced stages and final draft is expected to be finalized by the end of 2026. In Grenada, SLU and SVG, the progress in the component activities under fisheries, tourism and solid waste management are advancing with development of the National Tourism Policy underway in Saint Lucia, and the revision of the National Solid Waste Management Policies on-going in the participating countries. (b) Component 2- Scale up Access to Finance and Infrastructure Investment (Moderately Unsatisfactory): On account of prioritized CERC interventions, activities under this component have lagged. Incremental progress has been achieved in implementing the regional Matching Grants Program (MGP). The first series of grants under Window 1 targeting up to 20 MSMEs and totaling approximately US$0.5m has been awarded in July 2025. Window 2 targeting Value Chain groups was launched in February 2025, with completion of awards anticipated by March 20, 2026. Around US$1.0m is expected to be awarded under this window in the first call. Infrastructure investments across fisheries, tourism and solid waste in the three participating countries have been initiated, and there is a need to increase efforts and accelerate the implementation to ensure all activities are completed before project closing. (c) Component 3 - Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC) (Moderately Satisfactory): On May 5, 2023, in response to the mounting food insecurity crisis in the participating countries, the three countries triggered the CERC. CERC activities were completed in March 2025 following a three-month extension from December 2024. Implementation of CERC activities was strong across all three countries with exceeded targets on two of the three related intermediate result indicators linked to the CERC. More than 10,000 farmers and fisherfolk received production inputs and services, 53 key infrastructures were rehabilitated, and 946 animals of improved breeds were procured. Grenada and SLU have submitted their CERC completion reports, while it is under preparation for SVG. (d) Component 4 - Project Management, Monitoring and Evaluation, and Communication (Moderately Satisfactory): Staffing of the three country-level PIUs has faced challenges mostly linked to the availability of qualified human resources and associated remuneration. Despite delays, the OECS Commission, SLU and Grenada have strong permanent and fully staffed PIU in place, while SVG continues to depend on interim arrangements with delayed and slow recruitments of permanent staff due to procurement bottlenecks. 10. Fiduciary: Overall procurement performance rating for the project is Moderately Satisfactory, despite mixed procurement implementation progress across the participating countries. Challenges include delayed implementation and reporting and sub-optimal fiduciary compliances. The Project's overall financial management (FM) ISR rating is Moderately Satisfactory. Grenada, SVG and the OECS Commission have demonstrated adequate FM performance. The project has overall disbursed 40.6 percent of net IDA commitments as of February 27, 2025, Official Use Only Page 3 The World Bank Unleashing the Blue Economy of the Caribbean (UBEC) (P171833) with US$8.07 million undocumented as advances to the project Designated Accounts. There are no outstanding audit reports for Grenada (accepted on August 31, 2025), SVG (accepted on November 28, 2025) and OECS Commission (accepted on February 3, 2026). For SLU, the audit report has been received on January 14, 2026, but returned by the World Bank requesting clarifications and for revision. 11. Environmental and Social Standards (ESS): In the last reporting period, the performance of Environmental and Social Standards (ESS) across the three participating countries, as well as the OECS Commission, is assessed as Satisfactory. The project has shown significant progress in implementing the ESS, including effective ESS screening, proactive stakeholder consultations, establishing afunctional grievance redressal mechanisms, and diligent application of the Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMF) in accordance with the Environmental and Social Commitment Plans (ESCPs). 12. Covenants: Legal covenants of the project are fully complied with. D. Rationale for restructuring 13. During the MTR of the project conducted in May-June 2025 and in-person missions and virtual technical discussions held with implementing agencies, the key elements of the level 2 restructuring were agreed. The formal letters of request for restructuring were received by the World Bank from the OECS Commission on September 23, 2025; Grenada on September 25, 2025; SLU on September 29, 2025; and SVG on March 6, 2026, respectively. 14. The results of the MTR confirmed (i) the relevance of the Project Development Objective and its alignment with the priorities of the participating countries and the OECS Commission; (ii) the adequacy of the project design for achieving the PDO, (iii) the appropriateness of project implementation and management arrangements with a need for establishing and strengthening regional and national project steering committees, and (iv) the need for timely and stronger compliance with fiduciary and ESS requirements. 15. With BE-related activities deprioritized during the implementation of CERC till March 2025, findings from the MTR highlighted the need for adjustments to revise the original ambition of the project for realistic delivery of concrete results and project outcomes. This necessitates the following changes to the project covering all three participating countries and the OECS Commission: a) Activities will be modified with a focus on refining fisheries, tourism and solid waste management interventions under Component 1 and adjusting physical investments under Component 2. This will help to reflect the realism of interventions in the remaining implementation period (2 years and 4 months) and account for higher construction costs resulting from the inflation experienced during the implementation of the project. These changes are expected to bring enhancements in regulatory strengthening in the three sectors, targeted sectoral policies, climate-resilient investments, and improvements in operations and maintenance of investments. Several foundational activities to support the following phases of the SOP were prioritized. b) The component-wise allocations will be revised to reflect the above-mentioned changes and new activities under Components 1 and 2 and to account for increased project management costs. The increase in project management costs results from an 11-month extension of the project closing date approved in the first restructuring of April 2024. Official Use Only Page 4 The World Bank Unleashing the Blue Economy of the Caribbean (UBEC) (P171833) c) The results framework will be modified consequently, introducing country-level disaggregation of all indicators with specificity in indicator endline targets for accuracy in reporting. The M&E Plan will be correspondingly revised to reflect adjustments in selected indicator definitions, data collection methods, and confirm responsibilities for data collection by National or OECS Commission PIU. These changes are expected to simplify reporting and enable the participating countries and the OECS commission to monitor and track their respective achievements. 16. Further, with the sub-optimal utilization of now-closed PROBLUE grants, activities planned under the grants have been reviewed and select activities have been prioritized for implementation using IDA resources under the originally planned components for SLU, SVG, Grenada and the OECS Commission. Overall, all the results framework indicators will be disaggregated by regional (OECS) and national (SVG, SLU and Grenada) targets and two indicators will be dropped (original IR 2 and 6). The gender dimension associated with PDO indicator 2, Intermediate Result (IR) Indicators 2, 3 and 6 is better clarified through gender-wise disaggregated supplementary indicators. Minor revisions will be made to the definition of IR 1, IR3 and IR6 for enhanced clarity in measuring outputs. A downward adjustment of the target values for Women owned/managed MSMSEs is proposed to better capture the reality of female ownership of eligible MSMEs (supplementary PDO Indicator 2.1). The original indicator IR2. Regional Blue Economy data sharing platform operational (Yes/No) will be dropped for less relevance in the current context and lack of corresponding activity or budget as originally planned. Indicator numbering has been revised for clarity to capture supplemental nature. The original IR 6 will be dropped as many Marine Protected Areas (MPAs) across the countries are currently not under active management, and instead activities related to updating management plans or guidelines will be included under IR1. The revised IR6 will be reworded to “fishworkers” to include workers along the fish value chain which are predominantly women. 20. The Monitoring and Evaluation Plan will be refined to include relevant clarifications in definitions and descriptions, data sources, methodology and responsibility for data collection. Annex 2 summarizes changes to the M&E plan. 21. Paris Alignment: A Paris Alignment assessment was conducted for the new activities introduced under this restructuring — (i) the procurement of fuel-efficient waste collection vehicles and basic separation systems in Union Island, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines (SVG); and (ii) the improvement of the Deglos landfill in Saint Lucia (works and equipment to extend its lifespan). All new activities are consistent with the respective countries' Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs), which prioritize climate resilience and emissions reduction including in the solid waste sector. On mitigation, replacing aging high-emission trucks with fuel-efficient vehicles reduces direct GHG emissions from fleet operations, without creating carbon lock-in; the landfill improvement and upgrade activities, while involving existing landfill infrastructure, are designed to enhance operational efficiency and extend service life without introducing new fossil fuel infrastructure or carbon lock-in risk. Any residual methane emissions implications are considered acceptable given the absence of viable alternative waste disposal infrastructure in these small island contexts. On adaptation and resilience, all three countries face significant exposure to hurricanes, flooding, and volcanic activity. The investments have been sited and designed to account for these risks and aim to strengthen the resilience of waste management services under adverse conditions by upgrading aging infrastructure and improving operational continuity, without introducing material new exposure to climate hazards beyond the existing project context.
