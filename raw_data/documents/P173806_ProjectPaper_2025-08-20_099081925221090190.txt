PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING Project Status 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks approval for a proposed Level Two restructuring of the Malawi COVID-19 Emergency Response and Health Systems Preparedness Project. The parent project, financed by an International Development Association (IDA) grant in the amount of US$7.0 million, prepared under the Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA) Fast Track COVID-19 Facility, was approved by the Board on April 14, 2020, and became effective on April 30, 2020. 2. The project’s Development Objective (PDO) is to “prevent, detect and respond to the threat posed by COVID- 19 and strengthen national systems for public health preparedness and provide immediate and effective response to an eligible crisis or emergency”. The project is comprised of four components: Component 1 – Emergency COVID-19 Response; Component 2 – Supporting National and Sub-National Prevention and Preparedness; Component 3 – Implementation Management and Monitoring and Evaluation; and Component 4 – Contingent Emergency Response. 3. The project has been restructured three times. The first restructuring was part of the additional financing (AF1) to the project, an IDA grant in the amount of US$30 million approved on June 18, 2021, and declared effective on August 23, 2021. The restructuring reflected the inclusion of vaccination activities and revisions to the components, Results Framework, institutional and implementation arrangements, and closing date. The second additional financing (AF2) consisting of US$49.9 million from IDA (a credit in the amount of US$25.4 million equivalent and a grant in the amount of US$24.5 million equivalent) and a US$10.0 million grant from the Global Financing Facility for Women, Children and Adolescents was approved on June 21, 2022, and became effective on September 13, 2022. The purpose of AF2 was to further scale up affordable and equitable access to COVID-19 vaccines, sustain support to ensure effective vaccine deployment through vaccination system strengthening, and enhance preparedness and response activities. 4. The third restructuring, approved on April 30, 2024, did not include AF. This restructuring amended the Project Development Objective (PDO) to incorporate an objective related to the Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC); added CERC-funded activities on cholera response; revised selected indicators and target values in the Results Framework; reallocated funds across components and disbursement categories to accommodate the CERC activation using the uncommitted balances from the COVID-19 vaccine procurement and deployment budget; and revised the disbursement projection accordingly. 5. The project’s progress towards achievement of the PDO and overall implementation progress have been rated Satisfactory and Moderately Satisfactory, respectively, in the last Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) of July 17, 2025. All five PDO-level indicators have been achieved and 16 of the 26 intermediate results indicators (IRIs) have reached or surpassed their end-of-project targets. Seven IRIs are on track to be achieved by the project closing date, while three are off-track. The project's environmental and social performance has remained moderately satisfactory, and the Ministry of Health has demonstrated adequate capacity to manage environmental and social risks. 6. There are no outstanding financial audits for this project. Rationale for Restructuring Official Use Only Page 1 The World Bank Malawi COVID-19 Emergency Response and Health Systems Preparedness Project (P173806) 7. The Government of Malawi (GoM) formally requested for project restructuring in a letter dated August 1, 2025. The project supports the construction of an Infectious Disease Unit and extensive renovations at four central (referral) hospitals. The restructuring is prompted by significant cost overruns identified as the scope and scale of these civil works became more defined during project implementation. The original budget assumptions did not fully account for inflationary pressures due to recent macroeconomic factors, which has affected costs of building materials, labor, and equipment. Hence, this has necessitated increased flexibility in funding allocation. The restructuring proposes reallocating funds across disbursement categories to cater for these financing shortfalls. The funds will be sourced from savings generated from completed activities and from activities that the GoM has determined are no longer required. These activities include procurement of motor cycles, solarization of selected health facilities and deployment of COVID-19 vaccines. This reallocation will not adversely affect the achievement of the PDO. To date, US$75.6 million or 80 percent of the total project financing has been disbursed. By the closing date, the remaining balance is estimated to be fully disbursed. The construction work is expected to be fully completed by November 30, 2025. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 8.
