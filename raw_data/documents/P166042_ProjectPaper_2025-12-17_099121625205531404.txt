PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Introduction 1. This paper seeks Regional Vice President’s approval to restructure the Guinea-Mali Interconnection Project (P166042). The restructuring proposes the following changes: (i) extending the project closing date by 12 months from December 31, 2025, to December 31, 2026; (ii) updating the results framework to reflect the change in end targets to match with the new closing date; and (iii) reflecting corresponding changes in disbursement estimates. B. Project Background 2. The project was approved by the World Bank’s Board of Executive Directors on July 13, 2018 and became effective on March 24 and May 29, 2019, respectively for Mali and Guinea, with an original closing date of June 30, 2024. The total project financing is US$380.8 million equivalent, of which U$84.00 million equivalent from IDA (US$37.50 million IDA credit (Credit No. 62610) and US$37.50 million IDA grant (Grant No. D3360) to the Republic of Guinea, and US$4.50 million IDA credit (IDA Credit No. 62620) and US$4.50 million IDA grant (IDA Grany No. D3370) to the Republic of Mali) and co-financing from the African Development Bank (AfDB Unit Account (UC) 60 million - US$71.9 million equivalent), the European Investment Bank (EIB - EUR 130 million-US$149.6 million equivalent), the Economic Community Of West African States (ECOWAS)’ the ECOWAS Bank for Investment and Development (EBID - US$42.2 million) and the West African Development Bank (BOAD - 18 billion FCFA1- US$33.1 million equivalent). 3. The overall Project Development Objectives (PDOs) are to (i) increase electricity supply to the Eastern part of Guinea; (ii) enable electricity trade between Guinea and Mali; and (iii) increase Guinea’s electricity export capability towards other West African Power Pool (WAPP) countries. As presented in Table 1, the overall operation involves the: (i) construction of a 715.6 kilometers (km) double circuit transmission line from the Nzérékoré Substation in Guinea to the Sanankoroba Substation in Mali; (ii) extension of the Sanankoroba Substation in Mali; (iii) construction of five new substations in Siguiri, Fomi, Kankan, Kérouané, and Beyla (in Guinea); and (iv) extension of the Nzérékoré Substation in Guinea. In addition, the project is part of a network of interconnections that contribute to the realization of Guinea’s electricity export potential to the WAPP countries. The interconnector is complementary to the Côte d’Ivoire-Sierra Leone- Liberia-Guinea Power System Re-Development Project (CLSG-P113266), the Senegal River Basin Development organization (Organisation pour la Mise en Valeur du fleuve Sénégal OMVS) Transmission Expansion Project (P147921), and The Gambia River Basin Development Organization Project (Organisation pour la Mise en Valeur du fleuve Gambie OMVG) (P146830) financed by the World Bank. 4. The IDA-financed project (US$84.0 million out of US$380.8 million) consists of the following activities: • Component 1—Power Transmission Infrastructure (US$71.8 million out of US$343.8 million)—finances, in Guinea: (i) the construction of a 53.7 km transmission line from the Northeastern Guinean city of Siguiri to the Guinean border with Mali and (ii) the construction of three new substations in Siguiri, Kankan, and Kérouané. In Mali, Component 1 finances the extension of the Sanankoroba Substation. • Component 2—Implementation Support and Capacity Building (US$12.2 million out of US$37 million)—finances (i) support for the development and adoption of the power purchase agreement between the two countries; (ii) the supply of operation and maintenance equipment for the Guinean and Malian power utilities; (iii) the supply and installation of procurement and financial management systems; and (iii) operational costs of the project implementation units (PIUs) and the Joint Implementation Steering Committee. 1 FCFA: Franc de la Communauté Financière Africaine (African Financial Community Franc). The project implementation experienced significant delays in the first four years. The delays since effectiveness in May 2019 to the Mid-Term Review (MTR) in November 2023, were due to (i) border closures and ineffective remote work during the COVID-19 pandemic; (ii) suspension of disbursements after the coups d’etat in Guinea and Mali; (iii) lack of leadership within the PIUs after the coordinators were suspended in Guinea and fired in Mali; (iv) slow mobilization of funding from the Guinean and Malian Governments to compensate project affected persons (PAP); and (v) slow response to contractors’ requests to adjust bidding prices (leading to slow mobilization of contractors’ staff and delayed design studies). Consequently, at the MTR of November 2023, after almost four years of implementation, only 33.35 percent of the project financing was disbursed, and the overall implementation progress rate was estimated at 55 percent. To enable completion of delayed project activities, the World Bank approved a first restructuring, in June 2024, to extend the original project closing date (June 30, 2024) by 18 months until December 31, 2025. C. Project Status and Key Issues 6. Though the project implementation accelerated from June 2024 to June 2025, the overall infrastructure can no longer be completed by the current closing date mainly because of untimely mobilization of government funds for compensations, lengthy price adjustment negotiations, and prioritization of a flagship mining project in Guinea. The joint AfDB/BOAD-financed transmission line in Mali and the EBID-financed transmission line in Guinea are the most delayed. Both the EIB-financed and the World Bank-financed lines and substations had advanced significantly till June 2025 but decelerated because of prioritization of a mining project. On the World Bank-financed project activities, the pace of construction of the power substations and the transmission line (Component 1) almost doubled from June 2024 to June 2025, while the implementation support component (Component 2) provides satisfactory assistance. The noteworthy progress translated into a disbursement increase from 29 percent in November 2023 (MTR) to 70 percent in October 2025. About 87 percent of the World Bank financing has been committed. Overall implementation progress is rated “Moderately Satisfactory,” while progress towards achievement of the PDO was “Moderately Unsatisfactory” in June 2025 and is expected to be upgraded to “Moderately Satisfactory” in the next Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) (after approval of this restructuring). The following paragraphs provide details on the progress made under each component, update on the fiduciary aspects, safeguards, and summarize the implementation status of other donor-financed projects 7. Component 1 - Power transmission infrastructure: The construction of the IDA-financed power transmission infrastructure progressed significantly since the restructuring (June 2024) till June 2025. The Government of Guinea (GoG) provided a portion of compensation funding and responded positively to price adjustment requests from contractors, clearing critical implementation issues. Thus, the pace of implementation accelerated. The construction of the Siguiri, Kankan and Kérouané Substations (all three in Guinea) went from the start-up stage (sites being cleared and less than a Page 8 The World Bank Guinea – Mali Interconnection Project (P166042) third of the equipment delivered) in November 2023 to an advanced implementation stage in April 2025, characterized by: (i) over 60 percent of all power substation equipment delivered on the three sites; (ii) 70 percent completion rate of the construction of both the Siguiri Substation and the Kankan Substation; and (iii) 74 percent completion of the construction of the Kérouané Substation. The construction of the Siguiri - Guinea/Mali border transmission line, which was moving very slowly with few tower foundations made in November 2023, progressed and reached a completion rate of 75 percent as 56 new towers have been erected and 18 new foundations completed out of a total of 167 towers. The extension of the Sanankoroba Power Substation (in Mali), which was at the equipment manufacturing stage (10 percent of completion rate) in November 2023, accelerated and reached 75 percent completion rate in June 2025, as key equipment (transformers, reactors, circuit breakers, etc.) was delivered; civil works advanced; and assembly of external high-voltage equipment underway. Overall, the completion rate of both the power substations and the transmission line segment surged to 74.97 percent in June 2025, up from 34 percent in November 2023. 8. Recent challenges slowed down the pace of implementation, and the infrastructure can no longer be completed by the current closing date of December 2025. In the run-up to the Guinea referendum (with took place in September 2025), the GoG prioritized the completion of the Simandou Iron Ore Project2—the largest privately-financed mining and infrastructure project in Africa—which led to delays in docking boats that carried the energy project equipment at the Conakry Port, and a shortage of cement for civil works. Thus, the delivery of critical equipment for both the construction of the substations and the transmission line was delayed for three months as well as other works requiring cement. By the end of September 2025, the completion rate for the Siguiri, Kankan, and Kérouané Substations (in Guinea) stood at 85.37 percent, 83.81 percent, and 84.06 percent respectively, up from 70 percent, 70 percent, and 74 percent in June 2025, but slower than expected. The construction of the Siguiri - Guinea/Mali border transmission line (in Guinea) progressed slower than expected and was estimated at 83.45 percent on September 30, 2025. As few critical equipment remains to be shipped and delivered, the construction plannings show that both the substations and the transmission line segment will be completed by July 2026, about 7 months after the current closing date (December 31, 2025). In Mali, the ordering of telecommunication equipment for the Sanankoroba Substation was significantly delayed, which was due to the late conclusion of the selection of the manufacturer by the Contractor and its approval by EDM.SA. The revised schedule indicates site delivery by March 2026 and installation around July 2026. 9. Component 2 – Implementation Support and Capacity Building: The component implementation has been satisfactory. Almost all activities (except community activities) have been completed. It supported dialogue and consultations on the development of the Souapiti Hydropower Plant, which has been operating since 2021, with an installed capacity of 450 MW, significantly contributing to increase Guinea export capacity. The component enabled the drafting and updating of the PPA between Guinea and Mali. It further supported rounds of negotiations between the two national energy utilities—Energie du Mali (EDM) and Electricité De Guinée (EDG). Though the two parties have agreed on key elements, agreement on the quantity of electricity to be exported/imported is yet to be finalized. Nonetheless, the two parties have committed to reach final agreement by December 31, 2025. In addition, the component completed a study to connect mines to the transmission infrastructure; provided the Malian power utility, EDM, with operation and maintenance equipment; and purchased/shipped two maintenance trucks expected to be delivered to EDG by December 25, 2025. As part of the implementation of the environmental and social management plan, few community activities remain to be implemented to empower women and support income generating activities. Those activities are planned to be completed by March 2026. It should be noted that services from the supervisory engineering firm (Owner’s Engineer), financed by the AfDB through European Union funding, were renewed until February 2026. Environmental and Social: Environmental and social safeguard performance is rated Moderately Satisfactory, and there are no unresolved issues. The Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) system is functional in Guinea and Mali. The portion of the compensation funding provided by the GoG was used to compensate all 2,923 people affected by the construction of the World Bank-financed Siguiri-Guinea/Mali Border transmission line. On other transmission line segments in Guinea, there are 97 people awaiting compensation. They will be compensated in the following months, as the GoG recently provided another portion of compensation funding. In Mali, the World Bank-financed Sanankoroba Substation extension does not require compensation as works are carried out within the closed substation site, owned by EDM. However, the implementation of the resettlement action plan for the section of the transmission line (from the Guinea-Mali border to Sanankoroba, financed by the AfDB and WADB) has been delayed. The AfDB recently agreed that a portion of its financing can be used to pay compensations, pending the submission and clearance of the 2024 annual financial audit, now under AfDB review. 12. Status of other donor-funded projects: While all donor-financed infrastructure will not be completed by the current closing date (December 31, 2025), the EBID-financed transmission line in Guinea and the joint AfDB/BOAD- financed transmission line in Mali are the most delayed and are planned to be completed by November 2026. The completion rate of both the EIB-financed infrastructure and the World Bank-financed infrastructure are similar; estimated between 82 to 87 percent. With the recent delays in boat docking at the Conakry Port and cement shortage in Guinea, both infrastructures are planned to be completed by July 2026. However, the construction of the EBID-financed Fomi - Siguiri Transmission Line is significantly behind schedule, with a completion rate of only 54.7 percent. This significant delay is mainly caused by the unresolved contractor’s price adjustment request (which has taken over 12 months) by EBID, which depends on Exim Bank India’s feedback as EBID financing came from a loan from Exim Bank India. EBID recently indicated that the price adjustment request will be positively addressed. Nonetheless, considering the delay, the common supervisory engineering firm (Owner’s Engineer) estimates that the construction of the line will be completed by October 2026, 10 months after the current closing date. In Mali, the transmission line financed by both the AfDB and the BOAD is also considerably behind schedule. 104 kilometers (km) of the line route—out of a total of 127 km—have not yet been released to the contractor due to lack of funds (from the Government of Mali) to compensate people affected by the project. To address the issue, the AfDB agreed that part of its financing be used to pay for compensation, subject to submission and clearance of the 2024 annual financial audit to release funding. The financial audit was received and the World Bank cleared it on December 02, 2025. As the audit was submitted, the transmission line is expected to be completed by November 2026, 11 months after the closing date. Rationale for Restructuring 14. More time is required to complete the World Bank-financed infrastructure activities and to enable the commissioning of the overall multi-donor financed infrastructure required to achieve the PDO. As explained above, both Guinea and Mali have encountered significant delays, primarily due to late delivery of essential equipment and a shortage of cement, which are critical to construction progress. In addition, to ensure adequate commissioning of the World Bank- financed transmission line and power substations, the remaining segments of the transmission infrastructure must be completed. As the joint AfDB/BOAD-financed transmission line in Mali is the most delayed and planned to be completed by November 2026, the commissioning of the overall infrastructure—including the World Bank-financed infrastructure segment—will be carried out in December 2026. Therefore, a 12-month extension, from December 31, 2025, to December 31, 2026, is critical to ensure full completion and operation of the multi-donor-financed infrastructure. 15. The deteriorating security situation in Mali could lead to additional delays, which underscores the need for a 12-month extension of the closing date. Since September 205, a terrorism-affiliated group has imposed a fuel supply blockade to Bamako (capital city) and key supply routes. The blockade triggered severe fuel shortages, causing electricity blackouts, school closures, and surge in fuel prices. Public transport and logistics were severely impacted. Some countries encouraged repatriation of their citizens. The contractor for the Sanankoroba substation extension has announced its intention to repatriate foreign staff, and the PIU is discussing replacements with local staff. Works have slowed down and the repatriation of foreign contractor staff could lead to further delays on supply of remaining equipment and installation. 16. The PDO remains highly relevant for the two countries whose governments committed to its achievement. By increasing electricity supply to Northern Guinea and enabling electricity trade between Guinea and Mali (PDO), the project will contribute to the achievement of the electricity access goals, which will support socioeconomic development. In Guinea, the project is one of the key projects in the Government’s flagship national development program, “Simandou 2040”, and is also highlighted in the Guinea 2025 Energy Compact, adopted as part of the M300 initiative. The project will provide a critical 588 km power transmission backbone, from which distribution networks are being constructed (funded by the European Union and the AfDB) to provide communities with access to electricity. Thus, both the Minister of Energy and the Prime Minister of Guinea have visited the project sites and have requested contractors to provide firm commitments to complete the infrastructure. In Mali, the Government relies on the project to significantly reduce the Page 11 The World Bank Guinea – Mali Interconnection Project (P166042) country power deficit and shortages. The available installed power capacity in 2025 (500 MW) is less than the peak power demand (621 MW) during the dry seasons, leading to widespread power outages. Therefore, the Malian power utility, EDM, has been negotiating the amount of power to import through the transmission line with the Guinea power utility, EDG. In addition, from the transmission line in Mali, distribution networks are under construction to provide electricity services to communities located along the line corridor. 17. The restructuring with12-month extension of closing date will enable satisfactory achievement of the PDO. The PDO outcome 3—to increase Guinea’s electricity export capability towards other WAPP countries—was met, and the two other PDO outcomes will be achieved by the proposed extended project closing date. The PDO Component 1—to increase energy supplied in the Eastern part of Guinea—will be achieved, as the three infrastructure segments that are required to transfer power from Southern Guinea to Eastern Guinea are expected to be completed by October 2026. It should be noted however that the PDO achievement is dependent on the completion of all donors-financed investments and not just the ones financed by the World Bank. As indicated in the project status section, (i) the EIB-financed 355 km Nzérékoré- Kankan transmission line will be completed by July 2026, whereas the (ii) the AfDB-financed Kankan-Fomi transmission line will be completed by April 2026, and the EBID-financed Fomi-Siguiri transmission line by October 2026. The PDO Component 2—to enable electricity trade between Guinea and Mali—is at an advanced stage and will be achieved. The project enabled the drafting of the PPA by the two state-owned power utilities (EDG in Guinea and EDM in Mali). It supported rounds of negotiations between Guinea and Mali representatives. The draft PPA is being shared for a final round of comments. It is expected that a PPA will be signed and effective as soon as the infrastructure is completed and commissioned. II.
