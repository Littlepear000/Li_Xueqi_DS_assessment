STRATEGIC CONTEXT 1. This Project Appraisal Document (PAD) outlines the rationale and objectives of Phase III of the Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA) to support the implementation of the Progestão Program in Brazil, comprising the state of Sergipe. The MPA, approved by the World Bank’s Executive Directors on March 18, 2025 (P181608), along with a first operation in the amount of US$50 million to the state of Rio Grande do Sul as Phase I of the MPA, is backed by a US$1 billion credit line guaranteed by the Government of Brazil (GoB). Its objective is to enhance public expenditure efficiency and fiscal stability in Brazilian states. The MPA is designed to incentivize and support structural reforms across Brazilian states, incorporating lessons learned from previous stand-alone operations. Its design enables innovative interventions across states, promotes economies of scale, and allows for tailored solutions to meet the specific needs of each state. Phase III will adhere to the MPA’s objectives and framework of advancing public sector reforms, enhancing resource management efficiency, and complementary modernization efforts to improve fiscal sustainability. This document outlines the details of Phase III, the “Progestão Sergipe: “Public Sector Management Efficiency” operation. A. Project Strategic Context 2. Brazilian states continue to face structural fiscal vulnerabilities underpinned by three interlinked dynamics. First, real growth in personnel expenditures, driven by automatic career progression, fragmented payroll systems, and uneven implementation of pension reforms, has persistently outpaced revenue growth, intensifying fiscal pressures. Second, the increase in mandatory spending has further constrained budget flexibility, crowding out strategic investments, and limiting the states’ capacity for responsive policy action. Third, while Payment Capacity Index (Capacidade de Pagamento, CAPAG)1 ratings have improved in the post-pandemic period, reflecting stronger debt repayment capacity, this progress contrasts with continued deterioration of expenditure dynamics,2 underscoring the urgency of implementing comprehensive administrative and fiscal reforms. 3. States also continue to experience fiscal challenges, with total personnel spending reaching R$730.3 billion (US$138.1 billion) in 2024, up from R$540 billion (US$102.1 billion) in 2021. Subnational governments employ around 88 percent of Brazil’s public servants and account for 75 percent of the public wage bill, significantly limiting fiscal space. By the second semester of 2025, the Executive branch payroll absorbed 41.7 percent of states’ net current revenue (NCR), close to 50 percent when all branches are included. Structural rigidities, including automatic career progression, fragmented payroll systems, and inequitable pension arrangements, continue to exert fiscal pressure, further compounded by uneven implementation of the 2019 pension reform. With constitutional constraints limiting options such as dismissals or wage reductions, current reforms in human resource management (HRM) focus mainly on cost containment through system upgrades, automated payroll audits, workforce optimization, and process automation to improve expenditure efficiency to support fiscal sustainability. 4. Essential services in health, education, and social assistance, representing over 40 percent of states budgets, are undermined by inefficiencies and weak financial management. Between 2019 and 2024, federal reimbursements covered only 27.98 percent of states health spending due to verification delays. Education resources for student transport, school meals, and direct funding are poorly managed with outdated processes and procedures, while the Unified Social Assistance System (Sistema Único de Assistência Social, SUAS) in most states lacks financial management tools and systems. B. Sectoral and Institutional Context 5. Sergipe has improved fiscal management, but challenges remain. The state secured an upgrade in its CAPAG rating from B+ in 2023 to A in 2024, reflecting adherence to accounting standards and fiscal adjustment measures. A strong primary surplus of R$1.6 billion (US$302 million) in 2024 was achieved, supported by a nominal revenue growth of 18 percent, while net consolidated debt stood at 11.6 percent of current revenues in 2023. The fiscal position was pressured by a uniform national fuel tax set below the state rate, higher national minimum wages, and weak federal income tax performance, prompting progressive consumption tax increases and strict control of current expenditures. Despite these gains, exposure to federal transfers (Fundo de Participação dos Estados, FPE), rigid mandatory spending, and rising personnel and pension costs continue to constrain investment capacity, leaving sustainability dependent on the advancement of ongoing structural reforms. 6. High non-discretionary human resources (HR) expenses persist. Payroll pressures are mounting with the wage bill for 47,771 active civil servants reaching R$3.8 billion (US$718 million) in 2024, increasing at an average of 13.3 percent per year since 2021. Outdated payroll and HR systems hinder audits, workforce sizing, and effective decision-making. Planned reforms aim to contain payroll growth, with estimated savings of R$432 million (US$81.7 million), and at least R$116 million (US$21.9 million) from reducing human error and fraud. Pensions remain a key fiscal challenge for states. In 2024, about 60,300 pension beneficiaries received monthly payments totaling R$204.9 million (US$38.7 million). The pension system runs an annual deficit of nearly R$1.1 billion (US$208 million). Weaknesses in predictive analytics, actuarial modeling, and investment oversight, combined with mass fund segregation, highlight the need for stronger data and controls. Ongoing pension reforms target R$163 million (US$30.8 million) in savings and faster processing of benefits. Although Progestão does not specifically target pension reforms, it addresses critical related issues through the establishment of unified management units, actuarial offices, and the use of annual reports and automated, interoperable systems to reduce fragmentation. Standardized procedures, along with refined indicators and targets focused on expenditure growth and audit savings, will be employed to monitor fiscal impact. 7. Public procurement costs accelerate amid systemic weaknesses. Public purchases account for about 24.8 percent of the government total expenditure, and spending on consumables grew by 17 percent per year between 2020 and 2024. Procurement governance, manuals, key performance indicators, and acquisition strategies remain fragmented and lack standardization. Planning and contract management are weak, limiting oversight and control. Outdated systems and manual processes persist in the absence of Artificial Intelligence (AI), robotic process automation (RPA), and smart contracts, constraining efficiency and transparency. The supplier base is narrow, reducing competition and price alignment, while procurement agents often lack professional certification. In addition, limited hardware and Information Technology (IT) investments restrict the adoption of modern digital tools needed to support reform. 8. Public investments lag behind national standards and execution remains constrained. In 2024, the investment rate reached 4.34 percent of NCR (R$665 million, US$125.7 million), below the national average of 10 percent. Delivery continues to lag due to a semi-structured project repository lacking consistent technical, financial, and risk filters, weakening prioritization, readiness, and project selection across the pipeline, from identification through budgeting to implementation. Limited transparency, and inadequate control in investment and transfer management often result in cost overruns, delays, and misallocation rather than the full realization of intended benefits. In parallel, proposals to establish a State Sovereign Fund and enhance its governance highlight the need for clear mandates, eligibility criteria, and oversight mechanisms to ensure strategic investments are well-anchored and to prevent fragmented decision-making with weak accountability, which could hinder private sector engagement. 9. Public asset management faces systemic challenges that undermine efficiency and value creation. Outdated, semi-manual controls and incomplete inventories of immovable and movable assets drive underutilization, weak maintenance, and recurrent losses. The lack of reliable data and integrated systems hampers lifecycle management, covering inventory, valuation, maintenance, and disposal, while limited methodologies and insufficient training reduce consistency and oversight. These gaps are reflected in the real estate portfolio, which posts a negative net balance of R$2.2 million (US$0.4 million), underscoring the poor exploitation of state properties and the broader governance weaknesses that constrain fiscal returns and alignment with the investment agenda. Implementing a robust asset management strategy, supported by a comprehensive inventory and integrated system, will strengthen asset management, enabling states to optimize their portfolios and improve revenue generation. 10. Sergipe’s fiscal management of health and education is constrained by structural budget rigidity , especially personnel and pension costs, and by operational inefficiencies that weaken value for money. In health, fragmented systems limit control over a R$2.98 billion (US$563 million) budget, with weak hospital stock management, decentralized execution, and increasing pressures from judicialization and avoidable costs. In education, weak information systems and limited oversight undermine school transport and meal programs, leading to inefficiencies and delays. About 13 percent of the 5,148 basic education classes in the state network enroll fewer than five students,3 inflating unit costs. School transport operates with limited data and poor controls; in the absence of an integrated management system, transfers to providers are delayed and financial reporting is late, undermining accountability and service reliability. Similar system gaps affect school meals, where procurement, contracting, and logistics require modernization; without robust integrated tools, controls remain weak, raising risks of waste, stock imbalances, and mistimed deliveries. Optimizing class offerings by 5 percent could generate savings of up to R$17.6 million (US$3.3 million) per year.4 11. Medium and long-term sustainability will depend on Sergipe’s continued commitment to structural reforms. The state needs to control the growth of mandatory expenditures, strengthen governance, and enhance public investment through evidence-based planning and prioritization criteria. Accordingly, the proposed project as phase III of the Progestão Program MPA aims to promote the continuous improvement of fiscal, budgetary, and asset management in the state government through actions focused on rationalizing public spending and generating sustainable results. C. Multiphase Programmatic Approach 12. The Program and its Phase III remain fully aligned with the World Bank Group’s Country Partnership Framework (CPF) for Brazil FY24-28. The proposed operation for the state of Sergipe aims to reduce fiscal constraints and improve public sector efficiency, supporting better services, productivity, and growth. Phase III will support high-level objective 1, “Greater Productivity and Employment”, particularly objective 1.1, which aims to strengthen fiscal management at all levels of government. The MPA is closely aligned with Brazil’s key policy frameworks, including its Multiannual Plan 2024–2027 (Plano Plurianual, PPA), the federal fiscal framework, and the National Strategy for Economic and Social Development 2020 – 2031 (Estratégia Nacional de Desenvolvimento Econômico e Social, ENDES). By promoting fiscal sustainability, strengthening state capabilities, and improving public sector governance, the MPA supports long-term objectives of higher productivity, stronger national cohesion, and better service delivery. Developed in partnership with the Ministry of Finance (MoF), it provides states with tools to advance economic growth and enhance citizens’ access to quality public services. It also aligns with Sergipe’s PPA 2024–2027, the government’s Strategic Plan 2023–2026, and the long-term vision “Sergipe 2050.” It advances transversal priorities of the PPA including modernization of public management, digital transformation, and fiscal sustainability by integrating systems, automating processes, and strengthening governance to enhance efficiency, transparency, and value for money. At the sector level, it supports education, health, social assistance, and investment management programs in the PPA and strategic plan, aiming to improve service delivery, ensure financial sustainability of the Unified Health System (Sistema Único de Saúde, SUS), strengthen education resource management, and reinforce project portfolio governance. 14. The MPA will continue to support the World Bank’s Maximizing Finance for Development strategy and contribute to both the private capital mobilization and private capital enabling agendas. The Program supports the adoption and implementation of critical reforms to restore fiscal stability and strengthen institutional arrangements that attract and mobilize private sector investment. Specifically, it provides support to the state of Sergipe to transition to a new public investment management (PIM) framework, which will enforce strict procedures for project prioritization and selection, including for public-private partnerships (PPPs). Following the approval of the MPA in March 2025, subsequent Phases will deepen collaboration with the International Finance Corporation (IFC) and the Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency (MIGA) to maximize private and capital mobilization opportunities in areas such as licensing, guarantees, and additional financing for investments and management of real estate assets. 15. The MPA and its Phase III align with Brazil’s commitments in the Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC, 2023) and address some of the recommendations of the Country Climate and Development Report (CCDR). Brazil pledges to reduce its greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions by 43 percent by 2030, compared to 2005 levels. This target includes measures such as reducing deforestation, promoting reforestation, increasing the use of renewable energy, and improving energy efficiency. By implementing a new PIM framework and guidelines and incorporating lifecycle requirements and energy efficiency standards in the acquisition of services, equipment, and supplies, the project aligns with the NDC objectives while increasing the overall government efficiency and improving fiscal sustainability at state level. 16. The rapid launch of subsequent Phases following MPA approval reflects strong demand for fiscal and financial management support among Brazilian states. Importantly, the core elements of the MPA, including the Program Results Chain, Program Development Objective (PrDO), outcome indicators, and Program Framework, remain unchanged across subsequent Phases. The PrDO continues to focus on enhancing the efficiency of public resource management and fiscal sustainability, monitored through five key program outcomes, including increased efficiency in whole-of-government processes, improved financial management in health, education, and social assistance, and expanded use of digitally enabled services. The Theory of Change (ToC) outlined in the MPA PAD for Phase I remains relevant in guiding the program’s strategic direction. PROJECT DESCRIPTION 18. Progestão Sergipe, as Phase III of the MPA, is fully aligned with the Program’s objectives and approach. The project adheres to the established ToC and directly contributes to the overarching PrDO. The state has requested support to consolidate recent reforms and improve efficiency in whole-of-government functions and strategic sectors under the Progestão Program. This includes most of the Program’s cross-cutting areas, such as HRM, pensions, public procurement, intergovernmental transfers, PIM, budgeting, and asset management. Sectoral interventions were defined based on their expected impact on efficiency and fiscal management, as well as their implementation readiness. The project will support management systems across most of the whole-of-government areas and in key strategic sectors, namely health, education, and social assistance. Support in areas such as debt management and SOEs is not being considered due to strategic priorities established by the state. A. Project Development Objective 19. Improve efficiency in public resource management in selected departments of the State of Sergipe. B. Theory of Change and PDO Indicators (i) Phase III Project Theory of Change Figure 1. C. Project Beneficiaries 21. The direct beneficiaries of the project will be all participating state agencies in Sergipe, which will benefit from improvements in whole-of-government management practices and improved access to information. These institutions and the 45,576 active state public officials will benefit from more efficient and effective systems for managing services and public finances, including better quality, and more timely information for decision-making as well as enhanced technical capacity to manage and utilize that information for improved service delivery. Around 60,360 retirees and pensioners will benefit from improved and transparent processes that will allow the state to manage existing pensions funds more efficiently to ensure timely payments to the state inactive workforce. 22. Indirect project beneficiaries include citizens and businesses who will gain from increased transparency and public sector productivity. Service users in health and education will benefit from more efficient management systems. Civil society organizations and researchers will benefit from access to higher-quality and more timely data for use in research, accountability efforts, policy advocacy, and public management. Indirect beneficiaries also include micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises, as well as Sergipe’s innovation ecosystem, which will benefit from more agile procurement systems, innovation programs, and improved fiscal decision-making based on accurate, complete, and timely financial information and stronger policy analysis. D. Project Components 23. Phase III of the MPA for the state of Sergipe will consist of three components that will support, among other actions, the following activities under the Progestão Program. Component 1. Whole-of-Government Management Systems (US$31.2 million) 24. Subcomponent 1.1: Human Resource Management. The subcomponent aims to strengthen efficiency and fiscal stability by optimizing human resource governance, aligning staffing with institutional priorities, and deploying data- driven tools for workforce planning and performance monitoring. It will generate comprehensive workforce data to support evidence-based fiscal decisions and promote balanced resource allocation across the state’s administrative and social sectors. Activities include: (a) carrying out a comprehensive assessment of human resources management structures, including legal and governance recommendations for improvements; (b) upgrading the human resources management and information system and integrating it to existing system; (c) improving payroll management, including regulatory review, process automation, and deployment of advanced analytics for automated auditing and predictive payroll growth; (d) strengthening occupational health and safety, including through the deployment of an operating model and the implementation of mandatory programs and dedicated systems; (e) digitizing and managing functional records, including employee personnel files and implementation of an electronic records system for human resources; (f) building the Borrower’s capacity on human resources management, including learning pathways and results evaluation; and (g) procuring hardware to support the rollout and implementation of the above-mentioned activities. The implementing agency will be the State Secretariat of Administration of Sergipe (Secretaria de Administração, SEAD). The subcomponent aims to improve public sector efficiency by strengthening the governance, integration, and transparency of public investment and budgeting processes. These reforms will promote more efficient resource allocation, reinforce fiscal discipline, and support long-term financial sustainability. Activities include: (a) carrying out a comprehensive assessment of public investment management, including recommendations for improvements and restructuring, and integrating it to the Borrower’s existing financial management system; (b) designing and implementing a governance strategy for PIM projects; (c) developing and implementing an integrated project management system for public investments, including preparation, prioritization, selection, monitoring, and evaluation; (d) establishing a project bank and developing a portfolio of technically pre-appraised, implementation-ready PIM projects; (e) implementing an intergovernmental transfers management module, including integration to the federal government’s transfer system; (f) carrying out diagnosis, audit, and enhancement of planning, budgeting, and financial management systems, including integration and development of planning and budget analytics; (g) carrying out technical assistance to structure the investment plan for the Sovereign Fund; (h) carrying out public expenditure quality analysis, aligning accounting practices with current norms, managing of the Borrower’s court-ordered government debt payments inventory, consolidating tax expenditure data with compensation studies, and developing a methodology for managing partnerships; and (i) procuring hardware to support the roll-out and implementation of the above-mentioned activities. The project builds on lessons learned from the World Bank’s experience with state-level initiatives, focusing on public sector reform, service delivery, and expenditure management. These experiences, including ongoing Progestão projects and others in Brazil,6 highlight the importance of high-level political commitment, engagement and ownership by technical teams, an active learning agenda, continued stakeholder participation, close supervision, and the value of international expertise. Additional key lessons include the need for coordination among implementing agencies, alignment of project monitoring and evaluation (M&E) with government systems, consistency with national reform agendas, and articulation with Development Policy Financing (DPF) operations. 36. Lessons were also drawn from programs financed by other multilateral development institutions. The National Program to Support the Modernization of Management and Planning in States and the Federal District (PNAGE, 2006– 2012), and PROFISCO I, II, and III (2008–ongoing), supported by the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB), underscore the importance of knowledge exchange among state governments. They also highlight the need for assessing the level of commitment, institutional readiness, and potential fiscal impact before allocating funds to state-level projects. 37. The project’s scope and activities are grounded in previous and ongoing analytical work . This includes studies on: (a) the implementation of pension reform at both federal and state levels, which highlighted the need to strengthen pension management at the subnational level (P172230); (b) the public sector wage bill, human resource management, and intergovernmental fiscal transfers, using state-level microdata to assess the impact of policy measures on payroll and HRM (P172560); and (c) corruption risks, the use of public data for anti-corruption efforts, and pilots using artificial intelligence to detect potential fraud in public procurement (P171744). 38. A key lesson from the Progestão operations under implementation is the importance of early procurement planning to overcome delays caused by Brazil’s complex project approval processes. To mitigate this, upcoming projects have adopted a proactive approach by initiating procurement activities, such as finalizing Terms of Reference and mobilizing staff during the preparation phase. This has been made possible through the Grant Facility for Project Preparation (GFPP), which enables states to begin critical procurement processes ahead of effectiveness, saving valuable time and ensuring smoother implementation from the outset. IV. PROJECT APPRAISAL SUMMARY A. Technical, Economic and Financial Analysis 48. Strategic relevance. The project is strategically relevant to Sergipe and aligned with the state’s PPA and the participating institutions’ action plans. It focuses on core whole-of-government management systems and selected management systems in strategic sectors identified by the participating agencies. 49. Technical design. The project will develop and implement complex systems, including customized commercial off- the-shelf (COTS) as well as in-house developed and customized software solutions. Contracted information system developers will provide direct support, skill transfer, and user training. The PMU will help agencies prepare terms of Page 15 The World Bank MPA Phase III - Progestão Sergipe: Public Sector Management Efficiency Project (P513080) reference for contracts, leveraging the World Bank’s experience and the knowledge captured through the MPA and technical support from peer states. Users and beneficiaries will be consulted during the design and implementation of reforms and systems. The project will support change management activities under each component, including extensive training in systems use, data analytics, and evidence-based decision-making for management staff, thereby building in- house capacity for sustainability. Just-in-time technical assistance, approved by the PMU and the World Bank, will help with problem solving. The project loan agreement permits flexibility for adjustments to subcomponents and activities to accommodate changes in state policy priorities and allows the project to adapt to varying levels of progress in implementation. 50. MPA and learning agenda. The MPA financing modality for the Progestão Program enhances efficiency and impact by establishing a long-term, scalable framework that reduces transaction costs in project preparation and implementation, ensuring compliance with federal regulations, and promoting a robust learning agenda. Collaboration with the Council of Secretaries of Administration (Conselho de Secretarios Estaduais de Administração, CONSAD), Council of Secretaries of Planning (Conselho de Secretários Estaduais de Planejamento, CONSEPLAN), and the Committee of Secretaries of Finance (Comitê de Secretarios de Estado da Fazenda, COMSEFAZ) will be complemented by strengthened partnerships with the National School of Public Administration (Escola Nacional de Administração Pública, ENAP), state councils, and other multilateral development institutions (MDIs) such as the IDB. Cross-state learning will be facilitated through the Progestão Forum communities of practice and digital platforms, while analytical work, including updated wage bill studies and subnational analytics on key areas of the program, will be formalized. This adaptable framework enables phased progression that responds to evolving state needs, ensuring continued relevance and effectiveness in addressing development challenges and informing future operations. 51. Technical capacity and readiness to implement. The project entails a challenging set of management reforms and systems across a wide range of functions and multiple institutions in Sergipe’s public administration. The state has significant experience with management reforms, having recently introduced pension reform and initiatives to review the catalog of eligible procurement items. The project provides significant technical assistance to support the reform process, including technical advisers and consultants, capacity building, and change management activities. Change management is particularly important, as the project entails changes in organizational culture, promoting the use of integrated data and analytics to support evidence-based decision-making and citizen-oriented services. SEFAZ leadership and managers at all levels will play an important role in mobilizing support for the implementation and use of outputs developed under the project. The state received a grant from the GFPP to build the PMU’s capacity on fiduciary, M&E, and project management to ensure readiness to implement. 52. Paris Alignment. The MPA and its Phase III are aligned with the goals of the Paris Agreement on both mitigation and adaptation. The climate and disaster risk screening (CDRS) identified minor climate risks to the investments in information systems financed by the project. It is anticipated that climate-related hazards will not significantly affect the project’s progress. Activities such as upgrading government information and financial management systems and building capacity are considered universally aligned with the Paris Agreement, and consequently, the project is assessed to have low or no risk on adaptation or mitigation efforts. 53. Assessment and reduction of mitigation risks. Project activities will have a negligible impact on GHG emissions and are not expected to have an adverse effect on Brazil’s low-GHG-emissions development pathways.
