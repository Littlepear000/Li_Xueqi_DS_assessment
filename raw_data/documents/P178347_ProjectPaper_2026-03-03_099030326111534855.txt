PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. INTRODUCTION 1. Due to delays stemming from Yemen’s political situation, implementation of the Yemen Emergency Electricity Access Project – Phase II (YEEP II, P178347) has faced significant challenges. To address these implementation constraints and ensure the project achieves its objectives, UNOPS has requested a project restructuring. The proposed restructuring aims to extend the project closing date by 18 months and reallocate US$11.8 million in savings to enhance project impact, while preserving the original Project Development Objective (PDO). B. PROJECT STATUS 2. The financing for the YEEP II in the amount of US$100 million of IDA Grant was approved by the World Bank Board of Executive Directors on June 16, 2022. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to improve access to electricity in rural and peri-urban areas within Yemen and plan for the restoration of the Yemen power sector. Financing Agreements (FA) were signed on July 14, 2022, and became effective on October 7, 2022, with closing date of September 30, 2026. 3. The project finances investments in rural and peri-urban areas to expand electricity access for households and electricity-dependent public critical services for the rural and peri-urban population and support preparation of interventions to improve electricity supply across Yemen in a sustainable manner. 4. Despite the challenging context in Yemen, the project has demonstrated encouraging progress toward achieving its PDO indicators. As of December 31, 2025, the number of people provided with new or improved electricity services reached 4,728,231, significantly exceeding the target of 3,500,000. In addition, 497 critical service facilities have been supplied with new or improved electricity services, against a target of 700. The project’s renewable energy capacity target was exceeded, achieving 10 MW compared to the original target of 8.8 MW by the end of the project. These achievements provide strong justification for the restructuring request, allowing the project to expand and enhance its benefits to the population of Yemen. 5. Regarding disbursements, World Bank funds disbursed to UNOPS totaled US$89.4 million, representing 89.4% of the allocated amount, as of December 31, 2025. Disbursements from UNOPS to sub-components amounted to US$52.9 million (52.9 %), with commitments reaching US$21.0 million (21%). 6. The detailed description of the implementation progress of the project components as of December 31, 2025 is described below. Component 1 - Electricity access in rural and peri-urban areas (US$83.0 million) 7. Subcomponent 1.1 (US$20 million): Electricity for Households: This sub-component focuses on subsidized solar home systems for households with 157,555 units have already been imported into Yemen and 99,465 have been sold, primarily through Microfinance Institutions (MFIs) by using Grant Supported Agreements (GSAs). Sales momentum has improved, especially for smaller Tier 1 systems, given the prevailing market and liquidity constraints. The productive use of electricity (PUE) pilot has begun on a modest scale, with solar-powered refrigerators being the most promising appliance for economic activities such as fishing and small retail, though uptake remains limited due to Official Use Only Page 1 The World Bank Yemen Emergency Electricity Access Project-Phase II (P178347) prevailing constraints. The value of the seven signed GSAs under the project first Call for Proposals launched in 2023 was $8.8m. Given that the MFIs were importing smaller-capacity systems, the expected number of household systems under the original GSAs was approximately 300,000. With the latest adjustments, this figure is now estimated at 200,000 household systems, which aligns with the Project’s results indicator of 200,000 household systems for this sub-component. Therefore, the total GSA value has been readjusted from $8.8m to $8.1m, resulting in savings of $0.7m. 8. Overall, the subcomponent has reached 50% of its objective with 100,697 households provided with off-grid solar home systems out of the taget of 200,000. 9. Subcomponent 1.2: Electricity for Critical Service Facilities (US$53 million): This component targets solar systems for critical service facilities, including health centers, schools, and water pumping systems. Contracts have been awarded for 386 health facilities, 108 schools, and 154 pumping systems, totaling approximately 16.5 MW in capacity. As of December 31st , 2025, 492 facilities (76 schools, 312 health centers, and 104 water pumping systems) have been energized with a combined installed capacity of 10 MW, resulting in significant energy savings and emission reductions, and directly benefiting over 4.7 million people. 10. This sub-component is well on track well with 497 critical Services Facilities electrified out of the target of 700, representing a progress rate of 71%. 11. Subcomponent 1.3: Electricity for COVID-19 Isolation & Vaccine Units (US$10 million): This sub-component supports COVID-19 isolation and vaccine cold chain units. It continues to face delays due to dependencies on other agencies' facility readiness, but progress is ongoing with several packages in various stages of implementation including 5 facilities energized (representing 10% of the targeted objective), and 20 facilities with awarded contracts. Component 2: Implementation support, market development, and technical assistance for power sector sustainability (US$17 million) 12. Subcomponent 2.1: Project Implementation Support (US$10 million): This sub-component finances UNOPS' direct and indirect costs for project management and supervision; and the contract of the Third-Party Monitoring (TPM) agent for independent performance verification and field monitoring. 13. Subcomponent 2.2: TA to Support Solar-PV Market (US$5 million): This subcomponent centers on technical assistance to support Yemen’s off-grid solar market. Activities are ramping up, with the below updates: - Off-grid solar market assessment: completed - Mini-grid market assessment: completed - Quality standards and capacity building: completed and training is ongoing for (150 Engineers & 150 Technicians) - Batteries management scoping and capacity building: Underway. It was expected to be completed by December 2025 but delayed due to the politocal context - Sector electricity needs assessments: yet to start, with a revised scope under discussion between the World Bank and UNOPS - Gender Impact Assessment: yet to start. The scope is being revised due to political sensitivity Official Use Only Page 2 The World Bank Yemen Emergency Electricity Access Project-Phase II (P178347) 14. Subcomponent 2.3: TA for Power Sector Recovery (US$2 million): It focuses on institutional strengthening for the power sector. Institutional assessments for GARE and the PIU have been completed, with a Capacity Development Action Plan now under review. This sub-component also has supported the Yemen National Energy Conference organized in November 2025 and will also support the preparation of a sector note to guide strategic reforms and highlight opportunities for job creation and economic benefits in the energy sector. Further capacity-building initiatives are under review, with an emphasis on actionable, time-bound strategies for key agencies. 15. Component 3: Contingence emergency response component (CERC): With respect to this component, the CERC was not activated. Safeguards 16. The overall Environmental and Social (E&S) performance of the project is rated satisfactory, with continued compliance with the Environmental and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP), Environmental and Social Management Frameworks (ESMFs), GBV Action Plan, Labor Management Procedures (LMP), and Force Labor Action Plan (FLAP). The project maintains its adherence to required environmental and social safeguards. 17. Screening & Instruments : UNOPS has screened all project investments in line with the project’s ESMF, excluding ineligible activities and determining, through consultation with the World Bank team, the appropriate Environmental and Social (E&S) instruments for eligible investments. The screening process covered hundreds of health facilities, schools, water wells, and vaccine units, ensuring ongoing compliance with ESMF and ESCP. Since the project start (September 2022–October 2025), UNOPS has prepared 4 ESMPs, 8 ESAPs, and 1 E&S requirements document for contractors, covering 382 health facilities, 115 schools, 184 water wells, and 23 vaccine/isolation units. 18. Training & Capacity Building : Targeted training is regularly provided to contractors, engineers, and safety officers, focusing on environmental and social risks. Modules include safety, emergency preparedness, and risk assessment, with advanced courses completed by key personnel. 19. Monitoring & Supervision : A dedicated E&S team conducts risk-based monitoring, prioritizing high-risk activities. No severe incidents were reported since the last year. Site engineers and HSE supervisors perform weekly visits, while contractors conduct daily inspections. Worker consultations and incident logs are maintained, and near misses are now being tracked. From May to October 2025, over 200 inspections addressed 619 hazards and documented 1,078 good practices. Waste minimization measures are ongoing, and a technical study on battery disposal has been prepared. 20. Stakeholder Engagement: Since inception, nearly 6,000 participants have engaged in consultations, including authorities, managers, and community leaders. Recent discussions focused on project details, risks, mitigation, and grievance mechanisms, helping improve implementation and contractor performance. 21. Labor Management : Labor procedures are integrated into contracts, with all workers trained and signing a Code of Conduct. Child labor checks and labor registers are routine. Forced Labor Declaration Forms are signed by all Official Use Only Page 3 The World Bank Yemen Emergency Electricity Access Project-Phase II (P178347) contractors and MFIs. The project employs over 4,400 workers, with no primary suppliers or community workers involved. 22. Grievance Mechanism : Beneficiaries can submit grievances via phone, email, or messaging apps. As of December 31st , 2016, 338 grievances have been received since inception, all resolved within five days. Issues range from technical and administrative to financial and safety concerns. 23. Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Sexual Harassment: A comprehensive SEA/SH Action Plan is in place, including training, referral pathways, and accountability. No cases have been reported recently. 24. Forced Labor: Strict compliance is maintained through signed declarations and regular awareness sessions. No forced labor incidents have been found during the project. Fiduciary 25. Finacial management (FM): Overall, the FM performance is rated Moderately Satisfactory, while the FM risk remains high, given the country's high inherent risk including the banking sector liquidity crisis in Yemen and risk of fund being frozen as a result of sanction of Houthis. UNOPS follows acceptable accounting standards (IPSAS), and internal controls system. UNOPS FM system is considered to be adequate and relies on adequate FM staffing on the ground. 26. Procurement : The project rating is Satisfactory. The procurement activities, excluding GSAs figures for MFIs, that are completed, under implementation, or closed are 38 (with 60 awarded contracts) totaling approximately US$ 44.1 million. Adding the US$8.1 million of the GSAs value brings the total of disbursement to US$52.9 million. In addition, one activity is currently under evaluation for US$ 1 million and another is under award recommendation for US$ 0.8 million. UNOPS confirmed that they had not received any procurement complaint. C. RATIONAL FOR RESTRUCTURING 27. The volatile security environment in Houthi-controlled areas—including Houthi-imposed restrictions, the detention of UN staff, and a stalemate in import and export logistics with the Internationally Recognized Government (IRG)—has adversely affected the project and led to implementation delays. These conditions have significantly constrained implementaiton of the project activties in Houthi-controlled areas, resulting in postponed activities and the reallocation of remaining funds to IRG-controlled area of Yemen. 28. Additionaly, based on project commitments and disbursement projections, total actual cost savings amount to US$11.8 million. Of this amount, US$10.5 million is attributable to Subcomponent 1.1 (Electricity for Households), reflecting increased market competition and cost reductions driven by advances in solar technology. The remaining US$1.3 million arises from Subcomponent 2.3 (Technical Assistance for Power Sector Recovery), where certain activities could not be fully implemented due to security constraints in Houthi-controlled areas. These savings could be reallocated to further enhance the project’s overall impact. 29. Consequently, these combined effects support the need for an extension of the project closing date from 30 September 2026 to 31 March, 2028 to ensure all activities are completed and the project achieves its development objectives. The proposed project restructuring will lead to changes in the PDO indicators and the intermediate results indicators. First, a new intermediate result indicator will be introduced to reflect the addition of three mini-grid activities. The solarization of these mini-grids is expected to add 1 MW to the renewable energy capacity indicator . Second, the people provided with new or improved electricity services has currently reached 4,728,231, significantly exceeding the initial target of 3,500,000. By the project closing date, following restructuring, this figure is expected to rise to 5,500,000. Fourth, the replacement of batteries will raise the number of electrified critical facilities by 55. Accordingly, the number of critical service facilities is expected to increase from the original target of 700 to 755. 33. The proposed restructuring is in line with the World Bank Policy on Investment Project Financing (IPF) and fully justified considering that: (i) PDO remains highly relevant and achievable subject to extension of closing date; (ii) the project remains likely to fully achieve its PDO within the extended period; (iii) UNOPS has prepared a revised implementation schedule to ensure implementation of all project activities and disbursement of all funds by the proposed revised closing date; (iv) the project is not subject to suspension; and (v) the audit reports are satisfactory to the World Bank and there are no outstanding ones. The project will continue to comply with environmental and social provisions throughout the proposed extension. The World Bank’s team will continue to provide close follow-up on the IDA financed activities to ensure their completion by March 31, 2028. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 34. The proposed Level 2 Restructuring does not entail any change to the Project PDO. Closing date extension: It is proposed to extend the project closing date from the current closing date of September 30, 2026, to March 31, 2028.
