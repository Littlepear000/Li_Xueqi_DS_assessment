PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the Country Director’s approval for a Level 2 restructuring of the Strengthening the National Statistical System in Ecuador Project. The restructuring was requested by the Government of Ecuador through letter MEF-SFPAR-2025-0535-O, received by the Bank on June 12, 2025. If approved, this would be the project’s first restructuring. Changes to the project include: (i) reallocation of US$10.7 million from Component 1 to be redistributed across Components 2,3, and 4; (ii) Changes to the Loan agreement to reflect the addition of a new national survey (ENVIGMU); (iii) Updates to the Results Framework to reflect the new survey and revise an intermediate outcome on data dissemination; (iv) Adjustment to the implementation schedule and disbursement estimates. The Project Development Objective (PDO) and implementation arrangements remain unchanged as well as the current closing date (July 28, 2027). 2. The Strengthening the National Statistical System in Ecuador Project (P178564) was approved by the Board of Executive Directors on June 30, 2022, for an IBRD loan of US$80 million and became effective on December 15, 2022. The project is being implemented by the National Institute of Statistics and Censuses (INEC). The PDO is to enhance Ecuador's national statistical capacity for the production and dissemination of timely, high-quality economic and sociodemographic statistics to facilitate evidence-based policymaking. The project comprises four components: (i) Population and Housing Census, (ii) Strengthening statistical production from surveys, (iii) Strengthening statistical production from administrative records and INEC’s managerial capacity, and (iv) Project management, monitoring, and evaluation. As of June 30, 2025, US$60.72 million (75.9%) of the funds had been disbursed. All Legal Covenants have been fulfilled as of this date. Project Status 3. Progress toward achieving the PDO and overall Implementation Progress are rated Satisfactory in the latest Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR). The project experienced delays in budget authorization from the Ministry of Finance due to political instability, which necessitated rescheduling the data collection timeline for the surveys in component 2. Also, the vacancy in the Financial Specialist position since December 2024 caused delays in the preparation of several financial reports. As of July 2025, a new Financial Specialist has been appointed, and the reports are currently being regularized. The rating for Procurement and financial management are Moderately Satisfactory. The ratings for Project Management, Counterpart Funding, and Monitoring and Evaluation are all rated Satisfactory. The Bank has not identified any unsatisfactory audit reports. The 2024 audit report, which was due on June 30, 2025, was submitted by the audit firm on August 8, the audit report is considered acceptable to the Bank. 4. The project maintains a Satisfactory environmental and social rating, with risks classified as Moderate under the ESF. This classification is based on the following: (i) the environmental risks resulting from the other collection exercises (surveys) to be executed are not significant as the activities refer to basic logistics and social interactions; (ii) the operation is neither financing or requiring civil works activities nor it supports other activities likely to cause environmental harm; and (iii) the project will use the previously acquired information technology purchased for the population census, which could lead to adverse environmental impacts in the event of inadequate or improper handling or disposal. To mitigate the third risk, the PIU conducted an ex-post assessment and donated 14,399 tablets to institutions following proper e- waste protocols. The remaining 2,740 tablets will be reused by INEC. The social-environmental specialist joined the PMU on September 1, 2024. 5. The project's social risk classification remains Moderate, despite having been exposed to political pressures from civil society groups and organizations for some months during the last period, as well as scrutiny from national oversight entities such as the Office of the Comptroller General of the Republic and the National Assembly. Additionally, complaints were filed by Indigenous Peoples, Afro-Ecuadorians, Montubios, and LGBTIQ+ groups, among others, primarily related to the National Population and Housing Census (Component 1). These complaints were even escalated to the Bank’s Official Use Only Page 1 The World Bank Strengthening the National Statistical System in Ecuador Project (P178564) Inspection Panel; however, following the Panel’s field visit and the review of the Management’s Response, the case was not recommended for investigation. The Task Team has successfully managed the situation and reduced the pressure by working closely with the client. 6. The project is now implementing Components 2 and 3, which include conducting new national sample surveys, including (i) Income and Expenditure, (ii) Labor Force, (iii) Producer Price Index, and (iv) Agricultural Production and Surface Area Survey. Consequently, INEC staff will once again interact with various sectors of the population in each survey, leading to interactions between field data collection teams and the social environments where the surveys will be conducted. This could entail certain risks, such as (i) community health and safety risks during field data collection, including assault, and exposure to crime; (ii) occupational health and safety risks, as INEC field teams may face physical or health hazards while working in unsafe neighborhoods or conflict zones, as well as risks related to staff transportation on dangerous roads; (iii) risks of sexual harassment and workplace discrimination, especially due to the large-scale hiring of enumerators, supervisors, and administrative personnel, many of whom will need to travel and stay in temporary accommodations; and (iv) risks associated with cultural sensitivities, particularly regarding inclusion, during field visits and communication campaigns. Nevertheless, all these risks are considered manageable with the tools available within the project, which have been reviewed and strengthened by the client with the support of the Bank team. A. Rationale for Restructuring 7. During 2023 and 2024, the implementation of the project faced significant delays due to postponed budget authorization from the Ministry of Finance due to political instability. These delays affected the original implementation timeline, making it necessary to adjust and reschedule project activities to ensure that the remaining ones can be implemented and financed with the loan proceeds before the closing date in July 2027. In addition, a new survey will be included that does not affect the project’s PDO and fits within Component 2. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES A. Reallocation of resources 8. The restructuring responds to US$10.7 million in savings from Component 1. The main adjustments are: (i) an extension of the timeline and a budget increase of US$7,647,012 under Component 2 to cover higher data collection costs, full-time field staff for the agricultural survey’s technological module, and an expanded household survey sample to meet quality standards; (ii) inclusion of the National Survey on Family Relationships and Gender-Based Violence Against Women (ENVIGMU) under Component 2 (US$1,843,639) following a client request to improve survey design and complete the pending round originally planned for early 2024 with fiscal resources; (iii) allocation of US$1,168,580 under Component 3 for IT acquisitions and data dissemination, covering price adjustments, delayed purchases, and upgrades to the Laboratory of Labor and Business Dynamics to integrate administrative data with census and household surveys, promote innovations like web scraping, and monitor labor and business trends—requiring specialized consultants and software; and (iv) a budget increase of US$68,245 under Component 4 to maintain Project Management Unit operations amid rising costs, including external audits, PIU staffing, IT support, and performance reporting through the Bank’s fiduciary and safeguards systems. Implementing the National Income and Expenditure Survey. The sample size was changed from 39,744 to 41,184 urban and rural households. This change was due to the consideration of self-represented domains, which are now conceived as cantons rather than cities, expanding geographic and population coverage. The nine cantons considered self- represented are: Ambato, Cuenca, Loja, and Quito in the Sierra region; Esmeraldas, Machala, Manta, Guayaquil, and Santo Domingo in the Coastal region, plus the Province of Galápagos. The additional allocation covers costs arising from the delay in implementation, such as keeping staff active even without fieldwork, retraining, rehiring some personnel, more household visits, a significative increase in rental vehicles, fuel, and inflation. 10. Changing the base year of the Price Indexes and updating Basic Family Baskets. Due to the late allocation of resources for fieldwork for the National Household Income and Expenditure Survey (Encuesta Nacional de Ingresos y Gastos de los Hogares Urbanos y Rurales) (ENIGHUR), the timeline now requires to be rescheduled to 2026 to allow for result analysis. No closing date extension of the project is needed. 11. Implementing the New Labor Force Survey. There is a need to provide more precise information for growing cantons and to meet national needs. Therefore, cantons housing the 9 self-represented cities must be included as geographic domains for stratification; Riobamba is also included, completing the 10 most influential cantons. Previously, the design only included 5 self-represented cities as in the current ENEMDU survey. 12. Additionally, the proposal includes not using replacements for the International Conference of Labor Statisticians Survey (ENCIET) (unlike the National Survey of Employment, Unemployment, and Underemployment Survey (ENEMDU by its Spanish Acronym)), applying a fixed number of 8 dwellings per Primary Sampling Unit (PSU), with 10,304 monthly household visits to ensure consistent coverage across time periods (123,648 total households). The survey will run through 2026, including result analysis and harmonization with ENEMDU series. Also, a cartographic update is planned prior to the transition. The reallocation of resources for this activity is based on the increase in rental vehicle and fuel costs, the increase in sample size, delays in implementation, and most importantly, the need for a cartographic update—not originally considered—to integrate the traditional and new labor surveys, which is key to assuring the quality of the survey. 13. Incorporating the rotating modules of the Agricultural Survey. - The budget increased because the original plan and related budget did not include hiring full-time field personnel, since these enumerators were paid by fiscal resources. These contracts were necessary and are now included in the project to allow this activity to be implemented and the related targets to be met. Component 3 14. Strengthening statistical production from administrative records and INEC managerial capacity. This component is extended to 2026, with increased targets related to administrative records. While implementation has followed the original plan, a cost update is necessary due to the inclusion of technological acquisitions. 15. Given the potential and expected results, at least 6 additional months are needed to complete these activities. This component will also benefit from technical assistance from the United Nations in areas such as Ecuador’s Base Statistical Population Register (REBPE), Habitual Residence Register (RERH), and Base Property Register (REBIE). 16. Make a virtual information laboratory for the INEC: Regarding activity 3.4, the Labor and Business Dynamics Laboratory (LDLE) needs to be modernized by incorporating non-traditional data sources and advanced analytical techniques. These resources will allow the Project Management Unit to continue operating through the current closing date of July 27, 2027 A. Changes to the Loan Agreement. 19. Collect a new survey under project PDO. Inclusion of a new activity in component 2 of the project: the National Survey on Family Relationships and Gender-Based Violence Against Women (ENVIGMU). This survey intended to be conducted every five years (the first was carried out in 2011 and the second in 2019), aimed at generating statistical information on the prevalence and characteristics of various forms of violence against women, including physical, psychological, sexual, economic, and gynecological-obstetric violence., in both private and public spheres. The second round of the ENVIGMU has been planned since 2018, following the approval of the Comprehensive Organic Law to Prevent and Eradicate Violence Against Women, introducing significant changes in its methodological, conceptual, and analytical design. Data collection is planned from January to March 2026, requiring resources from August 2025 (to begin the contracting process) until July 2026. The inclusion of the ENVIGMU survey will provide Ecuador with up-to-date data on violence against women, enabling the government to design targeted policies, allocate resources efficiently, and align with recent legal reforms.
