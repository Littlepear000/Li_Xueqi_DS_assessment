OBJECTIVE 1.1 Development Objective Original Development Objective (Approved as part of Approval package on 30-Jul-2020) The Project development objectives of the Fourth Phase of the Central Asia Regional Links Program are to enhance the efficiency of cross-border trade for participants of the regional economy and to improve the resilience and safety of regional connectivity infrastructure in Sughd region and Gorno-Badakhshan Autonomous region. The project contributes to the Central Asia Regional Links Programs higher-level development objectives of increasing cross- border connectivity and enhancing integrated regional development to revitalize historically active economic exchanges in Central Asia and beyond along the Silk Route. KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions The project is making overall satisfactory progress toward its development objectives as it approaches the October 31, 2026 closing date. The major physical investments and core institutional reforms have been substantially delivered, while a limited number of activities remain to be completed during the final weeks of implementation. Under the Infrastructure Component, civil works in both GBAO and Sughd regions have been completed, with 61.30 km of roads rehabilitated and opened to traffic. The project has achieved significant resilience and connectivity results, including the elimination of natural-hazard-related road closures in Barsem during the 2025–2026 winter period. The average travel time between Konibodom and the Bekabad BCP has also improved to 122 minutes, exceeding the end target of 126 minutes. These results demonstrate that the principal connectivity and resilience objectives of the project have been substantially achieved. Under the Road Safety Component, strengthened seat belt legislation has been enacted and safety infrastructure and institutional reforms have progressed. The safety barrier indicator has already exceeded its target in terms of recorded retrofitted road sections; however, some end-treatment works remain outstanding and are expected to be completed by October 15, 2026. The National Road Safety Strategy 2025–2040 and Action Plan 2026–2030 have been finalized and submitted for Government approval, with approval expected by September 30, 2026. These activities remain the principal implementation risks under the Road Safety Component. In addition, the latest observed seat belt compliance rate of 50.10 percent remains below the 60 percent end target, and the project is unlikely to fully achieve this outcome by closing. The project has nevertheless delivered important enabling reforms, including strengthened legislation, public awareness activities, and institutional measures to support continued improvements after closing. Under the Customs Modernization Component, nationwide deployment of ASYCUDA World has been achieved, and customs clearance times have declined substantially. The remaining activities include activation of electronic signatures, completion of source-code transfer, and implementation of agreed sustainability measures, which are expected to be completed by October 15, 2026. These activities are important for full operationalization and sustainability but do not materially detract from the substantial customs efficiency results already achieved. The project also records strong performance on several indicators, including average travel time, customs clearance time, roads rehabilitated, road closures in Barsem, climate-resilience results, and community engagement. At the same time, some indicators are unlikely to reach their end targets by closing, notably the female representation indicators within the Customs Service and the seat belt compliance target. The underlying infrastructure and resilience results have been achieved or exceeded in several dimensions. Overall, the project has substantially delivered the core connectivity, resilience, and customs-efficiency outcomes underpinning the PDO. As of September 22, 2026, approximately US$10.33 million remains undisbursed. The implementing agencies have submitted payment plans indicating that the remaining funds are largely committed under signed contracts, with the Ministry of Transport expecting to process outstanding payments by approximately October 15–20, 2026 and the Customs Service expecting to utilize its remaining commitments during the same closing period. Given the limited time remaining before project closing, several payments and contract activities are scheduled close to The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Fourth Phase of the Central Asia Regional Links Program (P166820) Sep 27, 2026 Page 3 of 15 Official Use Only the closing date. There is therefore a residual risk that goods, works, or services that are not delivered, accepted, substantially completed, or supported by acceptable documentation by October 31, 2026 may not be eligible for financing, potentially resulting in a residual undisbursed balance. The team is maintaining close monitoring of contract execution, payment documentation, and expenditure eligibility through project closure. 5.
