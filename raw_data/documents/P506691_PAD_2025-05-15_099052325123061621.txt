STRATEGIC CONTEXT A. Project Strategic Context 1. Bangladesh faces several significant challenges that successive governments have been striving to address. These include sustaining growth-generating employment, reforming the financial sector, improving the business environment, and enhancing governance. The student-led uprising during the summer of 2024 resulted in political transition that has put transparency and accountability high on the reform agenda. The current Government intends to address these pressing issues through substantial governance reforms with a sense of urgency. Bangladesh aspires to develop a public sector that is open and responsive, including a citizen-centric system that fully leverages technology to ensure that all enjoy equal rights and opportunities. 2. Bangladesh has experienced rapid economic and social progress in recent decades, achieving lower middle- income country status in 2015. However, economic growth has slowed since fiscal year 2022 (FY22). Between 2010 and 2024, stable macroeconomic conditions supported an average annual real gross domestic product (GDP) growth of 6.3 percent. Between 2010 and 2022, poverty and extreme poverty declined by 18.4 and 6.6 percentage points, reaching 18.7 percent and 5.6 percent, respectively. However, real GDP growth fell from 7.1 percent in FY22 to 4.2 percent in FY24, with a further decline projected to 3.3 percent in FY25. The pace of poverty reduction correspondingly slowed, along with job creation, particularly in urban areas and the formal sector, while urban inequality increased (Bangladesh Development Update, April 2024). 3. To achieve faster growth and transition to an upper-middle-income country (UMIC), Bangladesh needs to implement multisectoral structural reforms. These include targeted interventions to build a conducive and competitive business environment, diversify exports, deepen the financial sector, and create more productive and efficient urban areas (Bangladesh Country Economic Memorandum 2022). These reforms also need to be accompanied by better labor market outcomes. Bangladesh needs to improve its human capital and create an enabling environment for more and better jobs for the large number of youths entering the job market every year. Such reforms will strengthen international competitiveness as Bangladesh prepares to graduate from least-developed country (LDC) status. LDC graduation will reduce concessional financing and preferential market access for its exports. Bangladesh has a low risk of overall and external debt distress according to the June 2024 joint International Monetary Fund (IMF)-World Bank Debt Sustainability Assessment (DSA). The Joint DSA is expected to be updated by June 2025. Bangladesh is currently not subject to debt limits conditionality under the Sustainable Development Finance Policy. 4. The Global Climate Risk Index ranks Bangladesh as the world’s seventh most affected country over 2000–2019 faced with a high level of vulnerability to climate and disaster risks often characterized by floods, tropical cyclones, storm surges, extreme heat stress, and prolonged drought. The Country Climate and Development Report (CCDR) identified that since 1971, more than US$10 billion was invested by the government on disaster risk management; yet, frequent and overlapping climate-induced disasters and extremities slowed poverty reduction and economic growth. Although significant priorities were set by the government (through prioritizing disaster preparedness by strengthening coordination and proactive approach to climate change adaptation), support to improving governance by integrating climate and environmental externalities (for example, emission reduction, green tax reforms), resilience building and mitigation embedded investment policy decisions across government portfolios is still limited. B. Sectoral and Institutional Context 5. Bangladesh’s public sector capacity has weakened over the past decades. According to publicly available indicators aggregated using the Country-Level Institutional Review and Assessment framework, the government’s effectiveness and accountability have deteriorated in the past two decades compared to peers such as Bhutan and Viet Nam. It indicates weaknesses in the quality of public services and the civil service, as well as the ability to formulate and implement policies, which are critical for reaching UMIC status. Indeed, to become a UMIC, Bangladesh will require Page 1 The World Bank Strengthening Institutions for Transparency and Accountability (SITA) (P506691) a transparent, accountable, climate-sensitive, risk-informed, and citizen-centric public sector able to mobilize adequate domestic resource and effectively deliver public services. 6. Despite significant progress in improving its National Statistical System (NSS) over the past two decades, Bangladesh still faces critical challenges in leveraging data for policy making . These include gaps in transparency, weak administrative data quality, poor coordination around data sharing, and slow digitization of systems and processes. The NSS—which includes the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS) and other data-producing administrative agencies—faces challenges related to infrastructure, institutions, and human resources, as well as a crisis of data reliability, compounded by restricted accessibility. As a result, it struggles to meet the growing demand from citizens and data users for timely and transparent access to high-quality data. Key challenges include the following: (a) institutional barriers limiting timely sharing of core official statistics and administrative data; (b) coordination issues within BBS, across administrative agencies and data users, compounded by a lack of digital public infrastructure; (c) limited financial and technical resources to improve administrative data quality, including the absence of focal point officers from BBS in other government agencies; (d) inadequate physical infrastructure and human capital, in particular shortage of skilled resources in data production, processing, management, and access, which hampers data digitization within BBS; and (e) insufficient technical skills to leverage emerging technologies such as data science and big data. 7. Bangladesh’s revenue mobilization is extremely low and inadequate for its development financing needs. As documented in the forthcoming Bangladesh Public Finance Review, the tax-to-GDP ratio stagnated between 7 and 8 percent of GDP in FY12–FY24, reaching 7.4 percent in FY24, one of the lowest in the world and significantly below its peers, including the average of other South Asian countries (19.4 percent average from 2017 to 2022), structural peers (16.1 percent), and aspirational peers (20.5 percent). The main challenges include (a) a heavy reliance on trade-related taxes; (b) a value added tax (VAT) system characterized by numerous exemptions and truncated rates that reduce collection without being pro-poor; (c) low tax compliance; and (d) significant exemptions (6.9 percent of GDP in FY22), deductions, rebates, and tax holidays including lack of environmental cost recovery with user fees. The challenges have been identified in several policy documents, including in the annual government budgets. However, revenue generation has continued to remain low because of the political economy challenges in reaching a consensus on concrete policy reforms. Given Bangladesh's acute vulnerabilities and the escalating frequency of adverse climate events, the persistently low level of revenue mobilization not only constrains public spending but also severely undermines the country’s ability to mitigate and adapt to climate risks, further exacerbating long-term economic and environmental instability. 8. There are significant inefficiencies in public investment management (PIM).1 PIM efficiency is hampered by lack of budget discipline, long implementation delays and cost overruns (minimum cost overruns average about 30 percent and delays about 3 years, and poor quality of service delivery (116 out of 137 countries in the overall quality investment of the Global Competitive Index). In many investment projects, key elements such as objectives, inputs, outputs, and log frames are not adequately documented. This lack of clarity leads to challenges in implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. Projects are taken without proper feasibility studies. Projects initiated without proper feasibility or stakeholder consultations often face significant obstacles during implementation. Given the level of spending per capita on infrastructure, Bangladesh could have better quality of infrastructure and logistics, including for transport, roads, ports, air transport, and electricity. Bangladesh needs to strengthen public investment project management, by adopting effective management practices at the planning, allocation, implementation, maintenance, monitoring, and evaluation phases, which will improve infrastructure service delivery. Full digitalization of the development planning process, which is currently mostly paper based, is required. Also, there is a crucial need to establish robust monitoring and evaluation (M&E) frameworks and IT systems at the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) level, as well as at the Implementation Monitoring and Evaluation Division (IMED), and have these systems interconnected. Applying budgetary discipline to prioritizing investment projects will be critical given the large number of existing public investment projects and limited fiscal resources. 9. Strengthening public procurement will be central to improving public investment efficiency and effectiveness and service delivery. The government launched the e-Government Procurement (e-GP) System in 2011, starting with three processes: Annual Procurement Plan (APP), Bidding and Contract Award, and Contract Management. Though the e- GP is widely adopted in the country, its usage has stagnated at 65 to 70 percent for the last couple of years as the e-GP is not legally mandated and procurement of large works and multilateral development bank (MDB) procurement procedures are not available in the e-GP. More generally, the public procurement system still needs improvement, including contract management (about 70 percent of civil works contracts are not completed on time); lack of incentives for sustainable public procurement (SPP) that aims to achieve value for money on the life cycle of goods and services procured and embed climate risk management; delays in bid evaluation and approval of contracts; and inadequate integration of the procurement system with the budget and accounting system. The consolidation of procurement responsibilities and authorities under the Bangladesh Public Procurement Authority (BPPA) needs to be fully operationalized and complemented with critical reforms. 10. Women in Bangladesh face significant barriers to participation in public procurement, with only 7.2 percent2 of businesses being women-owned, mostly Small and Medium Entreprises (SMEs), and just 1.7 percent3 of formal firms being women-owned. In addition to the challenges they face to starting and growing a business, women encounter further barriers to participating in public procurement, commencing with registration. The women perceive that the e-GP registration process is a bit complex, involving fee payments, document notarizations, and profile verification. Women, in particular, face difficulties due to lower awareness of the requirements and limited capacity to acquire trade licenses, secure necessary funds, and navigate digital systems. As a result, only 3.6 percent of registered bidders are women (with 4,205 registered4 women bidders out of 116,119 total bidders as of September 2024). Despite initiatives by government organizations, capacity building for women-owned businesses to become successful tenderers is lacking. Currently, a firm is only eligible to receive training after it has registered with e-GP. Therefore, there is a strong need to support efforts to increase women’s registration. Further, among current registered women, there is demand to have more hands-on training on e-GP which is expected to result in higher submission of bids. 11. The government is emphasizing the strengthening of oversight and accountability of public sector institutions for improved service delivery to its citizens. The government has developed significant digital public financial management (PFM) capabilities, including automation of critical PFM processes. However, achievements remain fragmented, and strengthening oversight and accountability of public sector institutions will be critical to ensure better service delivery. The latest Public Expenditure and Financial Accountability (PEFA) performance of 2021 and 2016 reveals that external scrutiny and audit continue to remain a weak spot in the PFM system. There is a significant backlog of audit reports, and the follow-up system is not always applied, resulting in a grade of D+ (basic) for external audit. The prolonged delay of audit undermines the accountability of public spending, mainly because of the delay in preparation of Financial Statements, consolidation of Financial Statements of Self Accounting Entities, Special Accounts, Bangladesh Railway and so on. Lack of professionalization, inadequate interface with other digital systems on PFM and its periphery remain key impediments for the timely preparation of Financial Statements and audit reports. The Supreme Audit Institution (SAI) is an important actor in a country’s accountability chain. Its external oversight role is established by the Constitution. Bangladesh’s SAI, the Office of the Comptroller and Auditor General (OCAG), needs to be enhanced for greater efficiency and effectiveness through the technological transformation of audit systems across public institutions and support for specialized audit capacity. PROJECT DESCRIPTION 13. The proposed project aims to strengthen the governance, transparency, and accountability of key public sector institutions through critical investments, capacity enhancement, and institutional reforms. The project design responds to the current government’s request for support from the World Bank to promote critical governance, transparency, and accountability in the public sector, particularly on data transparency, domestic revenue mobilization, PIM and procurement, and financial oversight. The operation aligns with the ongoing modernization of crucial public sector functions, essential for building a citizen-responsive service delivery in Bangladesh. The proposed project activities aim to ensure that core data systems, institutions, and capacity are in place for strengthened data governance, integration, use and dissemination; domestic revenue mobilization; PIM; public procurement; and financial oversight. The Strengthening Institutions for Transparency and Accountability (SITA) project will be funded by a proposed credit of Special Drawing Rights (SDR) 81,850,800 (US$110,998,800 equivalent) (Credit A, Number 7828-BD) and a proposed Shorter Maturity Loan credit of SDR 102,500,000 (US$139,001,200 equivalent) (Credit B, Number 7829-BD), the combined dollar equivalent being US$250 million. A. Project Development Objective 14. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to enhance transparency, reliability, and access to official statistics, increase tax compliance and improve efficiency and accountability of public spending. B. Theory of Change and PDO Indicators 15. The proposed project is designed to strengthen selected public sector institutions that will undergo the first wave of policy and institutional reforms on governance, transparency, and accountability that the government is championing. The Strengthening Governance and Institutional Resilience (SGIR) Development Policy Credit (DPC) (P511201) supports critical governance, transparency, and accountability policy reforms in the public sector, particularly on domestic revenue mobilization, PIM and procurement, and audit and accountability. It also supports data transparency through reforms that enhance the institutional independence of the BBS in the release of official statistics while expanding public access to data. The proposed project will support the capacity building necessary for the implementation of the SGIR DPC-supported policy reforms in selected five institutions5 for a citizen-centric service delivery. Project Beneficiaries 17. The project aims to strengthen the capacity of the five implementing agencies (IAs), while the ultimate beneficiaries are citizens and businesses at large. Employees of Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS), National Board of Revenue (NBR), Planning Division, BPPA, and OCAG and citizens and firms directly utilizing their services will be the direct beneficiaries of the SITA Project, through more efficient and effective systems for managing services and public finances, including better quality and more timely data and information for decision-making, as well as enhanced technical capacity to manage and utilize that information for improved service delivery. Citizens and businesses at large will benefit from improvements in government transparency, accountability, and increased public sector productivity. D. Project Components 18. The project will provide technical and financial support to five critical agencies to implement and institutionalize urgent transparency and accountability reforms undertaken by the government. The five agencies are BBS, NBR, Planning Division, BPPA, and OCAG. The project is designed to have a clear line of sight to the policy actions supported by the SGIR DPC to strengthen governance and institutional resilience in Bangladesh. It has three ‘buckets’ of intervention (data, revenue, and expenditure as illustrated in the Theory of Change) described as Parts with 5 components. Component 4: Public Procurement Modernization to Improve Service Delivery (US$36 million; US$10 million government counterpart funding) 38. The goal of this component, to be implemented by BPPA, is to enhance procurement practices, project implementation, e-GP scope, and coverage and to implement SPP. To increase the usage and to improve public investment implementation, the project will finance the following key IT enhancements to the e-GP system: (a) large Works with Fédération Internationale Des Ingénieurs-Conseils Contract template following open tender method (OTM)/limited tender method (LTM)/direct procurement; (b) procurement of works and goods following international market approach using MDB Standard Procurement Documents (SPDs) with rated criteria; (c) works and goods with multiple items and multiple lots; (d) Open Contracting Data Standards (OCDS) compliance; (e) upstream integration of APP with the IBAS++ Budget module; (f) security upgrade from symmetric to more secure hybrid of latest symmetric and asymmetric encryption; and (g) data center upgrade. 40. The subcomponent will also start the development of a second-generation e-GP system. Since the e-GP system's launch, significant technological advancements have emerged. For example, artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning are mainstreamed into business applications. Over the past couple of years, monolithic applications have been modernized into microservices architectures to reduce downtime. The adoption of API gateways and workload balancing across worker nodes has become a standard practice for optimizing system performance. Additionally, large-scale databases are increasingly segmented by service, minimizing database size and significantly improving query response times. The trend is to move the applications from data center to cloud. Adopting these technological changes will significantly improve e-GP systems’ performance. In addition, there is a need to develop sustainable operations and maintenance plan. The project will finance detailed study to evaluate the system design options, cloud deployment readiness, microservices architecture, business process reengineering, and user experience assessment to improve public procurement efficiency. The outputs from this study will be used to procure and deploy the upgraded Bangladesh e- Procurement system version 2.0 with this project financing. The project will also finance the annual maintenance and e- GP security audit and address the shortcomings if any. Component 5: Strengthening Public Audit through Digital Transformation and Capacity Enhancement (US$25 million; US$2 million government counterpart funding) 45. The component, implemented by OCAG, aims to improve timeliness and effectiveness of external audits of all government expenditures to hold public entities accountable through oversight by the legislature and citizens. While there were a number of initiatives in recent years to enhance capacity of OCAG standardization including adoption of International Standards of Supreme Audit Institutions (ISSAI) and IT-based auditing, inadequate number of auditors, lack of technological advancement and integration with other digital system PFM and peripheral institutions, OCAG is yet to bring the IT tools and techniques to a level essential for auditing specialized sectors such as IT systems, environmental sustainability, health supply chains, and large-scale infrastructure projects. The absence of expertise in these areas further restricts its capacity to address emerging challenges. Tools such as big data analytics, fraud detection software, and real- time auditing remain largely untapped, limiting OCAG’s effectiveness and resulting in a skill gap. Hence, the project will aim to address the impediments causing delays in timely preparation finance accounts, preparation and publication of audit report through end-to-end technological transformation of all processes involved with the ecosystem of audit report, capacity building, and professionalization of auditors with technology-enabled auditing. PROJECT APPRAISAL SUMMARY A. Technical, Economic and Financial Analysis 56. An economic analysis of the SITA Project was carried out based on estimated financial and nonfinancial benefits and costs of the project investments. Such an analysis assesses the project’s value to society through a social cost-benefit analysis (SCBA). The project’s impacts are compared to the business-as-usual scenario. This comparison allows for identification of welfare changes between different groups as a result of the project investments. To assess the overall viability of the project, the net present value (NPV) and the economic internal rate of return (EIRR) are calculated. A positive NPV (greater than zero) indicates that the project is beneficial and would be considered a worthwhile investment. 57. The SCBA requires assumptions about prices, tax rates, inflation, and discount rate for the duration of the project. The implementation of the project is evaluated over a five-year period, and current prices are used for projections over the projection period. A standard VAT rate of 15 percent is applied, along with a reduced rate of 5 percent. Inflation affects the future value of costs and benefits over time. It influences the discount rate choice and the accuracy of long- term economic projections. IMF World Economic Outlook Database October 2024 is used for assumptions about inflation for the duration of the project. The social discount rate reflects the social view of how future benefits and costs should be valued against present benefits and costs. The social discount rate suggested by the Bangladesh Planning Commission (2024) is 12 percent which reflects a high preference for present consumption, implying that future benefits are valued significantly less than immediate benefits. The high social discount rate reflects significant uncertainty regarding investments in Bangladesh. 58. With these assumptions, the SITA Project is viable due to the strong returns to social impacts. It will provide twofold targeted training of women-owned businesses: (a) the first phase will focus on encouraging women-owned businesses to register and participate in the e-GP system, aiming to widen the pool of eligible firms and (b) the second phase will offer capacity- building training for businesses already registered in the system, equipping them with the necessary skills and knowledge to prepare and submit competitive bids. The training will cover essential topics such as understanding procurement requirements, navigating the e-GP platform, and preparing technical and financial proposals. These efforts to strengthen the capacity of women-owned firms to submit public procurement bids complement the recently approved Sustainable Procurement Policy, a Prior Action in the Second Bangladesh Green and Climate Resilient Development Credit, which aims to make public procurement more accessible to women-owned firms. The BPPA will also conduct an annual assessment which will highlight the increased registration of women in the e-GP and capacity-building efforts for women-owned businesses, with a focus on those that are awarded contracts, if any. The gender indicator in the results framework is: share of women entrepreneurs registered in e-GP and eligible to submit bids (percentage). Overall, the project will support women’s capacity building, enhance their digital skills, and improve their access to information and data. The project will also strengthen the capacity of BBS to collect sex-disaggregated data. 76. Citizen engagement. Active engagement with the five IAs as well as the authorizing environment to be secured by the PSC will mitigate implementation risks. Although the interim government has a clear mandate to implement critical reforms of key institutions before credible elections can be organized, the timeline of its tenure is uncertain. Hence, the rating of the overall residual risk of this operation is Substantial, which is reflected in the following four dimensions: • Political and Governance risk is High. Safety and security concerns persist despite the priority the interim government is placing on law and order. The interim government is implementing critical reforms before holding elections. However, reaching a consensus on reforms and the announcement of the election date could heighten political tensions among political parties and the interim government. These risks will be mitigated during implementation through continuous engagement with all relevant government and nongovernmental entities and dialogue with other stakeholders. • Macroeconomic risk is Substantial. Downside risks have increased, related to uncertainties in the political process and the security situation which affects the recovery of economic activities. The financial sector remains vulnerable to deviations from international regulatory and supervisory standards and weak corporate governance. Despite recent improvements, the balance of payments remained in deficit and foreign exchange reserves remained under pressure. The macroeconomic risks are partially mitigated by the government’s reform program, supported by the IMF arrangements and ongoing World Bank and other development partners’ policy lending. • Institutional Capacity for Implementation and Sustainability risks are Substantial since addressing heads- on transparency and accountability in the five selected IAs can face resistance from the bureaucracy. This is mitigated by closely aligning the project interventions with the stand-alone DPC that pursues critical reforms on transparency and accountability with the support of the interim government. Change management activities will also be embedded in each of the five project components to minimize implementation and sustainability risks. • Fiduciary risk is rated Substantial due to the complex design of the project with the involvement of multiple agencies from different ministries. Specific actions to improve capacity and internal controls, and thereby mitigate FM-related risks, will need to be taken by each IA on staffing, mainstreaming use of iBAS++, conducting risk-based audits, and specifying details of fiduciary arrangement and internal controls in the POM.
