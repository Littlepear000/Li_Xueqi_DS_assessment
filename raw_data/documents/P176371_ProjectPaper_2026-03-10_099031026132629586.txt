PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. The Eastern African Regional Statistics for Results Program (EARSP) was approved by the Board of Directors in May 2022 and became effective in August 2022. At approval, the Program Development Objective (PDO) of the regional Program was to strengthen the regional harmonization, dissemination and use of core economic and social statistics for Kenya, Rwanda and Tanzania. The Program was a blend of PforR components for the three participating countries (Rwanda, Kenya, and Tanzania) and IPF components for the East African Community (EAC) and the African Union Commission (AUC), for a total size of US$301 million. In 2024, Burundi, another EAC member state, was added to the Program as an IPF component through additional financing, which raised the total size of the Program to US$321 million. With the addition of Burundi, the PDO was changed to strengthen the regional harmonization, dissemination and use of core economic and social statistics for participating countries. The Program is structured around three Results Areas: (i) Harmonization, quality, dissemination, and use; (ii) Availability and new data sources; and (iii) Infrastructure and institutional development. Achievement of results is measured by 10 Disbursement-Linked Indicators (DLIs). As per the last Implementation Status Report (ISR) of December 2025, progress towards the PDO is rated as satisfactory while implementation progress is moderately satisfactory. 2. As observed during the Mid-Term Review (MTR) of mid-2025, the Program has made strong progress towards the PDO. At the time of the MTR, two of the three PDO indicator targets had already been achieved. On harmonization, the four participating countries had harmonized five common statistical dimensions by the time of the MTR, already attaining the end target of PDO indicator 1. Statistical systems and dissemination in the participating countries have improved, as witnessed by an increase in the score on the Statistical Performance Indicators (SPI - PDO indicator 2). Since baseline, Kenya improved its score on the customized SPI by 13 percentage points, Tanzania by 10 percentage points, and Rwanda by 6 percentage points1. The end target for the SPI is 75 and will likely be attained by end of project (and has already been attained by Rwanda). Use of data in the drafting of high-level policy and strategy documents (PDO indicator 3) has seen strong improvements as well achieved at mid-term. Overall, the indicators reflect (i) strong progress made by participating countries on statistical harmonization and performance and (ii) some PDO targets that may have been set too conservatively at design stage. 3. While progress towards the PDO has been strong, implementation progress has lagged for some of the countries and/or Regional Economic Organizations in the Program. Implementation progress in Kenya – the largest component in the Program with an envelope of US$120 million- has been slow due to insufficient budget allocation from National Treasury to the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS). The Kenya component of the Program received no funding in FY23 and only KES1 billion (out of a planned KES3.3 billion) in FY24, hampering the execution of Program activities and achievement of Disbursement Linked Results (DLRs) by the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS). As a result, the disbursement rate for Kenya stood at 33 percent in November 2025, with only 20 months of project implementation left. The African Union (AU) IPF component, implemented by STATAFRIC, is lagging as well, mainly due to severe delays in staffing the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) and an inflexible AU budget process. Disbursement of the AU component stood at 32 percent in November 2025. The Program’s overall disbursement rate stood at 43% in November 2025 – mainly a reflection of low disbursement in the Program’s largest component. 4. DLI 1: Improved harmonization and quality of statistics (US$25.5 million). This DLI aims to improve harmonization and quality of statistics by supporting the implementation of regional guidelines, extending the data quality and comparability framework at the regional level to include additional data dimensions and developing concepts and frameworks for the integration of new technologies and big data. Progress: Strong progress has been made on regional harmonization of statistics and extending quality frameworks. Of the 9 Disbursement-Linked Results (DLRs) under this DLI, 5 have already been achieved. In addition, the PDO indicator on number of statistical dimensions harmonized according to the applicable regional guidelines has already exceeded its end target of 5. 6. DLI 2: Improved dissemination and use of statistics (US$13.5 million). This DLI aims to contribute to statistical literacy and improve dissemination and use of statistics by supporting digital dissemination through online portals and dashboards, inclusion of gender-, disability- and spatially disaggregated statistics as well as improving the timeliness of statistics by using advanced release calendars. Progress: Overall, strong progress has been made on dissemination and use. Of the 17 DLRs under this DLI, 9 have already been achieved. In addition, the PDO indicator on use of data for decision-making (Number of strategy and policy documents utilizing or referring to data or statistics produced by the operation) has already exceeded its end target. Results Area 2: Availability and new data sources 7. DLI 3: Improved availability of household-based survey data (US$55 million). This DLI aims to improve the production and availability of poverty-, labor- and other socio-economic statistics through household budget and labor force surveys, including data on household vulnerability to climate change. Progress: Progress here has been slower than expected, mainly due to insufficient budget allocation to KNBS in Kenya and the implementation of the 2022 Population and Housing Census in Tanzania, which absorbed all the attention and human resources of the NBS at the start of the Program, resulting in slippage on other activities. Of the 12 DLRs under this DLI, only three have been achieved so far. 8. DLI 4: Improved availability of enterprise-based survey data (US$36 million). This DLI aims to scale up the production of enterprise statistics with details on labor demand and productivity while also maintaining a business registry. Progress: Progress here has been slower than expected, mainly due to insufficient budget allocation to KNBS in Kenya. Of the 18 DLRs under this DLI, four have been completed so far. 9. DLI 5: Improved availability of agricultural statistics (US$28 million). This DLI aims to support agricultural statistics, including by producing agricultural census information and agricultural productivity statistics, which allow tracking the use of agricultural inputs to improve crop resilience, the reduction of energy consumption and GHG emissions, as well as climate risks and impacts. Progress: This DLI is on track, with three of the six DLRs completed so far. Out of 4 annual agricultural surveys in Tanzania, the final and fourth survey will be replaced by a result on administrative agricultural data. 10. DLI 6: Improved availability of administrative data (US$52.5 million). This DLI tackles administrative data gaps by integrating ministries and agencies into the national statistical system to harness, complement and improve available data Page 2 Official Use Only The World Bank Eastern Africa Regional Statistics Program-for-Results (P176371) that they gather through routine data collection systems. Progress: Rwanda and Tanzania have made strong progress towards the DLRs, while Kenya is lagging. Overall, 11 of the 17 DLRs under this DLI have been achieved. 11. DLI 7: Improved availability of price and macroeconomic statistics (US$22 million). This DLI ensures continuous implementation of price statistics feeding into the consumer and producer price indices and will revise and upgrade the System of National Accounts, while improving comparability within the region. Progress: Progress here has been slower than expected. Due to limited budget allocation from Treasury, Kenya has not fully achieved any DLRs under this DLI. Rwanda has achieved 2 of its 4 DLRs, while Tanzania’s progress has been relatively slow (mainly due to the delay in obtaining source data to rebase national accounts). Of the 17 DLRs under this DLI, only three have been achieved so far. 12. DLI 8: Gained experience with big data sources and new technologies (US$10 million). This DLI includes the set up of emergency observatories, rapid phone survey deployments and big data analytics to create capacity to monitor and track emerging crises in real-time. Progress: Most of the DLRs under this DLI were planned to be achieved in the later years of the Program. Guidelines for the use of new data sources and new technologies have been developed by the EAC IPF component and are scheduled to be approved by the Sectoral Council of Statistics in calendar year 2026. Once approved, countries will start working towards achieving their DLRs. Of the 9 DLRs under this DLI, 3 have been achieved so far. Results Area 3: Infrastructure and institutional development 13. DLI 9: Upgraded IT and statistical infrastructure (US$17.5 million). This DLI aims to upgrade IT infrastructure including the setup of data servers and equipment to implement tablet-surveys while establishing technology to monitor and analyze incoming data on-the-fly. The DLI also includes statistical infrastructure, for example the preparation of sampling frames based on population census and the development of modular data platforms that can form the basis for linking datasets across government such as connecting administrative data to monitoring indicators and the budget. Progress: Tanzania and Rwanda are on track to meet the DLRs, though Kenya is lagging. Of the 9 DLRs under this DLI, 2 have been achieved so far (with one additional DLR awaiting verification for Kenya). 14. DLI 10: Enhanced institutional capacity and development (US$15.5 million). This DLI aims to enhance institutional capacity and development by building capacity and improving the institutional setup at the regional, national and sub-national level as well as supporting monitoring and evaluation frameworks. Progress: This DLI is on track. Most of the DLRs are due towards the later years of the Program. So far, 4 out of the 10 DLRs under this DLI have been achieved. 15. Program Action Plan and IPF Components. The EAC IPF component has made strong progress on establishing guidelines for regional harmonization and supporting countries to apply these guidelines, resulting in strong progress on PDO Indicator 1 (harmonization). Progress has, however, been slower on infrastructural and human capacity building, and efforts in these areas are being scaled up. The AUC IPF component experienced substantial start-up delays, with 2.5 years passing until the PIU was fully established. The PIU is now fully operational, and project implementation has accelerated in the past six months. All but one PAP in Tanzania are either completed or in progress. The PAP in Rwanda is progressing smoothly with no delayed actions, and monitoring continues for all recurrent activities. Although the funding shortfall led to delays in PAP implementation in Kenya, all actions are now either in progress or completed. Burundi joined the regional project as an IPF component in July 2024, with a compressed three-year implementation period. Current disbursement is 29%, but Page 3 Official Use Only The World Bank Eastern Africa Regional Statistics Program-for-Results (P176371) implementation is picking up momentum on major activities with the population census completed, the agricultural census and the household living conditions survey in the field, and the construction of a new building on track for completion by project closing. Burundi is not affected by this restructuring. 16. Fiduciary and Safeguards Compliance. There are no substantial issues on fiduciary and safeguards compliance. As per the last ISM, fiduciary systems are rated satisfactory. The social and environmental risk rating of the Program is moderate, and ESS performance is rated moderately satisfactory. 17. The rationale for the restructuring is two-fold: First, ambitions for regional statistical harmonization will be raised in light of strong progress on the PDO. Second, slow implementation progress for some of the components requires a scale-down of activities and cancellation of part of the original credit or grant. Next to these two main changes, the restructuring will also do a number of changes in the results framework, both to correct errors in baseline values of certain indicators and reflect changes to the scope of some of the components. Finally, the restructuring will change some of the DLRs in light of changes to the scope of some of the components. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 18. The restructuring proposes cancellaton for Kenya and restructuring for Kenya and Tanzania. There are also changes to the results framework of the project, with an increase in targets for PDO indicators, as well as changes to the intermediate indicators. The EAC, AU, Rwanda and Burundi components will not be affected by the restructuring, except for the changes in the Results Framework. Detailed changes are described below. 19. Kenya component will undergo the most comprehensive restructuring and cancellation. Kenya’s restructuring is required due to persistent budget shortfalls and limited time left before program closure in June 2027. Out of the planned KSh 3.3 billion (around EUR 22 million) annual allocation, the Program received no funding from the National Treasury in FY23 and only KSh 1 billion in FY24. In addition, a recent EUR 17.5 million advance provided to the National Treasury in September 2025 had not yet been transferred to KNBS by end of November 2025, further delaying implementation. Although the World Bank has disbursed EUR 35.7 million, only EUR 14.1 million has been spent, and only EUR 17.1 million in activities have been verified, with EUR 7.6 million awaiting verification. This gap makes it unlikely that Kenya can disburse the full EUR 107.2 million Program within the remaining period. To maximize impact, the restructuring will focus on retaining resources for large-scale activities that can be completed in time, such as the Kenya Integrated Household Budget Survey (KIHBS), the Integrated Labor Force Survey (ILFS), and the Census of Agriculture. Activities with other funding sources namely the Micro Small and Medium Enterprise Survey (DLR 2.2 (c)) and the Non-Communicable Diseases Survey (DLR 2.4 (d)) or where the implementation window has lapsed (e.g., post-enumeration surveys (DLI 1.1(a)) and the Victimization Survey (DLR 2.1(d)) will be dropped. Additionally, surveys including the International Trade in Services Survey (DLR 2.2(a)), the Survey of Non-Profit Institutions (DLR 2.5 (d)) and the Census of Industrial Production (DLR 2.2 (f)) will also be dropped due to insufficient time remaining to the Program’s closing date. While these activities are important - and still considered a priority by KNBS – they cannot be implemented under the current program timeline given slow implementation progress. . The total amount of financing to be cancelled is equal to EUR 32 million. Page 4 Official Use Only The World Bank Eastern Africa Regional Statistics Program-for-Results (P176371) 20. Tanzania. Program implementation in Tanzania started with a delay due to the post-enumeration activities related to the 2022 Population and Housing Census. As a result, two Labor Force Surveys (DLR 2.1 (c)) scheduled for 2022 and 2023 could not be completed as planned. It was also deemed a priority to conduct one more round of the household panel survey (known as the NPS – DLR 2.1 (b)) to extend the longitudinal household data. The Program envisioned two rebasings of National Accounts (DLR 2.5 (e)), one in the first year of implementation (to a new base year of 2019) and the second in the final year (to a new base year of 2024). However, the first rebasing has been delayed, and is expected to be completed in 2025. In this context, the rationale for the second rebasing is weak, therefore the DLR will be cancelled. The Program planned to conduct four annual agriculture surveys under DLR 2.3 (a). However, to strengthen the administrative agriculture data system, Tanzania requested to replace the final agriculture survey under this DLR with a result on administrative agriculture data. As a result, the restructuring will replace the final (fourth) agricultural survey with a result on administrative agriculture data. Finally, an informal sector survey will be added (DLR 2.2 (f)) to provide firm-level data of informal businesses. Additional restructuring proposals include changing the disbursement schedule to make the result scalable and minor changes in the wording (e.g., changing the reference period of surveys from calendar year to fiscal year or agricultural seasons). 21. Changes to the Results Framework. Given the strong progress on statistical harmonization, the restructuring proposes to double the target on the first PDO indicator (“ Number of published statistical dimensions that are harmonized according to the applicable regional technical guidelines by participating countries”) from 5 to 10. The new target is ambitous yet achievable, and represents significant progress towards the ultimate goal of fully harmonized statistics. Despite the cancellation of some activities for Kenya, the end target for the PDO indicator on “Number of strategy and policy documents utilizing or referring to data or statistics produced by the operation across participating countries” will be increased from 12 to 20 based on strong progress on this indicator.
