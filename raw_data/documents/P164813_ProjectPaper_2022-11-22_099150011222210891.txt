PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING Project Status 1. The Lao PDR Competitiveness and Trade Project (LCTP) was approved on September 27, 2018 and declared effective on December 10, 2018, and is scheduled to close on December 31, 2024. The project initial financing was SDR7.2 million or US$10 million equivalent from an IDA Credit, and US$3 million from the Lao PDR Competitiveness and Trade Multi-donor Trust Fund (LCT-MDTF) – TF073096. An Additional Financing (AF) of US$6.5 million from the LCT-MDTF, contributed by the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) of Australia, Irish Aid and United States Agency for International Development (USAID), was approved on June 25, 2021 and became effective on October 5, 2021. 2. With the AF, the project total financing increased from US$13 million to US$19.5 million, with US$10 million equivalent from an IDA Credit, and US$9.5 million aggregate amount of the LCT-MDTF grant. The additional amount provided through the Grant Agreement at the time the AF was approved was limited to US$ 2.72 million, reflecting the funds available in the LCT-MDTF at that time. It was agreed with the Government of Lao PDR (GoL), that as and when additional installments and additional contributions are made by the donors to the LCT-MDTF, these would be processed through an amendment of the Grant Agreement to reach the full amount of the LCT-MDTF grant of US$9.5 million without a need for restructuring. As part of the restructuring linked to the AF, the closing date of the IDA financing (IDA-63180) was extended from December 31, 2022 to December 31, 2024. However, the extension of the closing date of the LCT-MDTF grant (TF0A9747) was not possible as it was subject to the extension of the End Disbursement Date (EDD) of the LCT-MDTF from June 30, 2023 to June 30, 2025. This extension of the EDD of the LCT- MDTF was approved by the Regional Vice-President on June 16, 2021 and the EDD was extended to June 30, 2025 after signature of all three contributing donors. 3. The latest Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) dated August 16, 2022 confirmed that the project objective continues to be highly relevant to the ongoing priorities of Lao PDR and the Bank. Progress towards achievement of the PDO and implementation progress are rated Moderately Satisfactory and Satisfactory respectively. There are no outstanding audit reports or other fiduciary issues, and no safeguards issues were encountered in the most recent implementation support mission. Disbursement to date is US$10.22 million, out of which US$7.43 million is from IDA and US$2.79 million is from the LCT-MDTF Grant. This is 65 percent of the project current financing received of US$ 15.72 million. Rationale for restructuring 4. An official request from the Ministry of Finance (MOF) No. 0240/MOF, dated January 25, 2022, for a project restructuring was received by the World Bank on January 31, 2022. The details of the restructuring were also discussed and agreed during the LCTP additional financing and implementation support missions from November 8 to 18 2021 and from May 16 to June 16, 2022. These missions discussed the revised results framework, based upon corporate guidance on the replacement of projects indicators linked to Doing Business. The proposed new detailed results framework with revised indicators and targets was received from the counterpart on October 4th, 2022. 5. The proposed restructuring would: (i) replace Doing Business indicators following the discontinuation of the World Bank Doing Business report, including two at the PDO level and four at the intermediate level; (ii) extend the MDTF grant closing date to align with the closing date of the IDA credit. The amount in the MDTF grant agreement will also be increased by US$ 3.78, from US$ 5.72 million (US$ 3 million original grant plus US$ 2.72 million grant top-up during the AF) to the aggregate amount of US$ 9.5 million to reflect the additional contributions received under the LCT- MDTF. The World Bank Lao PDR Competitiveness and Trade Project (P164813) II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 6. Replacement of Doing Business (DB) indicators in project results framework. A restructuring is required to replace the DB indicators in the results framework with others which can be measured at the national level. Two PDO level and four intermediate level indicators have been adjusted (see Table 1 below for detailed changes in definition, baseline and target). In addition, one intermediate indicator is replaced with a new indicator to track progress more adequately under Pillar A by gender. 7. Summary of key changes to indicators. Table 1 below offers detailed description of the changes in definition, baseline and target. The key changes are as follows: (a) PDO Indicators:  PDO Indicator 1— “Time needed to start a business”: Replace the DB data source with administrative survey of newly registered businesses to measure the time required through all steps to start a business. The survey will be conducted at least twice between now and the end of the project, the first one is scheduled to be conducted at the end of 2022 and the second in 2024.  PDO Indicator 2—"Time to prepare documentary requirements for imports and exports”: Replace this PDO indicator with the intermediate indicator 10—"Mean time to clear imports, exports, and transit, through customs”. The Time Release Study, a World Customs Organization-based instrument, will be used to replace the DB data on Trading Across Borders. Two TRS surveys will be conducted between now and the end of the project, one in 2022 and one in 2024. (b) Intermediate indicators  Intermediate Indicator 4—"Time to issue Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC)”: Replace the DB data source with administrative survey of newly registered businesses to measure the time required through all steps to start a business including steps to obtain an ERC. The survey will be conducted twice between now and the end of the project, the first one end of 2022 and the second in 2024  Intermediate Indicator 6—"Procedures to start a business for women” will be formulated as “Time needed to start a business owned by a woman as a percentage of time to start a business owned by a man”. Data source will be the newly established firm survey previously mentioned.  Intermediate Indicator 7—"Number of new enterprise registrations per year” and 7.1—"Percentage of women-owned enterprises registrations per year (Percentage)”: Adjust baselines and targets.  Intermediate Indicator 10—"Mean time to clear imports, exports, and transit, through customs”: Replace this Intermediate Indicator with PDO indicator 2—"Time to prepare documentary requirements for imports and exports”. The Time Release Study, a World Customs Organization-based instrument, will be used to replace the DB data on Trading Across Borders. Two TRS surveys will be conducted between now and the end of the project, one in 2022and one in 2024. 8. Extend the Multi-Donor Trust Fund (MDTF) grant closing date. The project timeframe as well as the IDA credit closing date was already extended as part of the restructuring linked to the AF. Through this proposed restructuring, the closing date of the MDTF grant will be extended to December 31, 2024 to align with the closing date of the IDA credit. The new closing date of the Grant is before the EDD of the LCT-MDTF. 9. Revise MDTF Grant Amount. The overall grant amount of $9.5m is already reflected in the project document and therefore does not require a restructuring. The signed amount of the LCT-MDTF grant is US$5.72 million – US$3 million of the original grant plus US$2.72 million out of the US$6.5 million of the AF additional grant. The additional amount The World Bank Lao PDR Competitiveness and Trade Project (P164813) of US$2.72 million was based on the available funds in the LCT-MDTF at the time the AF was approved. Additional installments and additional contributions were made by the donors to the LCT-MDTF by March 23, 2022. These additional grant resources will be provided to the GoL through an amendment to the Grant Agreement to reach the aggregate amount of the LCT-MDTF grant of US$9.5 million.
