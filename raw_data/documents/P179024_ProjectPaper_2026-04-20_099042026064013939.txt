PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING Project Status 1. The Project Development Objective (PDO) continues to be fully relevant, and the project has delivered tangible results to date including emergency support with increased benefits under the UBK (Uy-bulogo komok–“family assistance”) program; strong outcomes under the Social Contract Subcomponent with target number of beneficiaries achieved; and good progress under the Youth Employment interventions with a significant number of youth having been placed in internships and subsequently employed. At the same time, implementation has been slower than anticipated in the reform-oriented subcomponent on improving the UBK targeting methodology and newly restructured activities supporting migrants and disability rehabilitation services. Disbursements during the last 12 months remained low, requiring stronger execution planning and timely high-level decisions, especially on pending procurements and the use of funds initially allocated for UBK piloting, to meet remaining targets by the closing date of April 30, 2027. Component 1: Providing Emergency Support through, and Enhancing the Effectiveness of, the UBK Program 2. Progress on the UBK targeting reform (Subcomponent 1.2), closely linked to the Second Sustainable and Inclusive Growth Development Policy Operation (DPO 2),1 has been slower than expected. While technical work on improved eligibility criteria advanced, the planned pilot of a revised targeting methodology was not implemented due to a change in approach. Consequently, reforms are proceeding through incremental parametric adjustments within the existing legal framework, which may have limited standalone impact. Given these constraints, a clear, time-bound workplan is needed to strengthen the UBK delivery mechanism. The proposed workplan should focus on operational and institutional measures (including digitalization, data exchange, income imputation, frontline capacity, communication, and program evaluation). Finalizing and adopting the workplan is critical to sustaining reform momentum, aligning with DPO 2, and improving program effectiveness. The Ministry of Labor, Social Security and Migration (MLSSM) has prepared a draft workplan, which is expected to go into effect with the decree of the Cabinet of Ministers approving the new UBK methodology. Component 2: Increasing the Employability of Vulnerable Groups Including Labor Migrants 3. Subcomponent 2.1 demonstrated strong performance, with 3,000 beneficiaries supported under the project and the Social Contract program scaled up nationally using government resources. This reflects strong ownership and institutionalization. The MLSSM proposed additional consultancy activities to support program integration into regional clusters and to evaluate program effectiveness and design improvements, given the scale of implementation and available project resources. 4. Under Subcomponent 2.2, Youth Employment activities progressed, though implementation diverged from the original design due to delays in approvals, procurement, and completion of skills‑demand research. As a shift from the original program design, due to substantial number of unemployed youths already with formal education, the MLSSM placed those with completed higher or technical and vocational education (TVET) directly into internships. As a result, 1,851 youths were placed in the internships, with 72 percent subsequently employed. 1Second Sustainable and Inclusive Growth Development Policy Operation for the Kyrgyz Republic (P510257): https://opswork.worldbank.org/home/P510257 Official Use Only The World Bank Strengthening Social Assistance and Labor Market Programs Project (P179024) 5. Subcomponent 2.3 showed renewed progress following agreement on data‑sharing arrangements enabling development of the Migrant Registry. Work is ongoing to resolve technical integration requirements. Progress on establishing migrant resource centers has been slower, with decisions on identifying the location of potential migrant resource centers under the TVET institutions taking longer to confirm. Considering the delayed decision on identification of centers and the overall lengthy procurement processes under the project, the remaining activities may not be completed before project closure and the Intermediate Results Indicator (IRI: Number of labor migrants provided with improved employment opportunities through established pre-departure centers) is unlikely to be fully achieved. 6. Subcomponent 2.4 remains at an early stage; construction of originally planned facilities has not advanced, prompting discussion of alternative rehabilitation center facilities under the MLSSM. Site visits highlighted significant infrastructure and equipment gaps. Prioritizing facilities that support labor market reintegration and expediting planning and consultant hiring are important to ensure completion of all activities by the project closing date. To date, the draft plan of activities and procurement packages has been prepared. Component 3: Project Management, Monitoring, and Evaluation 7. Under Subcomponent 3.1, recent institutional changes have introduced uncertainty regarding procurement modalities and compliance with Bank rules. While most IT procurements have been completed, new activities were added, including employment e‑books, e‑contracts, and potential Labor Market Information System upgrades. IT responsibilities under this Subcomponent were transferred to the Open Joint Stock Company “Tunduk” recently, necessitating a review of the procurement modalities considering Tunduk’s mandate to develop IT systems for government agencies and the Bank procurement procedures. The MLSSM is engaging with relevant stakeholders and the Bank procurement teams to clarify next steps. 8. On Subcomponent 3.2, the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) remains adequately staffed, but procurement decision‑making delays continue to affect implementation. Closer coordination between PIU and MLSSM is needed to strengthen planning and execution and ensure that remaining activities are completed and project funds are fully disbursed by the closing date of April 30, 2027. To address these challenges, the PIU and MLSSM will update the implementation plan and disbursement projections to support delivery planning and financial forecasting. 9. Fiduciary performance is rated as Satisfactory. The project’s procurement risk has been assessed as Moderate due to the nature of the remaining procurement packages, which are relatively low in value and risk, are not complex and aimed mostly at the local market. Procurement performance rating is Satisfactory because Procurement planning in general is adequate and realistic, procurement plans aligned with the budget are prepared routinely by the agency, procurement process defined “on paper” is effectively applied consistently “in practice” with minor deviations. Financial Management arrangements at the MLSSM PIU continue to be acceptable to the Bank. There are no overdue audits of the project financial statements. 10. Environmental and Social (E&S) Compliance. As per the World Bank's Environmental and Social Risk Classification, the project's social risk is Moderate, and its environmental risk is Low. The overall E&S performance is Satisfactory. E&S management is implemented in line with the Environmental and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP), supported by the PIU and monitored through semi-annual reporting. Following the 2025 restructuring and introduction of Component 2.4, the ESCP was updated (agreed with the Bank and disclosed on July 11, 2025) to reflect revised reporting arrangements and the use of Environmental Official Use Only The World Bank Strengthening Social Assistance and Labor Market Programs Project (P179024) and Social Management Plan (ESMP) checklists for renovation activities; a precautionary screening approach will be applied, as the environmental and social standard 5 (ESS 5 Land Acquisition, Restrictions on Land Use and Involuntary Resettlement) does not apply. Stakeholder engagement, labor management, and grievance redress mechanisms remain fully operational and compliant with the ESSs, and the project will continue to be implemented in accordance with the ESCP, Labor Management Procedures (LMP), and the Stakeholder Engagement Plan (SEP). Rationale for Restructuring 11. Implementation of the Youth Employment pilot under Subcomponent 2.2 has evolved since appraisal and early implementation. In response to labor market demand and beneficiary profiles, the project now supports two complementary pathways: (i) employability and technical and vocational training followed by paid internships, and (ii) direct placement of unemployed youth who already possess relevant education or formal qualifications into paid internships. Specifically, it was originally envisaged that internships for unemployed youth would follow the mandatory in-class training (short-term vocational courses). However, at the launch of the pilot, there was a significant number of youths with completed formal vocational or higher education registered as unemployed, who were ready to be directly placed in internships. The restructuring request submitted by the Borrower seeks to align results measurement with this approved and operationalized design. However, existing PDO‑level and intermediate indicators remain narrowly focused on completion of in‑class training, resulting in systematic under‑reporting of beneficiaries participating in the program and employment outcomes achieved. To ensure consistency between the results framework and implementation guidance, the Youth Employment Sub-Manual will be updated to reflect the revised results definition and associated reporting arrangements. 12. Subcomponent 2.3 was introduced under the June 2025 restructuring to pilot pre‑departure and reintegration support for temporary labor migrants. As documented in the Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) of February 2026 and acknowledged in the Borrower’s restructuring request, initial implementation has focused on establishing institutional arrangements, operational procedures, and partnerships with participating vocational institutions. Due to the late introduction of the Subcomponent, capacity constraints, and the remaining short implementation period, progress toward service delivery has been slower than originally anticipated. 13. Revising the Results Framework is therefore necessary to ensure credible monitoring and reporting, align indicators with approved implementation adjustments, and accurately capture the project’s contribution to improved labor market outcomes for youth and migrants, without altering the PDO, component structure, or component costs.
