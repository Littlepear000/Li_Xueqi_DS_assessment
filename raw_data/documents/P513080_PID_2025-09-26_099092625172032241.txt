Introduction and Context 1. This Project Appraisal Document (PAD) outlines the rationale and objectives of Phases II-IV of the Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA) to support the implementation of the Progestão Program in Brazil, comprising the states of Amazonas, Pernambuco, and Sergipe. The MPA, approved by the World Bank’s Executive Directors on March 18, 2025 (P181608 Report number PADHI00559), along with a first operation in the amount of US$50 million to the state of Rio Grande do Sul as Phase I of the MPA, is backed by a US$1 billion credit line guaranteed by the Government of Brazil (GoB). Its objective is to enhance public expenditure efficiency and fiscal stability in Brazilian states. The MPA is designed to incentivize and support structural reforms across Brazilian states, incorporating lessons learned from previous stand-alone operations. Its design enables innovative interventions across states, promotes economies of scale, and allows for tailored solutions to meet the specific needs of each state. Phases II-IV will adhere to the MPA’s objectives and framework and will focus on advancing public sector reforms, improving the efficiency of resource management, and addressing existing fiscal challenges. The poverty rate declined from 21.7 to 20.9 percent (US$6.85 per day, 2017 PPP) in 2024, thanks to the strong labor market. However, it is expected to decline more slowly from 2025 onward due to moderated economic growth and limited fiscal space for social spending. Recent structural reforms have improved the business environment, the financial sector, and indirect taxation. Sectoral and Institutional Context _For Official Use Only_ Sectoral and Institutional Context 4. Brazilian states remain under significant fiscal strain despite some improvement since the pandemic. Before COVID-19, seven of 27 states had declared a fiscal emergency and 17 were ineligible to borrow under federal rules, with 20 delaying payments to public servants and suppliers. During the pandemic federal transfers totaling R$97 billion (US$17.63 billion, or 1.4 percent of GDP) helped sustain activity and tax revenues, but personnel expenditure still rose by 19.7 percent between 2020 and 2022, with payroll and pensions growing 6.3 percent in 2022 and 3.7 percent in 2023. By 2025, the number of states rated ‘C’ or ‘D’ under the Payment Capacity Index (Capacidade de Pagamento, CAPAG) dropped to six from eight in 2024, yet 12 states exceeded the 44.1 percent Fiscal Responsibility Law (Lei de Responsabilidade Fiscal, LRF) threshold for personnel spending. In 2023, states employed 88 percent of public servants, accounting for 75 percent of the national payroll. In 2024, states spent R$730.3 billion (US$132.8 billion) on personnel, up from R$540 billion (US$98.1 billion) in 2021. Rigid, fragmented career structures, rapid progression without performance-based criteria, and costly, inequitable pensions—worsened by uneven adoption of the 2019 pension reform—continue to drive deficits, requiring administrative, governance, and pension reforms for fiscal sustainability. 5. Structural inefficiencies in high-cost areas further constrain fiscal space. Procurement accounted for 15.7 percent of most states spending in 2024, but fragmented systems, poor data, and uncoordinated purchases drive avoidable cost variations. Public investment averaged 6.1 percent of budgets in 2024, and lacked rigorous appraisal, risk assessment, and cost planning, leading to delays and overruns. Intergovernmental transfers suffer from weak resource mobilization and limited accountability in many Brazilian states, while in 2024, states gross debt averaged 65.2 percent of net current revenues with debt service at 4.5 percent. State-owned enterprises (SOEs) often require subsidies, and asset management is poor—generating just R$892.7 million (US$162.3 million) from real estate in 2023, under 0.5 percent of estimated value—while Information and Communication Technology (ICT) costs more than doubled from R$4.9 billion (US$870 million) in 2019 to R$11.2 billion (US$2.03 billion) in 2023 amid decentralized, overlapping functions. 6. Essential services in health, education, and social assistance—representing over 40 percent of states budgets— are undermined by inefficiencies and weak financial management. Between 2019 and 2023, federal reimbursements covered only 23.9 percent of states health spending due to verification delays. Education resources for transport, school meals, and direct funding are poorly managed with outdated processes and procedures, while the Unified Social Assistance System (Sistema Único de Assistência Social, SUAS) in most states lacks financial management tools. In 2023, Page 3 Official Use Only The World Bank MPA Phase IV - Progestão Sergipe: Public Sector Management Efficiency Project (P513080) these sectors executed over R$350 billion (US$63.63 billion) across the 27 subnational entities, with a 12 percent deviation from planned budgets. Even a 1 percent improvement in financial management could save billions, underscoring the urgent need for better planning, execution, and auditing to protect fiscal health and improve service delivery. 7. Sergipe has improved fiscal management, but challenges remain. The state secured an upgrade in its CAPAG rating from B+ in 2023 to A in 2024, reflecting adherence to accounting standards and fiscal adjustment measures. A strong primary surplus of R$1.6 billion (US$300 million) in 2024 was achieved, supported by a nominal revenue growth of 18 percent, while net consolidated debt stood at 11.6 percent of current revenues in 2023. The fiscal position was pressured by a uniform national fuel tax set below the state rate, higher national minimum wages, and weak federal income tax performance, prompting progressive consumption tax increases and strict control of current expenditures. Despite these gains, exposure to federal transfers (Fundo de Participação dos Estados, FPE), rigid mandatory spending, and rising personnel and pension costs continue to constrain investment capacity, leaving sustainability dependent on ongoing structural reforms. 8. High non-discretionary human resources (HR) expenses persist. Payroll pressures are mounting with the wage bill for 47,771 active civil servants reaching R$3.8 billion (US$709 million) in 2024, increasing at an average of 13.3 percent per year since 2021. Outdated payroll and HR systems hinder audits, workforce sizing, and effective decision-making. Planned reforms aim to contain payroll growth, with estimated savings of R$432 million (US$78.5 million), and at least R$116 million (US$21 million) from reducing human error and fraud. Pensions remain a critical pressure point. In 2024, about 60,300 pension beneficiaries received monthly payments totaling R$204.9 million (US$37.2 million). The pension system runs an annual deficit of nearly R$1.1 billion (US$200 million). Weaknesses in predictive analytics, actuarial _For Official Use Only_ modeling, and investment oversight, combined with mass fund segregation, highlight the need for stronger data and controls. Ongoing pension reforms target R$163 million (US$29.6 million) in savings and faster processing of benefits. 9. Public procurement costs accelerate amid systemic weaknesses. Public purchases account for about 24.8 percent of the government total expenditure, and spending on consumables grew by 17 percent per year between 2020 and 2024. Procurement governance, manuals, key performance indicators, and acquisition strategies remain fragmented and lack standardization. Planning and contract management are weak, limiting oversight and control. Outdated systems and manual processes persist in the absence of Artificial Intelligence (AI), robotic process automation (RPA), and smart contracts, constraining efficiency and transparency. The supplier base is narrow, reducing competition and price alignment, while procurement agents often lack professional certification. Finally, limited hardware and Information Technology (IT) investments restrict the adoption of modern digital tools needed to support reform. 10. Public investments lag behind national standards and execution remains constrained. In 2024, the investment rate reached 4.34 percent of net current revenue (R$665 million, US$121 million), below the national average of 10 percent. Delivery continues to lag due to a semi-structured project repository without consistent technical, financial, and risk filters, which weakens prioritization, readiness, and selection, from pipeline to budget to implementation. Limited transparency, and inadequate control in investment and transfer management often result in cost overruns, delays, and misallocation rather than the full realization of intended benefits. Gaps in Integrated Finance and Administration System – System of Public Administration (Sistema Integrado de Administração Financeira e Control - Sistema de Gestão Pública, SIAFICI-Gesp), the state’s core financial system, further disrupt integrated planning, budgeting, and execution workflows, reinforcing manual workarounds and fragmented data. In parallel, proposals to establish a State Sovereign Fund and enhance its governance highlight the need for clear mandates, eligibility criteria, and oversight mechanisms to ensure strategic investments are well-anchored and to prevent fragmented decision-making with weak accountability, which could hinder private sector engagement. 11. Public asset management faces systemic challenges that undermine efficiency and value creation. Outdated, semi-manual controls and incomplete inventories of immovable and movable assets drive underutilization, weak maintenance, and recurrent losses. The lack of reliable data and integrated systems hampers lifecycle management — covering inventory, valuation, maintenance, and disposal—while limited methodologies and insufficient training reduce consistency and oversight. These gaps are reflected in the real estate portfolio, which posts a negative net balance of Page 4 Official Use Only The World Bank MPA Phase IV - Progestão Sergipe: Public Sector Management Efficiency Project (P513080) R$2.2 million, underscoring the poor exploitation of state properties and the broader governance weaknesses that constrain fiscal returns and alignment with the investment agenda. 12. Sergipe’s fiscal management of health and education is constrained by structural budget rigidity—especially personnel and pension costs—and by operational inefficiencies that weaken value for money. In health, fragmented systems limit control over a R$2.98 billion (US$545 million) budget, with weak hospital stock management, decentralized execution, and increasing pressures from judicialization and avoidable costs. In education, weak information systems and limited oversight undermine school transport and meal programs, leading to inefficiencies and delays. About 13 percent of the 5,148 basic education classes in the state network enroll fewer than five students, inflating unit costs. School transport operates with limited data and poor controls; in the absence of an integrated management system, transfers to providers are delayed and financial reporting is late, undermining accountability and service reliability. Similar system gaps affect school meals, where procurement, contracting, and logistics require modernization; without robust integrated tools, controls remain weak, raising risks of waste, stock imbalances, and mistimed deliveries. Optimizing class offerings by 5 percent could generate savings of up to R$17.6 million (US$3.2 million) per year. 13. Medium and long-term sustainability will depend on Sergipe’s continued commitment to structural reforms. The state needs to control the growth of mandatory expenditures, strengthen governance, and enhance public investment through evidence-based planning and prioritization criteria. Accordingly, the proposed project as phase IV of the Progestão Program MPA aims to promote the continuous improvement of fiscal, budgetary, and asset management in the state government through actions focused on rationalizing public spending and generating sustainable results. _For Official Use Only_ C. D. Project Description 15. The State of Sergipe has requested the World Bank’s assistance to consolidate recent reforms and improve efficiency in whole-of-government public sector management systems and service delivery in sectors under the Progestão Program. The proposed scope for the project is fully aligned with the Progestão Program objectives. Sergipe requested assistance in all the cross-cutting areas in the program, including human resource management; pensions; public procurement; public investment management and budgeting; and asset management. It will also support investments in financial management and information systems aiming at improving fiscal efficiency in health, education, and social assistance. E. Implementation Institutional and Implementation Arrangements A. Institutional and Implementation Arrangements For the MPA 26. The Progestão Program implements a robust multitiered framework for M&E to support effective execution and accurate results tracking. PrDO indicators are monitored at the national level by the World Bank in coordination with the STN within its existing responsibilities and to be reported semiannually by participating agencies through their PMUs to both entities. To ensure data accuracy, the STN transparency portal serves as the primary data source, enabling Page 8 Official Use Only The World Bank MPA Phase IV - Progestão Sergipe: Public Sector Management Efficiency Project (P513080) cross-verification with official vetted data. This allows the World Bank to check PMU submissions for consistency. The World Bank implementation support team will also provide technical support to the STN to implement the MPA M&E framework. For the Progestão Sergipe Project (Phase IV) 27. SEFAZ will lead project implementation and host the Project Management Unit (PMU). Specific implementation arrangements include a Steering Committee (SC), a PMU, a state decree establishing the roles and responsibilities of SEFAZ and implementing agencies, and interagency agreement with Sergipe Previdência. SEFAZ was selected to lead the project and host the PMU due to its technically qualified and tenured staff The project will leverage SEFAZ’ experience in managing externally financed operations, including previous ones with the World Bank and the Interamerican Development Bank (IADB) ensuring continuity across administrations, its mandate to coordinate reform programs; and its experience with planning, budgeting, and technical assistance projects. 28. The SC is an advisory body comprising representatives from each participating agency, appointed by the State Governor. Chaired by SEFAZ, the SC provides strategic guidance and oversight for the reforms and project implementation. It is responsible for coordinating policy, setting priorities, resolving conflicts, ensuring interagency collaboration, and monitoring project progress. 29. To ensure smooth and well-coordinated project implementation, the PMU will host three technical coordinators responsible for key areas of the project: (i) human resources, pensions, and asset management; (ii) planning, investment, and budget management; and (iii) education and health sectors. 30.
