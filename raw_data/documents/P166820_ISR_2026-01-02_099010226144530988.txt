OBJECTIVE 1.1 Development Objective Original Development Objective (Approved as part of Approval package on 30-Jul-2020) The Project development objectives of the Fourth Phase of the Central Asia Regional Links Program are to enhance the efficiency of cross-border trade for participants of the regional economy and to improve the resilience and safety of regional connectivity infrastructure in Sughd region and Gorno-Badakhshan Autonomous region. The project contributes to the Central Asia Regional Links Programs higher-level development objectives of increasing cross-border connectivity and enhancing integrated regional development to revitalize historically active economic exchanges in Central Asia and beyond along the Silk Route. KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions Component 1 – Regional Connections (Sughd & GBAO): Civil works are well advanced with about 38.9 km of rehabilitated roads in Sughd were opened to traffic in November 2025 (three sections inaugurated by the President), and climate-resilient works in GBAO (new Khorog bridge, avalanche gallery) are 100% complete and operational. However, contract supervision quality has been below standard, and the supervision consultant must strengthen on-site oversight and technical diligence. Environmental & social (E&S) safeguards need closer attention: construction safety measures and OHS practices require urgent improvement (a worker fatality in Sughd in August 2025 prompted a Corrective Action Plan). The Bank rates overall E&S performance as Moderately Satisfactory and urges the Ministry of Transport to enforce safety compliance, ensure proper use of PPE, and promptly address community grievances (e.g. wheelchair access and local damage issues around the Khorog bridge). These measures are critical to maintain progress and meet standards as the remaining road works (about 12 km in Sughd) finish by early 2026. Component 2 – Road Asset Preservation & Road Safety: Key road asset initiatives are underway. Two Weigh-in-Motion stations near Dushanbe are under installation (contract commenced Oct 2025, completion due June 2026) to protect road infrastructure from overloading. A national Road Asset Management System (RAMS) is being developed (software contract signed May 2025) and is on track, with testing planned by Dec 2025 and full acceptance expected by Feb 2026. On road safety, two critical legislative actions are pending, a new National Road Safety Strategy and an enhanced seat- belt law have been drafted and submitted to the Government but not yet approved. This delay is hindering implementation of safety campaigns and enforcement; high-level follow-up is needed to secure timely approval of both the strategy and the seat-belt law (the latter is essential to raise seat-belt use from the current ~20% to the 60% target). Other road safety measures are in progress: a pilot for installing safety barriers along 60 km of high-risk mountain roads is contracted (equipment has arrived and installation is supervised by the consultant). Additionally, a Road Safety Observatory has just been initiated (consultant mobilized Oct 2025); it is urgent to procure required IT systems so the crash database and training activities can be completed before project closure. Component 3 – Cross-Border Trade Facilitation (Customs): The customs modernization program shows strong progress, but a few outstanding actions require attention. ASYCUDA World (the new customs IT system) has been fully deployed nationwide as of October 1, 2025, replacing the old UAIS system. The transition has been smooth with significantly reduced clearance times for traders (current average customs clearance times for both imports and exports are already well below baseline, meeting or exceeding targets). The focus now is on consolidating these gains: several pending bylaws under the revised Customs Code still need to be issued to cement the new procedures, and the Bank has requested a detailed roadmap with timelines for enacting all outstanding customs bylaws in the coming months. During the ASYCUDA rollout, certain controls (e.g. mandatory document uploads) were temporarily relaxed; the Customs Service should restore full controls as the system stabilizes. Procurement under this component is nearly complete: a new Data Center is operational and most ICT equipment delivered, and a contract for non-intrusive inspection scanners was signed in Oct 2025. Given the technical nature, rigorous oversight of the scanner installation is needed (the project may engage an independent expert to verify the factory tests, on-site installation, training, etc.) so that border scanning capability is delivered as planned by mid-2026. Jan 02, 2026 Page 2 of 17 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Fourth Phase of the Central Asia Regional Links Program (P166820) Gender outcomes in customs require renewed emphasis – despite training and HR initiatives, women still hold only ~5% of customs management positions (down from a 5% baseline, target 10%) and about 14% of all customs officer posts (up from 10.5% baseline, target 20%). This falls short of the project’s gender targets. A Gender Specialist has now been hired to work with the Customs Service on improving female recruitment, retention and promotion. Senior management support will be crucial to implement recommended policy changes and outreach to achieve a more inclusive workforce. Environmental and Social Safeguards: Both implementing agencies have dedicated E&S staff, and compliance frameworks are in place, but field supervision must be strengthened. As noted, construction zone safety shortfalls (traffic management, fencing, OHS oversight) need immediate corrective action by the MoT’s project team and contractors. All ESMP measures (e.g. dust control, waste management, land rehabilitation) should be rigorously implemented before project completion. The project’s Grievance Redress Mechanism is functioning (124 grievances resolved to date), and it is important to swiftly resolve the few remaining cases – including a community complaint in GBAO regarding Khorog bridge access for disabled persons and other local impacts by the agreed December 2025 deadlines. Procurement of all major contracts have been completed. However, several key contracts have end-dates very close to the project closing (Oct 31, 2026) – for example, the Road Safety Observatory consultancy runs until October 2, 2026, and the RAMS and some road safety works also finish in mid-to-late 2026. This tight timeline poses a risk of fully completing deliverables. The project teams are advised to closely monitor these activities with monthly progress updates, fast-track decisions (e.g. any contract amendments or extensions needed), and ensure no slippage beyond the closing date. Moreover, the Ministry of Transport and Customs PIGs should plan to reallocate any savings or uncommitted funds promptly to priority tasks, to utilize the remaining ~30% undisbursed funds and achieve the project objectives within the next year. 5.
