Underpinning this improved performance was a level two restructuring, which resulted in an amended and restated financing agreement that was signed on July 4, 2023. The restructuring included: (i) an early closure of the project, with the closing date advanced from June 30, 2025 to June 30, 2024 and a partial financing cancellation of US$14,276,279; (ii) a revision of the project development objective and 4/18/2024 Page 1 of 9 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report GAMBIA FISCAL MANAGEMENT DEVELOPMENT PROJECT (P166695) results framework to ensure realism and strengthen the link between the revised project activities and project indicators; and (iii) a revision of the project institutional arrangements to include missing implementing entities and further clarify their roles and responsibilities. Based on these changes, the disbursement rate rose to 74 percent as of April 2024, and is likely to increase further with the completion of several large procurements before project closure. The overall project Implementation Progress (IP) remains Moderately Satisfactory given recent improvements in the progress of project activities. Critical progress has continued to be recorded over the past year across all three components, including, among others: (i) the completion of the Gambia Revenue Authority (GRA) business re-engineering and ITAS contract negotiations with the selected vendor, as well as the completion of the Tax Compliance and Risk Assessment Policy; (ii) the passage of the 2022 Gambia Public Procurement Authority (GPPA) Bill by the National Assembly and the training and certification of 80 procurement officials on core principles of procurement; (iii) the passage of the 2023 SOE Bill by the National Assembly as well as the appointment of the SOE Oversight Authority Board of Directors; (iv) the acquisition of the Gambia Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA) quality of service monitoring tool for 4G, billing verification system, electromagnetic radiation measurement tool, as well as the completion of a Spectrum Assessment Report; (v) the development of an Electronic Government Procurement (e-GP) readiness report, a validated strategy and roadmap for implementing an e-GP system, procurement manual, and standard bidding documents in line with the 2022 The Gambia Public Procurement Authority (GPPA) Act; (vi) the organization of a market test for The Gambia Telecommunications Company (GAMTEL)/The Gambia Telecommunications Cellular Company (GAMCEL); (vii) the public disclosure of three SOE performance audit reports (e.g. The Gambia Public Authority [GPA], the National Water and Electricity Company [NAWEC], and the Social Security & Housing Finance Corporation[SSHFC]) by NAO with an additional two forthcoming (e.g. GAMTEL and GCAA); as well as (vii) the cabinet approval of the national cloud strategy and migration policy. The PDO-1 sub-indicator measuring the “percentage of on-time tax filing rates (Corporate Income Tax [CIT] and Private Income Tax [PIT]) by large taxpayers” achieved a performance of 91 percent against an end-target of 95 percent, while the PDO-1 sub-indicator measuring the “percentage of on-time tax filing rate by all Value Added Tax (VAT) registrants” achieved a performance of 48.8 percent, thus exceeding the end target of 30 percent. The performance of both sub-indicators related to PDO-1 is likely to improve further given the additional time available to CIT, PIT, and VAT collection rates. The PDO-2 indicator measuring the number of “performance audit reports of State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs) prepared by the National Audit Office (NAO), discussed with the Public Enterprises Committee (PEC) of the National Assembly, and published on the website of the Ministry of Finance and Economic Affairs (MoFEA)” achieved a performance of three against an end target of five. Two additional performance audits of the Gambia Telecommunication Company (GAMTEL) as well as the Gambia Civil Aviation Authority (GCAA), are in final review stages and the final target is expected to be achieved before project closure. Finally, the PDO-3 indicator measuring the “percentage of government procurement tenders by value that are single sourced” achieved an end-December 2023 rate of 35.84 percent against a project end target of 22 percent, thus not achieving the 2023 end of year target. While progress towards the achievement of PDO-3 did not meet its target in 2023 given a number of recent emergency procurements in the electricity and health sectors, no additional emergency single source procurements are anticipated given the current drive to curb single source procurements in 2024. As of the end of March 2024, single source procurements stood at 6.98 percent against an end of project target of 22 percent, and further improvements to the performance of this indicator are expected before project closure, with an overall objective of eliminating single source procurement in the future. Supporting these efforts is the forthcoming of The Gambia Second Boosting Resilience and Unlocking Productive Potential DPF (BRUPP-P502012) operation, which includes as a prior action the adoption of a legal framework and implementation regulations for e-procurement in line with the 2022 Procurement Act, thus encouraging competitive procurement processes by enhancing transparency and oversight as well as establishing and reinforcing emergency procurement rules.
