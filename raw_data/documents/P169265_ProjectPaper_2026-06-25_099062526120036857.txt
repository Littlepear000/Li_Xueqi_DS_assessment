PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING Introduction 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the Regional Programs Director’s approval for a Level 2 restructuring of the Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa Project (HISWA) (P169265) for Burkina Faso as outlined in Section II below. A. Project Description and Status Project Description 2. The Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa project was approved by the World Bank Executive Directors on March 26, 2020, with a total financing amount of US$379.0 million equivalent. The project includes seven participating countries, namely, Burkina Faso (IDA Credit 65730 - US$30.0 million and IDA Grant D5810 - US$30.0 million), Cabo Verde (IDA Credit 65740 - US$15.0 million), Cote d’Ivoire (IDA Credit 65750 - US$54.0 million), Ghana (IDA Credit 65760 - US$150.0 million), Liberia (IDA Credit 65770 - US$15.0 million and IDA Grant D5830 - US$15.0 million), Sierra Leone (IDA Grant D5840 - US$30.0 million) and Togo (IDA Credit 65780 - US$15.0 million and IDA Grant D5850 - US$15.0 million); as well as two regional organizations, namely, the African Union (AU) (IDA Grant D5880 - US$2.50 million) and the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) (IDA Grant D5820 - US$7.50 million). Following the first, second, and third Additional Financings (AFs), the total project financing amount has increased to US$413.80 million equivalent. 3. The first Level 2 project restructuring of the project was approved on December 20, 2024. The restructuring focused on revising the results framework by: (i) removing Project Development Objective (PDO)-level Indicator 2—the share of beneficiary countries National Statistical Offices (NSOs) scoring at least 30 out of 50 on the Institutional Capacity Index (ICI)—as it was not implemented across all participating countries and proved difficult to measure at an aggregate level; and (ii) correcting data entry errors in the aggregate baseline and end-target values for three other PDO-level indicators. 4. The second Level 2 project restructuring of the project was approved on November 25, 2025. The restructuring focused on : (i) extending the closing date for Burkina Faso and African Union from December 31, 2025 to June 30, 2026 and for Togo, Cabo Verde, and ECOWAS from December 31, 2025 to December 31, 2026, (ii) reallocating funds between expenditure categories for Côte d’Ivoire; and (iii) adjusting the Results Framework end target date to align with the new closing dates for Burkina Faso, Togo, Cabo Verde, African Union, and ECOWAS. 5. First, second, and third Additional Financings (AFs): To cover cost overruns affecting key statistical activities. The first AF was approved on June 16, 2025, the second AF was approved on June 30, 2025, and the third AF was approved on May 11, 2026. These AFs also included a restructuring of the parent project, extending the closing date from December 31, 2025, to December 31, 2026, for Côte d’Ivoire, Sierra Leone, Liberia, and Ghana. • The first AF, approved on June 16, 2025, totaled US$9.60 million (US$6.0 million for Côte d’Ivoire and US$3.60 million for Sierra Leone). The Sierra Leone AF prioritized completing the Sierra Leone Living Household Survey (SLIHS), modernizing the Consumer Price Index (CPI), updating national accounts, and constructing the Statistics Sierra Leone headquarters. Meanwhile, the Côte d’Ivoire AF focused on sustaining and finalizing critical Official Use Only Page 1 The World Bank Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa (P169265) activities, including: (i) the Harmonized Household Survey (EHCVM); (ii) CPI modernization; and (iii) improvements to economic statistics, particularly national accounts. • The second AF, approved on June 30, 2025, totaled US$6.20 million (US$3.0 million for Ghana and US$3.20 million for Liberia). The Ghana AF addresses cost overruns and supports completion of key statistical activities under the HISWA Project, including: (i) the Ghana Living Standards Survey Round 8; (ii) National Accounts rebasing; (iii) CPI rebasing; and (iv) the quarterly Labor Force Survey—critical for achieving the Project Development Objective (PDO) and closing data gaps in poverty, labor, and economic statistics. The Liberia AF similarly covers cost overruns to advance the Household Income and Expenditure Survey (HIES), Labor Force Survey (LFS), CPI expansion, improvements in economic statistics, and institutional capacity building. • The third AF, approved on May 11, 2026, totaled US$19.0 million for Ghana. This AF addresses cost overruns and supports completion of key statistical activities under the HISWA Project, including: (i) the Ghana Living Standards Survey Round 8; (ii) National Accounts rebasing; (iii) CPI rebasing; and (iv) the quarterly Labor Force Survey—critical for achieving the PDO and closing data gaps in poverty, labor, and economic statistics. 6. The Project Development Objective is to strengthen the statistical systems of participating countries and regional bodies in Africa to harmonize, produce, disseminate, and enhance the use of core economic and social statistics. 7. The project is designed to address critical data gaps by harmonizing, producing, disseminating, and promoting the use of essential economic and social statistics. Its objective is to strengthen the statistical capacity of participating countries, enabling their National Statistical Systems (NSS) to effectively meet user needs and generate more comparable data to support informed decision-making. In addition, the project advances the modernization of statistical methodologies, organizational structures, physical infrastructure, and Information and Communication Technology (ICT) within NSOs and relevant Ministries, Departments, and Agencies (MDAs), thereby enhancing their efficiency and effectiveness in fulfilling their mandates. 8. Structure of the Parent project and the AFs: Component 1: Regional Component: Harmonization, Data Collection, Quality Improvement, Dissemination and Use of Core Social and Economic Statistics (US$364.03 million IDA equivalent of which Burkina Faso US$51.5 million equivalent). This component focuses on promoting harmonized methodologies, producing core economic and social statistics, strengthening administrative data systems, delivering training on methodological innovations, building human resource capacity, and enhancing the quality and timeliness of statistical outputs. Component 2: Country Specific Components: Improving and modernizing physical and statistical infrastructure (US$30.29 million equivalent of which Burkina Faso US$6.5 million equivalent). This component targets the specific constraints and priorities of each participating NSO through tailored interventions in physical, technological, and statistical infrastructure. It also includes initiatives to strengthen human capacity and improve legal and regulatory frameworks, ensuring that each NSO has the foundational capabilities required to effectively implement activities under Component 1. Component 3: Project Management and Monitoring and Evaluation (US$19.48 million IDA equivalent of which Burkina Faso US$2.00 million equivalent). This component ensures effective project management and outcome monitoring across participating countries and regional organizations. It supports the efficient execution of activities, with NSOs serving as implementing agencies responsible for technical oversight and coordination. Each Official Use Only Page 2 The World Bank Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa (P169265) country’s project management team handles implementation, reporting, auditing, environmental and social safeguards, and compliance with fiduciary policies. Specifically, this component covers costs for project staff, coordination (including steering committee operations), general operating expenses, and other essential expenditures. B. Project Status 9. The project is currently under implementation in all seven participating countries and two regional organizations. Official implementation of the parent project commenced on July 13, 2020, following the effectiveness of the Ghana Financing Agreement, while the Burkina Faso Financing Agreement became effective on October 9, 2020. 10. As of June 16, 2026, the overall project disbursement rate stands at 90 percent. In Burkina Faso, the disbursement rate stands at 100 percent. The project has made significant strides in strengthening the National Statistical Systems (NSS) by addressing critical data gaps and supporting the production of key statistical outputs—such as population, business, and agricultural censuses, as well as a variety of core household and socio-economic surveys. Additionally, it has improved the harmonization, dissemination, and use of high-quality data to inform, monitor, and evaluate public policies. 11. The Results Framework shows strong overall performance, with most PDO and intermediate indicators achieved or exceeding end-of-project targets. Overall progress toward achieving both the PDO and Implementation Progress are rated Satisfactory. At the PDO level, statistical capacity, data availability, and compliance with international standards have all surpassed targets, including the Modified Statistical Capacity Index (73.91% vs. 71.43% target), open access to core indicators (81.73% vs. 50% target), and adherence to standards and release calendars (80.95% vs. 62.28% target). The share of countries producing analytical reports has already reached 100 percent. While progress on user satisfaction remains below target at the time of reporting, it is improving and expected to be met by closing. 12. At the intermediate level, all indicators are largely achieved, reflecting significant progress in strengthening statistical systems. Countries have adopted harmonized methodologies, implemented data platforms, expanded gender and analytical outputs, and significantly increased capacity (over 1,300 staff trained). The availability of datasets and statistical products has improved substantially, and most countries now produce and disseminate statistics in line with international standards. Remaining gaps are limited and primarily relate to uneven performance across countries (e.g., Cabo Verde and Liberia) and delays in user satisfaction surveys and data dissemination timelines. Overall, the results framework confirms that the project is on track to achieve its development objectives and deliver sustained improvements in statistical capacity and data use. 13. For Burkina Faso, the Results Framework indicates strong performance, with all indicators achieved or exceeding targets. The Modified Statistical Capacity Index reached 79 percent, surpassing the end target of 75 percent, and 6 out of 7 core statistical indicators and datasets are produced and made accessible, reflecting significant progress in data availability and harmonization. The country is also performing well in statistical production aligned with international standards, with 5 out of 6 core statistical products regularly produced and disseminated. The user satisfaction index has already exceeded its target (12.6 percent vs. 10 percent), indicating improved relevance and accessibility of data. 14. At the intermediate level, for Burkina Faso, all key indicators have been achieved, including adoption of harmonized methodologies, use of international statistical standards, establishment of data quality frameworks, and regular updates of data release calendars. Capacity building has been substantial, with 487 staff trained, and Burkina Official Use Only Page 3 The World Bank Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa (P169265) Faso has produced a significant number of analytical outputs (41 reports). Data dissemination timelines are relatively efficient, with reports released within about six months after data collection. C. Rationale for Restructuring 15. During the Mid-Term Review in May 2024 and subsequent implementation support missions, the World Bank project team and the Borrower jointly assessed progress under the HISWA Project. Based on this assessment and a shared understanding of implementation challenges, both parties agreed that an extension of the closing date was necessary to address delays caused by a combination of internal and external factors. Following this agreement, the Borrower submitted a formal request to extend the closing date for Burkina Faso by 12 months, along with a request for additional resources. However, due to limited funding availability at the time, the World Bank team reviewed the request, the proposed action plan, implementation progress, and available financing, and agreed instead on a six-month extension—from December 31, 2025, to June 30, 2026—which was approved in November 2025. 16. An Additional Financing is under discussion to support critical statistical activities initiated under the HISWA project but not completed due to cost overruns and the approaching project closing date (June 30, 2026). In parallel, extending the HISWA project (P169265) closing date from June 30, 2026, to December 31, 2026 (bringing the cumulative extension to 12 months) is necessary to finalize ongoing activities, including the second wave data collection for the EHCVM and MICS surveys, as well as regional workshops on data processing and analysis. Delays in implementation have resulted from both internal and external factors, as outlined below: • Internal factors: During the initial phase, both the implementing entity and fiduciary agencies needed time to build operational capacity, which slowed the execution of planned activities. These constraints were further compounded by weaknesses in contract management and oversight, creating procurement bottlenecks. Delays in procurement planning and lengthy approval processes also hindered timely implementation. The restructuring seeks to address these challenges by adjusting project timelines to enable more effective execution and ensure achievement of the overall PDO. • External factors contributing to delays: Project implementation was significantly hindered by external shocks, most notably the COVID-19 pandemic. The pandemic disrupted global supply chains, causing delays in procurement and bidding processes for essential goods and services. Delivery timelines for vehicles, computers, tablets, interviewer kits, and other data collection equipment were extended well beyond initial estimates. Additionally, lockdowns and movement restrictions created logistical challenges for survey implementation, requiring major adjustments to field operations and resulting in higher costs and prolonged timelines for enumerator training and data collection. Collectively, these disruptions led to substantial delays in project execution. D. Fiduciary, Environmental, and Social Management 17. The procurement performance rating is Moderately Satisfactory, and the risk level is Moderate. Procurement will be carried out in accordance with the World Bank Procurement Regulations (7th edition - September 2025). Systematic Tracking of Exchanges in Procurement (STEP) will serve as the platform for procurement planning. The existing procurement implementation arrangements will be maintained, with procurement responsibilities retained by the INSD procurement unit. The key procurement activities identified will support project coordination and data collection and analysis as well as consultancy services to improve administrative statistics. The PPSD will be updated to include new items Official Use Only Page 4 The World Bank Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa (P169265) if needed. Procurement risk rating is moderate. Primary risks identified include (i) lengthy government procurement processing and approval timelines and (ii) delays in obtaining commencement authorization and disbursement caps imposed by the Ministry of Finance. To mitigate these risks, the Borrower will begin preparing Terms of Reference and technical specifications early. 18. Financial Management (FM) arrangements remain Satisfactory. Financial management arrangements for the HISWA project in Burkina Faso have been assessed as adequate and continue to meet the World Bank’s requirements for IPF operations. FM performance under the project has been satisfactory overall, supported by an experienced Project Implementation Unit (PIU) within INSD and strong disbursement progress. The proposed extension is not expected to introduce any changes to the existing financial management arrangements, and disbursement arrangements will remain unchanged from the parent project. The residual FM risk is assessed as Moderate and is considered manageable through continued implementation support. 19. Environment and Social: The Social and Environmental assessment category for the project was classified as Category B – Partial Assessment, due to the anticipated low to moderate environmental and social risks and impacts. This classification remains relevant and appropriate for the extension. The potential environmental and social impacts are site-specific, reversible, and can be readily mitigated through the application of good environmental and social practices and appropriate mitigation measures. 20. The implementation of the HISWA project has made steady progress, including the integration of environmental and social safeguards into project activities. The INSD, as the implementing agency, includes an environmental and social (E&S) specialist and has demonstrated commitment to environmental and social risk management. The INSD has mainstreamed safeguards into the planning and execution of project activities, ensuring compliance with the applicable World Bank safeguards policies and applicable national regulatory requirements. As of the latest reporting period, the overall environmental and social performance rating for INSD is Satisfactory. While E&S implementation is on track, some areas, particularly those related to documentation, monitoring, and reporting, require continued attention to fully align with the requirements. In addition, given the frequent nature of field activities, there is a need to further strengthen road safety and occupational health and safety (OHS) risk management, including through enhanced training, supervision, and institutional capacity enhancement. Capacity building and closer coordination with relevant national agencies are expected to strengthen implementation under the extension. Furthermore, continue the implementation of the provision of the existing Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment Management Plan. 21. The Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) established under the parent project was updated to reflect SEASH anticipation mitigation measures and will be implemented throughout this extension as well as the Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Sexual Harassment (SEASH) action plan. The GRM is operational and will continue to offer project beneficiaries and stakeholders as a platform to address their grievances under the extension. The parent project has demonstrated a strong track record incorporating gender and social inclusion considerations. It is anticipated that the implementation of the project will further enhance the outcomes already achieved. The security risk of the extended activities shall be assessed, and appropriate mitigation measures will be outlined in a security management plan to be implemented throughout the project's life cycle. 22. Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Performance: Overall, M&E performance under all project participating countries and regional organizations is satisfactory. 23. General Risks and Mitigation: The overall risk rating for the project remains Moderate with respect to achieving the Project Development Objective. The proposed restructuring does not change this assessment. However, residual risks Official Use Only Page 5 The World Bank Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa (P169265) persist in two key areas: Institutional Capacity for Implementation and Sustainability and Environmental and Social (E&S), as both are currently rated Substantial. To address these risks, the following measures have been agreed upon and implemented across participating countries: • Capacity Building on World Bank Operational Policies: Targeted training sessions have strengthened understanding and application of World Bank procedures for projects under the HISWA initiative. • Technical Assistance and Implementation Support: Ongoing support has improved procurement processes, particularly contract management, ensuring timely and high-quality delivery of project outputs. • Monitoring and Planning Enhancements: Implementation plans now include quarterly disbursement projections to facilitate better monitoring and follow-up. • Environmental and Social Risk Management: Environmental and Social Framework (ESF) related risks are being managed through structured frameworks and stakeholder engagement, with a strong focus on timely reporting and compliance.
