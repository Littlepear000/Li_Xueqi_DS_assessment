BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING A. Introduction 1. This project paper seeks the approval of the Director of Regional Integration (RI) to provide additional financing (AF) in the amount of US$3.8 million from the Food Systems 2030 (FS2030) Umbrella Program to the Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD) under Phase 1 of the Food Systems Resilience Program for Eastern and Southern Africa (FSRP, P178566). IGAD has submitted an official request for the AF to scale-up its engagement in early warning efforts, aiming to further strengthen the ongoing resilience initiatives under the FSRP. 2. The FSRP MPA was approved by the World Bank (WB) Board of Directors on June 21, 2022, with an overall envelope of US$2.3 billion equivalent. This includes US$788.1 million allocated for Phase 1 which provides financing to Ethiopia (US$600 million), Madagascar (US$158.1 million), the Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD) (US$25 million), and the Centre for Coordination of Agricultural Research and Development for Southern Africa (CCARDESA) (US$5 million). 3. On May 31, 2023, the Board approved the second and third phases of the MPA together with an increase in the available International Development Association (IDA) financing, bringing the total IDA) envelope to US$2.75 billion. Phase 2 (P179818) provides US$300 million in national and regional IDA financing to Tanzania (Program-for- Results – PforR). Phase 3 (P177816) provides US$603 million in national and regional IDA financing to Kenya (US$150 million), the Union of Comoros (US$40 million), Somalia (US$150 million), Malawi (US$250 million), and the African Union (US$13 million). Additionally, Phase 3 included a Global Agriculture and Food Security Program (GAFSP) grant of US$15 million for the Malawi FSRP and a Global Program for the Blue Economy (GPBE) grant of US$3 million for the Comoros FSRP. 4. Several projects under the MPA have received AF. In June 2024, the Madagascar FSRP received US$70 million in AF to support its Contingency Emergency Response Component (CERC) operation, comprising US$50 million from the Crisis Response Window Early Response Financing (CRW ERF) and US$20 million from the GAFSP. In September 2024, the Tanzania FSRP received an AF of US$8.3 million. On February 20, 2025, the Board approved US$61 million in grants for Malawi for the project comprising US$45 million from the Malawi Second Agricultural Commercialization and Resilience Enhancement Project Multi-Donor Trust Fund (AGCOM 2.0 MDTF 1 ), and US$16 million from the Food Systems 2030 Umbrella Program (FS2030). On June 23, 2025, an additional finance was approved in the amount of US$50 million from the Crisis Response Window Plus and US$14.6 million in national IDA resources to the Malawi FSRP; and US$30 million in national IDA resources to the Somalia FSRP. The same report also underscored that one of the key drivers of the current situ ation is the region’s continued vulnerability to frequent and severe climate extremes. These include a prolonged drought from 2020-2023 in the Eastern Horn of Africa, widespread flooding associated with El Niño, and recurring transboundary pest outbreaks such as desert locusts. Persistent dry conditions throughout 2023 and the first half of 2024 have further strained agricultural production. Meanwhile, heavy rainfall in late 2023 led to widespread flooding, a crisis that continued into 2024, resulting in loss of life, displacement, and damage to infrastructure and agricultural assets, all of which have deepened food insecurity across the region. 7. The desert locust (DL) outbreak from 2019 to 2021 had a particularly devastating impact on countries like Djibouti, Kenya, Ethiopia, Sudan, South Sudan, Uganda, and Somalia. Beyond desert locusts, other pests have also hampered agricultural productivity, raising serious concerns about the continued emergence of transboundary pest threat to the region’s already fragile food security. Moreover, widespread conflicts across the region have led to population displacement and further disruption of food systems. 8. Recognizing the continued importance and urgency of investing in robust early warning mechanisms, IGAD has launched several interventions under the FSRP: (i) establishing a gender-responsive regional framework for digital agro-climate advisory services (DACAS) for farmers and pastoralists, with linkages to national DACAS; (ii) producing bi- weekly food security reports to provide timely updates on the region’s situation; and (iii) developing a regional online platform, including e-learning components, to serve as a repository for food systems information and best practices. 9. To safeguard and further enhance long-term resilience investments of the FSRP and other resilience-building interventions, it is essential to scale-up early warning investments by building upon existing and/or establish effective disaster early warning systems (EWS) to enhance FSRP’s development impact. Investing in early warning is not just about responding to crises—it is about preventing them. In a region increasingly affected by climate shocks, conflict, and food insecurity, EWS are a vital strategic asset. They save lives, lower humanitarian costs, and help build resilience across borders. Every dollar invested in early warning saves up to seven dollars in disaster response.4The return is high, and the cost of inaction is far greater. As a regional leader in coordination and data sharing, IGAD is uniquely positioned to strengthen integrated EWS that enable timely, evidence-based action. By investing in early warning, IGAD empowers member states to act before disasters escalate, improves food security planning, and enhances regional stability. 10. While long-term investments by IGAD are prioritized, no dedicated resources exist for integrating advanced monitoring, surveillance, and forecasting tools to strengthen climate risk management. Until recently, these systems have relied heavily on international development financing. While these funding streams are now in question, critical gaps are emerging in the existing system. 11. IGAD is well positioned and has the mandate to play a major role in strengthening food systems resilience through early warning. Firstly, IGAD is mandated to coordinate early warning information. Secondly, it is the only regional organization with connections to navigate the political economy ecosystem that can impact the flow of information. Relevance to Higher Level Objectives 12. The AF, like its parent project, fully aligns with WB global, regional, and country priorities. The AF is highly relevant and directly linked to the WB’s mission to end extreme poverty and boost shared prosperity on a livable planet; and supports the WB’s priorities for Eastern and Southern Africa, which include strengthening climate resilience and food security. The AF is also aligned with the Food Systems 2030 (FS2030) Umbrella Program by supporting its overarching goals of resilience and sustainability within food systems. In addition, this AF activities are aligned with several regional initiatives such as the African Union (AU) Agenda 2063, the Malabo Declaration on African Agriculture, the the Comprehensive Africa Agriculture Development Program (CAADP), and the Forum for Agricultural Research in Africa (FARA) strategic plans. This AF is also aligned with strong analytical foundations, including CAADP National Agriculture Investment Plans (NAIPs), which set out a development strategy for countries’ agriculture sectors (all countries), Climate Smart Agriculture (CSA) profiles, and investment plans that identify priority technologies and delivery channels to increase climate smartness in the sector. DESCRIPTION OF ADDITIONAL FINANCING A. Rationale for Additional Financing 1. IGAD is currently implementing activities under the original FSRP financing, focusing on interventions that support the Program’s resilience objectives. These include supporting harmonized policies in the IGAD region, strengthening a regional Center of Excellence on CSA, and developing Knowledge Platforms on selected thematic areas of agri-food systems resilience. IGAD recently launched the establishment of the DACAS, an online platform aiming to improve digital climate advisory services in the region, thereby strengthening EWS and disaster risk management. The DACAS will help farmers adapt and build resilience to weather variability and climate change as well as provide information, agronomic practices, market availability, input supply and general information on the entire agricultural value chain. 2. The primary objective of this AF is to scale-up DACAS investments under FSRP. The AF is not intended to address cost-overruns or financing gaps. The AF will expand the geographic coverage of the DACAS network to include Arid and Semi-Arid Areas of the IGAD region as its current geographic focus is limited to the region’s surplus producing areas. In addition, the AF will also directly address gaps recently identified through baseline assessments, which highlights the need for digital product development, which has not yet been implemented under DACAS. The AF will bridge this critical gap by leveraging advanced technologies to improve data acquisition and integration; building capacity for system management, analytical modelling and forecasting; and strengthening the governance of EWS to enable data-driven decision making for proactive response. Additionally, the AF components will also build on the achievements of the Emergency Locust Response Program (ELRP, P173702) MPA by strengthening East Africa Pest Watch, an established EWS for pests. 3. The remaining funds (US$16.4 million) available to IGAD under the FSRP for the 2025 –2029 period have already been fully committed to planned, longer-term resilience-building activities across the Program Pillars. For example, funds will be used to (a) establish regional Centers of Leadership focused on promoting policy reforms in Climate Smart Agriculture and Soil Health and Fertiliser; (b) create knowledge networks for One Health approach, improved groundwater management, and trade; and (c) IGAD will lead on processes seeking to improve regional policy harmonization on key issues such as input supply, post harvest management, and digitalization. While the funds are already committed, the activities under this AF will focus on slightly different yet complementary intervenions to those currently implemented by IGAD to further advance the resilience objective under the FSRP. The additional activities to be financed exclusively by the AF under sub-component 1.1 include: a. Deploying sustainable, cost-effective technologies to enhance the region’s ability to collect, harmonize, and analyze key datasets for early warning. Through the AF support, the project will procure and deploy advanced Information and Communication Technology (ICT) and data systems to strengthen DACAS by improving regional data collection, integration, and analysis. This will include high-performance servers, analyst workstations, network-attached storage systems, display monitors, and backup power units (UPS) to enhance data processing, visualization, and reliability. Data acquired from open-access and commercial satellite imagery (≈0.3–0.5 m resolution) will be validated using automated weather stations, soil moisture sensors, and GPS-enabled tablets to ensure precision and consistency. Specialized software such as ENVI and ArcGIS will support remote sensing and geospatial analysis, while advanced modeling tools such as SimClim will facilitate climate impact and scenario assessments. A data integration platform (ETL) and database management systems such as Amazon Web Services (AWS) will ensure secure, interoperable, and near-real- time data sharing across member states. Complementing these investments are consultancies focused on harmonizing Earth observation data, monitoring crop and rangeland conditions, developing climate impact scenarios, and conducting socio-economic assessments. b. Building institutional and human capacity to manage systems and use data effectively following the deployment of these technologies. The AF will support hands-on training to strengthen stakeholder (technical staff and decision-makers) capacity in accessing, processing, and interpreting satellite and field data related to crop conditions, rangeland health, and pest dynamics for early warning action. Capacity-building will be tailored to the mandates of implementing institutions – whether coordinated regionally or implemented through national governments – and aligned with efforts to institutionalize early warning operations. To ensure sustainability and relevance, IGAD region-specific training materials and manuals will be developed, reflecting local agro-ecological, climatic, and institutional contexts while promoting the consistent use of standardized tools and methodologies across member states. c. Strengthening coordination, interoperability, and governance of EWS in recognition of the cross-border nature of climate and food security shocks. The project will promote collaborative frameworks for data sharing, joint planning, and decision-making among IGAD member states and relevant regional bodies. IGAD includes governments from the Horn of Africa, the Nile Valley, and the African Great Lakes. The IGAD region spans an area of 5.2 million km², encompassing Djibouti, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Kenya, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, and Uganda. The organization is headquartered in Djibouti. The IGAD region is characterized by complex geological and topographical formations, exhibiting a wide diversity of terrains and landscapes. This diversity has resulted in regional variations, manifested in different ecological zones such as the East African Rift Valley, deserts, arid and semi-arid lands, dry and dry sub-humid areas, humid regions, mountainous terrains, and alpine lands. Historically, the IGAD region boasted a pristine environment and intact natural resources. However, over time, human activities coupled with climate change have dramatically altered the extent, type, composition, and quality of ecosystems and natural resources in the region. Recent studies indicate that currently, only 5 percent of the original ecosystems and habitats remain intact.
