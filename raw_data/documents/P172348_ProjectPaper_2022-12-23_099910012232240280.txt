No The World Bank Eastern Ukraine: Reconnect, Recover, Revitalize (3R) Project (P172348) I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. Up until the Russian Federation invaded Ukraine on February 24, 2022, the Eastern Ukraine: Reconnect, Recover, Revitalize (3R) project was being implemented as planned. The original project design was aimed at contributing to the social and economic recovery of the Luhansk oblast following the onset of armed conflict in 2014 and eventual partial occupation of the oblast by separatists. Partial occupation of the Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts cut the rest of Ukraine off from areas that were important to the national economy, while also preventing citizens of these oblasts from accessing public services, markets for the sale of goods, and other resources that impacted their overall welfare. The Project was focused on rehabilitating important transport routes and developing the agriculture sector as a means to stimulating economic growth while promoting constructive opportunities for citizen and government engagement in addressing local challenges. 2. There are four project components, as follows: a. Component 1: RECONNECT - Road Investments to Link Rural Communities to Processing, Markets, and Services (US$65 million) b. Component 2: RECOVER - Agriculture Investments to Support Sector Recovery and Modernization (US$32 million) c. Component 3: REVITALIZE - Implementation Support Platform, including communications, community, youth and private sector engagement (US$3 million) d. Component 4: Contingent Emergency Response Component to enable the Government of Ukraine (GoU) to reallocate project funds to response efforts in the event of an urgent need for assistance in response to an eligible crisis or emergency (~US$0) 3. At the onset of the current war, the bid submission closing dates had passed and contracts were about to be signed for several key project activities. These included contracts for: (a) all planned road reconstruction and supervision activities under Component 1, (b) a design study for an agriculture testing and food safety laboratory under Component 2, and (c) a baseline survey of socioeconomic conditions in the oblast under Component 3. A fully operational Project Implementation Unit (PIU) with seven full-time consultants was also in place in the Ministry for Reintegration of Temporarily Occupied Territories (MRTOT). Specialized consultants were also in the process of designing a Terms of Reference for a feasibility study for an agriculture services and logistics hub and agriculture cluster development. With financing from the Multi-Partner Trust Fund for Peacebuilding and Recovery (MPTF), studies on Agriculture Value Chain and Cluster Development, and Climate Smart Agriculture had been completed and were being used to inform the design of Component 2 of the project. The subcomponents of Component 2 which were to be informed by these studies included the design of: an agriculture logistics and service hub; a regional agriculture testing and food safety laboratory; and agriculture cluster development. All of this activity was put on hold or cancelled once the war began with only US$0.48 million out of US$100 million disbursed. 4. The war has made the project area inaccessible and dramatically shifted government priorities in terms of World Bank-financed activities. The road rehabilitation, agriculture and services ‘triangle’ which is the main project target area, including Starobilsk, Svatove, Sievierodonetsk and Troitske rayons of Lugansk oblast, have sustained massive damage. Furthermore, Government of Ukraine (GoU) has lost control of this area due to ongoing street fighting, artillery bombardments, and long-range missile and air strikes. Beyond the inability to access Luhansk oblast, the recovery of this region in the aftermath of the 2014 conflict has been superseded as a government priority by the basic need to maintain a functional government and finance critical services, including pensions for the elderly, wages for hospital workers, and The World Bank Eastern Ukraine: Reconnect, Recover, Revitalize (3R) Project (P172348) social programs for the vulnerable. To help with these urgent needs, on March 7, 2022, the World Bank approved a budget support package totaling $723 million called Financing of Recovery from Economic Emergency in Ukraine. As of November 5, 2022, the World Bank had mobilized nearly $12.5 billion in support of Ukraine’s urgent wartime needs. 5. As part of the reprioritization of the use of World Bank financing, on May 24, 2022, the Ministry of Finance (MoF) requested that the Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC) of the 3R Project be triggered. The eligible emergency cited by the MoF was the State of Emergency in Separate Regions of Ukraine introduced by the Decree of the President of Ukraine No. 63/2022 and martial law imposed in Ukraine by the Decree of the President of Ukraine No. 64/2022 as of 24 February 2022. The Emergency Action Plan (EAP) submitted together with the request to trigger the CERC provided sufficient evidence satisfactory to the World Bank that GoU had adequate staff and resources for the purposes of EAP activities. 6. To provide basic assistance to the millions of people who had been internally displaced within Ukraine, GoU requested that $99 million in 3R project financing be reallocated from Components 1, 2 and 3 to Component 4.1 Specifically, it was proposed that the CERC component be used to reimburse the GoU for expenditures made through its program entitled "Social assistance to internally displaced persons to cover living expenses" specified in Cabinet of Ministers Resolution # 332, approved on March 20, 2022 (hereinafter referred to as “social assistance to IDPs”). Under this Resolution, cash transfers to help pay for basic needs including food, health care and energy are provided to registered IDPs who are defined as (a) persons who have moved from the temporarily occupied territory of the Autonomous Republic of Crimea and the city of Sevastopol; (b) persons, who after the imposition of martial law (February 24, 2022, at 5:30 am Kyiv time) were forced to leave their place of residence located in 14 regions with active conflict; and (c) persons who as of March 1, 2022, were registered as IDPs and were receiving benefits under the pre-existing (from the 2014 conflict) “monthly targeted social assistance to IDPs to cover living expenses, including for housing and utilities”. 7. The 3R Project Loan Agreement included three disbursement conditions which would need to be met in order for the funds to be reallocated to the CERC Component. These are: a. The Borrower has determined that an Eligible Emergency has occurred; has furnished to the Bank a request to include said activities in Part 4 of the Project (Contingent Emergency Response Component) in order to respond to said emergency; and the Bank has agreed with such determination, accepted said request and notified the Borrower; b. The Borrower, through Ministry for Reintegration of Temporarily Occupied Territories (MRTOT), has: ensured that all environmental and social instruments required for Part 4 of the Project have been prepared, disclosed and consulted; and ensured that any actions which are required to be taken under said environmental and social instruments have been implemented, all in accordance with the provisions of Section I.D of the Schedule 2 to the Agreement; and c. The Coordinating Authority has provided sufficient evidence satisfactory to the Bank that it has adequate staff and resources for the purposes of said activities. 8. At the time of the Ministry of Finance’s request for the World Bank to trigger the CERC component, it had already met all of the disbursement conditions. The state of emergency was duly documented and accepted by the World Bank as an eligible crisis. The PIU in the MRTOT retained its role implementing the CERC Component and had several consultants engaged that could implement all proposed activities. As per the World Bank’s policy on CERC processing, an ex post, level two, project restructuring is needed in order to revise elements of the project affected by the triggering of the CERC. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 11. As the relevance of the original project has changed with the dramatic change in project context, and the CERC component has meant that 99% of the project funds have been utilized for activities not envisioned at the time of project approval, there are several elements of the project design which require revision. These are: project development objective (PDO), results framework, closing date, and disbursement category allocations. 12. Change in the PDO. The PDO is proposed to change from “improve transport connectivity and promote agricultural sector recovery in project areas with the active engagement of conflict-affected communities” to “improve the living conditions of IDPs by providing them with short term financial assistance to respond to urgent, basic needs”. As there were no results achieved under Components 1, 2 and 3, the PDO should focus entirely on activities financed under Component 4. 13. Component 4. Funding under the CERC component has been used to reimburse GoU for payments up to a total of US$99 million made to eligible families under the "Social assistance to internally displaced persons to cover living expenses" specified in Cabinet of Ministers Resolution # 332, approved on March 20, 2022. As none of the main project activities had been implemented at the time of triggering the CERC component, all of the original results indicators have been dropped and replaced by indicators that are relevant to the use of CERC funds. A beneficiary survey is being implemented under Component 3 of the project to collect data from households that have been recipients of support from the Social Assistance to IDPs program. This data will be the primary source of information for the revised results framework which is included as an annex to this project paper. 15. Change in loan closing date. As the relevance of the project has changed and all project activities including those implemented as part of the CERC have been completed with the exception of the beneficiary survey, it is proposed that the closing loan date be changed from November 30, 2025, to December 31, 2022. This is also because neither MRTOT nor Ukravtodor have allocations for the project in the 2023 government budget, they would be unable to make project- related payments beyond December 31, 2022. As per World Bank policy, GoU would still be able to make payments on expenses incurred prior to the closing date within four months of the closing date, the end disbursement date. 16.
