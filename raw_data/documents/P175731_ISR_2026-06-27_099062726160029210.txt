KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions Overall, the Project has made solid progress across all participating countries, with significant advances in strengthening the availability, quality, and timeliness of economic and social statistics. Disbursement, however, remains uneven, with Comoros, Malawi and Sao Tome and Principe leading; Mozambique, Zambia, and SADC at intermediate levels; and Madagascar lagging behind. As the project reaches its midpoint, the most significant activities implemented to date include: In Mozambique, the Project has delivered notable improvements in core economic and social data, including enhanced coverage and dissemination. The recent completion of the Agricultural Census will substantially strengthen the national evidence base for agricultural policy and analysis, while ongoing improvements in national accounts have enhanced the timeliness and granularity of GDP statistics at both national and provincial levels. In Madagascar, the Project has supported the implementation of several key data collection activities, including the 2025 Household Budget Survey, agricultural surveys, and high-frequency household surveys (on-going), contributing to the production of critical socioeconomic data. In Malawi, the Project has successfully implemented major statistical operations, significantly strengthening the availability, quality, and timeliness of key economic and social statistics, including household, agricultural, labor force, and demographic and health surveys. In Zambia, several core statistical activities have been completed or are underway, including the Census of Economic Activities, agricultural surveys, labor market statistics, and cross-border trade data, contributing to improved coverage and availability of key economic and social statistics. The Project has also supported the establishment of foundational ICT systems, data architecture, and dissemination platforms, including the National Data Archive (NADA). In São Tomé and Príncipe, key deliverables include the successful implementation of the Housing and Population Census and the Household Budget Survey, alongside other activities such as the Labor Force Survey and business surveys. In Comoros, the Project financed the 2024 Harmonized Survey on Household Living Conditions, the updating of national accounts to SNA 2008, improvements to the consumer price index, and data dissemination platforms, including INSEED’s website and the NADA. At the regional level, the SADC Secretariat has delivered regional workshops and technical assistance missions that have strengthened data collection methodologies, statistical standards, and the use of advanced tools for censuses, surveys, and administrative data across Member States.
