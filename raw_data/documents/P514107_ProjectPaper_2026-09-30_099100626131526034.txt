BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING A. Summary 1. This Program Paper seeks the approval of the Division Director to provide an additional IDA credit in an amount of US$200 million and a grant in an amount of US$10 million from the Global Financing Facility for Women, Children and Adolescents (GFF) to the Côte d'Ivoire Productive Social Safety Nets System Strengthening (PSSN) Program (P175594) (IDA credit 7109-CI). The Original Program (OP) was approved in May 2022, with a US$200 million IDA credit (including US$7.5 million IPF component) and a US$5 million GFF grant (IPF), to support the newly adopted Social Safety Nets System Support Program, (Programme multisectoriel d’Appui au système national des Filets Sociaux, PAFS). The Program became effective in December 2022 and has a closing date of December 31, 2026. As of September 28, 2026, disbursement rates are on track, reaching 93.9 percent for the IDA credit and 78.2 percent for the GFF grant.1 The Program was restructured twice: in February 2026 to better align with evolving priorities and implementation realities (RES01184),2 and in August 2026 to reprogram resources from a partially achieved DLR to other key results (RES02024).3 2. This Additional Financing (AF) responds to the Government of Côte d’Ivoire’s request, dated March 13, 2026, to continue and expand the PSSN Program for Results (PforR). This request reflects the government’s recognition of the PAFS as a central pillar of its poverty reduction and economic resilience agenda. This growing national ownership of the Program, both institutionally and financially, is illustrated in particular by the increase in the Treasury's contribution, which rose from FCFA 8 billion (US$14 million) in 2024 to FCFA 20 billion (US$35 million) in 2025. Building on the achievements and lessons learned from the PAFS 2022-2025, the government seeks to consolidate and scale up the program in line with its broader development ambition, as articulated in the National Development Plan (Plan National de Développement, PND) 2026–2030 and reflected in the Note d’ancrage du PAFS (2026-2030) endorsed by the Minister of National Cohesion, Solidarity and Poverty Reduction (Ministre de la Cohesion Nationale, de la Solidarité et de la Lutte contre la Pauvreté, MCNSLP) on September 8, 2026. 3. The AF operationalizes a next-generation safety net engagement to accelerate poverty reduction and move people into better jobs, in line with the World Bank’s Social Protection 500 (SP500) approach. It accompanies the government's renewed commitment for 2026-2030 to: (i) consolidate its adaptive social protection system to enable delivery at scale, leveraging digital solutions; (ii) deepen economic inclusion and resilience, including through financial inclusion; and (iii) leverage established mechanisms to reach additional vulnerable groups – most notably adolescents and young women. Building on the solid foundations of the first phase, the Program scales up and continues to evolve and innovate based on evidence and operational lessons, adding sophistication and seizing new opportunities to reinforce the country’s human and physical infrastructure and open up pathways to more and better paid jobs among the poorest and most vulnerable. School enrollment has expanded significantly, with gender parity now achieved at primary and lower secondary levels,6 and women's ownership of financial accounts has risen from 37 to 54 percent between 2021 and 2024 – largely driven by mobile money adoption.7 Central to the government's vision is a commitment to continue the efforts to convert this growth into shared prosperity – through strengthened investments in human capital, social inclusion, and economic resilience, as articulated in the PND 2026–2030 and the National Social Protection Strategy (Stratégie Nationale de Protection Sociale, SNPS) 2024–2028. 5. Realizing this potential requires addressing structural challenges affecting the country's most vulnerable populations. The progress recorded over the last decade has gradually improved living conditions for the population. However, consolidating these gains requires addressing several obstacles, notably the need for a stronger transmission of growth gains into poverty reduction. The growth-poverty elasticity of 0.6 could be improved with a view to strengthening the links between economic growth and poverty reduction. Sustaining poverty reduction calls for particular attention to rural areas, where households remain more exposed to climate-related risks, where the greatest margins for productivity improvement persist, and where income diversification and the formalization of economic activities remain essential. Human capital constraints – reflected in a “Human Capital Index plus” of 114.6 out of 325 in 20258 – call for continued and deepened efforts to further increase the productive potential of a rapidly growing workforce and to support the entry of new cohorts into the labor market. Improving health outcomes is essential to strengthening households’ productivity and reducing their vulnerability. By relieving patients of potentially burdensome out-of-pocket expenditures, better health also helps break the vicious cycle in which poverty and poor health reinforce one another. Women face specific barriers: female labor force participation is 43.3 percent compared to 69.7 percent for men,9 they earn on average two-thirds of what men earn,10 and concentrate in lower-productivity and more vulnerable jobs, with 72.7 percent self-employed versus 58.4 percent of men.11 Although the share of women-owned businesses has grown by 25 percent over the past five years, 78 percent of women-led Micro-, small-, and medium- enterprises (MSMEs) have lower access formal credit.12 Less-educated, rural, and low-income women stand to benefit the most from expanded access to financial services,13 which can significantly strengthen their capacity to advance their productive activities, sustain their means of subsistence, and cope with climate shocks. 6. Building on the remarkable progress achieved in recent years in access to education and health for girls, new opportunities are emerging to consolidate these gains and build an even stronger human capital base. The government has responded to these challenges with sustained institutional commitment and growing domestic investment, and early results are already visible. Pro-poor social spending has increased by 25 percent between 2021 and 2024,22 and the PND 2026–2030 establishes social inclusion and human capital as a strategic priority, consistent with the objectives of the SNPS 2024–2028, grounded in a comprehensive and integrated approach to improving wellbeing, strengthening resilience, and expanding access to social services and economic opportunities for vulnerable populations. At the institutional level, several ministries are involved in the social protection sector. The Ministry of Solidarity and Fight against Poverty (Ministère de la Cohésion Nationale, de la Solidarité et de la Lutte contre la Pauvreté, MCNSLP) for non-contributory social protection, manages the country's largest social assistance program and humanitarian responses to shocks. The Ministry of Employment and Social Protection (Ministère de l'Emploi, de la Protection Sociale et de la Formation Professionnelle, MEPSFP) is in charge of contributory social protection programs, including the CMU, pensions programs, and the rapidly expanding Pension Scheme for Self-employed Workers (Régime Social des Travailleurs Indépendants, RSTI) for informal workers.23 The Ministry of Women, Family and Children (Ministère de la Femme, de la Famille et de l'Enfant, MFFE) is in charge of the protection and empowerment of women. Investments in delivery systems are beginning to improve coordination and mutualization of efforts across these actors: the recently operationalized Unique Social Registry (Registre Social Unique, RSU) is starting to act as a federating platform across programs, social centers and decentralized ministerial structures are offering integrated local-level support to vulnerable populations, and promising initiatives – such as the linkage between the Productive Cash Transfers Program (Programme des Transferts Monétaires Productifs, PTMP) and the non-contributory health coverage scheme targeting the poor (Régime d'Assurance Maladie, RAM) – are demonstrating what an integrated system can deliver. Digital opportunities are increasingly leveraged, including for cash transfer payments, interoperability between operators and the payment platform, while the RSU and Identification systems are nascent. Together, these dynamics point to a system that has made real strides, but where the integration and scale-up agendas remain central to unlocking its full potential. 8. In this context, the PAFS has emerged as a key anchor of Côte d'Ivoire's social protection system and its most powerful vehicle for scaling impact. The AF is now the opportunity to materialize this potential, expand the program's reach, deepen its impact, and establish the PAFS as a sustainable engine of poverty reduction, economic inclusion, and resilience for the decade ahead. C. Parent Program and Implementation Progress 9. The PSSN PforR was approved in May 2022, with a US$200 million IDA credit (including US$7.5 million IPF component) and a US$5 million GFF grant, to support the newly adopted PAFS. Its Program Development Objective (PDO) is to increase access of poor and vulnerable households to safety nets programs and improve the performance of delivery systems. The Program is anchored around three key Results Areas (RAs), aligned to the three pillars of the PAFS aforementioned: (1) Strengthened targeting mechanism and delivery systems for social programs – related to the expansion and operationalization of the RSU, social centers strengthening, and the development of tools such as the payment system and the grievance redress mechanism (GRM); (2) Expansion and sustainability of the PTMP – covering the extension of the PTMP (cash transfers and economic inclusion measures), with a focus on women and the poorest regions and strengthened institutional and monitoring frameworks; and (3) Developing a shock response mechanism – including a pilot and the development of a formal response framework. 10. The PSSN PforR has consistently displayed very strong performance, with a Satisfactory rating since its Mid-Term Review (MTR). As the second PforR in the country, the Program has successfully tackled the challenges associated with this new instrument. Government ownership of the Program has translated into a continuous strengthening of operational leadership and the national financial contribution. The AF will support the progressive consolidation of the shock response mechanism into a durable national mechanism, building on the foundations laid during the current PforR. In line with the OP and existing DLI formulation, the AF’s overarching ambition remains the operationalization of a national framework for response to covariate shocks through a safety nets – one that is institutionally anchored, evidence-based, and capable of reaching the most vulnerable households rapidly and systematically. This vision builds directly on the lessons of the Cavally pilot in response to floods in December 2024, which has: (i) demonstrated the feasibility of the approach and process; (ii) established a cross-ministerial TWG to operationalize the response; and (iii) generated the operational knowledge needed to adjust the protocols and inform a broader rollout. The AF takes stock of this progress, but also of the reality of Côte d’Ivoire’s shock-response ecosystem that is still being developed. This resulted in limited progress on developing a comprehensive risk financing framework and the decision to drop DLR 9.4 through the February 2026 restructuring. It thus adopts a more gradual but deliberate trajectory, prioritizing consolidation of the foundations recently laid. The effective implementation of the shock response according to the operational framework and protocols adopted in DLI 9.3 will demonstrate the consolidation of the mechanism. The AF will therefore support a flood-response targeted cash transfer, to be triggered and implemented within the delay established in the approved operational protocols, using the RSU, through a coordinated approach by local and national actors. To achieve this, the AF will support capacity strengthening on the mechanism's operational arrangements targeting the ecosystem of actors engaged in disaster preparedness and response, so as to foster genuine appropriation of the mechanism at all levels and ensure that the relevant institutions are equipped and aligned before any future activation. At the local level, this means deepening the operational readiness of the response mechanism, leveraging the presence of social workers and CACs, to conduct rapid data collection and targeting, based on or aligned with the RSU. At the central level, it means refining the triggering architecture and consolidating the role of the TWG as the standing interministerial consultative body for shock response through safety nets, integrated in the broader risk preparedness and response ecosystem. Building on these actions and following the operational protocols adopted under DLI 9.3, the response is expected to be triggered and implemented effectively. A result on the timely delivery of payments will demonstrate such achievement. As the size of the shock is unpredictable and beyond the Program’s control, the emphasis of the result has been placed on the quality and rapidity of the response mechanism, rather than the number of expected recipients, which has been kept to a minimum in the RF.49 36. The IPF component will continue to provide targeted technical assistance, especially where the engagement is less mature and institutional capacity weaker. First, the IPF will support systematic engagement of the broader ecosystem of actors – including national disaster management agencies, line ministries, civil society, and humanitarian partners – on the rationale and operational modalities of adaptive social protection and the specific role of targeted cash transfers in emergency response to floods and other climate-related shocks. Secondly, it will support development of the analytical and financing architecture underpinning shock response, including exploring the development of risk financing options and opportunities for contingency and climate adaptation financing – laying the groundwork for the more ambitious financing arrangements that could be formalized in the longer-term. Third, it will provide technical assistance on risk-mapping, to ensure flood-prone zones across the country and drought-exposed areas, using updated RSU data, and the development of response mechanisms to other shocks such as droughts and macroeconomic shocks, including food and fuel price spikes. Lastly, it will support the development of operational tools to inform potential adjustments of safety net programs in the face of climate change and the potential surge in extreme climate events. Theory of Change and Results Chain. Poor and vulnerable households in Côte d’Ivoire face three interconnected challenges: (i) fragmented access to social protection programs; (ii) limited opportunities to build productive; and (iii) sustainable livelihoods, and insufficient protection against shocks. The Program addresses these constraints through three complementary RA. First, it strengthens the social protection delivery system, including the RSU, social centers, and key delivery mechanisms, to effectively identify households and population groups for access to safety nets and economic opportunities. Second, it expands and deepens the PTMP through digital cash transfers, financial inclusion, and economic and human capital interventions, helping households, and in particular women within those, build more productive livelihoods. Third, it strengthens adaptive social protection by establishing a clear shock-response framework and building institutional and local capacity to provide timely assistance to affected households. These interventions will create a more integrated, productive, and shock-responsive social protection system, ultimately reducing poverty and vulnerability among the poorest households, particularly women. The AF is strategically relevant and technically sound, based on the original Technical Assessment (TA) and economic analysis, as well as insights from the first phase of implementation. It addresses the same core constraints to poverty and vulnerability reduction identified at OP Appraisal. The AF contributes to a better distribution of economic growth gains, to strengthening human capital, and to facilitating access to higher-productivity employment. It is anchored in the government's renewed institutional and financial commitment to social protection through the PAFS. The activities build on strong implementation performance and lessons learned from the OP, drawing on qualitative and quantitative evaluations as well as global evidence. The main takeaways are below. For details, please see TA addendum document.54 52. The AF’s RSU expansion prioritizes enrolments in new villages (active search) and the establishment of addition registration guichets (on-demand registrations) – both based in localities combining high poverty concentrations with low enrollment coverage, as operationally feasible. Using a coverage diagnostic, sous-préfectures where the poverty-to-enrollment ratio is 2 or higher are flagged as underserved, and settlements exceeding 10,000 inhabitants without an existing guichet are identified as priority sites. This approach is expected to result in a focus in several areas in regions such as Guémon, San-Pédro, and Moronou, with updated maps and indices to be integrated into the RSU operations manual ahead of each expansion drive. At this stage, the overall approach is identified, but the final selection of areas for expansion will only be conducted following completion of the ongoing RSU roll-out and taking into account future operational considerations. 53. The AF scales up a digital financial inclusion intervention grounded in both global evidence and PTMP-specific research. The Program’s desk review found that, while AVECs are effective at building savings habits and women's economic empowerment, they have greater long-term impact when combined with access to formal digital financial services. Beneficiaries increasingly express demand for individual savings solutions and growing familiarity and use of mobile wallets as informal “digital safes”. Moreover, early pilot results provide tangible evidence of impact, particularly for women, informing the design of the AF-supported scale-up, including a tenfold increase in digital savings account ownership and a twofold reduction in cash-in/cash-out behavior. The package to be scaled up through the AF is operationally embedded within the PTMP delivery platform: beneficiaries are reached through existing CACs, which deliver structured financial and digital literacy training, ongoing coaching and behavioral support. PTMP payment schedules are leveraged as touchpoints through reminder mechanisms, and volunteers drawn from beneficiary communities reinforce adoption within a peer-learning framework. The collaboration with the mobile operator is based on the existing partnership and contractual obligations between the BCPFS and Orange Money (the payment operator for the PTMP). The pilot monitoring arrangements include gender-disaggregated indicators to track women's participation and outcomes, and incorporates an experimental design comparing successive intervention packages to identify the most effective and cost-efficient combination for scale-up. Orange agents operate in coordination with CACs to support account opening, activation, product utilization, and ensuring support at the point of service delivery. 54. The AF’s design of the adolescents and young women’s empowerment intervention is grounded in global and regional evidence and builds on the PTMP's existing delivery infrastructure. Global and regional evidence consistently shows that adolescents and young women face compounding barriers that cannot be addressed through a single intervention. Programs such as SWEDD, EAGER, and AGILE have demonstrated that meaningful and lasting improvements in adolescent girls’ outcomes require simultaneous action across education, life skills, SRH, livelihoods, and social norms, and that engaging men, husbands, and community leaders is as critical as direct support to girls and women. The interventions will also build on the PTMP’s strong beneficiary and community engagement practices, which provides a proven and cost-effective platform for reaching this population, adapt delivery systems to better cater to this population and link them to services across sectors, without creating parallel systems. Evidence from the SWEDD project confirms that when social protection delivery systems are intentionally designed to address women's specific constraints, they can generate substantial gains in participation, earnings, savings, agency, and resilience. The AF therefore seizes a strategic opportunity to leverage an established program platform, and a strong evidence base to reach this underserved population under Côte d'Ivoire's social protection system. 55. The Program is expected to generate substantial economic returns. This is through four main channels: (i) increased household consumption and local economic activity; (ii) higher productive capacity and earnings; (iii) greater financial inclusion and women’s economic participation; and (iv) improved efficiency of social protection delivery. Climate Change 68. The climate risk screening conducted for the parent operation remains applicable to the AF, with no anticipated changes. Côte d'Ivoire faces increasing exposure to climate variability, which is manifested in floods and droughts occurrences. These risks directly affect the program's target population, who is in part reliant on rainfed agriculture and has limited coping capacity. The screening confirms that the program's activities — including the shock response mechanism and the geographic extension of the RSU – directly address identified climate vulnerabilities by strengthening the institutional and data infrastructure needed for anticipatory and responsive social protection. The residual risk to the program from climate hazards is assessed as Moderate and is being actively reduced through the design of the AF. Beyond poor rural households broadly, climate impacts particularly affect specific sub-groups within the target population: children and pregnant women face risks of malnutrition and waterborne disease during floods and droughts; people with disabilities and older people are exposed to mobility and access constraints in disaster situations; and adolescents, especially girls, are exposed to school dropout, and GBV in the aftermath of climate shocks. The AF's integrated approach – combining shock-responsive cash transfers, social services, and targeted programming for young women – is designed to address both their immediate and longer-term climate vulnerability. 69.
