KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions Project activities implemented by the National Forestry Authority (NFA) and the Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) have advanced - there has been good progress in activities aimed at prevention of human wildlife conflict (89 km of electric fence was erected in Queen Elizabeth National Park and Murchison Falls National Park, 21.8 km of elephant trenches and boardwalk-type barriers were completed in Kibale National Park, and renovation of the buffalo wall in Mgahinga Gorilla National Park was completed. Removal of invasive vegetation in target wildlife protected areas has achieved over 3,000 ha. NFA has fully supported establishment of 19 collaborative forest management groups in 13 central forest reserves and provided them with training and inputs for various livelihoods activities (bee keeping, sewing, craft making, tour guiding). UWA has supported collaborative resource management groups in PA-adjacent communities to construct 4,415 energy-efficient stoves and supported training and inputs for installation of over 700 rainwater harvesting systems with water tanks. NFA has completed initial planting for the forest restoration activities covering close to 7,200 ha of degraded forests within CFRs; this was done using local community labor under contracts, providing direct local economic benefits and reinforcing a sense of shared responsibility for these forests. Under the tourism activities, UWA has initiated construction of 5 visitor information centers in target protected areas and continued maintenance of tourism roads, for better tourism experience. Within refugee hosting areas, MWE, with support from technical service providers, commenced the agroforestry activities and provision of firewood to the most vulnerable refugees (persons with specific needs) in target refugee settlements since the last implementation support mission. As of June 30, 2025, 22,390 PSNs have benefitted from the firewood distribution support. For agroforestry, the service provider has reached 12,900 farmers through agroforestry trainings and enrolled over 7,000 farmers to participate, with the first round of planting ongoing during the current rainy season. 5.
