No The World Bank Rural Economy Development Project (P168326) I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING Project Status 1. The Mid-Term Review (MTR) held in April-May 2023 rates progress towards achieving the Project Development Objective (PDO) as Satisfactory. Four out five original PDO indicators have either reached or exceeded their targets1: i. Increased number of tourists in Gorno Badakhshan Autonomous Oblast (GBAO) and Khatlon (Target: 36,000; Actual: 415,811). ii. Increased tourism spending in GBAO and Khatlon (US$ million) (Target: 18; Actual: 19.8). iii. Increase in the volume of processed agri-food products in GBAO and Khatlon (Tons/year) (Target: 18; Actual: 341). iv. Number of jobs created through the project interventions in GBAO and Khatlon, of which 50% for women and youth (Target: 7,000 (50% for women); Actual: 1,500 (65% - for women)). v. Decreased NEET rate of 15-to-24-years-old men (Target: 5%; Actual: 7.2%). vi. Decreased NEET rate of 15-to-24-years-old women (Target: 42%; Actual: 49%). As of January 2024, the project’s disbursements stand at 63.3% (US$18.4 million). The Results Framework (RF) which includes all PDO and intermediate results indicators is included into Annex A. 2. Under Component 1 (Improve public infrastructure for development of tourism, agribusiness, and related sectors for which US$12 million was planned and US$6,3 million disbursed), the Project has financed public investments in historical and cultural tourism sites, including through grants to communities, municipalities, and non-for-profit organizations to improve tourism and agribusiness infrastructure, and public investments in touristic signage. The progress can be summarized as follows: i. The rehabilitation of the two cultural heritage sites in Khatlon (Hulbuk Castle, and Chiluchorchashma) will be completed in the summer of 2024, which is ahead of the schedule. ii. Khoja Mashad Madrassa construction works have been already completed. iii. The construction works to rehabilitate Yamchun Fortress, which is in GBAO, will start in March 2024. iv. The total number of community grants reached 30 for the total amount of US$1.9 million. v. Out of 30 grants, 19 grants support agribusiness and tourism activities in Khatlon and 11 in GBAO. vi. 421 tourist signs have been installed in 41 districts of Khatlon and GBAO. 3. The overall implementation progress of the Project and Component 1 are rated Moderately Satisfactory due to the delay in launching the construction works at the Yamchun Fortress. As COVID-19 imposed travel and social gatherings restrictions, an inception report for the rehabilitation was prepared with a delay. This delay postponed the development of the design and drawings, which the Government approved in the summer of 2023. In addition, as the fortress is in GBAO, the construction works are limited to spring and summer seasons due to weather conditions and poor site accessibility. Component 2 supports MSMEs and entrepreneurs in the tourism, agribusiness, and related sectors through matching grants. The results to date can be summarized as follows: i. The total number of approved matching grants reached 526 for US$5.6 million. ii. 266 grants support agribusiness MSMEs and 260 grants support tourism related MSMEs. iii. 140 MSMEs are female headed. iv. 9 matching grants (for US$0.8 million) were provided to farmers and processors in two agribusiness value chains. 5. Component 3 finances capacity building and business development services in tourism, agribusiness, and related sectors. To date: i. 42 capacity-building activities for US$1.1 million were carried out by the Ministry of Finance (MoF), Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), Tourism Development Committee (TDC), State Committee on Investments and State Property Management, and the Tourism Information and Service Center”. ii. The Center of Tourism and Health was hired to conduct training courses to improve the skills of staff of hotels, dormitories, boarding houses, restaurants, and canteens. The center conducted 14 training courses in GBAO and Khatlon region. 360 people attended the training courses, 144 of them were women. iii. The PIU hired five local companies to perform as enablers (facilitators) for the project’s grants program. Their role is to ensure the program maximum outreach and enable potential beneficiaries of matching grants program to become successful program participants. The enablers also monitor and provide technical assistance to successful beneficiaries during the implementation of their sub-projects. 6. Activities under Component 4 (Project Implementation and Monitoring) reflect the strong ownership of the Project by the MoF, MoA, and TDC. Overall, coordination and roles of key staff responsible for implementation, including those in the PIU, have been defined and implemented in a satisfactory manner. In addition, financial management arrangements at the PIU, including planning and budgeting, accounting, financial reporting, funds flow, internal control, external audits, and staffing continue to be acceptable to the World Bank. Project management is rated Satisfactory. Procurement is rated Moderately Satisfactory due to the change in procurement staff at the PIU. Applicable environmental and social standards measures are under implementation and have been rated Moderately Satisfactory. The MoF maintains the PIU with qualified staff and resources to support the management of the Project. 7. There are considerable discrepancies between original and achieved to-date targets of several PDO and intermediate results indicators. The following are the reasons behind such discrepancies: i. Agribusiness: two indicators, namely (i) Increase in the volume of processed agri-food products in GBAO and Khatlon (Tons/year) (Target: 18; Actual: 341); and (ii) Increase of the number of small scales post harvesting processing and cold storage facilities established (Target: 50; Actual: 164). The first indicator reflects the existing high level of demand for value addition activities in the food sector by farmers, businesses, and communities and the fact that the project activities were able to fill the gap and address this demand. Although the indicator on post-harvest/cold storage demonstrates the overperformance of 328%, this overachievement should be read in combination with the other indicator on the establishment of milk collection points, which shows an underachievement. Post-harvest centers for fruits and vegetables are easier to establish and operate. Such centers provide larger market opportunities as they can operate for almost all types for fruits and vegetables. Milk collection centers require advanced technologies for establishment and operationalization. High level of milk vulnerability dictates a necessity to have a prior agreement with an off taker to make a milk collection point The World Bank Rural Economy Development Project (P168326) successful. Another factor to consider is the high investment cost required to establish a milk collection point compared to fruits and vegetables post-harvest centers, which is governed by the size of the grants allocated to project beneficiaries. For the second indicator, the modern technologies that have become available to final beneficiaries through the project’s grant program greatly contributed to the increase of agribusiness products and overperformance compared to original targets. The final targets of these two indicators will be adjusted to reflect the latest achievements. In particular, the target for the first indicator will become 400 tones/year; and the target for the second indicator will become 265. ii. Tourism: The indicator “Increased number of tourists in GBAO and Khatlon (Target: 36,000; Actual: 415,811)” has considerably exceeded the original targets. Following Decree No. 464 of October 26, 2021, “On the introduction of a one-sided visa-free regime for several foreign countries” since January 1, 2022, citizens of 52 foreign countries do not require a visa to enter the Republic of Tajikistan. The visa-free regime has greatly impacted the tourists’ arrival to the Republic of Tajikistan. Another important factor is a two-way visa-free regime between the Republic of Tajikistan and the Republic of Uzbekistan introduced in March 2018. The final target for this indicator is adjusted to reflect this travel boost and become 800,000. Rationale for restructuring 8. The proposed restructuring includes a 12-month extension of the Project closing date from December 2, 2024, through December 31, 2025. This is the first extension of the Project. 9. The extension is necessary to complete the rehabilitation of the Yamchun Fortress, as requested by the MoF in their letter of November 23, 2023. The requested extension will allow to fully utilize US$4.4 million allocated for rehabilitation, leveraging this site as a tourism anchor in Tajikistan. The primary reasons for the implementation delay are discussed in detail in paragraph 3 above. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 10. The PDO and fund allocation remain unchanged. This restructuring includes: (a) a 12-month extension of the Project closing date from December 2, 2024, to December 31, 2025, to accommodate the completion of the Yamchun Fortress Rehabilitation; (b) a revision of the RF to reflect the changes of four PDO indicators; consolidation of two PDO indicators into one, and the change of one intermediate results indicator as discussed and agreed upon during the MTR held in April-May 2023; and (c) removal of a legal covenant for the establishment of a Project Steering Committee (SC). The following results indicators are proposed to be changed: i. The PDO indicator, “Number of jobs created through the project interventions in GBAO and Khatlon, of which 50% for women and youth (Number)”. The original target of this indicator, 7,000, did not differentiate between documented and non-documented jobs expected to be generated under the project. During the MTR, it was clarified that only documented jobs can be measured. The proposed target shall include only documented jobs and is proposed to be modified from 7,000 to 3,500, out of which 50% are for women. ii. The PDO indicator, “Increased number of tourists in GBAO and Khatlon (Target: 36,000; Actual: 415,811)”. As extensively discussed in paragraph 7.ii of this Restructuring Paper, the target for this indicator will be increased to 800,000 in order to reflect a travel boost followed by the adoption of a visa-free regime for 52 countries in 2022 and visa-free regime between the Republic of Uzbekistan and the Republic of Tajikistan in 2018. The World Bank Rural Economy Development Project (P168326) iii. Consolidation of two PDO indicators, Decreased NEET rate of 15-to-24-years-old men and Decreased NEET rate of 15-to-24-years-old women, into one indicator “Number of Young People trained under the project to work in agribusiness and tourism sectors, disaggregated by age and gender”. The consolidated indicator will allow the PIU to evaluate the project’s impact on the young population in Tajikistan more accurately. The proposed target is 1,500 jobs out of which 50% are for young women. iv. Increase of the volume of processed agribusiness products in GBAO and Khatlon. Based on the analysis of approved agribusiness grants and technical characteristics of purchased equipment, the target for this indicator will be increased from 18 tons per year to 400 per year, by December 31, 2025. v. Increase of the number of small scales post harvesting processing and cold storage facilities established. Based on the analysis of approved agribusiness grants and technical characteristics of purchased equipment, the target for this indicator will be increased from 50 to 265. vi. Removal of a legal covenant for the establishment of a Project Steering Committee (SC). The project’s financing agreement included the establishment of a national-level SC to oversee the three projects comprising the Tajikistan Resilience Strengthening Program (RSP) financed under the Risk Mitigation Regime (RMR)2 within three months following project effectiveness. The SC’s purpose was to provide strategic guidance, coordination, implementation support, and interlinkages between the individual projects that form part of the RSP and the relevant Government of Tajikistan’s strategic policy priorities and reforms. To date, the Government of Tajikistan has not established the SC and its absence has not impeded project implementation. The project management teams and ministries responsible for implementing the RMR projects have established coordination mechanisms at the working level. Thus, the proposed restructuring would drop the legal covenant for the SC establishment, and the same will be considered for the remaining two projects. 11. There are no overdue audits under the Project and previous audits are found acceptable to the Bank.
