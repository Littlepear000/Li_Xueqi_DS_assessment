STRATEGIC CONTEXT 1. The proposed Project covers Phase 3 of the Brazil Proactive, Safe and Resilient Road Asset Management Program – Brazil Pro-Roads, a horizontal and simultaneous1 Multi-phase Programmatic Approach (MPA). The Board of Executive Directors of the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) approved the MPA’s overall financing envelope on September 10, 2024, in the amount of US$1,662.4 million across at least five phases. The Board approved Phase 1 of the MPA (P180555) on September 10, 2024, in the amount of US$150 million to support the MPA in the state of Bahia. Phase 2 will provide US$162.4 million to support the MPA in the state of Espírito Santo. This Phase 3 will provide US$300 million to support the MPA in the State of Santa Catarina (SSC).2 A. Project Strategic Context 2. Brazil’s real GDP grew by 3.2 percent in 2023 and is estimated to have maintained the same pace in 2024, driven by strong consumption, supported by a resilient labor market and fiscal transfers. Growth in 2023 was fueled by private consumption, fiscal stimulus, and declining inflation, while exports benefited from a record grain harvest and favorable external conditions. From January to September 2024, GDP grew 3.3 percent compared to the same period a year prior, underpinned by solid consumption and recovering investment. Growth is expected to moderate to 2.2 percent in 2025 and stabilize at 2.3 percent in the medium term, reflecting gains from recent structural reforms. The government has made progress in improving the business environment, financial sector, and indirect taxation. However, faster, inclusive, long-term growth requires additional reforms to enhance competitiveness, reduce financial and market distortions, expand infrastructure investment, integrate into global value chains, and improve education quality. 3. Poverty and inequality remain significant challenges. After a sharp increase from 25.3 to 29.4 percent over 2019-21 amid the COVID recession, the poverty rate has decreased since 2021 and is projected to reach 20.5 percent by 2026. This is thanks to the recent economic rebound, labor market improvements (with the unemployment rate falling to 6.9 percent in June 2024), and consistent social protection policies. However, as of 2023, 43 million people (21.3 percent of the population) still lived below the poverty line (US$6.85 per day). Brazil ranks ninth globally in levels of inequality,3 and access to services and opportunities varies sharply across subregions and population segments. On average, rural areas are twice as poor as urban areas.4 Women and minorities, especially Afro-descendants and members of indigenous populations (IPs), have the lowest rates of access to job opportunities and lower wages on average,5 and poverty rates among them are the highest.6 4. Over the past decades, transport infrastructure development in Brazil has faced challenges due to fluctuating public investment policies and periodic economic crises. As a result, Brazil continues to rank low in terms of infrastructure quality, which poses challenges to the country’s growth and inclusion efforts. In Brazil, Santa Catarina is one of the states most affected by natural disasters and climate change. Its vulnerability to extreme weather has resulted in numerous disasters, causing significant economic and human losses. In the past two decades, over 30 tornadoes have impacted the western region, particularly around Chapecó, affecting more than 70,000 people. Meanwhile, floods have devastated communities in the east, leading to high death tolls and widespread displacement. Over a 32-year period, the state recorded 6,560 incidents, with 311 deaths and 1.1 million people displaced. 46 percent of these incidents occurred in the last decade. B. Sectoral and Institutional Context 8. The road network in Santa Catarina is in poor condition. The road network in Santa Catarina consists of 6,289 kilometers of state highways, of which 82 percent are single-lane paved roads, primarily asphalted. Eighteen percent are unpaved roads. The municipal road system adds another 66,607 kilometers—mostly unpaved. According to the National Confederation of Transport (Confederação Nacional de Transporte – CNT16) in 2023 approximately 74 percent of the SSC ‘s paved roads were in regular, poor, or very poor condition, despite their strategic role for mobility and logistics in the SSC and in Brazil. 9. Poor road network conditions contribute to the deterioration of road safety, a significant issue in Brazil and critical in the SSC. According to Ministry of Health data, there were 34,881 traffic-related deaths in Brazil in 2023, a 9.2 percent increase from 2019 and 2.9 increase from 2022. In the SSC the death toll reached 18 per 100,000 inhabitants in 2022, against a national average of 16 per 100,000 inhabitants, affecting life expectancy in the state.17 In total, 925 people died on roads in the SSC in 2022.18 Beyond the human toll of this figure, road safety has also high economic costs for the country resulting from: (i) the management of traffic crashes; (ii) injuries with long-lasting impacts, affecting ability to work or care for others; and (iii) future productivity of young generations, as most of affected individuals are young, educated people. Overall, it is estimated that road safety costs represent a staggering 5 percent of the country’s annual GDP. 10. Considering the significant adverse impact of climate events in Santa Catarina, climate change is a key criterion when prioritizing road selection for the project. In agreement with the Government of Santa Catarina, Phase 3 of the MPA will benefit around 1,262 kilometers of roads, organized into clusters to enable more efficient and effective management. This clustered approach will also facilitate the broader implementation of long-term Contratos de Reabilitação e Manutenção (CREMA)—or performance-based contracts for rehabilitation and road maintenance— across the entire state road network. The selected lots are all in areas of moderate to high geological and hydrological risk according to the Brazil Geological Service’s susceptibility maps concerning mass movements and flooding. Those lots are distributed across the Alto Vale (257 km), Brusque (192 km), and Planalto (588 km) regions for CREMA DBM – totaling 1037 km; and for CREMA PPP across Norte (134 km) and Oeste (91 km) – for a total of 225 km. Climate risks in those regions stem from a combination of high moisture from the ocean and high mountains, favoring heavy precipitation, while Oeste region may also suffer with droughts. Moreover, according to the latest assessment conducted by the State, the roads included in the program are predominantly in good (34.2 percent rated as good or great) and regular (41.2 percent) condition. However, 24.6 percent of the roads in bad or very bad condition are already being covered by existing rehabilitation contracts funded with SSC own resources, so the Project interventions will be focused on improving safety and resilient aspects while providing long-term maintenance. 11. The MPA's primary focus is to prioritize interventions that enhance resilience to climate impacts and road safety. Therefore, in the SSC phase, the roads will benefit mostly from long-term maintenance and road safety and climate resilience improvements. As a signatory to the Paris Agreement, Brazil has committed to reducing greenhouse gas emissions, aligning the MPA Program and Phase 3 with its Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC), the State of Santa Catarina's measures for climate adaptation, and the climate strategies.19 In its 2024 NDC submission,20 Brazil is setting an economy-wide target of reducing its net greenhouse gas emissions by 59 to 67 percent below 2005 levels by 2035 while updated its National Adaptation Plan (NAP), including transport-related actions. Phase 3 supports these objectives by enhancing road management, promoting non-motorized transport, and championing sustainable logistics in the Greater Florianópolis area. Furthermore, it focuses on building climate-resilient roads in vulnerable regions, aligning with Brazil’s decarbonization strategies in the National Logistics Plan 2035, future 205021 priorities, and the ongoing updates to the State of Santa Catarina's transport and climate adaptation policies.22 13. The project will support the SSC to embark on a wide adoption of performance-based conditions (PBCs) and to develop a PPP program for roads. The MPA seeks to break the cycle of reactive road asset management by replacing it with proactive maintenance. The proposed Phase 3 of the Brazil Pro-Roads MPA will support the State Secretariat of Infrastructure and Mobility of Santa Catarina (SIE-SC) to improve the management of road assets by delegating road infrastructure management to the private sector. This will be achieved through two modalities of long-term PBCs: CREMA DBM and CREMA PPP (see box 1.1 below). Those contracts will enable improved climate resilience and road safety. The SSC will both benefit from and contribute to the learning agenda that aims at improving the technical and institutional capacity of the MPA participants in the use of PBCs. Phase 3 will also support the SSC in developing a roads PPP Program by piloting one PPP operation, leveraging the positive results 23 of private participation in road management in Brazil. These were called Contratos de Reabilitação e Manutenção (CREMA)—or performance-based contracts for rehabilitation and road maintenance. Maintenance services will be paid upon achievements of pre- determined standards of road conditions. Mechanisms of guaranteed retentions on payments during the rehabilitation phase, which is expected to represent up to 70-80 percent of the contract value, will require the mobilization of private finance in an amount estimated on average at 5 percent of the contracts.26 - CREMA- PPP: This will involve contracts, ranging from 10 to 25 years, and will follow a Design-Finance-Build- Operate-Maintain (DFBOM) approach, contracted under the PPP regulation. The contract’s full financing will be provided by the private sector to implement the rehabilitation works defined by the contractor itself, with improvement works defined by the delegating authority. Payment to the contractors will be made based on availability, and a road will be deemed available when it matches pre-determined standards of quality (resilient and safe roads in good condition). As the full amount of the contract is expected to be brought by the private sector, private capital mobilization (PCM) will be accounted for the amount of the contract. 14. The Phase 3 Project will introduce long-term performance-based contracts for rehabilitation and road maintenance (Contratos de Reabilitação e Manutenção – CREMA) at scale in the SCC to improve the management of the State’s road network in line with the multiphase programmatic approach (MPA) objective. The Project will cover about 1,262 km of the State’s roads (or 20 percent of the State’s Road network) with 8–10-year CREMA-DBMs and a pilot CREMA PPP. All state roads in the four selected administrative regions will be covered to enable easy future expansion of the program. Contract specifications will follow the MPA program guidelines. The guidelines define the organization of the works and services: (i) initial recovery of infrastructure functionalities at contract start; (ii) rehabilitation work wherever required after initial recovery; (iii) maintenance services from after rehabilitation work through to the end of the contract; and (iv) road safety and resilience improvement works to eliminate critical points identified. As per the Guidelines, the definition of initial recovery and rehabilitation work as well as the nature of road maintenance services will be delegated to the private sector as a way to transfer more technical responsibility to the contractors. This is expected to foster innovation. Payments will be made upon achievement of outputs (concluded works and/or road conditions) subject to achievement of pre-determined standards. Eventual payment deductions will be applied in cases where agreed standards (based upon pre-defined scales) were not achieved. A risk allocation matrix will establish respective responsibilities incurred by each contracted party according to the nature of the event. The aim is to keep a balanced risk allocation between both parties and identify who can take on the risk. (Typically, contractors are responsible for technical risks, while a public authority takes on exogenous risks). Improvement works will consist essentially of activities aimed at road infrastructure protection in the context of Climate Change (e.g., embankment and cuts stabilization, longitudinal and/or drainages upgrades) and road users’ comfort and safety improvements (horizontal and vertical traffic signs, road crossing improvements, passing zones, third lanes, etc.). They will be capped in size to not represent a significant share of the contract, will not exceed 30 percent of the contract, and will remain in the right of way. 39. The Phase 3 Project will pilot safe active mobility improvement in at least four municipalities. Main interventions will include pedestrian streets, larger accessible sidewalks, and safe cycleways with an aim at improving road safety, universal accessibility, and non-motorized transport along urban stretches of State roads. Works will also improve longitudinal drainage along state roads. Economic Analysis 40. An economic assessment was done using a socioeconomic cost-benefit analysis (CBA). The evaluation was based on a comparison of the respective costs and benefits with and without project scenarios, using a Highway Development and Management (HDM-4) model. Benefits stem from cost reductions for road users as well as public spending savings from reduced costs and frequency of works. The reduction of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, including the Social Price of Carbon (SPC), was also undertaken according to World Bank Guidance. 41. The Phase 3 Project shows robust economic justification. Net present value (NPV) is US$530.7 million calculated at 12 percent discount rate, and economic rate of return (ERR) is 52.6 percent. This significantly exceeds the opportunity cost of capital, set at 12 percent.51 These estimates were calculated based on an HDM-4 analysis for the four lots52 of the paved network to be implemented via CREMA-DBM, totaling 1,034 kilometers. The Road Safety Screening and Appraisal Tool (RSSAT) was applied to assess the road safety impact of the proposed Project. The RSSAT analysis used a representative road section—the rural section of SC-114—to obtain a project safety impact (PSI) score of 0.751, corresponding to a 25 percent reduction in road crash fatalities. Project benefits have been estimated based on: (i) vehicle operation cost reduction and travel time savings on the improved selected roads (NPV estimated at US$412 million and US$28.5 million, respectively); (ii) road safety benefits resulting from a reduction in road traffic fatalities and serious injuries (NPV estimated at US$204.5 million); and (iii) negative gains from mildly increased CO2 emissions (NPV estimated at US$2.8 million). These values are summarized in Project files. The costs included in the analysis were: (i) the costs of feasibility studies, construction, and supervision of works; and (ii) costs of implementation, management and implementation, and maintenance. The counterfactual (without project) scenario reflected a do- minimum scenario. Additionally, the analysis compared lifecycle costs in the project scenario against an alternative scenario reflecting the traditional reactive approach, in which road segments only receive minimal intervention until reaching a deterioration threshold, upon which they are reconstructed. Road agency cost savings amounted to 10 percent in a 20-year horizon53, which are conservative compared to the estimated savings from long-term CREMA contracts vis-à-vis traditional procurement contracts in the State of Bahia, which achieved 38 percent cost savings. The following are considered UA and do not pose any inherent risks: (i) performance-based contract (PBC) proactive road maintenance (upgrading, rehabilitation and reconstruction) with minor enhancements for resilience, road safety, and accessibility purposes without significant increases in traffic capacity (Component 1); (ii) institutional strengthening with a focus on climate resilience and infrastructure adaption, and developing feasibility studies for the urban mobility of Greater Florianopolis, which will, when implemented, reduce congestion and lower emissions (Component 2); and (iii) active mobility, road safety and universal accessibility interventions in urban areas (Component 3). Assessment and reduction of adaptation risks. The Project Phase 3 is aligned with the goals of the Paris Agreement on adaptation, focusing on mitigating climate vulnerabilities in road infrastructure. As roads were chosen based on their climate vulnerability, they face high to moderately high climate risks.
