KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions The progress towards achievement of the Project Development Objective (PDO) and overall implementation is Satisfactory. All four PDO indicators have been achieved. Aggregate budget outturn remains below the target of 105%, and the share of districts expenditure arrears to the consolidated district budget has remained below the 6% target. Budget transparency and citizen engagement have improved with the annual publication of citizens budgets. The number of public sector staff with professional qualification (in finance, procurement and project management) has increased by more than 50% from the baseline of 665. Component 1: PFM IT and Systems Roll-Out. The project supported the IFMIS roll out to service delivery units by enhancing functionality and stability of the system. The IFMIS roll-out to the education sector, which was not captured in IFMIS at the beginning of the project, is progressing well. 83% of education sector expenditure, with the exception of primary education, is captured as of June 30, 2024. 400 primary schools are expected to be on-boarded by March 2025 and full reporting of primary schools’ data by June 2025. Additionally, the upgrade of the Integrated Personnel and Payroll Information system (IPPIS) is progressing, with psychometric questions and interface platform already developed. Component 2: Accounting and Financial Reporting. Although the end target for full IPSAS compliant financial statement will not be achieved, FY 2024 financial statements will be prepared on accrual basis, recognizing several assets and liabilities for the first time. Full adoption of accrual standards was hindered by the need to adjust the opening balance of assets and liabilities as of July 1, 2023. After carrying out a cost benefit analysis to the users of the financial statement regarding adjustment of the opening balance, the government has decided to prepare the financial statements for FY 24 in accordance with the Organic Law on PFM N° 002/2022, with appropriate reference to IPSAS 33, without the need to prepare an opening balance sheet as of July 1, 2023. During the FY 2024/25, the GoR aims to achieve compliance with the accrual IPSAS accounting and financial reporting framework. Efforts to sustain the requirements of using accrual IPSAS and further capacity building are underway. A capacity building strategy has been prepared and approved. An MoU has been signed between MINECOFIN and the Institute of Certified Public Accountants of Rwanda (ICPAR) for sustained training on IPSAS qualification. The GoR will need to allocate the necessary budget so the implementation of the strategy can be conducted smoothly. Component 3: Performance-Based Budgeting (PBB) and Medium -Term Budgeting. PBB has been rolled out initially to selected priority sectors [Education, Infrastructure and Health] in accordance with the PFM law and further rolled out to all sectors and performance indicators included as Annex 10 of the budget estimates. IFMIS capability has been enhanced to capture non-financial performance information of the initial pilot PBB programs, the usage of which needs to expand to all sectors. The IFMIS project management module will be launched in November 2024, enhancing the use of IFMIS from project initiation to closure. To improve the data quality needed for PBB and to utilize the existing capabilities of IFMIS for monitoring, continuous training will be provided to sector working groups and targeted PFM staff. On strengthening the medium-term fiscal forecasting (MTFF), the use of MTEF baseline costing commenced in FY 2023/24 and was integrated into the PFM law. The citizen’s guide to the budget is developed each year. MINECOFIN carries out a citizen engagement process during budget preparation and dissemination. Through the preparation and publication of citizen guide booklets, the Government provides feedback with insights on resource generation and distribution of priorities. The project has achieved the expected result with regards to annual citizen’s budget publication. Component 4: Professionalization of PFM Staff-PFM Capacity Development. The project has met its target of expanding the number of qualified professionals in the public sector. The project has expanded qualification in PFM and related Nov 06, 2024 Page 2 of 12 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Rwanda Public Finance Management Reform Project (P164807) professions, supported PFM professionalization through the support to Institute of Certified Public Accountants of Rwanda (ICPAR), and supported the procurement function. Summary of key achievements include qualification of 1,015 professionals; an internship pathway to the public sector created for high performing university students; the establishment of a public sector unit under ICPAR; development of an online PFM certification program hosted by ICPAR; and support to procurement professionalization through establishment of institute of procurement professionals and development of national procurement certification. 5.
