Introduction and Context Introduction 1. This Project Information Document (PID) describes the additional financing (AF) in the amount of US$3.8 million from the Food Systems 2030 (FS2030) Umbrella Program to the Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD) under Phase 1 of the Food Systems Resilience Program for Eastern and Southern Africa (FSRP, P178566). IGAD has submitted an official request for the AF to scale-up its engagement in early warning efforts, aiming to further strengthen the ongoing resilience initiatives under the FSRP. The AF seeks approval of the Director of Regional Integration (RI) at the World Bank (WB). 2. The FSRP MPA was approved by the World Bank Board of Directors on June 21, 2022, with an overall envelope of US$2.3 billion equivalent. This includes US$788.1 million allocated for Phase 1 which provides financing to Ethiopia (US$600 million), Madagascar (US$158.1 million), the Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD) (US$25 million), and the Centre for Coordination of Agricultural Research and Development for Southern Africa (CCARDESA) (US$5 million). 3. On May 31, 2023, the Board approved the second and third phases of the MPA together with an increase in the available International Development Association (IDA) financing, bringing the total IDA envelope to US$2.75 billion. Phase 2 (P179818) provides US$300 million to Tanzania (Program-for- Results – PforR). Phase 3 (P177816) provides US$603 million in national and regional IDA financing to Kenya (US$150 million), the Union of Comoros (US$40 million), Somalia (US$150 million), Malawi (US$250 million), and the African Union (US$13 million). Additionally, Phase 3 includes a Global Agriculture and Food Security Program (GAFSP) grant of US$15 million for the Malawi FSRP and a Global Program for the Blue Economy (GPBE) grant of US$3 million for the Comoros FSRP. 4. Additional Financing to the MPA. In June 2024, the Madagascar FSRP received US$70 million in AF to support its Contingency Emergency Response Component (CERC) operation, comprising US$50 million from the Crisis Response Window Early Response Financing (CRW ERF) and US$20 million from the GAFSP. In September 2024, the Tanzania FSRP received an AF of US$8.3 million. On February 20, 2025, the Board approved US$61 million in grants for Malawi for the project comprising US$45 million from the Malawi Second Agricultural Commercialization and Resilience Enhancement Project Multi-Donor Trust Fund (AGCOM 2.0 MDTF1), and US$16 million from the Food Systems 2030 Umbrella Program (FS2030). On June 23, 2025, an additional finance was approved in the amount of US$50 million from the Crisis Response Window Plus and US$14.6 million in national IDA resources to the Malawi FSRP; and US$30 million in national IDA resources to the Somalia FSRP. The same report also underscored that one of the key drivers of the cur rent situation is the region’s continued vulnerability to frequent and severe climate extremes. These include a prolonged drought from 2020-2023 in the Eastern Horn of Africa, widespread flooding associated with El Niño, and recurring transboundary pest outbreaks such as desert locusts. Persistent dry conditions throughout 2023 and the first half of 2024 have further strained agricultural production. Meanwhile, heavy rainfall in late 2023 led to widespread flooding, a crisis that continued into 2024, resulting in loss of life, displacement, and damage to infrastructure and agricultural assets, all of which have deepened food insecurity across the region. 7. The desert locust (DL) outbreak from 2019 to 2021 had a particularly devastating impact on countries like Djibouti, Kenya, Ethiopia, Sudan, South Sudan, Uganda, and Somalia. Beyond desert locust, other pests have also hampered agricultural productivity, raising serious concerns about the continued emergence of transboundary pest threat to the region’s already fragile food security. Moreover, widespread conflicts across the region have led to population displacement and further disruption of food systems. 8. Recognizing the continued importance and urgency of investing in robust early warning mechanisms, IGAD has launched several interventions under the FSRP: (i) establishing a gender-responsive regional framework for digital agro- climate advisory services (DACAS) for farmers and pastoralists, with linkages to national DACAS; (ii) producing bi-weekly food security reports to provide timely updates on the region’s situation; and (iii) developing a regional online platform, including e-learning components, to serve as a repository for food systems information and best practices. 9. To safeguard and further enhance long-term resilience investments of the FSRP and other resilience-building interventions, it is essential to scale-up early warning investments by building upon existing and/or establish effective disaster early warning systems (EWS) to enhance FSRP’s development impact. Investing in early warning is not just about responding to crises—it is about preventing them. In a region increasingly affected by climate shocks, conflict, and food insecurity, EWS are a vital strategic asset. They save lives, lower humanitarian costs, and help build resilience across borders. Every dollar invested in early warning saves up to US$7 in disaster response.4 The return is high, and the cost of inaction is far greater. As a regional leader in coordination and data sharing, IGAD is uniquely positioned to strengthen integrated EWS that enable timely, evidence-based action. By investing in early warning, IGAD empowers member states to act before disasters escalate, improves food security planning, and enhances regional stability. 10. While long-term investments by IGAD are prioritized, no dedicated resources exist for integrating advanced monitoring, surveillance, and forecasting tools to strengthen climate risk management. Until recently, these systems have relied heavily on international development financing. While these funding streams are now in question, critical gaps are emerging in the existing system. 11. IGAD is well positioned and has the mandate to play a major role in strengthening food systems resilience through early warning. Firstly, IGAD is mandated to coordinate early warning information. Secondly, it is the only regional organization with connections to navigate the political economy ecosystem that can impact the flow of information. However, to maximize its role, IGAD needs to strengthen its capacity across the three proposed activities. 4United Nations Development Programme (UNDP). 2016. The strategic focus of the AF early warning investments is aligned with several regional initiatives. The proposed activities are aligned with the African Union (AU) Agenda 2063, the Malabo Declaration on African Agriculture, the Comprehensive Africa Agriculture Development Programme (CAADP), and the Forum for Agricultural Research in Africa (FARA) strategic plans. The proposed AF is also aligned with strong analytical foundations, including CAADP National Agriculture Investment Plans (NAIPs), which set out a development strategy for countries’ agriculture sectors (all countries), Climate Smart Agriculture (CSA) profiles, and investment plans that identify priority technologies and delivery channels to increase climate smartness in the sector. 13. The proposed AF is also aligned with the Food Systems 2030 (FS2030) Umbrella Program by supporting its overarching goals of resilience and sustainability within food systems. Investing in early warning systems enables timely, evidence- based decisions that prevent food crises and reduce the need for costly humanitarian responses. Early warnings also promote integrated risk management and long-term planning across agriculture and food systems. Therefore, investing in early warning contributes to food systems becoming more adaptive, stable, and capable of withstanding future shocks – leading to a transformation toward sustainable food security and resilience. Project Description 14. IGAD is currently implementing activities under the original FSRP financing, focusing on interventions that support the Program’s resilience objectives. These include supporting harmonized policies in the IGAD region, strengthening a regional Center of Excellence on CSA, and developing Knowledge Platforms on selected thematic areas of agri-food systems resilience. IGAD recently launched the establishment of the DACAS, an online platform aiming to improve digital climate advisory services in the region, thereby strengthening early warning systems and disaster risk management. The DACAS will help farmers adapt and build resilience to weather variability and climate change and as well provide information, agronomic practices, market availability, input supply and general information on the entire agricultural value chain. 15. The objective of this AF is to scale-up DACAS investments under FSRP. The AF will expand the geographic coverage of DACAS network to include Arid and Semi-Arid Areas of the IGAD region as its current geographic focus is limited to the region’s surplus producing areas. In addition, the AF will also directly address gaps recently identified through baseline assessments, which highlights the need for digital product development, which has not yet been implemented under DACAS. The AF will bridge this critical gap by leveraging advanced technologies to improve data acquisition and integration; building capacity for system management, analytical modelling and forecasting; and strengthening the governance of early warning systems to enable data-driven decision making for proactive response. Additionally, the AF Page 6 Official Use Only The World Bank Food Systems Resilience Program for Eastern and Southern Africa (P178566) – Additional Financing Request 3 components will also build on the achievements of the Emergency Locust Response Program (ELRP) MPA (P174546) by strengthening East Africa Pest Watch, an established early warning system for pests. 16. The remaining funds (US$16.4 million) available to IGAD under the FSRP for the 2025 –2029 period has already been fully committed to planned, longer-term resilience-building activities across the Program Pillars. For example, funds will be used to (a) establish regional Centers of Leadership focused on promoting policy reforms in Climate Smart Agriculture, and Soil Health and Fertiliser; (b) create knowledge networks for the One Health concept, improved groundwater management, and trade; and (c) IGAD will lead on processes seeking to improve regional policy harmonzation on key issues such as input supply, post harvest management, and digitalization. The activities under this AF will focus on complementary intervenions to those currently implemented by IGAD to further advance the resilience objective under the FSRP. 17. Component 1: (Re-) Building Resilient Agricultural Production Capacity will be directly strengthened through the AF. The total allocation for this component will increase by 47 percent (from US$6.50 million to US$9.54 million) compared to the previous allocation financed solely under the IDA grant bringing the new total allocation to US$9.54 million. The AF will finance the scaling-up of early warning activities under sub-component 1.1 “Developing National or Regional Agricultural Information Systems�?. This sub-component is currently implementing DACAS investments under FSRP. The additional activities to be financed exclusively by the AF under sub-component 1.1 include: a. Deploying sustainable, cost-effective technologies to enhance the region’s ability to collect, harmonize, and analyze key datasets for early warning. The AF will support the deployment of advanced ICT and data systems to strengthen DACAS. This includes hardware (servers, workstations, storage, monitors, UPS), satellite imagery validated by field sensors, specialized software (ENVI, ArcGIS, SimClim), and cloud-based platforms (AWS) for secure, real-time data sharing. Consultancies will focus on harmonizing Earth observation data, monitoring agricultural conditions, modeling climate scenarios, and conducting socio-economic assessments. b. Building institutional and human capacity to manage systems and use data effectively following the deployment of these technologies. The AF will support tailored, hands-on training for technical staff and decision-makers to improve their ability to use satellite and field data for early warning on crops, rangelands, and pests. Training will align with institutional mandates and promote standardized tools across member states, using region-specific materials that reflect local conditions. c. Strengthening coordination, interoperability, and governance of early warning systems in recognition of the cross-border nature of climate and food security shocks. The project will promote collaborative frameworks for data sharing, joint planning, and decision-making among IGAD member states and relevant regional bodies. It will also support the integration of advanced analytics into policy processes to ensure early warning outputs inform crisis response, strategic resource allocation, and long-term development planning. 18. Components 2, 3 and 4. Components 2, 3, and 4 will each retain their original funding with no changes to their scale or scope. 19. Component 5: Project Management and Coordination. Funding for this component will increase by US$0.76 million (15 percent) compared to the previous allocation financed solely under the IDA grant, bringing the total allocation to US$5.76 million. This increase will finance additional operating costs to support the AF activities. There are no physical activities planned under the proposed AF, so no environmental impacts are anticipated from the AF project activities. While it is not clear if the AF will result in specific policy changes in the IGAD member states, analytics to be supported under the Enhancing Governance of Early Warning Systems (EWS) section of the AF could potentially lead to policy formulations. These may result in downstream environmental risks and impacts, which will need to be identified during the implementation phase. Additionally, the PIU may hire consultants and other project workers for technical assistance activities, requiring measures to ensure their occupational health and safety. Considering these, the environmental risk of the project is rated as moderate, with the understanding that it can be reassessed during implementation. E. Implementation Institutional and Implementation Arrangements 20. Overall responsibility for the implementation and oversight of the AF, as with the parent project rests with the IGAD’s Agriculture and Environment Division (AED). To implement the original grant, IGAD Secretariat has established a Program Coordination Unit (PCU) within the existing Food Agriculture and Food Security Unit (AFSU) under the AED. IGAD will expand the PCU to include an early warning expert to manage and coordinate the AF investments. During implementation of both the project and the AF, the PCU will work closely with the IGAD Food Security, Nutrition and Resilience Analyses Hub (IFRAH), which successfully delivered the ELRP. In carrying out AF activities, the PCU will be supported by relevant IGAD executive units, such as the IGAD Climate Prediction and Applications Centre (ICPAC). IGAD has a proven track record in managing WB-funded projects and has the capacity to handle fiduciary requirements, including procurement and financial management (FM), as well as experience in meeting the WB’s Environment and Social Safeguards (ESF).
