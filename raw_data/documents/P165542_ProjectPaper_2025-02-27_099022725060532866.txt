PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the Country Directors (CD’s) approval for a Level 2 restructuring of the IDA credit 65450 in the amount of SDR 21.1 million (US$29.16 million equivalent) and grant D5640 in the amount of SDR 15.1 million (US$20.84 million equivalent) for the Strengthening Institutions for Refugee Administration Project from the IDA 18 Regional Sub-Window for Refugees and Host Communities (RSW) which includes a one-year extension, revision to the Performance Based Results (PBC) (previously referred to as Disbursement Linked Indicators or DLI) matrix, revisions to the results framework. This is the first restructuring of the Project which aims to adjust the results so that they are achievable within a shorter timeframe and incorporate the lessons learned so far. 2. The restructuring entails an extension until February 27, 2026, with an end disbursement date of June 27, 2026 as well as the revision of: i) the results framework, ii) DLI matrix (now referred to as Performance Based Conditions), iii) verification protocols, and iv) Monitoring & Evaluation plan. Legal covenants will remain the same. Fiduciary, environmental and social safeguards will remain the same. Implementation and Disbursement arrangements will remain the same. The original Risk ratings will be maintained, and the overall risk rating continues to be High. A. Background and Overview of Current Project Status 3. Strengthening Institutions for Refugee Administration Project (SIRA) (P165542) was approved as part of a package of four operations receiving highly concessional financing through the IDA18 Regional Sub-Window for Refugee and Host Communities (RSW). The other three operations, focused on services and livelihoods for host communities and refugees, are implemented by the provinces of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan 4. The Project Development Objective is to improve organizational and institutional capacity for managing refugees and host communities in Pakistan. Focusing on federal and provincial institutions that manage refugees and host communities, the project primarily benefits government institutions. No funds are directly allocated to refugees as part of this Project. 5. The Project has disbursed US$16.9 million (or 35.15%), of which US$10 million is an advance. The Operations Support Unit (OSU) has been recruited. The first Third Party Verification Agent (TPVA) report verifying the results achieved under component 1 was submitted to the World Bank in January 2024, against which a US$6 million was disbursed against results achieved. The Project consists of two components, a US$40 million results-based component consisting of seven Disbursement Linked Indicators (DLI) (now PBC) and a US$10 million Technical Assistance component. A description of the Project’s component and status by component, is presented below. 6. Component 1: Implementing organizational and institutional reforms for management of refugees and host communities. This component supports a program of activities designed to strengthen institutional and organizational structures for management of refugees and host communities, including strengthening information management and improving performance of selected departments. Progress under this component includes Page 1 The World Bank Strengthening Institutions for Refugee Administration Project (P165542) improvements in resolution of complaints from refugees and host communities (DLI 1) and improvements in knowledge and skills of professional staff of organizations responsible for refugee management (DLI 5), and publications of socio-economic data on refugees (DLI 6). Under this component US$ 6 million, or 15 percent, has been disbursed. 7. Component 2: Technical Assistance for improved management of refugees and host communities. This component supports the specific inputs required to achieve the results on the implementation of the Government policy supported by this Project. The Project supports activities to strengthen the Chief Commissionerate for Afghan Refugees (CCAR) and the Commissionerate for Afghan Refugees (CAR) organization and institutional capacities. Under this component, expenditures have consisted of Project operational support costs. Project Ratings 8. Progress towards achievement of Development Objective and Overall Implementation Progress rating are both rated Moderately Satisfactory. With the completion of this restructuring, the Project will be on track to achieve the Development Objective. 9. The Project has a Category ‘C’ Safeguards rating and Social Safeguards is rated as Satisfactory. The Grievance Redress Mechanism is functioning, as evidenced by the complaints handling information submitted to the Third- Party Verification Agent (TPVA). 10. Fiduciary Management There are no overdue audit reports or any ineligible expenses in respect to the project/implementing entity. The Fiduciary Management rating is Moderately Satisfactory. 11. Procurement Management was rated Moderately Unsatisfactory in the last Implementation Support Mission. Subsequently, several actions have been taken to improve procurement progress. This includes identifying a service provider for the Refugee Learning Program which will be contracted on completion of this restructuring, development of Terms of Reference for the Organizational and Functional review and Legal and Policy Review, collaboration with GIZ on the Terms of Reference for the Complaints Handling Mechanism and the Management Information System. 12. Monitoring and Evaluation is Moderately Satisfactory. Progress reports have been submitted, however, the quality and frequency of reporting could be improved. 13. The Project is in compliance with its Legal Covenants. B. Rationale for Restructuring and Extension Page 2 The World Bank Strengthening Institutions for Refugee Administration Project (P165542) 14. The restructuring is proposed because the Project faced several delays after Board approval and an extension is needed to complete key activities under component 1 and to achieve the PDO. 15. As with the three other operations receiving financing from IDA18 RSW, the Project experienced a delay of approximately 12 months between Board Approval and Effectiveness. It faced further delays in receiving the necessary approval (PC-1) from the Government of Pakistan. Approval of the PC-1 was obtained in March 2023, which effectively marked the start of implementation for the Government. Therefore, there was a two-year delay between the World Bank Board’s approval of the Project and the start of Project activities on the ground. 16. The Project was able to achieve results in FY2023, building on efforts by the government during the period preceding approval of the PC-1. In the first six- months after PC-1 approval, under the guidance of the Chief Commissioner for Afghan Refugees and the Project Director, progress was seen. This included the opening of a designated account, signing of an MoU with the Office of the Auditor General to act as the TPVA and verification of achievement of some of the DLIs, amounting to US$6 million. 17. However, given the significant delay, it was clear during the Mid-Term Review, conducted in June 2023, that a restructuring and extension would be necessary to achieve results before the close of the Project.The operating context was also impacted by the instigation of the first phase of the Illegal Foreigners Repatriation Plan (IFRP) by the Ministry of Interior at the end of 2023. The Chief Commissioner at the time was instrumental in ensuring the continued protection of Proof of Registration (PoR) and Afghan Citizens Card (ACC) card holders. While the Chief Commissioner's ensured SIRA's legal covenant on protection frameworks was upheld, this unfortunately diverted their attention from actively implementing the Project. In parallel, positive steps were taken, including the one-year extension until June 2025 of the PoR cards for registered refugees and development in the international dialogue, bolstered by the visit of the UN High Commissioner for Refugees in the second half of 2024. 18. A key lesson learned from the IFRP was the importance of the protection role in the management of refugees of the Commissionerate for Afghan Refugees, i.e. the ability of the CCAR and CAR to advocate for, raise awareness and ensure the implementation of the policy framework governing right of refugees in Pakistan. The IFRP also highlighted the need to increase capacities to handle grievances, the need to conduct more outreach and awareness amongst refugees, host communities, to ensure better inter-governmental coordination and find long- term solutions for hosting refugees in Pakistan. Furthermore, it was recognized that the Ministry of States and Frontier Regions (SAFRON) has a limited mandate to implement visa roll out. 19. This restructuring will bring these important lessons into the design of SIRA and ensure that the Project is supporting the Commissionerate to evolve to better match the current context of supporting refugee and the host communities in Pakistan. Specifically, the protection capacities of CCAR and CAR will be increased through the restructuring DLI 7 and through the addition of a new DLI focused on refugee protection. Page 3 The World Bank Strengthening Institutions for Refugee Administration Project (P165542) 20. Considering these challenges, the SIRA Project remains in a position to contribute to supporting the improvement of the administration of Afghan refugees in Pakistan, complementing the roles of other government agencies and partners. The restructuring of SIRA, in response to the request received from the Ministry of Economic Affairs in August 2024, reconfirmed in writing by the Ministry of Economic Affairs in February 2025 and as recommended by the Project Interministerial Steering Committee in February 2025, provides the opportunity to align the Project with the constraints and opportunities of the policy dialogue, as well as adjust the project design to the capacity constraints of the client (note that this is the first PSDP project to be implemented by SAFRON – a ministry set up originally to respond to a humanitarian crisis), so that the Project can deliver tangible results within the new timeframe. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES Revisions to DLI matrix 21. DLI related to visa policy and implementation of visa policies have been restructured as this is beyond the control of the Ministry of State and Frontier Regions (SAFRON). A new DLI related to strengthen existing capacities to provide protection services has been included which will support the establishment of formal Protection Units in CAR and CCAR. The revised DLI and results are achievable in the revised timeframe and the Project will be better integrated into the ongoing activities of other key partners such as United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) and GIZ. Successful achievement of project results and DLRs will depend on close collaboration and clear delineation of scope between SIRA, and the programs supported by UNHCR and GIZ. Clarity on the complementarity between these different resources will be critical to ensure efficient use of funds for supporting refugees in Pakistan. Further details can be found in Table 1.
