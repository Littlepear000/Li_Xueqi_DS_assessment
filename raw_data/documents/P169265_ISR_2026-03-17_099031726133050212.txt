KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions Mar 17, 2026 Page 1 of 21 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa (P169265) Burkina Faso: Progress toward this objective remains Satisfactory. The project has fully achieved its development objectives and intermediate results, with sustained improvements in the coverage, quality, and accessibility of official statistics. Since the last Implementation Status Report, three results stand out. First, national labor market information has improved through completion of the 2025 employment survey, using telephone-based data collection to ensure continuity and cost efficiency while maintaining data quality. Second, living standards and business statistics have expanded through completion of the first wave of the 2025/2026 household survey and the business census, strengthening the evidence base for poverty and private-sector analysis. Third, macroeconomic statistics have advanced, with quarterly national accounts and economic indices now regularly available, supporting more timely monitoring of economic trends. During the most recent mission, the team and authorities agreed to prioritize completion and publication of ongoing surveys and core macroeconomic datasets to consolidate development gains. Implementation has been adapted by expanding remote data-collection methods and strengthening digital data platforms, improving resilience and outreach. Previous challenges related to data availability and institutional coordination have been addressed through adoption of the National Strategy for the Development of Statistics for 2026–2030 and expanded public access to data platforms, reinforcing sustainability beyond project closing. Ghana: Progress toward this objective remains satisfactory, with the project meeting its core development outcomes and intermediate targets and continuing to improve the quality, availability, and use of official statistics. Since the last Implementation Status Report, three key results stand out. First, major population-based and sectoral surveys and censuses have been successfully implemented, significantly expanding the national data base used for policy formulation and monitoring. Second, data systems have been modernized, improving interoperability and enabling more systematic use of statistics across government institutions. Third, foundational reforms have strengthened the institutional framework, including the operationalization of a National Data Quality Assurance Framework and adoption of a new strategy for statistical development, which together reinforce consistency, credibility, and sustainability of official statistics. During the most recent mission, agreement was reached to refocus implementation on essential, high-impact outcomes to consolidate development gains. Implementation was adapted through a revised work plan that prioritizes advanced and ongoing initiatives aligned with the Project Development Objective. Progress has also been made in addressing earlier constraints to implementation, allowing critical activities to resume and ensuring continued momentum toward stronger coordination, improved data quality, and increased use of statistics in national decision-making. Liberia: Progress toward this objective remains satisfactory, with five of six development outcome indicators achieved and steady advancement toward the remaining target. Since the last Implementation Status Report, three key results are notable. First, economic statistics have been substantially strengthened, with updated gross domestic product estimates aligned with international standards and expanded coverage of business, agricultural, and producer price data, improving the basis for macroeconomic analysis. Second, progress on the Household Income and Expenditure Survey has significantly improved the availability of poverty and welfare data, with data collection nearing completion and positioning the country to meet its remaining statistical capacity target. Third, access to official statistics has improved through the establishment of a national statistical database and expanded online availability of reports and datasets, supporting transparency and public use. During the most recent mission, agreement was reached to prioritize completion, analysis, and dissemination of core survey results to consolidate development gains. Implementation has been adapted by expanding digital data collection and strengthening data quality assurance practices, improving efficiency and consistency. Earlier challenges related to data gaps and institutional capacity have been addressed through adoption of an updated National Strategy for the Development of Statistics and strengthened training programs, reinforcing the sustainability of Liberia’s statistical system beyond the project period. Mar 17, 2026 Page 2 of 21 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa (P169265) Sierra Leone: Progress toward this objective remains Satisfactory, with five of six development outcome indicators fully achieved. The core objective of strengthening the national statistical system has been met, with the Statistical Capacity Index exceeding its target, and results on harmonized data production and use fully achieved. Since the last Implementation Status Report, three key results stand out. First, household-based statistics have been significantly strengthened, with nationwide data collection completed and positioning the country to deliver up-to-date poverty and welfare evidence. Second, economic statistics have improved through adoption of the 2008 System of National Accounts and rebasing of gross domestic product, enhancing accuracy and international comparability. Third, price statistics have been fully modernized, with national coverage and systematic use of digital data collection, supporting more timely inflation analysis. During the most recent mission, agreement was reached to prioritize analysis, publication, and dissemination of completed datasets to maximize their policy value. Implementation has been adapted by expanding digital tools and standardized methods across surveys, improving efficiency and consistency. Previous challenges related to data gaps, fragmentation, and limited access have been addressed through strengthened administrative data systems and a redesigned national statistics website, making official data widely accessible and reinforcing sustainability of results beyond the project period. Cabo Verde: Progress toward this objective remains Moderately Satisfactory, with most intended results achieved despite uneven implementation across components. Since the last Implementation Status Report, several important outcomes have been delivered. First, the availability of core social and economic data has expanded, including completion of the Demographic and Health Survey, with preliminary results now informing health and social sector analysis. Second, agricultural and food security information has improved through completion of the first round of integrated agricultural surveys and the regular production of nutrition, vulnerability, and food insecurity surveys. Third, economic data coverage has been strengthened through completion of the enterprise census and progress on municipal information systems, with two modules already operational. During the most recent mission, the team and authorities agreed to focus remaining efforts on consolidating results and improving data usability, particularly by accelerating public access to anonymized microdata. Implementation has been adapted by providing closer technical support to survey preparation and by prioritizing digital systems that improve data sharing across institutions. Progress has also been made in addressing earlier challenges related to data accessibility. Seven survey microdata sets are now publicly posted on the National Statistics Institute website, marking an important step toward greater transparency and data use, even as further simplification of access procedures remains a priority. Cote d'Ivoire: Progress toward this objective remains satisfactory, with clear gains in statistical capacity and data availability. Since the last Implementation Status Report, three key development results stand out. First, the country’s Statistical Capacity Index increased to 76.2 out of 80, surpassing the end-project target and reflecting stronger institutional performance, improved dissemination practices, and closer alignment with international standards. Second, coverage of economic data has expanded significantly through completion of the Business Census, which now provides comprehensive information on more than 1.4 million enterprises and establishments. Third, user satisfaction with official statistics continued to rise, increasing by nearly nine percentage points in 2025, indicating greater relevance and use of data. During the most recent mission, agreement was reached to prioritize faster publication and dissemination of collected data to maximize its operational value. Implementation has been adapted by focusing resources on high-impact outputs, including short-term economic indicators and harmonized household and agricultural statistics. Progress has been made in addressing earlier challenges related to data timeliness, with average publication delays reduced over the past year. Preparations for adherence to the International Monetary Fund’s Special Data Dissemination Standard are well advanced, further strengthening transparency and credibility of Côte d’Ivoire’s statistical system. Mar 17, 2026 Page 3 of 21 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa (P169265) Togo: Progress toward this objective remains Satisfactory, with all Project Development Objective and intermediate indicators fully achieved. The Statistical Capacity Indicator reached the maximum score of 80, exceeding the end-project target and reflecting substantial system-wide improvements. Since the last Implementation Status Report, three development results are particularly noteworthy. First, the successful completion of the Population and Housing Census and the National Agricultural Census has significantly expanded the availability of core demographic and agricultural statistics, strengthening the evidence base for national planning. Second, macroeconomic and price statistics remain robust, with regular publication of national accounts, quarterly indicators, and harmonized consumer price indices, supporting timely monitoring of economic conditions. Third, the business census has produced comprehensive databases covering formal, informal, and nonprofit entities, improving understanding of the private sector. During the most recent mission, agreement was reached to prioritize timely implementation of the third Household Living Conditions Survey and to maintain momentum on rebasing national accounts to further modernize macroeconomic indicators. Implementation has been adapted by sequencing remaining surveys and focusing on consolidation and dissemination of completed datasets. Previous challenges related to coverage and harmonization have been effectively addressed, resulting in stronger coordination across statistical domains and sustained improvements in data quality, accessibility, and use. African Union: Progress toward this objective for the African Union component remains Satisfactory, with results largely achieved and consolidated. Since the last Implementation Status Report, three key development results stand out. First, implementation of the revised Strategy for the Harmonization of Statistics in Africa has advanced significantly, improving continental coordination, systematic monitoring of country and regional progress, and evidence-based follow-up on recommendations from the Committee of Directors General of National Statistical Offices. Second, capacity in modern data standards and dissemination has been strengthened through regional training on statistical data exchange and open data platforms, increasing readiness for harmonized data sharing across Africa. Third, collaboration among African Union institutions, Regional Economic Communities, and international partners has deepened, supporting greater alignment of methodologies and joint capacity-building efforts. During the most recent mission, agreement was reached to focus remaining efforts on completing and consolidating results, including final monitoring reports and sustainability actions. Implementation has been adapted by prioritizing knowledge transfer, standardized monitoring tools, and a phased roadmap for modern data dissemination. Previous challenges related to coordination and implementation capacity have been addressed, resulting in stronger institutional performance and a more transparent, harmonized continental statistical system. ECOWAS: Progress toward this objective remains Satisfactory, with strong delivery of regional public goods and sustained institutional performance. Since the last Implementation Status Report, three key development results stand out. First, the regional methodological foundation for harmonized statistics has been significantly strengthened through the validation of common frameworks for consumer prices, national accounts, government finance, public debt, and monetary statistics, supporting greater consistency across countries. Second, regional capacity to apply international statistical standards has improved, reinforcing the production of comparable and credible data aligned with the System of National Accounts 2008. Third, the use of harmonized statistics at the regional level has expanded, supporting regional surveillance, economic integration, and evidence-based policy dialogue. Implementation has been adapted through streamlined internal processes and strengthened staffing within the Directorate of Research and Statistics, helping maintain momentum at an advanced stage of the project. Previous challenges related to uneven methodological practices and coordination have been effectively addressed, resulting in a more coherent regional statistical system and sustained progress toward long-term harmonization objectives. 5.
