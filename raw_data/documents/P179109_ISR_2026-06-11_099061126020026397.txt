KEY ISSUES & STATUS Jun 11, 2026 Official Use Only Page 1 of 8 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Comoros Interisland Connectivity Project SOP2 (P179109) 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions 1. The project’s overall implementation remains consistent with the original project development objective. In collaboration with other donors (AfDB, IsDB, and AFD), the project aims at improving climate resilience, connectivity and safety of maritime transport among the islands in Comoros. To achieve this objective, the projects (SOP1 and SOP2) constitute three main components: (i) rehabilitation of Port Boingoma in Moheli, the most isolated island in Comoros, (ii) installation of minor infrastructure at the secondary ports in Moheli and Anjouan with complementary support to renew interisland passenger boats, and (iii) technical assistance to strengthen institutional capacity to manage port operations, and safety and other maritime affaires. 2. The project implementation has been gaining momentum but continues being impacted by various delays. The project implementation has progressed, notably following the borrow pit excavation at Domoni. While a rock crusher site was established at Bandar Eslam, access roads to Domoni and a temporary port at Hoani beach were constructed. However, the project implementation is currently impacted by delayed delivery of essential equipment, such as molds and barges, due to disrupted international maritime transportation. The project is looking for alternative shipping arrangements and suppliers and examining temporary implementation measures to resume construction works quickly. The port rehabilitation is currently expected to be completed by August 2027. Key prerequisites include timely delivery of equipment, efficient administrative import procedures, and a sufficient and reliable fuel supply. 3. The secondary port development and the passenger boat program are also delayed due to multiple revisions of detailed design (APD), which indicates significant potential cost overruns for developing secondary ports at Southern Grande Comore and Vassy, Anjouan. To contain costs, the project is considering to take a phased approach, focusing on key components, such as jetties and landside infrastructure. The final scope will be determined at a later stage, based on available resources. 5.
