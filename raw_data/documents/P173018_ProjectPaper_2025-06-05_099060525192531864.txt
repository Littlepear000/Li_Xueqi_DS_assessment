PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. This restructuring paper seeks the World Bank (WB) Division Director’s approval for a Level II Restructuring of the Solomon Islands Sustainable Mining Development Technical Assistance Project (SMDTAP, P173018). The purpose of the restructuring is to: (i) Update the Project Description to reflect changes in the project scope, with updated activities in Annex 2; (ii) Revise the allocation for selected activities outlined in the original project design, including Component 1: Completion of the Regulatory Framework ( increase the original budget amount from US$0.6 million to US$2.19 million), Component 2: Strengthening the Capacity of Regulatory Institutions (reallocate the US$1.84 million to component 1 & 3 from original budget of US$ 3 million ), and Component 3: Project Management (increase the original budget amount from US$1.4 million to US$1.65 million ); (iii) Revise the Project Closing Date, bringing it forward from the current date of March 31, 2027 to June 30, 2026; and (vi) Revise the targets for the intermediate level indicators (all other target values remain unchanged). Project Background 2. The SMDTAP is a 3.6 million SDR (US$5 million) grant to the Solomon Islands Government (SIG) that was approved on November 30, 2021 and became effective on January 25, 2022. The project development objective (PDO) is to strengthen the capacity of regulatory institutions to implement revised mining and related laws consistently, transparently, and effectively. The project has three components: (1) Completion of the Regulatory Framework; (2) Strengthening Capacity of Regulatory Institutions; and (3) Project Management. Project Status 3. As of March 2025, the overall implementation progress is assessed as Moderately Satisfactory. Further details on the status of each component are provided below. 4. Component 1: Completion of the Regulatory Framework (Unsatisfactory). This component has faced significant delays primarily due to postponements within the government in finalizing and approving the new Minerals Resources Bill which is still awaiting parliamentary approval. Although the Bill has progressed through several stages, parliamentary approval is not expected until the second half of 2025 to allow the Bills and Legislation Committee to conduct a public hearing prior. All other project activities for the Mineral Resources Bill within the project scope have been completed. The contract for the international consultant for the external review of the Strategic Environmental and Social Assessment (SESA) for the Solomon Islands mining sector is still under negotiation, which is delaying the finalization of the SESA report. 5. Component 2: Strengthening the Capacity of Regulatory Institutions (Satisfactory). This component has been successfully implemented, surpassing targets in the processing time for license applications. The launch and implementation of the Mining Cadaster Administration System (MCAS) and online repository has significantly improved data management and enhanced the efficiency and effectiveness of the implementing agency, the Ministry of Mines, Energy, and Rural Electrification (MMERE). For example, the average processing time for new exploration license applications has been reduced from three months to one month, surpassing the project’s target of two months. The success of this component is also attributed to the successful implementation of capacity-building activities for the Mines Division within MMERE and significant progress made in geodata management, including the integration of data into MCAS, staff training, and drone surveys, Official Use Only The World Bank Solomon Islands Sustainable Mining Development Technical Assistance Project (P173018) and on community engagement initiatives. Implementation of the Extractive Industry Transparency Initiative (EITI)1 is underway with a multi-stakeholder consultation already completed. The design of a core shed which will be used for storing geological and mining samples is nearing completion. The MMERE Gender and Social Inclusion Strategy (GESI) and communication strategy are still pending for final cabinet approval. 6. Component 3: Project Management (Moderately Satisfactory). In addressing the potential budget overrun in Component 3 due to increased administration costs, the restructure paper proposes a comprehensive plan to enhance efficiency and cost-effectiveness through a Project Management Unit (PMU) restructure. The paper outlines the current budgetary challenges, the causes of the overrun, and the impact on project milestones. It suggests specific organizational and process changes within the PMU to reduce costs and improve contract management, supported by a detailed cost-benefit analysis and budget reallocation strategy. An implementation plan with clear timelines (including bringing the closing date of the project forward), milestones, and assigned responsibilities are provided below, alongside risk management strategies to mitigate potential challenges. 7. The Financial Management rating is Moderately Unsatisfactory. Although all Interim Financial Reports (IFRs) to the semester ending December 31, 2024, have been submitted and accepted, all were delayed. The first audit of the project, covering the 19 months ending December 31, 2023, was due on June 30, 2024. This audit report was submitted on May 1, 2025, and the audit opinion is qualified. The auditors’ management letter reported weaknesses on internal controls and non-compliance with SIG procurement rules, for which MMERE has been requested to submit to the Bank an action plan to address these issues. A review of selected transactions has been conducted and MMERE has been recommended to submit relevant supporting documents addressing Bank’s observations. As of April 2025, the Project has disbursed US$3.04 million (63 percent) of the total financing of US$5 million. Currently, the net available budget after the forecasted amounts till June 2026 is US$ 0.6million. Please refer to the current budget details below. Procurement is rated Moderately Satisfactory. The project has faced significant challenges in hiring and retaining qualified consultants, resulting in slow implementation. The remaining procurement activities under the project are not complex but given the slow procurement progress, the project procurement risk is assessed as Substantial. This rating will be reviewed during the next assessment at the next mission and may be adjusted to Moderate subject to demonstrate progress. 9. The Environmental and Social (E&S) risk management rating is Moderately Unsatisfactory. The project is progressing, but ES risk management is rated moderately unsatisfactory due to the PMU experiencing frequent staff ES turnover and delays in filling the positions, the decision to cancel the construction of the core shed, and the delay in engaging the international consultant for the SESA. The project will proceed with the design of the core shed, however in the proposed restructuring, the construction of the core shed will be cancelled. The Environmental and Social Management Plan (ESMP) for the core shed construction and operation is currently in draft form and will need to be updated based on the detailed design. The architectural design, bill of quantity and bidding documents preparation is in progress. The design work still needs to comply with the relevant E&S standards, i.e., energy and water efficiency, provisions for stormwater, domestic and laboratory wastewater drainage and treatment for the latter, climate resilience, and mitigation plans. A draft SESA is in place, but it requires strengthening of the analysis and recommendations aspects. The PMU will proceed with negotiations and a contract agreement with the SESA report revision consultant. This process has been delayed due to the withdrawal of two interested parties at a late stage in the contract negotiations. The PMU has set up the grievance redress mechanism (GRM), and no grievances have been received to date. A. Rationale for Restructuring 10. In a letter to the World Bank dated March 13, 2025, the Solomon Islands Government (SIG) requested a restructuring of the Project. An MTR mission was concluded in November 2024, in which the proposed restructuring was discussed extensively and agreed with SIG. The proposed Level II project restructuring responds to both the request letter and MTR agreements. 11. This restructuring paper highlights key achievements in the mining sector from SMDTAP activities including the Mineral Resources Bill, aimed at leveraging mining investments for economic growth and community benefits, which is awaiting parliament approval. The government is enhancing transparency by re-implementing the EITI Official Use Only The World Bank Solomon Islands Sustainable Mining Development Technical Assistance Project (P173018) which will attract quality investors. Solomon Islands started implementing the EITI in 2012 but withdrew in 2018 due to limited activities in the country’s extractive sector. At that time, there was only one bauxite mining operation in the country. The decision to withdraw came while Solomon Islands was undergoing its second Validation to assess whether it has made progress in implementing the EITI Standard. Government had made known its intention then to rejoin the EITI when key mining operations in the country resumed. Administrative processes have been improved with the online MCAS, enhancing license management and regulatory oversight, while an integrated online system is being developed for better policy decisions and revenue reconciliation. The project has exceeded its royalty collection target, reaching 79% in its third year. A community liaison section has been established with four approved positions. The strategies like the Gender Equality and Inclusion (GESI) and Communication Strategy are pending for Cabinet approval. 12. The achievement of these milestones is favorably changing the landscape on which SMDTAP was designed and is being implemented. The Minerals Resources Bill now before parliament, will bring much needed long-overdue reforms to the country’s mining sector since the current Mines and Minerals Act (1990). The new legislation will require mining companies to commit to development benefits for the host communities, includes stronger enforcement measures and penalties, and has provision to regulate alluvial mining licenses. 13. As such, a project restructuring is proposed by SIG to re-align with the government’s new confidence to pursue a more proactive, longer-term vision for the sector through the development of the Mining Master Plan (MMP). The full implementation of the MMP is expected to transcend the boundaries of the current SMDTAP, hence the request for a restructuring so there can be more strategic focus of technical assistance on the preparation of the MMP. A key component of this preparation is the revision of the Strategic Environmental and Social Assessment (SESA) for the mining sector. The updated SESA will provide a comprehensive framework for integrating environmental, social, and governance considerations across the sector. Concurrently, the Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative (EITI) and a planned macro-level assessment of the Solomon Islands' mining sector will be instrumental in supporting the MMP's effective implementation. The MMP will serve as both (a) a living document of prioritized, sequenced activities, and public sector investments to derive greater socio- economic benefits; and (b) an implementation process through which the government may pivot toward realizing a concrete set of private sector investment opportunities. Currently, the MMP preparation is being aligned with the new Mineral Resources Bill and associated regulations, all other existing laws in the country related to mineral development and supply, value chains and associated infrastructure development, and in collaboration with industry and community stakeholders. Furthermore, the MMP will undergo a comprehensive and inclusive consultation process with all relevant stakeholders, including government entities, industry representatives, civil society, and community members, prior to its submission for Cabinet endorsement. This participatory approach is designed to ensure that the MMP reflects national priorities, local realities, and global standards for responsible resource governance. 14. The development of this MMP strategy document indicates the government’s wishes to prioritize mining as a sector of growth and derive greater socio-economic benefit from Solomon Island’s underlying mineral endowment, and to position Solomon Islands to contribute to the demand for critical minerals necessary for the energy transition. 15. The restructuring proposes to complete project critical activities as initially designed and accelerate the closing date in order to shape a potential new engagement with SIG, meeting the emerging objectives of the government. Selected activities will be dropped and the funds will be used to support preparation of the MMP which includes broad consultation and preparatory activities for its implementation. Funds will also be shifted Official Use Only The World Bank Solomon Islands Sustainable Mining Development Technical Assistance Project (P173018) from underperforming or less relevant components (e.g., certain aspects of Component 1 and 2) to prioritize the preparation and broad consultation process of the MMP, which is considered crucial for long-term sustainable mining sector development. This reallocation ensures resources are directed towards the most impactful areas. 16. The additional resources to be allocated to Component 1 through the restructuring will support the achievement of the PDO by funding critical activities such as the preparation of the Mineral Resources Bill, the SESA, and the MMP. These activities are essential for creating a comprehensive regulatory framework that aligns with the legislative framework. The enhanced regulatory framework will strengthen the capacity of regulatory institutions to implement revised mining and related laws consistently, transparently, and effectively. This strategic focus on high-priority activities ensures that resources are directed towards the most impactful areas, thereby improving project performance and facilitating the achievement of the PDO. 17. Accelerated Project Closure: Restructuring will allow for a faster closure of SMDTAP (from March 31, 2027, to June 30, 2026). This is achieved by reallocating funds from activities no longer aligned with the government's new vision towards the MMP's development. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 18.
