PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Summary 1. This Level II restructuring paper seeks to: (i) extend the project closing date by eight months to facilitate completion of the rural connectivity deployment under Component 2; and (ii) update the Results Framework to align with the new WBG Digital Corporate Scorecard indicators, revise targets for over-achieved indicators, and update baselines based on the latest implementation data. This will be the first restructuring of the project. B. Project status 2. The Niger Smart Villages for Rural Growth and Digital Inclusion Project (P167543), with a total financing envelope of US$100 million, was approved on July 8, 2020 and became effective on October 21, 2020. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to increase access to cellphone and broadband services in rural areas and to bring digital financial services to selected underserved areas. The project is now almost six years into implementation, with a current closing date of October 23, 2026. The Project has demonstrated strong and tangible progress across all components and has delivered significant results on the ground for the people of Niger, supporting inclusive digital transformation and improved access to services for rural populations. 3. As it stands, three of the five PDO-level indicators have already exceeded their targets, while the remaining two are well on track to meet their own targets, subject to an extension of the project closing date. A total of 10 out of 13 intermediate indicators have also met their targets, with the remaining indicators on track to meet their end-of- project target, subject to an extension of the project closing date. Several indicators have already achieved or exceeded their end-of-project targets. For example, the number of health centers, schools, and municipalities previously without broadband internet coverage that have been provided with broadband internet under the project reached 906, fully achieving the target of 906. Similarly, the number of beneficiaries utilizing project-subsidized point- of-sale (POS) terminals reached 918, significantly exceeding the target of 500. The number of agricultural producers and women using digital payments for their activities also reached 918, surpassing the target of 500. These results demonstrate the strong performance of the project in expanding digital connectivity and promoting the adoption of digital financial services among underserved populations. 4. Concrete results recorded to date include significant gains in enhancing the enabling environment, such as the training of 85 public officials and a substantial reduction in the cost of mobile data, while expanding broadband internet access to 1,007 previously unserved villages, contributing to achieving a 90-percent population coverage, while mobilizing private sector investment well beyond initial targets. In parallel, notable progress has been made in promoting digital financial inclusion, with the establishment of 124 digital centers, large-scale digital skills training programs, and the widespread deployment of digital payment infrastructure. The Project has also generated measurable impacts across key sectors, including the provision of broadband connectivity to schools, health centers, and municipalities nationwide. Additional achievements include progress in agricultural digitization and the operationalization of an effective grievance redress mechanism. 6. The disbursement rate has also shown steady improvement over the past few months, increasing from 64.2 percent as of March 3, 2026, to 81.21 percent (equivalent to USD$83.05M) as of June 30, 2026. 7. This solid performance explains the “Satisfactory” ratings for both progress toward the achievement of the Project Development Objective (PDO) and overall Implementation Progress (IP), as reported in the latest Implementation Status and Results (ISR) report (March 2026) and confirmed during a May 2026 implementation support mission. A more detailed update on progress under the Project’s three main components is provided below. Progress per component 8. Component 1 – Enabling Environment (US$4M) Significant progress has been made in strengthening the enabling environment through the delivery of key analytical, regulatory, and capacity-building outputs supporting broadband expansion and digital financial inclusion. The formal adoption and implementation of these deliverables by the Government remain critical to unlocking their full impact. In parallel, recent policy reforms—such as the removal of the international incoming traffic tax and the development of a long-term digital sector strategy—further reinforce the sector framework. Capacity-building initiatives targeting regulators, judiciary, and technical stakeholders continue to support sustainable sector development. 9. Component 2 – Rural Connectivity (US$63M) Overall progress under this component has been substantial. Connectivity of social infrastructure (schools, health centers, municipalities) has been fully achieved and has contributed to improved access to services. The roll-out of the other connectivity investments has been slower than expected: as of May 30, 2026, 49 percent of targeted localities have been connected. This is in part explains by an uneven implementation performance across operators: Zamani Telecom’s strong performance (68 percent completion rate) contrasts with slower-than-expected progress recorded by Niger Telecoms (25 percent). This two-speed progress has necessitated the implementation of corrective measures, including enhanced direct engagement and close oversight of equipment suppliers, to expedite deployment. A detailed Component 2 Action Plan is attached as Annex (Page 20) in a time-bound format, outlining specific actions, responsible parties, and deadlines to address implementation challenges and ensure the timely achievement of the project’s development objectives. 10. Component 3 – Digital Financial Inclusion (US$28M) Key analytical outputs have been completed, including fiscal reform recommendations and risk assessments for digital financial services. Awareness campaigns and consumer protection initiatives have been rolled out nationwide, while The World Bank Niger: Smart Villages for rural growth and digital inclusion (P167543) Page 3 Official Use Only support to digital centers and fintech ecosystems is progressing. The rollout of remaining digital centers is underway, alongside efforts to scale adoption of digital payments and strengthen financial inclusion outcomes. 11. Legal Covenants. All legal covenants under the project are fully complied with. 12. Procurement. During the May 2026 implementation support mission, procurement performance was rated Satisfactory and the risk was Substantial. The procurement activities are carried out in compliance with World Bank requirements and standards and most recommendations from the previous mission have been largely implemented. The Procurement Plan (PP) is regularly updated, and the Systematic Tracking of Exchanges in Procurement (STEP) is effectively utilized by the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) as the primary tool for planning and monitoring procurement activities. 13. The Procurement Risk Assessment and Performance Report dated June 22, 2026 upgraded the project’s Procurement Performance Rating from Moderately Satisfactory (MS) to Satisfactory (S), based on the findings of the implementation support mission conducted in May 2026. The mission confirmed significant progress under the rural connectivity component, including: (i) the completion of broadband connectivity for all targeted social infrastructure, comprising 301 secondary schools and educational institutions, 339 health centers, and 266 municipal offices; and (ii) substantial progress in the deployment of connectivity to rural localities, with implementation rates reaching 26 percent for Niger Telecoms and 68 percent for Zamani Telecom. To accelerate implementation and ensure timely completion of the remaining activities, the Government has approved a strategic adjustment whereby the entirety of the remaining deployment to be carried out by Niger Telecoms—representing 74 percent of the 1,123 localities still outstanding under its scope—will be executed by Huawei, with completion targeted by February 2027. In parallel, Zamani Telecom has submitted a detailed implementation schedule, together with a formal commitment letter, confirming the completion of the remaining 32 percent of the 1,052 localities under its responsibility by February 2027. Following a comprehensive review conducted by the Project Implementation Unit (PIU), and considering the assurances provided by both operators—including the issuance of the required bank guarantees covering advances previously disbursed—the proposed completion timeline is assessed as credible and achievable, taking into account the prevailing operational environment and country context. The PIU will continue to closely monitor implementation progress against the agreed action plans and deployment schedules to ensure the achievement of the February 2027 completion target. 14. Financial Management (FM). Financial management performance is rated Satisfactory, with the financial risk assessed as Moderate. The project is compliant with the World Bank’s FM requirements with the most recent in-depth FM review taking place during the implementation support mission in May 2026. There are no outstanding audit reports or interim financial reports, and no irregularities have been found. The most recent audit covering the period ending on December 30, 2025, was submitted according to the agreed timeline. 15. Environmental and social risk management. Environmental and social systems are functioning adequately, with the Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) fully operational, including a toll-free hotline accessible to beneficiary The World Bank Niger: Smart Villages for rural growth and digital inclusion (P167543) Page 4 Official Use Only communities. Strong collaboration with the National Environmental Assessment Bureau (BNEE) has been observed, particularly in supporting external monitoring of environmental and social compliance. Overall, the project’s environmental and social performance is rated Satisfactory, and the overall project risk remains Moderate. C. Rationale for restructuring 16. This restructuring seeks to: (i) extend by 8 months the closing date of the project (IDA-6688 and IDA-D6560) from October 23, 2026 to June 30, 2027; and (ii) update the Results Framework, including taking into consideration the WBG scorecard digital indicator. The proposed extension is in response to the official request from the Government, communicated to the World Bank in a letter dated June 22, 2026. This will be the first closing date extension of the project. 17. Extension of the project closing date. Over the past couple of years, the project has suffered from the compounded effects of security constraints, delays in the delivery of equipment, and regional logistical disruptions, further exacerbated by the impacts of the conflict in the Middle East, and earlier disbursement suspensions under OP 7.30 (which resulted in approximately one year of suspended disbursements). In that context, and at the current pace of implementation, the two Mobile Network Operators (Niger Telecoms and Zamani) are unlikely to meet the agreed deployment targets by the existing project closing date. To mitigate this risk, the World Bank and the counterpart project teams have put in place a robust action plan (please refer to the action plan in the Annex) that seeks to strengthen monitoring and coordination arrangements, including close and regular engagement with the senior management of the operators and the Ministry in charge of Digital Development. These modalities are expected to help accelerate deployment and better address implementation bottlenecks. However, an 8-month extension of the implementation period would still be necessary to achieve the project outcomes. Such extension is critical to (i) complete remaining rural connectivity deployments under Component 2; (ii) consolidate achieved results; and (iii) ensure a satisfactory project completion. 18. Results framework. Changes are proposed to fully align the Project’s RF with the World Bank’s new corporate scorecard indicators (CRIs); remove redundancies; ensure full attributability of results to the project; and update baseline and targets where necessary to align with the CRI indicators methodology. The replacement of some results indicators with the new corporate results indicators comes in direct response to the introduction of the new World Bank Group Corporate Scorecard, which was endorsed by the Board and systematically rolled out across all lending operations. In this context, the related changes to the RF do not necessitate an explicit request from the Government. The changes have, however, been discussed and agreed with Government counterparts. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 19. The proposed restructuring seeks two main changes: A. Closing date extension The World Bank Niger: Smart Villages for rural growth and digital inclusion (P167543) Page 5 Official Use Only 20. An 8-month extension of the closing date from October 23, 2026 to June 30, 2027 is proposed specifically to complete component 2 activities and consolidate the foundations for sustainable digital transformation. As outlined in the action plan agreed during the May 2026 implementation support mission, the additional implementation period will be used to: (i) accelerate the deployment of rural connectivity infrastructure and complete coverage of the remaining localities under the contracts with Niger Telecoms and Zamani Telecom; (ii) finalize ongoing procurement processes and ensure the full implementation of key strategic investments, including the deployment of the Public Key Infrastructure (PKI), the establishment of the Security Operations Center (SOC), and the modernization of ICT equipment across public administration; (iii) complete the technical acceptance, commissioning, and transfer of project-financed assets to beneficiary institutions; (iv) pursue key regulatory and sector reforms aimed at strengthening the enabling environment for digital development; and (v) complete all fiduciary, environmental and social, and project closing requirements. Disbursement estimates have been revised to be consistent with the extended period of implementation. 21. The scope of the project remains unchanged: all components, activities, and implementation arrangements will continue as originally designed. There are neither modifications to the Environmental Assessment (EA) category, nor new or revised Environment and Social Standards (ESS) triggered because of this extension. B. Changes to the Results Framework 22. The restructuring proposes to (i) replace the current PDO-level indicator on broadband connectivity with the corresponding World Bank corporate results indicator, including its gender and youth disaggregation, and introduce the CRI on digitally enabled services (see Table 1a); (ii) revise the unit of measure of the Intermediate indicator measuring the price of 1GB of Mobile data to better reflect affordability; and the retail price of 0.5 GB per month of pre-paid mobile data, in US$ (Amount(USD)). 23. This restructuring goes beyond a simple update of indicators, incorporating the establishment or revision of baselines, actuals, and targets for newly introduced indicators, particularly CRIs, in full alignment with the CRI and scorecard methodology (see Table 1b). 24. The other modifications to the results framework consist of updating the end date of each indicator to reflect the revised project closing date of June 30, 2027.
