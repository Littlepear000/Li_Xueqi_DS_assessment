KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions Under Component 1, substantial progress has been achieved in advancing regional and municipal development. The Osh Region and City of Osh Development Strategies (2025–2030), prepared with support from the Project[LH1] , are being actively implemented through coordinated investments across industry, infrastructure, agriculture, tourism, and social services. At the regional level, this includes the launch of the brick and coal beneficiation plant in Tash Aryk, commissioning of 120 new enterprises generating 1,474 jobs, and industrial investments of KGS 5.9 billion, alongside KGS 23.0 billion in infrastructure and housing investments supporting construction, roads, irrigation, and public facilities. Tourism attracted over 368,000 visitors, with notable gains in employment (17,453 jobs), fiscal revenues (KGS 6.47 billion), and expanded insurance coverage. At the city level, the strategy supported 27 percent industrial growth (KGS 13.3 billion), agricultural output of KGS 3.22 billion, and KGS 25.9 billion in fixed capital investments, enabling 82 new facilities and rehabilitation of 43 others, as well as housing for over 300 families and approximately US$47.7 million in foreign-financed projects. In parallel, the Digital Online Platform for Regional Development (aidstat.kg) was handed over to the Ministry of Finance on February 5, 2026. The platform strengthens transparency and efficiency in public investment management by providing real-time data on donor and government-funded projects, while also enabling online processing of disbursement requests, reporting, and related administrative procedures. Under Component 2, 26 targeted Productive Partnerships have been established across key agricultural value chains. Twelve partnerships are now fully operational, with additional ones launched in apples and poultry. More than 8,200 farmers have benefited from training, which supportedcapacity building and participation in value chains. At the same time, implementation continues to face constraints related to procurement processes, administrative approvals, and external factors affecting certain value chains, which are being addressed through ongoing coordination and efforts to streamline implementation. Under Component 3, progress continues on key tourism and urban development investments. The Sulaiman-Too Complex and Uzgen Historical and Archaeological Complex are advancing, with exhibition concepts being finalized and major infrastructure elements, such as city bridge, stadium, riverbank improvements, public parks completed. Public- private partnerships have already mobilized over US$23 million in private investment and supported the creation of nearly 300 jobs. Component 4 continues to support rural enterprises and tourism through small grants. To date, 172 businesses have received support, contributing to the creation of over 1,200 jobs. A second round of grants has supported an additional 18 businesses, expected to generate nearly 150 more jobs. While most beneficiaries are utilizing funds effectively to grow their enterprises, ongoing monitoring is in place to address sector-specific challenges and ensure proper use of resources and accountability. 5.
