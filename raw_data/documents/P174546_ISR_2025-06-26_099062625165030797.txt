KEY ISSUES & STATUS Jun 26, 2025 Page 1 of 15 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Emergency Locust Response Project (P174546) 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions Component 1 on desert locust control and surveillance aimed to reduce the growth and spread of the desert locust (DL) upsurge while managing the risks associated with control measures. This component supported efforts to establish and operationalize the control, surveillance, monitoring, and management of locusts. Training was provided to 200 DL control team members on swarm management, integrated pest management, and spraying techniques. They were also supplied with 1000 sets of personal protective equipment; 20 communication devices; 2 drum crushers for safe disposal of pesticide waste; and 150 camping kits for monitoring swarms and/or hoppers, collecting relevant data, and submitting it through the taskforce established to manage the DL invasion. The project also facilitated: (i) surveying 48,988 ha (109%) of the targeted 45,000 ha of locust-affected land area by ministry staff trained under this project; (ii) restoring productivity to 305 ha (159%) of the planned 185 ha of affected pasture rangeland; (iii) improving natural resource management by restoring and rehabilitating 33,574 ha (96%) out of 35,000 ha of affected crop production area. The project planned to spray 500 ha of land to control locusts and other invasive species. There was no significant DL invasion or other pest outbreak, but there was an aerial control operation for quelea birds in Melut County, Upper Nile State. Pesticide storage facilities were constructed in locust frontline states. The performance rating of this component is Satisfactory. Component 2 on livelihood protection and rehabilitation supported investment for farm and livestock households to return to production for improved food security as well as restore the biodiversity of the affected areas. Most of the targets under this component were achieved. The project provided direct income support to 23,799 households (of whom 19,098 are female headed) out of 27,176 households targeted. Additionally, 24,802 households (of whom 15,454 are female headed) out of 22,300 households targeted earned income through Labor Intensive Public Works (LIPW). Furthermore, 162,917 locust-affected farmers (including livestock owners/beekeepers) renewed their agricultural activities, exceeding the target of 135,000. Also, 155,270 farmers (of whom 113,087 are females) out of 50,000 targeted received input packets to grow and produce food. Component 3 on strengthening national preparedness systems focuses on reinforcing the country's capacity and preparedness against future locust and other invasive pest outbreaks. Under this Component, the project supported the Government in developing a locust control plan, establishing 9 desert locust control committees (DLCC) in each participating county, building a national entomology lab, and four pesticide storage facilities. It further supported the ministry in developing and operationalizing a locust monitoring system. A radio room linked to the 9 DLCCs was built at the ministry to provide real-time identification, communication, and response to any invasive species. The project built the capacity of 16 MAFS staff in entomology applied research at the International Center of Insect Physiology and Ecology (ICIPE) and trained 45 MAFS staff on pest surveillance, biocontrol, pest risk analysis, pest modelling, and policy formulation. This has facilitated the Government's review, approval, and validation of the plant protection policy. The component performance rating is Satisfactory. Component 4 on project management monitoring and communication focuses on project management and communication to ensure timely implementation and reporting of the project activities in close collaboration Jun 26, 2025 Page 2 of 15 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Emergency Locust Response Project (P174546) with the UN implementing partners (IP). The Single Project Implementation Unit (SPCU) that managed and supervised the two output agreements also ensured that all key environmental and social assessment documents (ESMF, LMP, IPMP, WMP, SA, SMP, GRM) were produced, disclosed, and operationalized. A GRM management information system was developed and put into operation with 324 GRM committees formed comprising 1,047 females, 1,165 males, and 63 persons with disabilities. A total of 3,179 grievances were received and resolved. The component performance rating is Satisfactory. The project has been implemented by MAFS through SPCU that was also implementing two other WB-funded and non-WB-funded projects. The Third-Party Monitoring Agent (TPMA) was recruited to conduct field verification of activities on the ground, and the first report was produced on October 15, 2024. The second report was under review at the time of the mission and will contribute to the project implementation and completion report. Due to the appreciation of the US dollar against the Special Drawing Rights (XDR) during the life of the project, the project managed within an exchange loss of US$3.2 million, which was slightly offset by US$0.75 million in interest earned from the advances to the two UN implementing partners (UNOPS and FAO). The project management rating is Satisfactory. 4.2 MPA Program Development Objective Original MPA Program Development Objective (Approved as part of (Seq. ) package on ) Current MPA Program Development Objective (Approved as part of (Seq. ) package on ) 4.3 Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA) Status (Public Disclosure) Emergency Locust Response Program (ELRP) is a regional initiative under the multi-phase programmatic approach (MPA). The program provides funding for three main components: Component 1 (Surveillance and Control Measures) addresses the immediate threat posed by the locust desert (DL) upsurge; Component 2 (Livelihoods Protection and Rehabilitation) safeguards food security of affected households and helps restore the productivity of farmers and livestock herders in the aftermath of a DL invasion; and Component 3 (Coordination and Early Warning Preparedness) aims to strengthen national systems and promote regional cooperation to better address future threats. The ELRP-MPA, with its Phase 1 first-moving countries, was approved by the World Bank's Board of Executive Directors on May 20, 2020, with an overall IDA financing envelope of US$500 million equivalent. Through three phases, the ELRP MPA has committed US$375.7 million equivalent to five countries and one regional organization. Phase 1 has committed US$207 million equivalent in grants and credits to Djibouti, Ethiopia and Kenya. Phase 1 is currently 71 percent disbursed. Djibouti Project was successfully completed on September 25, 2023 with 100 percent disbursement of the allocated funds of XDR 4.4 million equivalent (US$5.84 million). Ethiopia Project: A minor Jun 26, 2025 Page 3 of 15 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Emergency Locust Response Project (P174546) restructuring was completed in December 2021. An AF of US$60 million equivalent to Ethiopia was approved in June 2022. The Ethiopia project financing, therefore, arose to US$123 million. The Ethiopia project is scheduled to close on December 31, 2026. Kenya Project: An additional financing (AF) of US$35 million equivalent was approved in March 2022. It increased the Kenya project financing to US$78 million. The Kenya project is scheduled to close on December 31, 2024. Phase 2 was approved on June 29, 2020, providing a US$40 million equivalent grant to Somalia. An AF of US$75 million was approved in June 2021. The phase 2 is 100 percent disbursed. The project was successfully closed on March 31, 2024. Phase 3 was approved in June 2021, with a US$50.7 million grant to South Sudan and a US$3 million grant to the Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD). The phase is 80 percent disbursed. The project closing date has been extended to June 30, 2025. The desert locust upsurge was declared over as of March 2022, and ELRP contributed significantly to swarm surveillance by financing operations on over 10 million hectares of land. It also financed locust control (ground and aerial) on over 530,000 hectares across Djibouti, Ethiopia and Kenya. Investments include training over 700 frontline staff on desert locust management and providing awareness raising activities directly with over 40,000 community members. ELRP has financed, inter alia, the purchase of over 24,000 pieces of personal protective equipment, ultra-low volume spraying equipment for back-pack and vehicle-based control teams, the lease of six airplanes, the purchase of drones and one spray plane, and the lease of helicopters. Before the ELRP was approved, the locust affected area already had over 22 million acutely food insecure people, so the project was designed to positively impact food security in two ways: (i) stop the DL invasion as quickly as possible; and (ii) protect food security by providing safety net support to the most vulnerable households and support them to return to farming and livestock rearing as soon as possible. ELRP direct support for food security started during the control phase and continues with some countries scaling up to meet the increased challenges presented by the confluence of COVID-19, desert locust, inflation, natural disasters, conflict drivers and shortages and price hike due to the Ukraine-Russian war. The project has targeted 1.6 million vulnerable households with direct income. Investments in national preparedness are underway and include investments in technology, training, and strategic partnerships to improve the ability of the countries to monitor, forecast, and provide early warning of a desert locust threat. The ELRP countries are already part of the FAO/Desert Locust Information System (DLIS) and are investing in the survey/data information collection tools to fully engage in ongoing surveillance. Much of the training and technology can be applied to surveillance against other transboundary pests, such as fall armyworm, quelea bird, and other invasive pests. The MPA supported transboundary pest platform managed by IGAD including two working groups—one on capacity building, and one on early warning systems are performing well. A forum for national focal points from the member states is in place to improve data collection and analysis by the member states and partners to identify any data gaps; upgrade the technologies used in data collection; establish data analysis and information developed from the collected data and supporting data; and a data sharing framework for an effective regional early warning system. IGAD Ministerial Meeting on August 9, 2023 endorsed the ELRP-IGAD prepared Policy for Sustainable Management of Desert Locust and other Transboundary Pests for implementation by the member countries. In Nov 2023, an IGAD organized regional event achieved the following: (i) Djibouti, Ethiopia and Somalia singed a MOU for Cross-boundary Pests Management; (ii) a harmonized framework for the Jun 26, 2025 Page 4 of 15 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Emergency Locust Response Project (P174546) revision and development of National Pest Management Plan; (iii) A committee formulated to guide the implementation of the articles of the MoU; and an agreed plan for the member countries to adopt their existing pest management plans to the agreed guidelines. Recently, Kenya has prepared a national pest management plan. Guidelines for the use of drones in agriculture and transboundary pest management are under preparation. 5.
