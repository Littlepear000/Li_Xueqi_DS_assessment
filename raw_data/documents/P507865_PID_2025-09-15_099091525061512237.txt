Introduction and Context Country Context 1. The education sector in India has undergone significant transformations over the years. This transformation has been marked by several major initiatives and reforms aimed at improving access, equity, and learning outcomes. The National Education Policy, 2020, has been a cornerstone in this transformation, introducing a new pedagogical structure, emphasizing foundational literacy and numeracy, and promoting holistic development of students. The focus on teacher professional development, digital learning, and school to work transition has further contributed to elevating the quality of education, ensuring that students are better prepared for the demands of the 21st century. Sectoral and Institutional Context 2. Assam had 31.2 million people in 2011 census constituting around 2.2 percent of the total Indian population. Scheduled Caste (SC) population of 2.2 million makes up 7.15 percent of the state’s population; and Scheduled Tribes (ST) population of 3.9 million makes up 12.4 percent of the state's total population. At 85.9 percent, the share of rural population in the state is one of the highest in the country (65 percent for India). As per National Institution for Transforming India (NITI) Aayog report on Multi-dimensional Poverty (2023), the poverty head count ratio has declined in Assam remarkably from 37 percent in 2013-14 to 14.5 percent in 2022-23, but the state still has one of the highest rates of multi-dimensional poverty in India. 3. School enrollment rates in Assam have improved in the last decade. However, learning levels in Assam remain low. As per the National Achievement Survey (NAS) 2021, 58 percent of grade 3 (age 8) and 55 percent of grade 5 (age 10) Page 2 The World Bank Assam: School Education and Adolescent Wellbeing Project (ASAP) (P507865) students do not meet minimum grade level proficiency in language (Assamese). By Grade 8 (age 14), this statistic worsens to 74 percent. Several factors explain the low learning levels, including student’s low school readiness, poor quality and delivery of Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE) service, and limited availability of trained teachers, among others. 4. The school network in Assam is characterized by a low ratio of secondary and senior secondary schools to primary schools (0.21 compared to India average of 0.26). As per UDISE 2023-24, Assam has one of the highest student dropout rates in primary (6.2 percent, India – 1.9 percent), upper primary (8.2 percent, India – 5.2 percent) and secondary level (25.1 percent, India – 15.1 percent). The fragmented school network in the state due to long distances between primary/elementary (grades 1 to 8) and secondary is a prime factor for the high dropout rates. A study conducted by SCERT Assam on government schools highlighted pressure to earn livelihood, early marriage, lower learning levels, and lack of structured counseling as the some of the other major factors contributing to high dropouts. 5. Assam’s adolescent population in the age group 10-19 years, comprising close to 21 percent (around 6.5 million, Census 2011) of the state population, faces several constraints. High dropout rates, early marriage, low employment and high inactivity rates, mental health issues and substance abuse are some of the common challenges. These challenges hamper adolescent’s academic progression or workforce readiness. Age-specific enrolment rate drops from 81.4 percent for age group 14-15 years to 59.4 percent for age group 16-17 years in Assam, much steeper than India average from 77 percent to 75.5 percent. This sharp drop in school enrolment without a clear alternate path leads to high proportion of not-in-education-employment-or-training (NEET) (nearly 27 percent of adolescents/youth - 12 percent males; 44 percent _For Official Use Only_ females - in the age-group 18-23 years) and high youth unemployment rate (14.1 percent for age-group 15-29 years vis- à-vis 10.2 percent India average). As a result, higher education GER in the state has been low (16.9 percent vis-à-vis 28.4 percent India average) and falling (from 18 percent in 2018-19 to 16.9 percent in 2021-22). 6. The state is undertaking several measures to improve the quality of education and adolescent wellbeing in the state including training of Anganwadi Workers (AWWs), upgrading Anganwadi Centers (AWCs), implementation of statewide student learning assessment system for grades 1 to 9, upgrading elementary schools to add secondary and senior secondary grades, implementation of a Management Information System (MIS) for monitoring academic and administrative date from schools to improve decision making. Despite ongoing initiatives, several systemic challenges continue to constrain the effectiveness and impact of these reforms and initiatives. Many more AWCs and elementary schools need upgrading, training of AWWs and teachers needs to increase coverage, data driven decision making requires more impetus, teacher training and development of teaching-learning materials need to be aligned with global evidence, and adolescents need more clear and supportive pathways to transition from school to higher education and work. Project Description 8. The project aims to provide a continuum of support through different stages of human capital development by improving ECCE and school readiness, equipping school-age children with foundational skills, improving access to and quality of secondary education, and preparing adolescents for quality jobs and livelihood opportunities. The primary project beneficiaries include children in the age group of 3 to 10 years enrolled in all government-funded AWCs and in pre-primary and primary grades in government schools; and adolescents in the age group of 10 to 19 years enrolled in government-managed upper primary, secondary, and senior secondary schools, or out-of-school. Additionally, teachers teaching at primary, upper-primary, secondary, and senior secondary grades in government schools as well as the AWWs across the state will benefit from teacher training programs under the project. The project is proposed as an Investment Project Financing (IPF) operation over a five-year period. It adopts a multi-sectoral approach to expand and strengthen the delivery of services required to improve education and adolescent outcomes through four components. 9. Component 1: Improving Foundational Learning Outcomes: This component is aimed at improving school readiness and reducing the disparities in learning outcomes in the state by focusing on the delivery and quality of ECCE services and improving foundational literacy in primary grades. The focus will be on improving the quality and delivery of ECCE in all AWCs and pre-primary schools through upgradation of AWC infrastructure in select locations, development of integrated ECCE curriculum for children, development of training and learning materials and delivery of ECCE training to AWWs and pre-primary teachers, interventions to improve early reading skills among children and the development of a _For Official Use Only_ reliable digital classroom assessment system, among others. Targeted information and education campaigns for parents and communities will be conducted for their engagement and monitoring. 10. Component 2: Strengthening the School System Capabilities: This component is aimed at improving students’ transition from elementary to secondary through development of a state-wide strategy for school network improvement, upgradation of schools to secondary and senior secondary, and a data driven school management system. The project will support infrastructure upgradation of select elementary and secondary schools to offer secondary and senior secondary grades. The education management information system (EMIS) of the state will be improved in alignment with the Vidya Samiksha Kendra (VSK) guidelines to improve the school management and data-driven policy decisions. 11. Component 3: Improving wellbeing, education and economic pathways for adolescents: This component supports adolescents (in the age group 10-19) prepare for the transition from school to work by imparting life skills education to in-school and out-of-school adolescents, providing academic and career guidance, and ensuring educational and economic pathways for out-of-school adolescents through interventions including Special Training Centers (STCs), Vocational Training cum Rehabilitation Centers (VTRCs), and other community organizations. 12. Component 4: Institutional Capacity Building and Project Management: This component will focus on strengthening the institutional capacity of the state for improved coordination, governance and sustainability of interventions. It will also support day-to-day operations and management of the project and strengthen the monitoring and reporting mechanism, information and communication and stakeholder engagement among others. 13. Gender: The project will address gender gaps in educational and economic pathways through (i) safe and inclusive learning environments that can help address infrastructural barriers and risks of school related gender-based violence, (ii) safe spaces for peer support and life skills education in AWWs and community spaces; (iii) parental and community outreach models, and (iv) academic and career counselling to support girls’ aspirations and further education/ economic empowerment pathways, especially in STEM fields. Citizen Engagement: The project includes beneficiary-centric and citizen engagement actions, including stakeholder consultations during preparation and implementation, outreach and capacity building activities for community institutions and teachers, enhanced transparency and accountability through improved data management system, provision for collecting beneficiary feedback from students, teachers, parents and communities, as well as grievance redressal mechanisms. A Stakeholder Engagement Plan (SEP) will aim to establish a systematic approach to identify and engage stakeholders, ensuring their views are integrated into project design and implementation. Key stakeholders include children, families, teachers, school personnel, NGOs, and government bodies. The SEP will outline methods for effective engagement, such as public consultations, focus group discussions, and community meetings, ensuring inclusivity and accessibility. It will also include a grievance mechanism to address concerns and feedback from stakeholders. 15. Climate co-benefits: The project will significantly contribute to climate co-benefits by enhancing the resilience of Assam's education infrastructure and promoting sustainable practices. It will strengthen the institutional and technical capacity of the state to mainstream responsiveness of the education sector to growing climate and environmental shocks/disasters. Any infrastructure/facilities enhancement under the project will embed green, climate and disaster resilient approaches in the planning and construction process. Additionally, the project will sensitize and build capacity of teachers, administrators and communities on adopting energy efficiency measures, emergency preparedness and usage of technology for education continuity in cases of unplanned school closures owing to climate shocks. _For Official Use Only_ 16. Environmental and Social: The project does not anticipate any land acquisition, land restrictions, diversion of forest land, or adverse impacts on natural habitats on account of proposed interventions. The proposed civil works/infrastructure upgrade will use land and buildings within the existing school/AWC campus for upgrading works. Relevant aspects of the Bank’s Environmental and Social Standards along with national/state (Borrower) systems have been assessed and required mitigation measures have been reflected in the Environment and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP). In addition, a project level Environment and Social Management Plan (ESMP) covering all key environmental, social, health and safety requirements for the project have been prepared. It will be adapted/adjusted for site/context specific requirements, particularly while preparing Detailed Project Reports/Designs and framing bidding documents for civil works. The Samagra Shiksha Axom (SSA), under the Department of School Education (DoSE), Government of Assam (GoA) will have overall responsibility to implement the project along with the Department of Women and Child Developmment (DWCD), and the Public Works Department (PWD). A PMU set up under the SSA, with staff, including consultants assigned under the DWCD and PWD will be responsible for the day-to-day implementation of the project. The Mission Director, Samagra Shiksha, will head the PMU as the Project Director, while Director, DWCD and Commissioner/Chief Engineer, PWD will serve as the Additional Project Directors (APDs). A Project Implementation Plan PIP will be adopted by the PMU to provide clear definitions of rules and guidelines on project implementation, monitoring and supervision and the roles and responsibilities of institutions/ personnel at all levels.
