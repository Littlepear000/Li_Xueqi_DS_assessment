PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Introduction 1. This restructuring paper seeks the approval of the Regional Vice-President for a Level Two restructuring of the Dominican Republic Resilient Agriculture and Integrated Water Resources Management Project (P163260). The proposed restructuring comprises: (i) a 15-month extension of the Loan Closing Date, from October 2, 2026, to January 2, 2028 (a cumulative extension of 42 months beyond the original closing date); (ii) an update of the disbursement estimates; (iii) an update of the implementation schedule; and (iv) an update of the results framework to reflect the proposed closing date and add new WBG Corporate Scorecard indicators. The proposed restructuring will not imply changes in the PDO, project components, financial management (FM) arrangements, procurement arrangements, project risks, environmental and social category, or the loan amount. This third restructuring responds to the Borrower’s request (Letter MH-2025-005190, dated February 19, 2025). 2. The Project was approved by the Board of Executive Directors of the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) on December 13, 2018, with a Loan Agreement signed on January 28, 2020, for an amount of US$80 million. The signing of Loan Agreement was delayed for 13 months due to budget constraints. The subsequent ratification was stalled due to the COVID-19 response, leading to the Loan Agreement’s termination on July 26, 2020. The Loan Agreement was reinstated on July 8, 2021, and the first restructuring of the Project extended the original loan closing date to October 2, 2026. The loan ultimately became effective on January 11, 2022. A second restructuring in May 2025 revised project activities, reduced the scope of work, and modified the results framework, conditioning a possible second extension on demonstrated implementation progress. This is the second phase of the phased approach adopted in the restructuring approved on May 23, 2025. B. Project Background 3. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to contribute to improved sustainable landscape management and access to water supply and sanitation services in targeted river basins. The Project comprises four components: 1. Sustainable Productive Management of Agroecosystems (US$15.1 million); 2. Improvement of Resilience and Management of Hydraulic Infrastructures (US$29 million); 3. Pollution Reduction through the Improvement of Water Supply and Sanitation Services in Priority Areas (US$24.8 million); and 4. Capacity Building for the Management and Governance of Natural Resources and Project Management (US$10.9 million). The project’s landscape approach requires interinstitutional coordination of four public institutions: the Ministry of Agriculture (MA), the Ministry of Environment and Natural Resources (MMARN), the National Institute of Potable Water (INAPA), and the National Institute of Hydraulic Resources (INDRHI). The INDRHI hosts the Project Implementation Unit (PIU), responsible for project management. C. Project Status 4. In the latest ISR, dated September 2025, the overall implementation progress rating was upgraded to moderately satisfactory, to reflect progress achieved in the past year across components and project management. However, the PDO remains rated as moderately unsatisfactory because the completion of all works and related Environmental & Social (E&S) obligations would not be possible before the current closing date. Disbursement performance, though lagging, is poised to accelerate sharply as major works begin. As of January 2026, US$22.9 million (29%) has been disbursed, with commitments of US$21.4 million. Disbursements are projected to reach US$10.5 million in FY27 with the launch of dam, irrigation, and sanitation works. Overall implementation has improved and key issues that had negatively impacted project implementation such as the staffing of the PIU and delays on critical procurement processes have now been resolved. As a result, implementation has accelerated across all components as shown below: (a) Component 1 (Sustainable Productive Management of Agroecosystems): Implementation has accelerated markedly as the MA has strengthened its operational capacity through the amendment of the Technical Assistance Agreement with the Food and Agricultural organization of the United Nations (FAO). The PIU has entered into Sustainable Landscape Management Subproject Agreements with Producer Organizations, and all approved business plans (BPs) are currently under implementation (19 BPs for coffee; 10 BPs for cocoa; and 5 BPs for livestock). Procurement of machinery for rice technification has been completed and the equipment was formally delivered to the MA, with service delivery to farmers starting in January 2026. Field activities are now progressing across all targeted value chains, representing a significant turnaround compared to early implementation delays. The contract with the supporting implementation under this component, particularly the Productive Alliances modality, will need renewal beyond the current Project Closing Date. (b) Component 2 (Resilience of Hydraulic Infrastructure): Rehabilitation of irrigation canals and preparatory works for dam rehabilitation are advancing steadily. The rehabilitation of irrigation canal Mijo has started, and the procurement processes for the Monsieur Bogaert and Villa Vásquez canals have received multiple compliant bids and the PIU expects to award the contracts by early February 2026. The bidding processes for Mao Gurabo and Luis Bogaert canals are expected to be launched by February 2026. The dam rehabilitation studies—a key prerequisite for major civil works—started in August 2025, with final outputs expected in September 2026. Together, these activities reflect solid progress toward full readiness for implementation of large-scale infrastructure works. (c) Component 3 (Pollution Reduction through Water and Sanitation): Sanitation and wastewater system designs in Mao, Esperanza, Jánico, Sabana Iglesia, and Monción are scheduled for completion between March and May 2026. This will enable the launch of the bidding processes for the construction of the sanitation collection and treatment systems in Esperanza and Moncion as well as supervision of civil works in late 2026, marking a transition from planning to investment stage under this component. (d) Component 4 (Capacity Building and Governance): The implementation progress described above reflects the PIU’s efforts to strengthen its planning and monitoring capacities through the rollout of the Project Acceleration Strategy. These efforts have been supported by the recruitment of a project management consultancy, the delegation of procurement authority, and the reinforcement of fiduciary and E&S staffing. 6. Fiduciary and E&S performances are both rated as Moderately Satisfactory. Financial management arrangements are adequate, IFRs are current, and no audits are overdue. Procurement performance has also improved, with key contracts (sewerage feasibility studies, dam safety designs) advancing and a refined procurement plan under preparation. E&S performance has improved to moderately satisfactory with dedicated staff in place, updated and disclosed E&S instruments, establishment of the Dam Safety Panel of Experts, and the signing of the dam safety study and design contract. 7. The Government’s recent ministerial reform merging the Ministry of Economy, Planning and Development (MEPyD) with the Ministry of Finance is not expected to impact project implementation. The Government enacted Law No. 45- 25 (July 2025), which merged MEPyD and the Ministry of Finance to establish the Ministry of Finance and Economy (Ministerio de Hacienda y Economía, MHE). Under the Loan Agreement, as the legal and institutional successor to MEPyD, MHE retains the mandate and capacity to carry out all obligations originally assigned to MEPyD under the Project without the need of an amendment to the Loan Agreement. The extension of the Closing Date was conditioned on the Project’s ability to demonstrate readiness through the execution of key activities under Components 2 and 3. Since the last restructuring, the Project has advanced substantially, completing key studies and designs, and launching procurement processes for the execution of works as described above. The extension is required to allow awarding and signing the contracts for the rehabilitation of Monsieur Bogaert and Villa Vasquez canals, with the last planned completion scheduled in January 2028. These investments are critical to restoring reliable irrigation services across approximately 21,875 hectares, directly benefiting an estimated 13,723 farmers and rural households and strengthening the productivity and climate resilience of key agricultural watershed zones. The need for a possible further extension of the Project closing date will be assessed based on progress in project implementation and procurement processes for the remaining major investments under components 2 and 3, which include the rehabilitation of dams, the rehabilitation of canals, and the sanitation and wastewater systems. A decision point for the relevant procurement processes could be reached at the end of 2026 and could entail an extension for 12 additional months encompassing only the activities expected to be completed in that period. 9. The extension is required to allow awarding and signing contracts for the rehabilitation of the Monsieur Bogaert and Villa Vásquez canals, with completion scheduled for January 2028. These investments are critical to restoring reliable irrigation and water management services that underpin agricultural productivity, climate resilience, and income stability for thousands of farmers and rural households in the project area, while generating short-term employment through construction and enabling sustained job creation across agri-food value chains. The activities are fully aligned with the World Bank’s AgriConnect agenda by strengthening climate-resilient infrastructure, improving water efficiency, and enhancing market-oriented agricultural production systems. The need for a possible further extension of the Project closing date will be assessed based on implementation and procurement progress for the remaining major investments under Components 2 and 3—including dam, canal, and sanitation system rehabilitation—with a decision point expected by end-2026, potentially entailing a targeted extension of up to 12 additional months limited to activities expected to be completed within that period.
