Summary Description of Proposed Project Activities The proposed project will complement existing interventions and sector policies that tends to lower the barriers to investment in new processing capacity and enhance the competitiveness of existing cashew processing units. It supports additional investments at critical point along the value chain that will: (i) increase RCN productivity on-farm, improve quality and lower aggregation costs; (ii) accelerate private investment in new processing capacity through the provision carefully designed and located physical infrastructure that increases operational efficiency and significantly lowers the initial cost of investment; (iii) improve the policy and regulatory framework for the sustainable development of the Sep 28, 2023 Page 2 of 10 The World Bank Cashew Value Chain Sustainable Development Project (P180040) cashew value chain in Cote d’Ivoire, including build capacity in all functions along the value chain (technical and managerial skills, knowledge and innovation); and (iv) facilitating market development and trade. It is taking into consideration the lessons learned from the implementation of the Cashew Value Chain Competitiveness Project (Projet de Promotion de la Compétitivité de la chaîne de valeur Anacarde - PPCA). Proposed project components are described below: Component 1: Institutional strengthening and value chain governance (US$10.8 million). This component intends to improve the organization and governance of the cashew value chain to reduce marketing costs and enhance the chain’s competitiveness. Sub-component 1.1: Strengthening of cashew value chain key organizations (US$6.1 million). The sub-component will support a diversified capacity building program targeting key organizations overseeing the cashew value chain, including : (i) the newly established cashew inter-professional body (Organisation Interprofessionelle de l’Anacarde -OIA); (ii) the cashew producers’ organizations and their unions; (iii) the MEMINADER directorate of professional agricultural organizations (DOPA); and (iv) the Cotton and Cashew Council (Conseil Coton Anacarde - CCA). The project will also support the scaling up of coaching programs for local cashew producers’ organizations implemented by the School of Agronomy (Ecole Supérieure d'Agronomie - ESA) of the National Polytechnic Institute (Institut National Polytechnique Houphouët Boigny - INPHB) in Yamoussoukro. Sub-component 1.2: Improving the business environment (US$4.7 million).The project will also continue to assess the regulations and licenses that are required to sell, buy and trade cashew products with the objective to reduce the costs of internal marketing (from producer to port/local consumer) and make the value chain ultimately more competitive. Investments efforts will focus on establishing the Cashew Innovation and Technology Center (CITA) as a center of excellence for cashew value chain technology innovations with : (i) the construction of a central laboratory; (ii) the development of an incubation center for cashew by-products, (iii) the establishment of an assembly workshop for applied research for the development of locally designed cashew processing technologies in collaboration with the national platform of agro- Public Disclosure processing technology (PETRAPACI); (v) the construction of a storage warehouse for 10,000 tons of cashew nuts; and, (vi) the acquisition of a demonstration pyrolizer for the production of cashew nutshell liquid (CNSL). This project will support the design of a methodology and monitoring plan for the calculation and verification of greenhouse gas emissions reduction at national level. In addition, carbon footprints of value chain activities, including raw cashew nuts and by-products processing, will also be evaluated to offer support to the private sector in developing credible mitigation plans and targets that aim at reducing the carbon footprint of their activities. Component 2: Productivity enhancement and improved access to rcn markets (us$43.4 million). The project will support investments focusing on: (i) the expansion of the national program for cashew research and seedling development; (ii) facilitating farmers’ access to improved extension of good agricultural practices (GAPs) and other specialized services such as high-yielding planting material and small-scale mechanization; and (iii) improving rural road infrastructure. Sub-component 2.1: National cashew research program (US$6.2 million). This sub-component will provide continuous support to the implementation of the revised national cashew research program with a focus on: (i) continued research on high-performing tree varieties (to be tested, referenced, and used for multiplication); (ii) production of a new germplasm resilient to climate change; (iii) construction and equipping of a specialized laboratory to produce improved planting material in vitro; (iv) continued strengthening of human resources in cashew research by funding specialized training, notably in plant genetics, variety development, and plant protection; (v) planting material technology testing, transfer, and dissemination of research results developed under the PPCA, including exchange seminars, workshops, and demonstration trials. Sub-component 2.2: Support for cashew extension services and technology transfer (US$25.7 million). This sup-component will support the construction and/or rehabilitation and equipment of the five remaining Cashew Development Centers initially intended under the PPCA. Specific extension activities to be financed are: (i) developing and disseminating related extension materials in various formats; (ii) scaling up the e-extension service developed under the PPCA with a focus on content, presentation, and delivery modes that would be most effective in meeting the needs of small cashew producers; and (iii) providing transport equipment (vehicles and motorcycles) and IT Sep 28, 2023 Page 3 of 10 The World Bank Cashew Value Chain Sustainable Development Project (P180040) and audio equipment to facilitate extension outreach to farmers. Selected sites will be identified during project preparation. The proposed project will also support the expansion of the existing nursery system, critical for the diffusion of high-yield cashew varieties. It will finance: (i) equipment and inputs to establish new nurseries and produce the initial stock of seedlings and grafts; and (ii) training for nursery operators in plant production, handling, storage, and distribution (public and private sector and research institute); (iii) a platform linking research on improved seedlings to growers to undertake applied trials in farmers’ fields; and (iv) the distribution of seedlings. Sub-component 2.3: Rehabilitation and maintenance of feeder roads (US$11.5 million).. This sub-component will finance the third phase of the PPCA feeder roads rehabilitation program covering 400.3 km in the Bere region. It will build on technical studies already conducted under the PPCA. Preliminary reports of environmental and social impacts assessments are available as well as bidding documents for the selection of construction companies. The Resettlement Action Plans are currently being carried out. Component 3: support to private investment in post-harvest and processing infrastructure (us$83.2 million). This component aims at: (i) providing support to the cashew post-harvest and processing infrastructure; (ii) facilitating access to investment capital and risk management instruments to cashew value chain actors; and (iii) developing cashew markets and trade. Sub-component 3.1. Cashew post-harvest and processing infrastructure development (US$62.1 million). The proposed project will directly support the development of integrated cashew platforms and warehouse facilities in Bouaké and Odiénné. Focus will be made on value addition/agro-processing services of cashew products and by-products (Cashew Nutshell Liquid – (CNSL), jam, juice, alcoholic beverage, biofuel, etc.). In addition, the project will support the upgrading of the existing industrial zone in Yamoussoukro where the concentration of processing capacity is constantly increasing with a projected capacity of 100,000 tons by the end of 2023. Support envisaged relate to: (i) asphalting a linear of roads in the zone; (ii) establishing a water drainage system, (ii) providing an electricity grid and public lighting; and (iii) constructing a trucks parking area. A strategic environmental Public Disclosure and social study for the Yamoussoukro Industrial Zone was conducted under the PPCA in anticipation of this support. The project will also finance: (i) the establishment of cashew service hubs (CSH); and (ii) the installation of cashew nutshells valorization centers (Centres de valorisation des coques - CVC) in various cashew integrated platforms. Sub- component 3.2. Facilitating access to investment capital and risk management instruments to domestic processors (US$17.8 million). This sub-component will aim at improving access to finance for the cashew value chain through:) the establishment of a Dedicated Financial Facility (DFF) for micro-projects and long-term investment in the supply chain. Several financing windows will be established under the DFF, as matching grants mechanisms adapted to the specific investment purposes above. Sub-component 3.3. Development of cashew markets and trade for sustainable cashew processing (US$3.3 million). The project will continue supporting PPCA interventions in developing a sustainable outlet for Cote d’Ivoire RCN and kernels for the realization of growth potentials in cashew production and processing. Therefore, this sub-component aims at an effective development of domestic, regional, and international markets for cashew products, particularly kernels. Component 4: program coordination, monitoring and knowledge management (us$12.5 million). This component will facilitate: (i) administrative, technical, and financial management of the program; (ii) coordination among all institutional partners to ensure efficient flow of information and support to all value chain actors, in particular the small cashew growers and the processing industry; (iii) effective contractual arrangements with key implementing partners (FIRCA, CNRA, ANADER, etc.) and other private sector operators; (iv) monitoring and evaluation of the performance and the financial, environmental, and social impact of the project; and (v) development of communication activities to publicize and disseminate the project results, best practices and success stories. Environmental and Social Overview D.1 Overview of Environmental and Social Project Settings Sep 28, 2023 Page 4 of 10 The World Bank Cashew Value Chain Sustainable Development Project (P180040) Cashews are grown in the north, north-west and north-east of Cote d’Ivoire which represents 07 regions of the country. These regions fall under a sub-Guinean, sub-Sudanese and Sudanese climate zone with respective averages of 2000, 1600 and 1000 mm of rainfall annually. Besides, these regions respectively fall within semi-deciduous forest and savannah ecosystems. The Integrated Biodiversity Assessment Tool (IBAT) indicates potential biodiversity concerns in the project area, including 173 protected areas mainly comprising of classified forests as well as few critical habitats such as National Parks (Comoe, Marahoue, Mont Sangbe), the Haut Bandama Fauna and Flora Reserve, the UNESCOMAB Biosphere Mount Nimba Reserve, the Lera gallery Forest (a Ramsar Site, Wetland of International Importance) and the total Wildlife Reserve of Dialakoro. Additionally, there are 7 Key Biodiversity Areas (KBAs), 16 critically endangered, 40 endangered and 65 vulnerable species. Côte d'Ivoire has a favorable climate for raw cashew nuts production, with a long dry season and good insolation which makes it an ideal location for growing cashew trees. Yields also increased from 420kg/ha in 2015 to 600 kg/ha in 2022. However, the rapid increase in cashew production has been largely driven by an expansion in the planted area, contributing to deforestation. Cashew nuts are grown by about 250,000 households (about 1.5 million people) mainly small-scale farmers. Female farmers account for about 17% of all cashew farmers. Productivity is still low compared with other producers in South-East Asia like Vietnam (over 1,400kg/ha). With the recent introduction of improved planting material thanks to the Cashew Value Chain Competitiveness Project (P158810), yields are expected to increase significantly. This is therefore subject to increased use of chemical inputs, disease and insect treatments, and better drying and storage practices using new warehouses. D.2 Overview of Borrower’s Institutional Capacity for Managing Environmental and Social Risks and Impacts The proposed project’s preparation and implementation will be led by the existing Project Coordination Unit (PCU) presently in charge of World Bank-funded Cashew Value Chain Competitiveness Project-PPCA-PCU (P158810) under Public Disclosure OPs. The PPCA-PCU reports to the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development and is currently managing several projects satisfactorily, including P175043, prepared using the Environmental and Social Framework (ESF). The PCU is staffed with an environmental safeguards specialist, a social safeguards specialist and E&S assistants. These specialists are familiar with implementing projects under the WB OPs as well as ESF requirements. They've recently prepared ESF instruments for P175043, which is currently under implementation. In 01/2023, the WB team supported the PPCA-PCU to improve the E&S rating of P158810 from MU to MS as per the latest ISR. The PPCA-PCU will execute the project with the support of implementation agencies (CNRA, ANADER, etc.) with limited experience of management of environmental and social risks and impacts. Considering the geographic distance from Abidjan and the need for close and direct support in regions during the implementation of the proposed project which will exponentially scale up the previous cashew project, the PCU will be bolstered with additional local safeguard’s specialists in a decentralized unit. The decentralized Unit will be based in the Northern region to help to facilitate day to day operations and provide close follow-up of the environmental and social risk management of the numerous project activities. Capacity building efforts on gender, child labor, sexual Exploitation and Abuse/Sexual Harassment (SEA/SH) and social inclusion among others will help to support the smooth implementation of recommendations included in the ESF instruments. The PPCA-PCU as well as implementation agencies will also receive guidance from the Banks’ environmental and social team to enhance their capacity on Bank’s ESS. II. Environmental and Social Risk Classification (ESRC) Substantial A.1 Environmental Risk Rating Substantial Sep 28, 2023 Page 5 of 10 The World Bank Cashew Value Chain Sustainable Development Project (P180040) At Concept, the environmental risk classification is anticipated to be Substantial for many reasons: (i) the risk rating of P158810 has been MS for a while from 06 to 12/2022 following OHS issues on one civil work site of the project; (ii) the limited experience of implementation agencies (CNRA, ANADER, etc.) to effectively manage the potential E&S risks and impacts associated with the project according to ESF. (iii) E&S risks and impacts such as to waste generation (including liquid effluents and solid waste), noise pollution, and occupational health and safety hazards such as exposure to corrosive juice of cashew pulp, sharp blades, dust and fumes emissions, vegetation clearance and possible forest degradation (depending on location of facilities), disturbances to habitats, etc. associated to some project’s activities of the components 2 and 3 are significative but reversible; (iv) The nurturing of newly planted seedlings as well as the matured plants will require application of chemicals e.g., fertilizers and pesticides which may expose personnel and the environment to contamination and may adversely affect biodiversity. Despite these risks and impacts, the project is expected to contribute to reducing incentive for deforestation and will strengthen the resilience of cashew production systems through intensification and improvement of existing cashew farms without necessarily expanding cultivable areas. The project could generally contribute to a net gain in biodiversity through sustainable agricultural practices and use of high yields cashew plants. A.2 Social Risk Rating Substantial At the concept stage, the social risk is classified as substantial. Potential social risks associated with the proposed project may include risks related to labour, community health and safety, and involuntary resettlement. The proposed project will finance various interventions such as (i) the improvement of road connections between the main cashew Public Disclosure nut production areas and markets and with the financing of the third phase of the PPCA feeder road rehabilitation program covering the Bere region, (ii) the construction of storage and processing infrastructure. These investments will require land acquisitions and could have a negative impact on the economic activities of the population. Thus, social risks and impacts assessed at this stage of project preparation are expected to include worker health and safety, community safety and insecurity, potential need for land acquisition and economic displacement, sexual harassment and exploitation. In regard to the the insecurity, the proximity of the project area to the Sahel, and the prevailing level of poverty makes the region more vulnerable to extremism, particularly in the regions of Bounkani, Tchologo and Bagoue where it is greater. Conflicts between herders and farmers are also common in these areas. Previous experience from the Cashew Value Chain Competitiveness Project (P158810) also highlights additional risk related to monitoring of resettlement planning and monitoring. B. Relevance of Standards and Policies at Concept Stage B.1 Relevance of Environmental and Social Standards ESS1 - Assessment and Management of Environmental and Social Risks and Impacts Relevant ESS1 is relevant. The proposed project will generate potential environmental and social impacts and risks that can be easily anticipated because of lessons learnt from the implementation of the Cashew Value Chain Competitiveness Project (P158810). In general, several potential environmental and social risks that could arise from Project activities are related to occupational health and safety (OHS), community and worker health and safety, waste management, pest management, efficiency in resource (re)use, and potential risks to biodiversity and living natural resources, land Sep 28, 2023 Page 6 of 10 The World Bank Cashew Value Chain Sustainable Development Project (P180040) acquisition and resettlement impacts; inadequate stakeholder engagement risks. To adequately manage those risks and impacts, the PCU will update the Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMF) of the Cashew Value Chain Competitiveness Project (P158810). The ESMF describes the procedures and processes followed in preparing and disclosing site specific safeguard instruments, after Bank's review and clearance. ESS10 - Stakeholder Engagement and Information Disclosure Relevant Designing and implementation of social risk management measures including child labor risk mitigation measures will require wide consultations with these stakeholders and the communities in the project areas. Stakeholders' engagement is key in both the successful preparation and implementation of the proposed project and holds the potential for project acceptance, functionality, and sustainability. The Borrower will prepare and implement a Stakeholder Engagement Plan (SEP) proportional to the nature and scale of the project activities and associated risks and impacts. With reference to lessons learned from the existing project Cashew Value Chain Competitiveness Project (P158810) regarding stakeholder engagement, the SEP will set out how the Borrower will engage in meaningful consultations with all stakeholders throughout the project life cycle paying particular attention to the inclusion of vulnerable and disadvantaged groups. ESS2 - Labor and Working Conditions Relevant The planned activities are not expected to have any negative impacts related to labor and working conditions. The project will be carried out in accordance with the applicable requirements of ESS2, in a manner acceptable to the Bank, including through implementing adequate existing occupational health and safety measures. ESS2 requirements Public Disclosure are applicable to all subprojects. Given experience in the agricultural sector in Côte d’Ivoire, there is a potential risk of child labor. While some measures have been put in place by the government and the private sector to address these risks, the project will support measures to prevent child and forced labor. The issue will require close monitoring and supervision throughout project implementation. To mitigate highlighted risks for seconded civil servants, direct workers, workers employed by contracted and subcontracted companies and primary supply workers, the Borrower will need to develop a Labor Management Procedures (LMP). ESS3 - Resource Efficiency and Pollution Prevention and Management Relevant This standard is relevant. The production of improved planting materials and the plantations of the cashew crops may involve the use of agrochemicals (e.g., fertilizers and pesticides) at the nursery stage and even at the plantations to replenish nutrients and control pest. The post-harvest processing activities may utilize water, raw materials and energy and may contribute to air pollution through emission of fumes/greenhouse gases (GHG) and water pollution. The project will prioritize support to proposals that incorporate technologies and innovations that efficiently use water, energy (e.g., renewable energy), and raw materials and have lower environmental and social footprints. The project will not finance activities or subprojects which do not conform to ESS3, and this will be determined by using the screening tool which will be developed as part of the ESMF to screen out such activities or subprojects. The project will update the Integrated Pest Management Plan (IPMP) of P158810. ESS4 - Community Health and Safety Relevant This standard is relevant. Risks of intoxication due to misuse of pesticides/chemicals and waste mishandling may also cause health and safety problems. The rehabilitation of cashew farms and planting intensification of may involve labor Sep 28, 2023 Page 7 of 10 The World Bank Cashew Value Chain Sustainable Development Project (P180040) influx of migrant workers who may present some risk of communicable diseases such as HIV/AIDS and public health diseases. Use of migrant workers and influx of workers in project communities may also create risk of GBV/SEA/SH to the communities. Developing and requiring the signing and adhering to Code of Conduct (CoC) will be mandatory for staffs and public authorities. Sensitizing, briefings/training/workshop to educate people about GBV/SEA/SH will be provided regularly. The use of security forces is not envisaged under the project, but should they be required, the project will comply with the requirements of ESS4 and will consider the ESF Good Practice Note on Assessing the Risks and Impacts of Using Security Personnel in project. ESS5 - Land Acquisition, Restrictions on Land Use and Involuntary Resettlement Relevant This standard is considered relevant. The project will finance the construction of: warehouses and storage facilities as well as agro-processing factories, consistent with international food safety standards; and rural roads to link producers to the markets and reduce transportation costs and support the reforms proposed in the strategy for the development of road infrastructure. This could also lead to permanent and temporary losses (both physical and economic) of populations located within the immediate vicinity of the proposed investments. As the location of these roads is currently not known, a Resettlement Policy Framework (RPF) will be developed for the project prior to appraisal. The project will develop site-specific Resettlement Action Plans (RAPs) once there have been any activity identified that will cause resettlement. All RAPs need to be monitored throughout the project and adjustments will need to be made as necessary. ESS6 - Biodiversity Conservation and Sustainable Management of Living Natural Relevant Resources Public Disclosure This standard is relevant. Generally, the project is expected to contribute towards enriching biodiversity through incorporation of agroforestry and poly-cropping systems in the cash crops production. Also, the project intends to utilize cashew and coconut, depending on suitability, to revegetate lands where cocoa production is no longer tenable. This could help improve the vegetation cover and biodiversity. However, there is a tendency to degrade forest and/or cause deforestation for the establishment of new cashew and coconut plantations if proper controls are not in place and enforced. Also, the potential use of pesticides at nursery and plantation fields as well as the use of arboricides to treat felled cashew trees could be deleterious to some biodiversity. These pesticides and arboricides, could potentially be washed into water bodies through run-offs and cause adverse impacts on aquatic life. These risks will be assessed and adequately mitigated in the ESMF and IPMP.
