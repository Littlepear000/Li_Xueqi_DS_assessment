KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions ETHIOPIA: The project’s overall implementation progress is rated Moderately Satisfactory. Of the targeted 1,709,617 direct beneficiaries to be supported during the project life, FSRP has so far reached 1,153,791 individuals which is 67% of the target. Of the 49 agreed actions from the third mission, 80% have either been completed or are on track for completion. The implementation progress, which accelerated across all components in the second year, remains Satisfactory. Conflict remains a challenge in parts of Oromia, Amhara, and Tigray due to travel restrictions, staff turnover, and limited local engagement. The mission recommended regular security mapping and clear criteria for disengagement and re- engagement, focusing on accessible woredas in Tigray, setting benchmarks for resuming activities in inaccessible areas, and accelerating GEMS data uploads for improved monitoring. The World Bank has approved the procurement plan and the selection of the Policy Studies Institute (PSI) to carry out the survey. The mission emphasized the need to accelerate both the design and implementation of the survey. With the delay in overall implementation of component 3 and hiring of the Value Chain Development Firms (VCDFs), the mission reiterated the need for a unified approach, starting with local value chain analysis to identify constraints and guide targeted investments through the project’s four subcomponents. It emphasized the urgent need to finalize the hiring of Value Chain Development Firms (VCDFs) to ensure coordinated implementation, support business plan preparation, and build MoA capacity. An action plan should be prepared to operationalize this approach, along with identifying capacity and resource needs at federal and regional levels to work effectively with VCDFs. MADAGASCAR: The project's overall implementation in Madagascar is rated Moderately Satisfactory. Of the 3,400,000 direct beneficiaries targeted, FSRP-MG has reached 2,732,290 individuals, or 80.3% of the objective. Of the 97 agreed actions from the previous mission, 74% are either completed or are on track for completion. During the recent ISR period, implementation progress across the parent project's technical components has improved, with disbursement reaching 42.3% of total OF/AF financed. Notable progress includes: i) the completion of the Project's Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC), which reached 753,600 beneficiary households—exceeding the initial target of 657,000 by 115% —with 67% female participation; ii) achieving 155,454 beneficiaries, or 51.7% of target (300,000) receiving improved nutrition products/services primarily through the Project's support to school feeding programs via local sourcing. IGAD: The project is making Satisfactory (S) progress towards the delivery of the Project Development Objectives (PDO). Examples of IGAD delivering on each PDO indicator can be summarized as follows: a) Policy Harmonization (indicator 1), IGAD has commissioned a regional assessment of land and water management policies. The aim is to use this study to build consensus among member states on areas for policy alignment, 2) Center of Excellence (indicator 2), IGAD has built consensus on the institutional design and thematic priorities for a center of excellent focus on regional soil health, and 3) knowledge network (indicator 3) IGAD co-lead the process for designing and delivering the first FSRP learning event. The event brought together all three phases of FSRP, creating a platform for sharing operational and technical knowledge. Examples of CCARDESA delivering on each PDO indicator can be summarized as follows: a) Policy Harmonization (indicator 1), CCARDESA is designing a trade scorecard. The scorecard will assess how each member state is progressing toward trade harmonization. CCARDESA will utilize the scorecard system to identify opportunities for fostering strong trade ties. 2) Center of Excellence (indicator 2), CCARDESA is working to build consensus on the institutional design and thematic priorities for a center of excellent focused on regional soil health, and 3) Knowledge Network (indicator 3) CCARDESA is in the process of establishing a knowledge platform focused on Climate Smart Agriculture (CSA). The platform aims to explore ways to enhance the accessibility and application of CSA technologies. 4.2 MPA Program Development Objective Original MPA Program Development Objective (Approved as part of (Seq. ) package on ) Current MPA Program Development Objective (Approved as part of Additional Financing (Seq. 3) package on 23-Jun- 2025) To increase the resilience of food systems and preparedness for food insecurity in the participating countries. 4.3 Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA) Status (Public Disclosure) The Food Systems Resilience Program for Eastern and Southern Africa (Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA), P178566) was approved by the Board on June 21, 2022, with an overall financing envelope of US$2.3 billion and four recipients under Phase 1: Ethiopia (US$600 million equivalent), Madagascar (US$158.1 million equivalent), the Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD) (US$25 million equivalent) and the Centre for Coordination of Agricultural Research and Development for Southern Africa (CCARDESA) (US$5 million). On June 6, 2024, Madagascar triggered US$50 million Contingency Emergency Response Component (CERC) to respond to a request from the Government and also received US$70 million Additional Financing, including US$50 million from the Crisis Response Window Early Response Financing (CRW ERF) and US$20 million from Global Agriculture and Food Security Program (GAFSP). Phase 2 for Tanzania (PforR, US$300 million equivalent) and a phase 3 for Comoros, Malawi, Somalia, Kenya, and the Africa Union Commission (Investment Project Financing (IPF), US$603 million) were approved by the Board on May 31, 2023. Malawi also received a grant in the amount of US$15 million. At the same time (May 31, 2023), the Board approved an increase of the overall envelope of the MPA to US$2.75 billion. Additional Financing to Tanzania of US$8.3 million was approved on September 30, 2024. On February 20, 2025, Malawi received Additional Financing of US$61.0 million from the Food Systems 2030 and AGCOM 2.0 Multi-Donor Trust Funds. 5.
