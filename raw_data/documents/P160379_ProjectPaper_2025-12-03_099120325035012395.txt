PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. Project Background: The Innovation in Solar Power and Hybrid Technologies Project (ISHTP) was approved by the World Bank’s (the Bank) Board on March 29, 2019. The Project was originally financed by an IBRD Loan (US$150 million), Clean Technology Fund (CTF) Loan (US$28 million), and CTF Grant (US$21.81 million), whose Agreements were signed on December 14, 2022, and became effective on March 7, 2023. On February 14, 2023, the financing amounts were revised downwards to US$67 million for the IBRD Loan, US$13 million for the CTF Loan, and US$9 million for the CTF Grant. The Borrower/Recipient and the implementing agency for ISHTP is the Solar Energy Corporation of India Limited (SECI), which is a central sector public utility that plays a strategic role in India’s renewable energy initiatives. 2. Project Development Objective (PDO): The PDOs are to demonstrate the operational and economic feasibility of utility- scale innovative renewable energy (RE) technologies and battery energy storage solutions (BESS), and to strengthen institutional capacity to facilitate scale-up of such technologies on a commercial basis in India. 3. Previous restructuring: The Project has been restructured four times in response to changing implementation conditions. • The first restructuring was conducted on March 11, 2022, to change the PDO Indicators, Intermediate Result Indicators and Disbursement Estimates, because of unforeseen delays in loan signing and effectiveness. In addition, the legal covenants were updated to reflect the change in the first subproject (solar-wind hybrid with BESS subproject at Ramagiri, Andhra Pradesh), after the new state government in the off-taking state declined to buy power from this subproject. • The second restructuring was conducted on February 14, 2023, to effect a partial cancelation of US$110.81 million, hence reducing the project size from US$199.81 million to US$89 million. This was done since SECI had decided to limit the financing to the two subprojects instead of three, since the first subproject at Ramagiri, Andhra Pradesh, could not be taken forward. • The third restructuring was conducted on December 20, 2024, to extend the Project Closing Date by 12 months, from December 31, 2024, to December 31, 2025, to allow for additional time to complete the design, installation and commissioning of the second subproject, 100 MW Floating Solar PV Subproject in Getalsud, Jharkhand. • The fourth restructuring was completed on April 24, 2025, to update the Results Framework. This was due to (i) the downsizing of the Project, both in terms of funding (from US$199.81 million to US$89 million) and the number of subprojects (from three to two in the second restructuring), and (ii) the extension of the closing date (in the third restructuring). • The current proposed restructuring is the fifth restructuring of the project, as described below. 4. Current status of the project: The Project supports two subprojects and strengthens the institutional capacity of SECI: a. 100 Megawatt (MW) Solar Photovoltaic (PV) with a 40 MW/120 MWh Battery Subproject at Rajnandgaon, Chhattisgarh: This subproject was commissioned on February 1, 2024. 1 Official Use Only The World Bank Innovation in Solar Power and Hybrid Technologies (P160379) b. 100 MW Floating Solar PV Subproject in Getalsud, Jharkhand: The contract for this subproject was awarded in May 2024, with an 18-month period allocated for the design, installation, and commissioning of the facility. Construction activities are ongoing. 5. Project Ratings: The PDO and the implementation progress (IP) are Moderately Satisfactory given the progress made on both the subprojects, despite initial delays. There are no overdue audit reports for the project. 6. Rationale for Restructuring: This restructuring paper proposes the extension of the Project Closing Date by three months, from December 31, 2025, to March 31, 2026, as per DEA’s request letter dated November 4, 2025. This extension is necessary to allow sufficient time for the installation and commissioning of the second subproject, 100 MW Floating Solar PV Subproject in Getalsud, Jharkhand. In addition, the paper proposes a revision to the Key Performance Indicators (“Results Framework”) due to the extended timeline. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 1. Extension of Closing Date: It is proposed to extend the loan closing date by three months, from December 31, 2025, to March 31, 2026. This extension is necessary to provide additional time for the installation and commissioning of the second subproject, the 100 MW Floating Solar PV Subproject in Getalsud, Jharkhand. This request represents the second extension of the project closing date. With this extension, the project will have been extended by 15 months in total from its original closing date (i.e., December 31, 2024). 2. Revision of Results Framework: The Project’s Results Framework needs to be updated to reflect changes in Key Performance Indicators from the proposed extension of the Project Closing Date.
