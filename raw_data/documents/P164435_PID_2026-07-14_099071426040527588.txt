Introduction and Context 1. Burundi, a landlocked country with a population of 12.9 million and an annual growth rate of 2.7 percent, faces significant challenges in delivering reliable basic services and generating sufficient jobs for its rapidly growing population. The country has one of the lowest electricity access rates globally, with only 25.9 percent of the population connected, compared to a Sub-Saharan Africa average of 52 percent. Access is highly uneven: while 72.5 percent of residents in Bujumbura and 42.6 percent in other urban centers have electricity, access in rural areas remains extremely limited at 1.8 percent, severely constraining economic activity, service delivery, and human capital development. 2. To address these broader energy access and energy transition challenges, and in line with the Mission 300 (M300) initiative, the Government of Burundi (GoB) is scaling up energy access through the National Energy Compact launched in September 2025, which targets 70 percent electricity access (approximately 9 million people) and 40 percent access to clean cooking solutions (5 million people) by 2030. Achieving these objectives will require an estimated US$3.49 billion, including 1.6 billion from private financing. The World Bank is supporting the GoB through policy dialogue, donor coordination, technical assistance, and financing to advance sector reforms, strengthen the enabling environment, and accelerate progress toward universal access by 2030. 3. In a context of structural vulnerabilities in fuel supply and a growing ambition for energy transition, the case for e- mobility is further reinforced by Burundi's improving energy outlook . Investments in domestic renewable energy and expanding solar and off-grid electrification provide a growing electricity base that can support charging infrastructure development. This alignment between transport electrification and the broader energy transition creates a strong foundation for a low-carbon mobility system, anchored in the country's National Energy Compact under the M300 framework. 4. Beyond energy resilience, e-mobility value chains present meaningful opportunities for private sector engagement, job creation, and green skills development. However, realizing this potential requires addressing persistent barriers, including high upfront costs, gaps in technical standards, inadequate financing instruments, and customs frameworks not yet Page 3 Official Use Only The World Bank Solar Energy in Local Communities (P164435) – Additional Financing Request 2 adapted to electric vehicles. Establishing battery take-back will also be essential to manage environmental risks. The government, led by the Ministry of Transport is developing a national e-mobility strategy to address these gaps, with coordinated support from UNEP and the World Bank to accelerate a sustainable and inclusive transition. 5. Burundi’s rising transport demand makes e-mobility a strategically attractive opportunity. The transport sector depends almost entirely on imported petroleum products and has been repeatedly disrupted by fuel shortages, resulting in price volatility, reduced mobility, and broader economic losses. Across the country, vehicle fleets — including taxis and moto- taxis — are dominated by high-emitting, ageing vehicles that compound both the fiscal burden of fuel dependency and the public health costs of poor air quality. Against this backdrop, e-mobility using solar solutions offers a cost-effective and scalable pathway to reduce exposure to fuel supply shocks while significantly lowering operating costs for users over time. The SOLEIL-Nyakiriza Project is designed to provide energy services to promote human capital development, enhance productivity of rural enterprises, and reach poor and vulnerable populations, including women. The Project focuses on: (i) electrifying up to 353 schools and 362 health centers with modular solar PV systems and implementing a multi-layered maintenance approach; (ii) providing 65,000 off-grid solar products to poor and vulnerable households; (iii) distributing 300,000 clean and efficient cookstoves to households and schools; and (iv) strengthening the enabling environment through improved policies, regulations, private sector participation, and capacity building for off-grid solar energy and clean cooking solutions. 10. This AF will contribute to increase the electricity access rate in Burundi, in line with the M300 initiative and in enhancing private sector participation and jobs’ creation. The AF will expand household access under Component 2 to both clean cooking and electricity through the RBF mechanism under FESEC. The AF will further scale up the RBF for both electricity and clean cooking, adding 40,000 and 60,000 new connections, respectively. It will prioritize support to the most dynamic and mature private sector players that meet the RBF eligibility criteria. Currently, 17 companies qualify for the RBF for off grid solar and 18 companies and cooperatives for clean cooking. High-performing companies and cooperatives will also be encouraged to distribute both technologies through their existing networks, leveraging synergies between solar and clean cooking value chains. This includes working through partner cooperatives to expand outreach and increase sales volumes. Expanding RBF support is expected to drive job creation and skills development while increasing the mobilization of private capital. The AF will also support the preparation of documentation to upgrade instruments under FESEC such as credit lines and support the most mature companies to become investment ready. 14. The AF will also scale up financing under Component 2 of FESEC by introducing, through GEF financing, a dedicated e- mobility window under the start-up grant pillar, in parallel to the Productive Use of Electricity (PUE) window. The PUE call for proposals launched in 2025 resulted in five qualified company proposals, focusing on the deployment of electric vehicles powered by solar charging stations, particularly in off-grid areas or regions with unreliable grid access. Overall, the PUE Window aims to support at least 7 companies with a total allocation of US$ 1.5 million. The Selection Committee has already approved 7 beneficiaries for an initial allocation of US$ 0.73 million, and another call for proposals for PUE will likely be launched by September 2026. From a private sector development perspective, the new e-mobility window will strengthen FESEC beneficiaries by enabling them to diversify and expand service delivery to communities, while supporting innovation in emerging market segments. Finally, the AF will complement UNEP’s 2023–2028 support aimed at improving the policy and enabling environment for e-mobility in Burundi. 15. The AF will strengthen technical assistance under Component 3 to enhance the sustainability of activities supported under Components 1 and 2, and in alignment with the implementation of the Energy Compact. The additional financing will also include technical assistance to support locally produced cookstoves in achieving ISO Tier 3 standards through the identification of 2 –3 champion manufacturers who will receive targeted support to optimize their products. It will also support the launch of a Clean Cooking Sector Working Group which aims at facilitating dialogue and coordination of clean cooking initiatives. 16.
