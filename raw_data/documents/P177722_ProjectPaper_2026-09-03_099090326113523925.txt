PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING Project Status 1. The Financial and Private Sector Development Project is an SDR 37.2 million (US$ 50 million equivalent)1 IDA Grant to strengthen deposit insurance, promote bank lending to MSMEs, and boost MSME integration in value chains. The Grant was approved on July 10, 2023, and became effective on February 15, 2024. The project is currently rated Satisfactory for both Progress towards Achievement of the PDO and Overall Implementation Progress. The overall risk rating has been downgraded from Substantial to Moderate, reflecting improvements in project implementation performance. Key risk factors that underpinned the Substantial rating have been mitigated: fiduciary and procurement systems have stabilized; the PIU’s implementation capacity has strengthened; and the disbursement rate has reached 70% two years prior to the project’s closing date. Political and macroeconomic risks, while still present, are assessed as manageable within the remaining project timeframe. 2. The Deposit Insurance Fund (DIF) Component 1 (US$15.00 million, out of which US$13.8 million has been disbursed, including US$13.51 million for the DIF capitalization) aims at improving depositor trust, mitigating the likelihood of bank runs, and contributing to greater deposit mobilization. The DIF Component was declared effective in March 2025. Several important steps designed as disbursement conditions have been completed prior to the DIF capitalization, including (i) the execution of the DIF Agreement between the Ministry of Finance (MoF) and the Deposits and Savings Insurance Fund of Tajikistan (DSIFT), ); (ii) the amendment of the Framework Agreement, satisfactory to the World Bank; (iii) the establishment of procedures for collecting extraordinary contributions from member banks; and (iv) the adoption of the updated DIF Investment Policy, satisfactory to the World Bank. Sub-components 1.2 (Payout Automation) and 1.3 (Public Awareness Campaign) are well under implementation, with the ongoing procurement of the Single Customer Value (SCV) hardware and software for automated payout processing, and the completion of the Strategy to implement the Public Awareness Campaign by the end of the calendar year 2026 3. The Partial Risk-Sharing Facility (PRSF) Component 2 (US$ 18.50 million, out of which US$ 16.73 million was disbursed to capitalize the PRSF account at the Ministry of Finance) finances the PRSF capitalization, launch, and implementation, as well as related capacity building to PFIs and MSMEs. Prior to the capitalization of the PRSF account, several important activities, designed as disbursement conditions, were completed and found satisfactory by the World Bank. These activities include: (i) the development of an agency agreement to be signed between the Ministry of Finance and a Guarantee Fund to act as an agent to the MoF to manage the PRSF account; (ii) the establishment of the PRSF account with Sodirotbank, and (iii) the adoption of the PRSF Manual, including the Environment and Social Management System (ESMS), satisfactory to the World Bank. 4. The launch of the PRSF account was planned for FY24. Under the original project design, two existing guarantee funds were identified to play a central role in operationalizing the Partial Risk Sharing Facility (PRSF). They were expected to sign agency agreements with the PIU to manage the PRSF account opened at the Ministry of Finance. The first, Tajik Credit Support Partner (TCSP), which was the branch and subsidiary of the Credit Guarantee Fund of Tajikistan (CGFT), had established operational infrastructure and a track record in the Tajik market. Both institutions were assessed and selected during project preparation as suitable agents to manage the Ministry of Finance's PRSF account, in accordance with World Bank Financial Intermediary Financing (FIF) assessment guidelines. Their participation was considered critical to the early operationalization of the PRSF, given their existing legal status, technical capacity, and relationships with Participating Financial Institutions. 5. Two force-majeure events, preventing the involvement of these Guarantee Funds, were entirely external to and beyond the control of the project: (i) the exit of the TCSP from the Tajik market in 2023, due to the decision made by the CGFT’s shareholders; and (ii) the unexpected death of the Chairwoman of the local GF, Zamonat, which severely impacted Zamonat’s operational capacity. In light of these two developments, the project shifted its approach to focus on establishing the Credit Guarantee Fund by the Government of the Republic of Tajikistan, an outcome that had been anticipated as one of the project's intended long-term objectives. The Government issued Decree #364 on June 9, 2026, which establishes the Credit Guarantee Fund (CGF) at the Ministry of Finance, creating the momentum to launch and implement PRSF. The launch is scheduled for the fall of 2026. 6. The Governmental Decree of June 2026 formally establishes the Credit Guarantee Fund as a legal entity with a mandate to provide credit guarantees to MSMEs. However, the Decree alone does not endow the CGF with the operational capacity to begin issuing guarantees. The organization is still being operationalized and, at present, lacks the physical infrastructure (office furniture and fit-out), core staffing, and operational resources required to become fully functional and to cover initial operating costs during the ramp-up period. The restructuring addresses this gap by providing targeted additional funding under Component 2 to cover these foundational operational needs. This sequencing — legal establishment followed by restructuring-funded operationalization — was as intended: the team prioritized securing the Government's formal commitment to the CGF before deploying project resources, ensuring institutional ownership and the sustainability of the guarantee program beyond project closure. The latter is evident in the Government's plans to reallocate budget funds for the development and maintenance of the CGF. 7. The Supplier and Export Development Program (SEDP) Component 3 (US$ 7.75 million, out of which US$ 1.2 million has been disbursed) funds capacity building and partial reimbursement grants for MSMEs to help them increase market linkages to large local and multinational corporations. The Component’s disbursement conditions were fulfilled, and the component was declared effective in early 2026. An international consulting company providing technical assistance to the Export Agency for the launch and implementation of the SEDP was competitively selected and contracted. Twelve regional SEDP offices have been established by the Export Agency to support on-the-ground SEDP implementation, and pilot MSMEs have been preliminarily identified for participation in the SEDP. The MoF PIU, in close collaboration with the Export Agency, launched the SEDP on July 8, 2026, with the participation of SEDP’s potential beneficiaries and other project stakeholders. 8. Activities under Component 4 (Project Implementation, Coordination, and Management — US$ 3.75 million, out of which US$2.1 million has been disbursed) provide funding to ensure the sound management of the Project by the MoF PIU. Financial Management is rated Satisfactory; Interim Financial Reports (IFRs) are submitted on a timely basis. Procurement is rated Satisfactory. Environmental and Social (E&S) Performance is rated Satisfactory; the original project was categorized as Moderate with respect to E&S risk. Rationale for Restructuring The World Bank Financial and Private Sector Development Project (P177722) Page 3 Official Use Only 9. The proposed restructuring involves the redeployment of SDR 3,720,000 (US$ 5 million equivalent) in unallocated funds (Category 8) into active expenditure categories to maximize the development impact of resources that would otherwise remain idle. No changes are proposed to the Project Development Objective, the closing date, or the overall Moderate Environmental and Social Risk Classification. 10. Key drivers of the restructuring: a) Maximizing the impact of unallocated resources is important. With 70% disbursed two years before closing, the unallocated reserve is no longer needed as a contingency buffer at its current level. Deploying these resources now ensures they generate development value before project closure. Project implementation so far has helped identify the following five directions for additional project support: b) Capital market development (Component 1, New Sub-Component 1.4). While the PDO focuses on strengthening deposit insurance and promoting bank lending to MSMEs, the sustainability of these gains depends on the resilience, depth, and breadth of the broader financial system in which they operate. Tajikistan's financial sector remains bank-centric, with limited non-bank instruments available to enterprises seeking longer-term capital for investments or risk diversification. The underdeveloped state of capital markets, with both primary and secondary securities markets at a nascent stage and largely illiquid, constrains the range of financing options available, particularly to growing enterprises. It also limits the sources of funding for financial institutions, particularly long-term funding, and non-deposit- taking financial institutions that often serve MSMEs. c) CGF operationalization (Sub-Component 2.2). Decree #364 of June 9, 2026, establishing the CGF, creates an immediate operational imperative. The CGF requires support for physical infrastructure, equipment, and its operating costs. Without this, the deployment of PRSF guarantee products — a key project milestone — would be at risk. d) National Quality Infrastructure (Sub-Components 3.1 and 3.2). Lifting the disbursement conditions for the SEDP Component has identified gaps in the regulatory and technical standards environment, constraining MSME access to value chains. Additional funding will address these through regulatory updates, digitalization of standards processes, and targeted laboratory upgrades e) PRSF Development Training Center works (Component 4). The training center has been formally designated by the Government of Tajikistan as the PRSF Development Training Center, with the perspective of becoming a permanent facility for MSME financial literacy and capacity development. In identifying an appropriate venue for this institutional function, alternatives were considered, including the Ministry of Finance's premises. However, the MoF's facilities were assessed as insufficient to meet the operational requirements of the PRSF Training Center, which must accommodate the full scope of training on financial instruments for MSMEs, in terms of both physical capacity and functional suitability. As a result, no viable alternative venue exists for this institutional function. The facility represents a long-term public investment in MSME development that will outlast the project and serve successive programs. However, its utility is currently seasonal: the Varzob River crossing is impassable for four to six months per year, rendering the center inaccessible to trainees and instructors during this period and reducing the effective training calendar by up to half. Construction of a permanent bridge is therefore not a discretionary convenience but a prerequisite for the facility to function as the Government intends. Without it, the project's investment in training curricula, equipment, and institutional capacity at the center cannot be fully realized, and the Government's long-term commitment to a permanent MSME training infrastructure will remain operationally unfulfilled. Component 4, therefore, finances the civil works required to operationalize the Center, including the building repairs and bridge construction, The World Bank Financial and Private Sector Development Project (P177722) Page 4 Official Use Only enabling it to deliver training on a full 12-month cycle and meet the project's beneficiary targets under the Results Framework. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 11. The PDO and the closing date remain unchanged. This restructuring includes: a) reallocation of SDR 3,720,000 (US$ 5 million equivalent) from Unallocated Funds (Category 8) to active categories as indicated in Table 2 below; b) expansion of eligible activities within Components 1, 2, 3, and 4 as indicated in Table 1 below; c) addition of a “Works” expenditure category. 12. Thus, the SDR 3,720,000 (US$ 5 million equivalent) is proposed to be deployed across four targeted interventions: d) Capital market development (new Sub-Component 1.4 under Component 1 – additional US$300,000 equivalent): The component would support the Government in drafting a capital market development strategy covering both the government debt market and the corporate securities markets, and in kick- starting its implementation. Full adoption and implementation of the strategy would extend beyond the project horizon and remain Government-led. To this end, the component would provide support to the National Bank of Tajikistan, the Agency for Securities and Special Registration, and other relevant stakeholders in the capital market ecosystem, covering: (i) technical assistance for the development of primary- and secondary-market legislation, regulatory guidelines, and supervisory capacity; (ii) technical assistance to strengthen market intermediaries, institutional investors, and issuance capacity across public and private sector entities; and (iii) investments in enabling capital market infrastructure to underpin a functional and efficient market. Technical assistance under Sub-Component 1.4 to the National Bank of Tajikistan, the Agency of Securities and Special Registration, and other capital market stakeholders is therefore not ancillary to the PDO. It addresses a structural gap that, if left unresolved, would limit the impact of MSME lending and deposit insurance reforms by constraining the broader financial ecosystem — consistent with the project's original intent to build an enabling environment for private-sector access to finance. e) CGF/PRSF full operationalization (Sub-Component 2.2 – additional US$2,850,000 equivalent): The component would support the operationalization of the CGF/PRSF by financing expenditures, including furniture and office equipment, staffing, and operating costs, required to bring the newly established Credit Guarantee Fund to full operational readiness. This would enable the planned fall 2026 launch of PRSF guarantee products. f) Support for National Quality Infrastructure (Sub-Component 3.1 – US$150,000 equivalent): Regulatory framework development, digitalization of standards processes, and targeted laboratory enhancements to strengthen MSME value chain integration, addressing gaps identified under the SEDP. Expected output includes updated technical standards for at least two sectors and at least one upgraded laboratory accreditation. g) PRSF Development Training Center renovation and access bridge (Revised Component 4, new Works category – US$1,700,000 equivalent): Rehabilitation of the Government-designated PRSF Development Training Center building and construction of a small vehicular and pedestrian bridge over the Varzob River. This activity is included in Component 4 due to its construction and logistical management requirements. The decision to finance the construction of the PRSF Development Training Center was preferred over The World Bank Financial and Private Sector Development Project (P177722) Page 5 Official Use Only reallocation of funds to ongoing program components — such as the matching grants facility — for several reasons. While additional matching grants would contribute to higher actuals for certain output indicators, the PRSF Development Training Center addresses a structural gap in the enabling environment for private-sector development that cannot be filled through grant disbursements alone. It provides durable, institution-based infrastructure for business development services that will continue to deliver value well after project closing. Furthermore, the Center is designed to serve a broader function as a capacity-building hub for all Project Implementation Units (PIUs) operating under World Bank-financed operations in Tajikistan, thereby generating institutional efficiency gains and knowledge-sharing benefits across the portfolio. This dual function — serving both entrepreneurs and PIU capacity building — significantly strengthens the case for this investment relative to alternatives. 13. The proposed scope additions do not require changes to the PDO or the PDO-level indicators, as the new sub- component and a new “Works” eligible category are designed to support and accelerate achievement of existing targets rather than introduce new development objectives. Specifically: (i) Sub-Component 1.4 on capital market development contributes to the broader financial sector stability agenda underpinning the deposit insurance reforms already captured under PDO Indicator 1; (ii) National Quality Infrastructure support under Component 3 directly strengthens MSME capacity to meet buyer and export standards, feeding into the existing PDO Indicator on MSME integration in value chains; and (iii) works under Component 4 are operational enablers with no separate PDO-level indicator, consistent with standard treatment of project management infrastructure. Regarding the Government's request to revise/introduce certain indicators, in particular decreasing the cumulative volume of MSME credits guaranteed under the PRSF, decreasing the size of the deposit insurance fund as a share of total individual deposits in the system, and introducing a new indicator on the issuance of Social Sovereign Bonds, the team has assessed this request and determined that the existing targets remain appropriate and attainable. Revising the Results Framework downward at this stage would not accurately reflect the project's trajectory and would risk understating its development impact. The proposed scope additions are expected to contribute to — rather than detract from — achieving the current targets, and no adjustment is therefore warranted. 14. The team has reviewed the existing intermediate results indicators and confirmed they remain sufficient to monitor implementation of the expanded scope. No changes to the results framework are proposed. Specifically, the new capital market activities under Sub-Component 1.4 contribute to PDO Indicator 1 (strengthening of the deposit insurance framework) and will be tracked through the existing intermediate indicator on regulatory and legislative improvements under Component 1. In addition, Sub-component 1.4 activities on capital market development will be monitored through a dedicated output measure within Implementation Status Reports: specifically, the number of capital market legal and regulatory instruments (including laws, guidelines, or supervisory frameworks) developed with project support. This output will be reported semi-annually and reviewed at each implementation support mission to ensure the sub-component remains on track within the project's broader financial sector development objectives. Support for the Credit Guarantee Fund's operationalization under Component 2 is captured by the existing indicator on PRSF activation and the volume of guarantees issued. NQI support under Component 3 feeds directly into PDO Indicator 3 on MSME value chain integration and will be reflected in the existing indicators on the number of MSMEs meeting export standards. Works under Component 4 are operational enablers for existing targets rather than a new programmatic area and therefore do not require a dedicated results indicator. Collectively, the expanded activities deepen the reach of existing pathways' results without introducing new development objectives that would require separate measurements. The restructuring introduces additional environmental and social issues and impacts associated with the PRSF Development Center's work and the construction of the access bridge over the Varzob River under Component 4 (Project Implementation, Coordination, and Management). As a result, while ESS1, ESS2, ESS9, and ESS10 remain relevant to the project, the relevance of ESS3 and ESS4 has increased because these site-specific civil works introduce environmental and social risks and impacts that were not anticipated in the original project design. Nevertheless, the Project is not expected to generate any large-scale, significant, irreversible, unprecedented, or cumulative adverse environmental or social impacts. 18. To manage the environmental and social risks introduced by the works, the Environmental and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP) will be updated prior to the commencement of any construction activities to reflect the expanded scope. The updated ESCP will be disclosed publicly in accordance with the Bank's Access to Information Policy. Site-specific Environmental and Social Management Plans (ESMPs) will be prepared for both the building rehabilitation and the bridge construction, addressing the principal risks identified for each site: dust and noise management during construction, prevention of water sedimentation and riverbank disturbance during bridge The World Bank Financial and Private Sector Development Project (P177722) Page 10 Official Use Only works, and community health and safety measures for the duration of the works. Given the PIU’s successful experience under the Rural Economy Development Project, which successfully completed four construction projects to rehabilitate cultural heritage sites, the Project Implementation Unit has demonstrated its capacity to oversee ESCP compliance, and E&S supervision will be incorporated into regular implementation support missions. The contractor will be required to implement the site-specific ESMPs as a condition of the works contract. Given the site-specific and time-limited nature of the risks and the absence of large-scale, irreversible, or cumulative impacts, the overall project of E&S risk classification remains Moderate. 19. The proposed works are limited in scale and footprint, are not expected to involve land acquisition, physical or economic displacement, significant labor influx, long-term community health and safety risks, or irreversible impacts on natural resources or sensitive receptors. Anticipated risks and impacts include construction-related environmental and social impacts such as dust, noise, vibration, construction and hazardous waste generation, temporary access restrictions, localized traffic disruption and traffic and road safety, water contamination and sedimentation risks affecting the Varzob River, occupational accidents, movement of workers, machinery and materials, labor influx–related risks, and temporary disturbances to nearby communities, businesses, and users of adjacent facilities. Social risks may also arise if project-affected parties and vulnerable groups are not adequately informed and consulted, or if safe access and grievance management arrangements are not maintained throughout the construction period. Additionally, these works will entail contractor management, labor, and working conditions. 20. These risks and impacts are expected to be site-specific, temporary, predictable, and manageable through implementation of the updated ESCP, site-specific ESMPs, Contractor ESMPs (C-ESMPs), and corresponding updates to the SEP and LMP. The restructuring will further strengthen environmental and social management through enhanced contractor management and supervision arrangements, integration of ESHS, including SEA/SH, requirements into procurement and contract documents, strengthened monitoring and incident reporting procedures, emergency preparedness and response arrangements, and targeted ESF capacity building and training for the MOF PIU, contractor, and supervising firm. 21. No significant indirect, cumulative, or long-term environmental and social impacts are anticipated under the activities proposed by the restructuring, as the PRSF Development Center works, and the access bridge construction is limited in scale, localized, and confined to existing developed or modified areas. The current and restructuring activities are not expected to result in significant future development, major land-use changes, labor influx, or other material downstream environmental or social impacts. Any temporary construction-related impacts are expected to be site-specific, reversible, and manageable through implementation of the site-specific ESMPs, Contractor ESMPs (C-ESMPs), and associated mitigation and monitoring measures in accordance with the ESF, GIIP, and national regulatory requirements. 22. Any relevant project alternatives considered to avoid, minimize, or mitigate adverse environmental and social impacts will be assessed and finalized during preparation of the site-specific ESMP for the PRSF Development Center and access bridge construction. This will include assessment of alternative construction methods, work sequencing, traffic management arrangements, spoil disposal and material storage locations, access management measures, and construction scheduling to minimize impacts related to occupational and community health and safety, labor influx, traffic disruption, water quality, noise, dust, and temporary disturbances to nearby communities and facilities. The MoF PIU has taken several measures to address environmental and social requirements under the Project. While the activities involving material, environmental, and social risks and impacts, including the newly introduced PRSF Development Center works and access bridge construction under the restructuring phase, had not yet commenced, the Project has established the key environmental and social institutional arrangements required under the ESCP. The Project Management Unit (MOF PIU) has been established within the Ministry of Finance with qualified environmental and social specialists recruited within the agreed ESCP timeframe to oversee environmental and social risk management and implementation of the ESF requirements. 24. A functional grievance redress mechanism (GRM) has also been established and remains operational. Component 1 (Deposit Insurance Fund) continues to pose no environmental or social risks, as it supports the National Individual Deposit Insurance Fund (IDIF) to improve depositor trust. Under Component 2 (Partial Risk Sharing Facility, PRSF), financing activities will continue to be implemented through Participating Financial Institutions (PFIs). In accordance with ESS9 and the ESCP requirements, each PFI must establish, maintain, and implement an Environmental and Social Management System (ESMS) for screening, due diligence, risk classification, monitoring, and reporting of subprojects financed under the Project. In line with the ESCP commitments, the MOF PIU prepared an ESMS and related environmental and social eligibility procedures for PFIs within the agreed timeframe following Project effectiveness. An ESMS has been cleared by the Bank so far. 25. All subprojects financed through PFIs will be screened against the applicable exclusion lists and eligibility criteria and will be subject to the approved ESMS procedures and, where required, Environmental and Social Action Plans (ESAPs). In relation to Component 3 (Supplier and Export Development Program — SEDP), the MOF PIU prepared a SEDP grants procedural manual that includes the environmental and social requirements, including ESMSs as relevant, which is mandatory. The SEDP will be finalized after the Bank's review, which will include the E&S aspects. Based on progress to date, the MOF PIU has demonstrated the capacity to plan and implement; however, this capacity will need to be maintained and further strengthened during implementation, particularly with respect to contractor management, supervision of works, implementation of the site-specific ESMP, and delivery of training to implementing entities, contractors, and supervising firms. The ESCP was updated as part of the restructuring process to reflect the revised scope of Project activities, including the E&S risk and impact mitigation measures for the new civil works under Component 4 (Project Implementation, Coordination, and Management). 26. Key stakeholders include implementing agencies, participating financial institutions, beneficiary MSMEs and SMEs, workers, users of facilities where the PRSF Development Center works and the access bridge construction will take place, and nearby communities where relevant. Particular attention will be given to people who may be affected, including facility users, workers, nearby residents or businesses, and vulnerable or underserved groups that may face barriers to accessing information or raising concerns. Stakeholder engagement and disclosure will continue to be carried out in accordance with ESS10 and Project SEP, which will be updated, as needed, to reflect the newly introduced works. The SEP will set out the mechanisms for consultation and disclosure of relevant environmental and social instruments, including the ESCP, the site-specific ESMP, and updates to the LMP, in a manner and language accessible to stakeholders. Consultations will be undertaken prior to commencement of the works and, as needed, during implementation, to inform stakeholders of the scope, timing, expected temporary impacts, access arrangements, and available grievance channels. Disclosure and engagement will use appropriate methods, including meetings with affected users and institutions, noticeboards at relevant sites, direct communication through responsible entities, and other accessible channels suited to the stakeholders concerned. A functional grievance redress mechanism will remain available to workers, beneficiaries, affected people, and other stakeholders to submit concerns and receive timely responses. Given the project's advanced stage of implementation — with 70 percent of funds disbursed and a closing date of September 30, 2028 — it is essential that environmental and social instruments are in place well in advance of work commencement to avoid any implementation delay. The Environmental and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP) and Stakeholder Engagement Plan (SEP) will be updated within 60 days of restructuring approval to reflect the addition of civil works under Category 11. Site-specific Environmental and Social Management Plans (ESMPs) for the PRSF Training Center renovation and the Varzob River bridge construction will be finalized and cleared prior to the signing of any works contracts. Procurement of works will not commence until ESMP clearance is confirmed. This sequencing provides the project with adequate preparation time while preserving a realistic works implementation window of approximately 18 months before project closing.
