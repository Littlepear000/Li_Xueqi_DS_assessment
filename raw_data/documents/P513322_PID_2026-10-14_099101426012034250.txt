Financial Management (FM) and disbursement arrangements are functioning effectively, Environmental and Social (E&S) performance is rated Satisfactory, and all legal covenants are being met. The Project Management Group has been established within MoSPFU, and an Operational Manual satisfactory to the Bank was approved within one month of the Effective Date of June 2, 2026. SPIRIT has demonstrated strong early implementation performance: within one month of approval, the Borrower submitted satisfactory evidence for achievement of three out of ten PBCs, triggering disbursements of US$303.75 million. Cumulative disbursements have reached US$598.75 million — approximately 70 percent of the total IBRD commitment — including an advance of US$295 million. These resources reimbursed expenditures on 18 social benefits delivered to over 760,000 people. Rationale for AF and proposed changes The GoU has requested the AF of US$327.975 million for SPIRIT to address financing needs and fiscal gaps arising from Russia's invasion of Ukraine. The rationale is threefold: (i) to provide fiscal space for the GoU to sustain and expand social assistance programs during a period of exceptional budgetary pressure; (ii) to address the financing gap identified at appraisal and enable implementation of key capacity-building and social service development activities under Component 2; and (iii) to scale up investments needed to continue digitizing and modernizing social protection administration and improve the efficiency and effectiveness of benefit delivery. Development Objective Original Development Objective (Approved as part of Decision package on 28-May-2026) The objective of the Project is to enhance the integration, access, and inclusiveness of Ukraine's social protection system. Components Changes to Project Components The Additional Financing (AF) for SPIRIT will introduce changes to both components of the project. Under Component 1 (Improving Social Support for the Poor and Vulnerable Groups), the AF will provide US$280 million in additional loan financing, backed by credit enhancement from the Advancing Needed Credit Enhancement for Ukraine Trust Fund supported by the Government of Japan. The funds will be used to (i) enhance results-based financing under SPIRIT through four new Performance-Based Conditions (PBCs) linked to progress in implementing the ambitious reform agenda supported by the original project; and (ii) reimburse expenditures of the Government of Ukraine (GoU) on 18 social benefits in the Eligible Expenditure Program (EEP) to provide immediate social relief to vulnerable groups. The new PBCs will be met upon: (a) expansion of the beneficiary base of six types of child benefits supported by the original project from 200,000 to 750,000 individuals; (b) provision of benefits under 39 programs administered by the Pension Fund of Ukraine, including the 18 EEP-financed benefits, to at least 2.5 million beneficiaries; (c) expansion of coverage of centrally funded social services to at least 1 million beneficiaries; and (d) provision of access to state budget financing for at least 500 service providers. Under Component 2 (Enhancing the Capacity of the Social Protection System and Developing Social Services), the AF will provide US$25 million in grant funds from the Ukraine Relief, Recovery, Reconstruction, and Reform Trust Fund (URTF) with contributions from Germany and the United Kingdom. Of this amount, US$20 million will close the financing gap identified at the time of the original project approval by supporting capacity-building activities at the central level and community-based activities at the local level. An additional US$5 million will support scaling up digital development activities.
