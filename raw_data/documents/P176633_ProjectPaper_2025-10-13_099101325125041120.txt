PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Project background and status 1. The Mali Electricity System Reinforcement and Access Expansion Project (Yelen Sira; P176633) ("Project”) was approved by the International Development Association (Association or IDA) Board on June 23, 2023. The Project has three sources of financing and three corresponding legal agreements: (i) Financing Agreement between the Republic of Mali (Recipient) and the Association - Credit No. 7376-ML, Credit No. 7377-ML (IDA Financing Agreement); (ii) Financing Agreement between the Recipient and the Association acting as an Accredited Entity of the Green Climate Fund (GCF) - Loan No. TF0C1876-ML, Grant No. TF0C1681-ML (GCF Financing Agreement); and (iii) Grant Agreement between the Recipient and the Association acting as an administrator of Energy Sector Management Assistance Program (ESMAP) Multi-Donor Trust Fund - (Grant No. TF0C1586-ML) (ESMAP Grant Agreement), each dated June 30, 2023. 2. The Project includes four components as follows. (a) Component 1: Reinforcement of grid infrastructure and access expansion, US$144 million (IDA US$112.5 million; GCF US$31.5 million). This component finances critical grid infrastructure and new connections through the upgrading and expansion of transmission and distribution networks in Bamako and its perimeter, upgrading and expanding of resilient distribution networks and new connections outside of Bamako in several priority secondary cities, to reduce system losses, improve grid efficiency, increase reliability of electricity supply, increase access, and absorb additional generation capacity, including generation from solar parks to be supported by the Project. It also includes the provision of transaction advisory services to mobilize independent power producers (IPPs), through competitive bidding, for the construction of solar PV power plants, and construction of the public grid infrastructure required to connect the future solar PV plants to the existing grid. (b) Component 2: Green mini-grid and off-grid electrification of selected localities in rural areas, US$49.5 million (IDA US$37.5 million; GCF Credit US$11.0 million; ESMAP US$1.0 million). The activities contribute to reducing disparity of access to electricity services between urban and rural areas and providing cleaner and more affordable electricity for socioeconomic development in rural areas. The component aims to deploy technologies and new business models that would reduce fossil fuel use, save costs, tap into local renewable energy resources, and ensure more reliable service provision. (c) Component 3: Institutional Capacity Building and Implementation support, US$7.5 million (IDA US$7 million; GCF Grant US$0.5 million). The component finances activities to create a robust institutional arrangement to implement the proposed activities and to support the energy sector’s overall development. It strengthens the capacity of the Ministry of Energy in sector planning, resource mobilization, and overall oversight of the Project’s implementing agencies: the utility Energy du Mali (EDM-SA) for Component 1 and the rural electrification agency AMADER for Component 2. (d) Component 4: Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC). A CERC with zero allocation may be used to contribute to an emergency response through the timely implementation of activities in response to an eligible national emergency. 3. The Project is effective since February 29, 2024, after two effectiveness deadline extensions at the request of the Recipient, from October 28, 2023, to December 29, 2023, then to February 29, 2024. To date, the implementation has not started yet as the Recipient expressed interest earlier in revising the initial Project design to remove private sector participation1, with a formal request sent to the World Bank on May 23, 2025. Given the long pause since the Project approval in June 2023 and the significant changes proposed, the World Bank suggested to the Recipient to cancel the 1 The Recipient explained having issues with private developers but did not share many details on the causes and responsibilities. Since the Project implementation was delayed for the reasons mentioned above, the PIUs accepted to move forward until they have agreed contracts to be signed pending the Ministry of Economy and Finance’s (MEF) authorization of the Project implementation. The recruitment status is as follows. (a) EDM-PIU was recruiting four (4) additional staff to reinforce fiduciary and safeguard functions, in accordance with the Project implementation support plan: a Senior Accountant, a Financial Management Specialist, a Procurement Specialist, a Junior Environmental Specialist, and a Junior Social safeguard Specialist. All the ToRs have been reviewed and accepted by the World Bank and the selection process was supposed to be finalized by June 30, 2025. (b) For AMADER-PIU, five (5) additional staff members would need to be recruited: a Procurement Specialist, a Senior Accountant, an Environmental Specialist, a Social Safeguard Specialist, and a Gender-based Violence (GBV) Specialist. All ToRs have been reviewed and accepted by the World Bank team and the selection process was planned to be completed by June 30, 2025. (c) Similarly, the recruitment process of an External Audit firm for both PIUs (in charge of the external audit of both Project Implementing Units, process managed by EDM-PIU) for 2024 – 2026, and an Internal Auditor for both PIUs (process managed by AMADER-PIU) was underway. The recruitment process was impacted by the delays on the implementation phase and was expected to be finalized by June 30, 2025. (d) The recruitments mentioned above were placed on hold as the PIUs were advised not to sign any contract prior to the MEF’s authorization to start the Project implementation. As the Project is being cancelled, these recruitments will be terminated. 5. Regarding Project components, EDM-PIU prepared ToRs for the recruitment of the owner’s engineer that will assist the PIU oversee the works planned on the transmission network (subcomponent 1.1). ToRs were accepted by the World Bank team in September 2024, after a series of comments addressed by the PIU. The formal approval would be provided through the World Bank Procurement system. Finally, the World Bank team reviewed and accepted the ToRs for a Transaction Advisor that would assist the Recipient in the selection of an Independent Power Producer to develop least- cost Solar Photovoltaic (PV) plants, as part of subcomponent 1.2. However, because of the Recipient willingness to revise the Project design and remove this activity, formal approval was not provided yet. 6. Additionally, a Project restructuring was expected to address the funding allocation issue with the GCF US$43 million contribution. The World Bank and GCF teams discussed and agreed on a reallocation plan that would be implemented during the next restructuring. B. Rationale for restructuring 7. Following the Project approval on June 2023, the World Bank worked closely with the Recipient to get the effectiveness conditions completed so that the implementation phase can start as soon as possible. Despite these efforts, the Recipient needed more time to complete those conditions as they requested an effectiveness deadline extension twice, from October 29, 2023 to December 29, 2023, and from December 29, 2023 to February 29, 2024. 8. On February 27, 2024, while the respective World Bank and the Recipient teams were actively coordinating to finalize the Project effectiveness declaration, the World Bank received a letter from the Recipient about their request to revise the Project design. The desired revisions were related to the planned mini grids to be built in collaboration with the private sector. The Recipient declared that the mini grids would be handed over to EDM for operation instead of private operators, and a financing agreement amendment request will be sent to the World Bank to that effect. Unfortunately, Official Use Only Page 2 The World Bank Mali Electricity System Reinforcement and Access Expansion Project (YELEN SIRA) (P176633) despite the World Bank numerous reminders and meetings on that subject, the Recipient sent the restructuring request only on May 23, 2025, detailing the proposed changes as follows: removal of the Project’s subcomponent 1.2 (Transaction advisory services to mobilize IPPs for the construction of least cost solar PV power plants and construction of grid infrastructure to connect the solar power plant to the grid) and component 2 (Green mini-grid and off-grid electrification of selected localities in rural areas), and reallocation of the associated financing to the construction of a new publicly funded solar PV plant. 9. On June 4, 2025, the World Bank responded to the Recipient recommending the cancellation of the Project given the significant proposed changes that would affect the PDO and the long pause since the Board approval in June 2023, and the preparation of a new operation that will take into account their current expectations. The new operation will be a Multi-Phase Programmatic Approach (MPA) to allow longer-term strategic support over a period of 10 years. 10. On august 14, 2025, the Recipient acknowledged receipt and confirmed acceptance of Yelen Sira cancellation and expressed their desire to reallocate the financing to the construction of a publicly funded solar PV plant at the Bougouni site as their main priority. 11. Based on the above, the current restructuring is prepared to cancel 100% of the Project total financing and close it. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 12. The proposed changes include loans and grants cancellation and a revision of the loan closing date. 13. Loans cancellation. It is proposed to cancel the total Project financing. As a result, there will be no more financing allocation to disbursement categories and the Project will be closed upon approval of the restructuring. 14. Loan closing date.
