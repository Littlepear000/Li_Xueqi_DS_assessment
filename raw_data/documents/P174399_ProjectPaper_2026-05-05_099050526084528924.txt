OPERATION STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks Division Director approval to introduce changes to the results (Disbursement- Linked Indicator (DLI) Framework and Results Framework) and category allocations of the Human Capital Recovery & Resilience Program. The proposed changes would apply to the original financing (IDA Credit 7029-BT) and first additional financing (AF) (GPE Grant TF0C1597) and are considered suitable in ensuring completion of activities and achievement of the Development Objective (DO). A. Operation Status 2. The Operation supports building, protecting, and utilizing human capital in Bhutan. Its interventions across the lifecycle, address three critical results areas for the human development (HD) sector – (i) People and Programs (enhancing capacity and quality of HD service providers and interventions), (ii) Facilities (enhancing physical infrastructure and connectivity of HD service locations), and (iii) Digital (enhancing information management systems of HD services). Implemented by the Ministry of Education & Skills Development (MoESD), Ministry of Industry, Commerce & Employment (MoICE) and Ministry of Health (MoH), the Operation provides financing to strengthen education, employment, and health services in the country. 3. The original financing and first AF provide results-based financing, via ten disbursement-linked indicators (DLIs), to a Program addressing services for early childhood care and development (ECCD) (US$4.5 million under DLIs 1 and 2), school education (US$8.0 million under DLIs 3, 4 and 10), skills development (US$6.5 million under DLIs 5, 6 and 9), and entrepreneurship and employment (US$3.5 million under DLIs 7 and 8).1 DLIs 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 and 9 have been fully met. 4. The second AF (IDA Credit 7718-BT, IDA Credit 7719-BT and IDA Grant E419-BT) deepens the support for ECCD (US$4.5 million under DLI 11), school education (US$13.0 million under DLIs 12, 17 and 19), skills development (US$5.0 million under DLI 13), and entrepreneurship and employment (US$10.7 million under DLIs 14, 15 and 20), while expanding the Program to the health sector (US$13.0 million under DLIs 16, 18 and 21).2 5. The Program has supported upskilling of the entire ECCD workforce and continues to promote higher qualifications – diploma and bachelor degrees – to further enhance service quality. It has concurrently supported the expansion of services in rural areas, and to deprived and poor communities, helping to finance the construction and renovation of ECCD centers across the country, and enabled greater access by households eligible for such services. 6. Primary and secondary schools have benefitted from the adoption of a competency-based curriculum, improved digital pedagogy for all teachers, and better internet connectivity enabling access to remote learning facilities. These efforts continue to be strengthened by additional upskilling for the teaching workforce, and further expansion of digitized learning platforms. To further address foundational learning, MoESD is integrating climate-resilient features into school infrastructure to enhance the learning environment and safeguard students from climate change impacts. In addressing the school-to-work transition, the Program has supported the establishment of the Skills Development Program (SDP) as part of the country’s technical and vocational education and training (TVET) landscape, which has adopted a gender-focus, while promoting climate-responsive trades. The SDP has significantly scaled up outside of the urban hubs of Thimphu, Paro and Phuentsholing to address spatial inequalities and has progressively demonstrated increased employability of its graduates. Going forward, these efforts are being supported by continuous reorientation of courses towards priority sectors and improved promotion of TVET/SDP. 8. Employment and entrepreneurship services have benefitted from Program support initially with the establishment of the Bhutan Labor Market Information System (BLMIS) which set up a single intake window for employment programs and the establishment of an “employment responsibility system” across economic clusters to further ensure appropriate data flows for decision-making. SDP graduates have benefitted from wage subsidies under the Youth Engagement & Livelihoods Program (YELP) while employment service center (ESC) services across various districts have been streamlined to ensure uniformity, efficiency, accountability, and accessibility. The Program has also supported the establishment of business incubation centers (BICs) and government entrepreneurship program graduates access financing opportunities towards providing self-employment opportunities. Efforts are being made to further improve job matching facilities via various tailored programs and improved BLMIS functionalities, as well as by providing financing for small businesses. 9. In the health sector, critical upgrades of health facilities are underway with Program support. Investments are being made in the continuous professional development of the health workforce, while digital delivery of services is being improved with interoperability arrangements being made across various healthcare systems. 10. The Operation is currently in its fourth period (i.e. fiscal year) of implementation. Its original financing and first AF will close on June 30, 2026, while its second AF will close on June 30, 2029. DO Achievement Progress and Implementation Progress have both been rated “moderately satisfactory” for over twelve months, and the Operation has disbursed (i) 93.1 percent of its original financing, (ii) 63.4 percent of its first AF, and (iii) 6.1 percent of its second AF. There are no outstanding audits, and all legal covenants are complied with. B. Rationale for Restructuring 11. The Royal Government of Bhutan (RGoB) has requested a restructuring to modify selected DLRs to realistically align their trajectories with broad socioeconomic trends and administrative changes across government. These relate to the decline in absolute numbers and composition of SDP program participants since the COVID-19 pandemic period, and civil service reforms in late-2022, which have respectively impacted DLI 5 (SDP Inclusion) and DLI 8 (Employment Support). Additionally, a reallocation from the IPF Component, i.e. the “Project” part of the Operation, to the Program part, is intended to ensure optimal utilization of the financing. 12. In 2023 and 2024, 77.8 percent and 78.1 percent of national diploma (ND) holders found employment within six months of graduation from their TVET program, compared to 52.9 percent and 49.2 percent of national certificate (NC) holders during the same period.3 The gap in employability rate between ND and NC holders, as well as the increasing rate year-to-year for ND graduates and declining rate for NC graduates, has necessitated a shift in skills training – from fewer or more focused NCs to the introduction of more rigorous ND programs – to better align qualifications with employer preferences. While the policy shift has been geared towards improved labor market participation, with efforts made to provide female-friendly courses, there has been a declining share of female enrollment in TVET (including SDP). At the same time, there has been a significant decline in overall TVET (including SDP) uptake, from 4,810 in 2023 to 3,019 in 2025.4 Therefore, a revision of DLRs under DLI 5 (SDP Inclusion) which intended to drive a growing share of female graduation from SDP, would be required. The revisions would aim to ensure steady female participation in skills training, rather than an increase in female graduation which has been impacted by external factors. The affected DLRs represent a value of US$0.75 million. 13. DLI 8 (Employment Support) intended to establish linkages between the BLMIS – administered by MoICE – and information systems managed by relevant ministries and agencies. Following the establishment of the Government Technology Agency (GovTech) as the nodal agency for all digital technology initiatives across RGoB, development of new, and enhancements to existing, e-government systems have been revisited. The originally expected establishment of system linkages, supported by this DLI, is therefore expected to take a longer time horizon. In the meantime, GovTech has supported MoICE in developing more rigorous protocols for systems interoperability, which would ensure a more robust data ecosystem for labor market-related decision-making. An adjustment to the relevant DLR would realistically align it with developments across RGoB while maintaining the original objective of improving data coordination to enhance employment services. The affected DLR represents a value of US$0.25 million. 14. Due to the significant underutilization of the IPF Component, a reallocation of part of its undisbursed balance to the Program is necessitated to ensure this financing does not lapse. The financing at risk is US$0.34 million. An adjustment to the allocated value of DLI 10 (WASH Facilities for Education) would thus ensure that the funds can be disbursed to RGoB and ultimately contribute to the part of the education sector supported by the Program. Additionally, the Results Framework would require adjustments to baselines and targets of selected results indicators, given the availability of data which was previously unavailable. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 15. DLI Framework: Changes will be made to three DLIs, with revisions to selected DLRs. (a) DLI 5 (SDP Inclusion) will have two DLRs modified. (i) Period 2 (FY 2024) DLR “(a)” will be revised from “20% increase in percentage of female trainees completing SDP over the baseline established in Period 1” to “40% female graduates of TVET/SDP cumulatively”. (ii) Period 3 (FY 2025) DLR “(a)” will be revised from “40% increase in percentage of female trainees completing SDP over the baseline established in Period 1” to “40% female graduates of TVET/SDP cumulatively”. (b) DLI 8 (Employment Support) will have one DLR modified. Period 2 (FY 2024) DLR “(b)” will be revised from “established system linkages and data sharing protocols between MoICE and select economic cluster sectors in line with the Employment Responsibility System (ERS) Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)” to “data sharing arrangements across economic clusters defined in approved technical documents”. (c) DLI 10 (WASH Facilities for Education) will have one DLR modified.
