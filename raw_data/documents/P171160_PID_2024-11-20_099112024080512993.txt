Introduction and Context 1. Somalia has been rebuilding state institutions and the economy since the end of the devastating civil war, with strong support from the international community. However, conflict and violence remain high, with a rise in conflict-related incidents and fatalities this year. Climatic shocks, particularly drought, have worsened existing fragility, compounded by global food supply and price pressures, leading to displacement and heightened food insecurity, which obstructs poverty reduction efforts. In addition, fragility has resulted in nascent state institutions with limited capacity, which in turn has hindered the delivery of basic services and has resulted in a fractured social Page 2 The World Bank Somali Integrated Statistics and Economic Planning Capacity Building (P171160) – Additional Financing Request 2 contract. Large and sustained investments in human and physical capital are essential for resilience and inclusive growth. According to the 2023 Somalia Poverty and Equity Report, over 54% of the population lived below the national poverty line of $2.06 per day in 2022. Despite these challenges, Somalia reached a historic Heavily Indebted Poor Countries (HIPC) Initiative Completion Point on December 13, 2023, and joined the East Africa Community on November 24, 2023, becoming a full member on March 4, 2024. Federal Government of Somalia (FGS) urgently needs evidence-based policies and national strategies for Somalia in the post-HIPC era. The current National Development Plan (NDP-9) ends in December 2024, and while progress has been made, more work is needed to integrate Somalia into the global economy. The Ministry of Planning, Investment, and Economic Development (MoPIED) is now drafting Somalia's five-year National Transformation Plan (NTP) for 2025-2029. 2. Building a reliable statistical system is integral to Somalia’s transition from fragility to shared prosperity. A statistical system supports evidence-based policy making, greater accountability, and improved business opportunities. Given its Fragility, Conflict, and Violence (FCV) status and limited fiscal space, Somalia needs continuous support—financial and technical—in developing its statistical system. The Bank has been supporting Somalia though an International Development Association (IDA) Grant in the amount of US$25 million (SDR 18.3 million) which was approved on June 30, 2020, and became Effective on September 29, 2020. 3. Despite the recent success of the parent project, Somalia still faces data gaps. The data and statistics produced by the original project have been useful for the HIPC progress and monitoring of the National Development Plan 9 (NDP9). For example, the FGS completed the HIPC Completion Point Triggers on Statistical Capacity early in the process. Data from the Somali Integrated Household Budget Survey (SIHBS) 2022, along with the poverty analysis, were used by the FGS to monitor the NDP9 and by the World Bank and International Monetary Fund to prepare Heavily Indebted Poor Countries Initiative (HIPC) completion documents. However, Somalia still faces considerable data gaps in its development process.1 Somalia can only report on 84 out of 215 applicable indicators to monitor progress on the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). The FGS requested an Additional Financing (AF) to address remaining key data gaps like in agriculture and livestock data, which is central to the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) compilation and to measure sustainable development goals and the support for national development plan, but also to strengthen and expand data systems in other thematic areas. 4. Somali women experience severe disparities in access to economic resources and endowments compared to men.2 Efforts to address these disparities are hindered by a lack of key gender statistics, including those listed in the United Nations Statistics Division’s (UNSD’s) Minimum List of Gender Indicators. The AF will continue to support the government in narrowing gaps in the production, analysis and dissemination of gender data, enabling the effective design, implementation and monitoring of policies and programs that address persistent gender disparities, including the NTP 2025-2029, the Human Capital Development Strategy (HCDS), and Vision 2060. The AF will scale up the production of gender statistics initiated by the parent project, particularly by supporting new rounds of the SIHBS and Somali Integrated Business Establishment Survey (SIBES), and the first nationally representative Multi-Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) since the civil war. Page 3 The World Bank Somali Integrated Statistics and Economic Planning Capacity Building (P171160) – Additional Financing Request 2 5. Somalia is highly exposed to climatic shocks. Somalia, a fragile state, is one of the most vulnerable countries in the world to climate change, particularly in key sectors such as agriculture and livestock.3 Frequent droughts and other climate shocks highlight the country's low resilience to climate risks. The AF aims to gather critical data on climate impacts, build resilience, and support evidence-based policymaking, capacity building, and adaptation efforts. Building on the parent project, which strengthened Somalia's statistical system, the AF focuses on addressing climate change impacts, enhancing mitigation, adaptation, and resilience, while guiding effective policy formation and decision-making in key sectors. The main climate-related risk associated with the project is climatic events affecting planned data collection activities. To mitigate these risks, the project will utilize Computer-Assisted Personal Interviewing (CAPI) and satellite data to minimize the time needed in the field and reduce exposure to these risks. 6. The proposed AF is aligned with Somalia Country Partnership Framework (CPF) 2024-2028. The AF will continue to support a portfolio-wide approach to data adequacy, quality, and transparency (para 63). The original project will support CPF’s Cross-cutting Foundational High-Level Objective (HLO): Macroeconomic Stability and Governance Foundations by improving core government systems and capacity and strengthening macroeconomic and fiscal management. The new areas of focus also coincide with the three HLOs of the CPF. Finally, the future rounds of SIHBS, which will be supported by this AF, will provide data to measure 6 HLO indicators in the CPF results framework. 7. The proposed AF is aligned with the Paris Agreement on both mitigation and adaptation, and it is consistent with Somalia’s National Adaptation Plan (NAP) and other national climate policy instruments. Somalia’s current GHG emissions are relatively low, estimated at 53.70 MtCO2eq, representing less than 0.03 percent of total global emissions. Somalia’s NAP and Somalia’ Updated Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) issued in July 2023 set an ambitious emissions reduction target of 30 percent below business-as-usual by 2030, conditional on international public and private support. The proposed project will contribute to this aim by establishing fundamental data infrastructure and facilitating data collection to partially monitor this target or to address climate change, in terms of adaptation and mitigation. Thus, the proposed project will promote climate change mitigation by reducing the need for in person data collection (and thus the associated vehicle emissions) as much as possible by utilizing satellite imagery. 8. The HCDS will aim to institutionalize service delivery, boost public spending, and prioritize human capital. Project Description 12. The AF will support the scaled-up activities of the parent project components, revise the Project Development Objective (PDO) to reflect this expansion, extend the project closing date, and adjust the results framework. It will also include the addition of Somaliland in the project scope. The project closing date will be extended by 36-month from August 31, 2025, to August 31, 2028. Proposed AF activities include: (i) the scale-up of Component 1 to support future data collection to strengthen fundamental data needs and fill sex-disaggregated data gaps on climate change, human capital, and private sector development, each of which will have a new sub-component; (ii) scale-up support under Component 2 to expand the investment in Information and Communications Technology (ICT) Development and Equipment to support statistical activities in Somaliland and FMS; and (iii) scale-up support under component 3 to support the national development plan and strategies and build capacity of the public sector to use data to address challenges on climate, human capital, and private sector development. 13. Project Components and Costs 15. Component 1: Strengthening Coordination, Statistical Infrastructure and Data Development of the National Statistical System (NSS). This component will include additional rounds of existing surveys and introduce new surveys to address critical data gaps. It will focus on climate, private sector development, strengthening the administrative data collection and analysis, and human capital. Specifically, it will fund an extra round of the SIHBS and one Integrated Business Establishment Survey (IBES), as well as one round of the MICS. The extra round of the SIHBS will include climate modules to assess how households and businesses are affected and what adaptation they are undertaking in response. Additionally, it will support an Agricultural Census, and a national mapping exercise to contribute to master sampling frame for the country. These activities will include training for staff across the NSS, including FGS and FMS statisticians. Component 1 of the Project involves capacity building through on-the-job training through survey implementation and analysis, as well as formal classroom training. 16. New activities are described below: • Sub-component 1.2: Somali Integrated Household Budget Survey. The upcoming 2026 round will largely follow the methodology of the 2022 SIHBS, while incorporating improvements based on lessons learned. To better capture seasonality, estimate Consumer Price Index (CPI) weights more accurately, labor statistics required for human capital development and national accounts, and assess the impact of climate shocks, the survey will be Page 7 The World Bank Somali Integrated Statistics and Economic Planning Capacity Building (P171160) – Additional Financing Request 2 conducted year-round and the questionnaire will be designed in line with the updated COICOP 2018.10 Additionally, a climate module will be included to offer new insights into household exposure, vulnerability, and resilience to climate shocks. • Sub-component 1.3: Strengthening Sources of Business and Administrative Data needed for the Compilation of National Accounts and Key Social Statistics. Under this sub-component, the AF will fund an additional round of the Integrated Business Establishment Survey, as well as activities to update and extend the coverage of the Statistical Business Registry. This increase in coverage covers the inclusion of Burao city in the Toghdeer region as well as 6 other cities. The SIBES-II economic survey should be continued at low cost, utilizing online and telephone surveys for 30,000 establishments willing to participate remotely, along with a sample of larger firms and informal sector businesses. This approach minimizes costs while providing essential data for business statistics and GDP estimates. The Statistical Business Register (SBR), created in 2024 using census and administrative data in Somalia's main cities, can be maintained and expanded to add more cities and select rural areas by leveraging public and private data sources and more recent surveys. The results from these rural areas can be extrapolated to others, adjusting for safety and business activity. • Sub-component 1.4: Strengthening Human Capacity in Poverty, CPI and National Accounts Statistics. Key new activities will include: o Price Statistics: A) Upgrade technology and infrastructure for CPI, in all FMS and for SNBS, notably including a transition from paper-based to computer-assisted data collection in Puntland and Somaliland. Revise the representative consumption basket using the additional SIHBS round, expand geographical coverage, a revision of the outlet sample and improve quality assurance. Implement web scraping techniques for CPI compilation. B) Design and pilot Producer Price Survey based on business census results. o GDP Estimates: Improve GDP estimates through the production and expenditure approaches using new establishment-based statistics and the additional SIHBS round. Align Somali National Accounts with SNA 2008 and EAC guidelines through technical assistance. • Sub-component 1.6: Climate change and environment statistics. o Agricultural Statistics. Agricultural sector in Somalia is heavily affected by climate change. Despite being more urbanized than other low-income and SSA countries, agriculture remains vital to Somalia’s economy. Livestock exports are a key source of foreign exchange. Since the civil war, Somalia has lacked an Agricultural Census or Survey. The AF will fund these, gathering data on farm types, yields, resources, input usage, and climate resilience practices. The surveys will also assess climate change impacts and help design climate-smart agricultural practices. Somalia lacks the resources for quality environmental statistics, economic accounts, and climate adaptation tools. The NSS also faces fragmented spatial data. To address this, the AF will fund an environmental statistics plan and a GIS center at SNBS to enhance data sharing and tackle climate risks.
