KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions Overall Implementation Status The project continues to make satisfactory progress toward achievement of the PDO. Since the previous ISR, implementation has advanced across the statistical capacity and production, and economic management components. Major statistical operations have been completed or have reached advanced stages, while new statistical programs and production processes have been established to support the regular production of socioeconomic data. Progress has also been recorded in macroeconomic modelling, budget planning and debt management. These achievements represent an important improvement in Djibouti’s capacity to produce and use timely and reliable statistics for decision making. Construction of the new MEFI/INSTAD building remains the main implementation challenge and accounts for most of the project’s undisbursed balance. Procurement and contract award were completed by June 22, 2026, and the site has been formally handed over, but works have not yet begun because the Government has not transferred its US$1 million counterpart contribution to the contractor. The minimum 16-month construction period now exceeds the 15 months remaining before project closure on December 30, 2027, leaving insufficient time for completion and formal acceptance even with immediate payment. Priorities are therefore to secure the transfer, clarify the authorities’ plans, and agree on feasible options for the building that remain consistent with existing contractual and financing obligations. Implementation weaknesses remain concentrated in financial management, internal audit and procurement management. The internal audit function is not yet fully effective, and further action is required to strengthen related arrangements. The Bank team continues to engage closely with the PIU to address these weaknesses while maintaining close oversight of the building contract. Progress by Component Component 1: Strengthening INSTAD’s institutional and statistical capacity Implementation under this component remains strong. The project supported the publication of the final results and thematic reports of the third Population and Housing Census, the publication of the third National Strategy for the Development of Statistics for 2025–2029, the completion of data processing for the fifth Djiboutian Household Survey (EDAM5), and the establishment of Djibouti’s first quarterly labor force survey. Progress has also been made in strengthening economic statistics, including the production of national accounts and preparation of quarterly national accounts. These activities have expanded the availability of current demographic, social, labor market, poverty and economic statistics and strengthened INSTAD’s capacity to undertake major statistical operations. The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Economic Management and Statistics Development for Policy Making (P171777) Oct 14, 2026 Page 3 of 13 Official Use Only Progress also continued on the Economic Census (REGED), a key operation aimed at establishing Djibouti’s first comprehensive statistical business register and strengthening the production of enterprise statistics. Preparatory activities, including the recruitment and training of field staff, acquisition of equipment and vehicles, deployment of the data collection infrastructure, and completion of the nationwide enumeration phase, have been completed. The Economic Census is expected to provide an updated statistical basis for analyzing Djibouti’s productive sector and support the regular production of business statistics. The project continued to support INSTAD’s institutional development through staff recruitment, specialized training and improvements in statistical planning and governance. As of June 30, 2026, INSTAD had recruited 67 new staff, including 18 women, strengthening the Institute’s technical capacity in statistics, economics, demography, GIS, communication, and information technology. Component 2: Modernizing economic and fiscal management Progress under Component 2 remains satisfactory. The project has supported the development and operationalization of macroeconomic forecasting and simulation tools that inform the 2025–2030 National Development Plan. All 24 planned macro-modelling missions have been delivered, and the upgraded quasi-macro model is being used to support the national planning framework. The project has supported the introduction of sectoral medium-term expenditure frameworks in six pilot ministries, contributing to a more structured link between sector priorities and budget planning. It has also strengthened public investment monitoring through the geo-referencing of public infrastructure assets. In debt management, the project has supported training on SYGADE and the establishment of a multi- institutional framework for debt sustainability analysis and preparation of the Medium-Term Debt Strategy. Continued follow-up is needed to ensure that these tools and coordination arrangements are fully institutionalized and regularly used by the authorities. 5.
