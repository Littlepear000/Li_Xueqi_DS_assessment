PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Project Status 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the approval to restructure the Yemen Desert Locust Response Project (DLRP). The proposed restructuring consists of an extension of the closing date by six months, from June 29, 2024 to December 27, 2024. This would be the second extension of the closing date of the project, with cumulative extension constituting 12 months. 2. The project was approved on June 17, 2020 and became effective on July 14, 2020. The Project Development Objective and Implementation progress (IP) are rated Moderately Satisfactory. Project funds have been fully disbursed to the Implementing Entity – Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). According to the financial projections presented to the Bank during the last implementation support mission in March 2024, the project would have around US$ 1.16 million in unspent funds. Most project activities have been completed and outputs have been delivered to intended beneficiaries, as reported by FAO. One procurement of flatbed cargo trucks has been pending since July 2023. 3. The project development objectives (PDO) are to control the desert locust outbreak, support livelihoods in locust-affected areas and strengthen Yemen’s preparedness for future locust infestations. In the context of Yemen, the expected outcomes of the project are described as follows: (i) a response to the locust infestation outbreak encompassing improved locust surveillance, monitoring, and control activities; (ii) livelihood support focusing on protection and restoration in the locust-affected areas; and (iii) strengthened national preparedness systems including three main elements—establishing a network of Desert Locusts Control Centers, establishing an early response system, and connecting Yemeni systems with regional networks to exchange information and data and improve coordination of the locust response. 4. The project is expected to achieve its objectives through the following components: a. Component 1 - Surveillance and Control Measures, which aims to improve the capacity for surveying and surveillance of breeding and infestation areas and for gathering meteorological data, support locust control activities (spraying), and implement risk mitigation measures for the workers and affected communities. b. Component 2 - Livelihood Protection and Rehabilitation, which includes two types of measures: (i) creation of temporary employment opportunities, and (ii) restoration of lost assets. c. Component 3 – Coordination and Early Warning Preparedness, which aims to strengthen the national capacity for early warning and early response, linking these efforts to regional locust surveillance and control networks thereby enhancing climate resilience. d. Component 4 – Project management, which covers the costs associated with project management, such as implementation support, financial management, procurement, Page 1 The World Bank Yemen Desert Locust Response Project (P174170) monitoring the project environmental and social aspects, and overall monitoring and evaluation. 5. The main achievement under Component 1 (Surveillance and Control Measures) has been the extensive surveillance and monitoring, which considerably exceeded the project’s target, generating valuable information about locust breeding areas and movements. This information is important for locust monitoring not only in Yemen, but also for the entire region covering North Africa, Middle East and Arabian Peninsula. Cumulatively, under the project 3,473,064 ha have been covered with 30,403 survey stops, including a total area of around 1,000,000 ha covering the hot spots for desert locust swarms, breeding groups and spreads. With closing of the project, these vital monitoring and surveillance activities might be discontinued, as Yemeni national authorities may not have funds for desert locust surveillance. This might lead to consequences beyond Yemen, as desert locust data collected in Yemen helps update the global forecasting models and analyze the movement of desert locust. Furthermore, 62 Toyota Hilux pickup trucks – 30 for the South and 32 for the North were procured and delivered to strengthen desert locust monitoring and surveillance capacity by establishing mobile monitoring teams. Third-party verification procedures were put in place to mitigate risks of vehicle misuse. The third-party verification of vehicles was conducted in December 2023-March 2024 (inclusive), and regular updates were shared with the Bank. 6. Under Component 2 (Livelihood Protection and Rehabilitation), eleven land and water infrastructure sub-projects were completed in four governorates. A total of 5,367 workers were involved with 121,240 person-days of inputs. FAO estimates that the total number of direct beneficiaries, i.e., people who directly benefit from labor or from the improved community assets, is 20,820. As part of focusing on rehabilitating agricultural and pastoral livelihoods, more than 5,485 affected households were supported with agricultural input packages, such as feeds, seeds, livestock, and beehives. 7. Under Component 3 (Coordination and Early Warning Preparedness), the project has established the desert locust early warning system, which was assessed by FAO to be operational. In addition to monitoring systems and staff capacity building, the system preparedness also requires proper infrastructure and facilities in place, which are supported by the project, and include construction of five desert locust control centers (DLCC) and a rehabilitation of pesticide warehouse. All infrastructure activities have been completed and handed over to the relevant desert locust authorities, as reported by the FAO. Delays in completion of the infrastructure activities were reported in late 2023, as some DLCCs were not fully completed by the original closing date of the project. The project was extended by six months to June 29, 2024 to ensure that all DLCCs are completed and handed over to the authorities. The latest financial reports of the project are up-to-date and deemed Satisfactory. The project applies the single audit principle, and no financial audit report is overdue. B. Rationale for Restructuring 9. The FAO has requested an extension of the project for six months to primarily resolve the issue around the pending procurement of seven flatbed cargo trucks. The extension would also aim to complete the implementation of any remaining items of the action plan agreed between FAO and the World Bank in December 2023. A key focus of the action plan – the finalization of the DLCC construction – has been completed. As agreed with the Bank, the FAO cancelled the procurement of remaining vehicles (beyond the 62 already procured), except for seven flatbed cargo trucks. The FAO requested an extension for six months to finalize the steps needed to address this issue. 10.In addition, the extension would allow to continue the project-financed desert locust monitoring and surveillance activities, generating locally and regionally valuable information for desert locust outbreaks. The project’s main achievement has been extensive surveillance and monitoring, generating valuable information about locust breeding areas and movements. This information is important for locust monitoring not only in Yemen, but also for the entire region. 11.The Task Team confirms that the requirements of the World Bank Policy on Investment Project Financing have been met; namely: (i) the PDO remains achievable; (ii) the performance of the Recipient (FAO) remains moderately satisfactory; (iii) the World Bank and the Recipient (FAO) have agreed on actions to be undertaken to complete the project on time (an action plan has been prepared and agreed upon); and (iv) the Project is not subject to suspension of disbursements. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 12. Closing date extension. The proposed restructuring would extend the project closing date from June 29, 2024, to December 27, 2024. The extension of the closing date would allow finalizing the resolution of the pending procurement of seven flatbed cargo trucks. Activities focusing on desert locust monitoring and surveillance would continue, utilizing project financial savings. As part of the action plan, FAO will present concrete steps, acceptable to the Bank, for finalizing the pending procurement within the extended six months of the project. 13.The project implementation arrangements and project management will remain the same. Other than the extension of the project closing date, the proposed Level 2 Restructuring does not entail any changes in allocation of the Grant amounts, scope or nature of project Page 3 The World Bank Yemen Desert Locust Response Project (P174170) objective(s) or activities, implementation arrangements, or any other amendments to the Grant Agreements. There are also no changes to the project’s E&S instruments and risks. To the extent possible, including access to the project sites, the third-party monitoring activities will continue, specifically focusing on the monitoring and verification of the vehicles and other inputs provided by the project. Summary of Performance of E&S Risk Compliance 14.The FAO has managed the project with a dedicated Project Management Unit PMU, which includes specialists in environmental and social risk, gender-based violence, and environmental safeguards. The FAO submitted and received approval for Environmental and Social Risk Assessment and Clauses (ESRAC) for various livelihood subprojects, as well as Environmental and Social Management Plans (ESMPs) and Pest Management Plans and for DLCCs and desert locust control operations, respectively. Overall, the FAO took measures to ensure occupational health and safety during limited bio-pesticide applications and construction of DLCCs. No incidents were reported during limited bio-pesticide applications for desert locust control. A work-related incident at a DLCC construction site was reported to the Bank, albeit with considerable delay. The FAO handled the incident according to the relevant procedures and guidelines. However, due to a shortcoming to fully adhere to safety measures at the construction site and a delay in reporting the incident to the World Bank, the E&S performance was downgraded to moderately satisfactory. 15.In addition to managing environmental and social risks, the project emphasized capacity building, training, and community awareness, with a wide range of training programs and awareness sessions that have reached a large number of beneficiaries, including women. The FAO regularly updated and disclosed its Stakeholder Engagement Plan, demonstrating a commitment to extensive engagement and consultation. Labor conditions have been closely monitored. The Bank assessed that the Grievance Mechanism (GM) was in place and majority of the reported cases were resolved. The project also took steps to prevent gender-based violence (GBV) through coordination, meetings, and training sessions. Lastly, the Security Management Plan was updated to address specific security risks and ensure the safety of workers and compliance with occupational health standards.
