PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Project Status 1. The Pakistan Crisis-Resilient Social Protection (CRISP) Program, was approved by the Board of Directors on March 25, 2021 and is currently in its fourth year of implementation with a closing date of June 30, 2027. The US$970.45 million IDA Credit is implemented by the Benazir Income Support Programme (BISP) and aims to address gaps identified during the COVID-19 emergency response, while expanding coverage to protect human capital and introducing mechanisms to mitigate risks for vulnerable populations not covered by regular safety net programs. The Additional Financing (AF), approved in June 2024, builds on the original program’s three result areas and supports further institutional reforms, system upgrades, and harmonization of federal and provincial social protection initiatives. 2. The program comprises three result areas: (i) Building crisis-resilient delivery systems; (ii) Developing Innovative Hybrid Social Protection Scheme; and (iii) Protecting human capital in the early and primary school years. The AF supports the adoption of recertification protocols for BISP programs, institutionalization of indexation mechanisms for cash transfers, extension of program outreach to the Union Council level, and measures to correct overlaps between federal and provincial CCT programs, particularly in Punjab and Sindh. 3. Overall progress under the CRISP is rated satisfactory, with most targets for the project development objective (PDO) and intermediate results indicators either achieved or on track. To date, under Result Area 1, the dynamic update mechanism for the National Socio-Economic Registry (NSER) is operational in all Tehsils, with over 22 million households’ data updated. The beneficiary-centric payment system is being finalized, while interim arrangements with multiple payment service providers is functional. In Result Area 2, the hybrid social protection scheme was launched and the design has been adjusted to cater scale based on field-based feedback. Service providers for the scheme are being onboarded to accelerate program uptake in the coming months. Under Result Area 3, the Taleemi Wazaif education CCT has expanded to all 160 districts, with over 10 million children ever enrolled, and the Nashonuma nutrition CCT has exceeded its coverage targets, with 3,645,160 number of ever enrolled children and mothers. Measures to harmonize federal and provincial CCTs are underway, with ongoing dialogue to resolve duplication and clarify roles, however this requires efforts beyond BISP, utilizing Ministry of Finance’s leverage and discussions around NFC. Key activities under the program’s action plan have been completed, including the implementation of an electronic file management system, approval of revised data sharing protocols, improvements to the grievance mechanism (GM), and the initial rollout of e-waste management Standard Operating Procedures. All actions have been completed except for the hybrid scale-up strategy, which is pending the results of the program’s evaluation that has been initiated. 4. The institutional arrangements remain robust, with BISP serving as the focal implementing agency and demonstrating strong capacity in program delivery, data management, and stakeholder engagement. The program has benefited from regular implementation support missions, which have commended BISP’s progress and internal assessments. The overall risk of the operation stands at “Moderate”. The program’s E&S and Fiduciary risks are rated as moderate, given the focus on system strengthening and technical assistance in these areas. 5. Ratings: The last Implementation and Status Results Report (August 2025) rated Progress towards achieving the development objectives and Implementation progress Satisfactory. The project is rated Satisfactory for both procurement Page 1 Official Use Only The World Bank Pakistan Crisis-Resilient Social Protection (CRISP) (P174484) and financial management. The program continues to maintain robust fiduciary arrangements, including timely submission of interim financial reports and annual audits. For environment and social safeguards, the project is rated as Satisfactory. 6. Disbursement: Overall program disbursements currently stand at US$704.44 million (73% disbursed) with an undisbursed balance of US$256.38 million. The original financing has disbursed around US$559.44 million, while the AF has disbursed around US$145 million. The pace of disbursement under the project is largely on track. There are no overdue audit reports or ineligible expenditures, and the financial management and procurement systems remain adequate and effective for the scale and complexity of the program. B. Rationale for Restructuring 7. This is the second restructuring of the project. The first restructuring was approved in September, 2022 to respond to flood emergency by adding DLI 12 that supported cash transfers to the affected vulnerable population. The purpose of the current restructuring is to revise the project’s Results Framework and indicators in line with the recently adopted World Bank Corporate Scorecard (CSC), following specific guidance on social protection measurement per the World Bank Group FY24- 30 Scorecard Methodology Note. The note advises the adoption of standardized Social Safety Net (SSN) Corporate Results Indicators (CRIs) with a focus on reporting beneficiaries as individuals rather than households or families with specific gender and youth breakup. These changes will ensure consistency, improve data aggregation, and align project-level results with the Bank’s corporate targets, such as reaching 500 million poor and vulnerable people and 250 million women with social protection programs by 2030. By retrofitting the Results Framework through restructuring, the CRISP will more accurately reflect World Bank corporate reporting requirements, avoid undercounting and increase transparency and accountability.
