STRATEGIC CONTEXT Strengthening statistical capacity and closing data gaps is key to formulating and monitoring policies to accelerate attainment of development and regional integration among participating countries 1. Good quality data – timely, reliable, accurate, detailed and accessible – are an integral part of good governance and contribute to key development objectives. The World Development Report 20211 highlights three institutional pathways that link data to development. First, public actors including governments and international organizations use data to support evidence-based policies and improve service delivery. Second, civil society uses data to monitor government policies and individuals use data to monitor and access services. Finally, private firms use data to improve production processes and grow their productivity, thus contributing to wider economic growth. Thus, people (society, governments, and firms) need to transform data into useful information to improve lives; without such a transformation, data do not support development. 2. Data are therefore essential to inform and monitor policies to maximize their development impacts . Such data are key to efficiently allocate resources under constrained fiscal space, to track progress, and assess the impacts of public programs and policies. Statistical production is also a pillar in the construction of systems to implement multi- sectoral programs, for instance to combat chronic malnutrition or manage disaster risks. When accessible to the civil society, data promote transparency and accountability in policy making, essential elements of good governance and public trust. 3. Good-quality, reliable, and harmonized statistics also contribute to the regional integration agenda. Inclusive growth and poverty reduction can be accelerated through closer regional collaboration and integration by expanding access to markets and generating more investment, innovation and competition.2 While Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) has made progress in economic integration, the pace has been slow. Strengthening regional economic blocks like Southern African Development Community (SADC) is important to accelerate sub-regional integration. Building SADC data infrastructure is crucial for generating evidence and knowledge about the benefits and costs of regional integration to inform implementation of regional policies at the national level. Finally, regional collaboration in data production will ensure that countries fulfill regional quality standards in their data production, including harmonization and better comparability, and thus contribute to the regional data infrastructure and policy making towards broader and more efficient integration. 4. The project seeks to modernize participating countries’ data ecosystems by addressing gaps in data production and use. Maximizing the value of data in development requires narrowing data and statistical capacity gaps through addressing both the demand and supply sides of the challenge. This requires “… long-term, stable financing of data; investments in statistical and technical capacity; and laws conducive to safe data production and reuse. Other areas that must be addressed include […] policy makers’ lack of incentives for and interest in using data, low trust in the quality of public intent data, and lack of infrastructure for accessing and using the data.”3 The proposed project seeks to strengthen the data governance framework by supporting the generation and use of data in a safe, ethical, and secure way, while also delivering value equitably. 1 World Bank, 2021 2 Choi et al. 2020. The project is closely aligned with (i) International Development Association (IDA) 20’s policy package and Special Themes, (ii) the World Bank’s Data for Policy (D4P) initiative and (iii) the Regional Integration and Cooperation Assistance Strategy (RICAS) for SSA.4 Working at national and regional levels, the actions proposed under this project will enable more regular and better quality data from a set of IDA countries with sizable and active programs, including supporting better availability of disaggregated data, especially sex- and disability-disaggregated data, and facilitating IDA20 to meet commitments to monitor and report on these and other dimensions of development. The D4P “package” includes production of a core set of economic, social, and sustainability statistics to monitor and evaluate public policies. By collecting data that improve the understanding of the regional social and economic progress and support national and regional integration planning and monitoring, this project contributes to three objectives of the RICAS for SSA, namely: (i) develop functioning regional markets in priority sectors; (ii) scale up access to quality public services and entrepreneurship through complementary regional solutions; and (iii) strengthen the connection between regional policy commitments and national planning. Overall, the activities will also help set the stage for National Statistical Offices (NSOs) in other countries to address key statistical gaps, creating potential regional synergies for progress beyond the immediate countries involved. 6. A regional approach will bring regional technical guidance on harmonization and national production of statistics closer to international best practices. Adopting a regional approach to statistical capacity and production that brings national and regional organizations closer will accelerate the production of harmonized statistics by aligning the incentives and funding. The implementation of the D4P package at the regional level will close data gaps with harmonized data compliant with international standards. This, in turn, will contribute to effective policy making and monitoring of progress, regional integration and achievement of global targets by improving quality and availability of data including internationally comparable gender and disability statistics. A regional approach will also facilitate coordination among NSOs by introducing and expanding innovations, peer-to-peer reviewing, and learning that can generate positive externalities. Prevention, mitigation and recovery of regional and global crises like Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) also require a regional concerted approach to set up emergency observatories establishing rapid data collection capacity and big data capabilities. A regional approach on statistics can also contribute to regional integration as comparable statistics are instrumental in areas such as promotion of free trade and creation of a unique currency, as well as meeting the SADC priorities. However, GDP per capita growth in SSA has been weak and erratic, averaging around two percent per year, and has been falling in recent years. 8. Economic and social development progress faces multiple cyclical and structural constraints. The COVID-19 pandemic caused a global recession in 2020 that led to the first increase in global poverty since the 1998 Asian financial crisis.7 In SSA, an estimated 26 to 40 million people were pushed into poverty as a result. More recently, Russia’s invasion of Ukraine exacerbated the spike in already increasing food and fuel prices. Structurally, high fertility rates perpetuate cycles of poverty by keeping dependency ratios high, investment in human capital low, and constraining female productive employment.8 High inequality is strongly linked to poverty, especially by structural factors such as isolation, underdeveloped markets and uneven access to them, and conflict. The region is also highly exposed and vulnerable to climate-related shocks, which have disproportionate impacts on the poorest populations. 9. These constraints are salient among the proposed project’s participating countries – Comoros, Madagascar, Malawi, Zambia–four SADC member states,9 and São Tomé and Príncipe.10 Using the poverty line of $US2.15 per day, the most recent estimated poverty rates in these countries vary between 15.6 percent in São Tomé and Príncipe to 70.1 percent in Malawi. Together, they are home to over 37 million poor people, 12 percent of the entire population of the SADC region (and 24 percent of the SADC region’s poor population). These countries have a high common reliance on primary sectors and high vulnerability to climate shocks, which time and again cause setbacks to their economic progress. Moreover, geographic poverty traps, such as high rural populations with low access to services and connectivity, are pervasive among them (Figure 1). The poverty reduction agenda can only be accelerated in this region through the implementation of evidence-based economic and social policies in a wide range of sectors, such as macro-financial to social, environmental, productive, and so on. The project seeks to strengthen the intersection of priorities for statistical development in the African Union (AU) Strategy for Harmonization of Statistics in Africa (SHaSA2) 2017-2026 and the SADC RSDS 2020-2030. SHaSA2 is a continent-wide initiative that the African heads of state adopted to address the constraints faced by African statistical systems and promote the regional integration agenda. Its principles guide all African regional organizations, regional integration communities, and regional development organizations that support statistical development in Africa. It is also timely because in recent years African governments and the international community have called for an improved effort to monitor international and regional development commitments, such as the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and the AU’s Vision 2063. It is implemented using a cascading approach whereby regional economic blocks and countries develop more specific regional and national strategies for the development of statistics, consistent with the higher-level strategies and frameworks (Figure 5). The SADC RSDS provides a strategy for building statistical capacity and production of harmonized regional statistics that are used for policy, planning, decision-making, monitoring, and reporting on development progress. The RSDS has the harmonization of regional statistics as one of its strategic intervention areas and can therefore be used as a regional body for this project’s implementation. By harmonizing economic statistics, filling the gap on important data, and producing harmonized SDG estimates, the project will be a powerful means of implementing the RSDS. Other intervention areas of the RSDS include institutional and organizational development, digital transformation of regional statistics, and statistical capacity building. The data value chain involves a process of data creation and use that incorporates analysis, dissemination, advocacy, and finally use to create impact (Figure 7). According to Open Data Watch27 the value of data increases as it moves through the production cycle into analysis, and ultimately as evidence to inform policy, planning and decision- making processes and their impact. Its essence is creating a process to transform raw data into actionable information. The project follows a clear theory of change to support SADC members and more generally SSA countries to strengthen national and regional statistical systems (Figure 8). Specifically, it begins with the identification of key challenges: data production gaps including core socio-economic statistics, lack of expertise in certain growing areas such as big data, partial integration of administrative data into official statistics; limited advocacy for data use; and a narrow capacity of key government ministries to use data to monitor economic progress and inform national policy decisions. The activities supported by this project tackle these challenges from multiple angles – beyond financing necessary surveys, the project invests in increased capacity in NSOs and dedicates resources to strengthening a more proactive dissemination and use of data. At the SADC level, increased capacity also results in higher ability to deliver support to all member states to improve their country statistical systems. This broader approach is expected to generate key outputs designed to increase capacity regionally and in country and strengthen the ability of technical units in governments to inform policy. Expected outcomes are improvement in statistical capacity, increased efficiency in data production and increased availability of statistics. These will increase public confidence in official statistics and improve their use for policy and decision making, as well as increase transparency and accountability. 62. There are several challenges at the regional and country level to improve data production and analysis. At the regional level, this includes a limited production of national core statistics, disaggregated statistics (including by poverty status, gender, disability, and so on), uneven implementation of statistical quality frameworks and principles within national statistical systems, and low comparability of national statistics at the regional level. The project incorporates recommendations from the Independent Evaluation Group (IEG) report Data for Development - An Evaluation of World Bank Support for Data and Statistical Capacity.35 Particularly, following Recommendation 2, the project intends to mobilize and deliver additional support to countries’ statistical systems, using a more comprehensive model of statistical capacity building that also factors in needs and opportunities to strengthen administrative data systems. Further, the project is incorporating Recommendation 4 to scale up promotion of data sharing and data use by ensuring that all data financed by the World Bank are shared widely by incentivizing governments to share more data with the public and scaling-up promotion of government and citizen demand for data and the voice of data users in the kinds of data that are produced. This project continues previous Statistical Capacity IPFs in Madagascar, São Tomé and Príncipe, and several grants provided to the other countries to fill critical data gaps and build a modern and effective national statistical system. In addition, the project works with SADC to leverage their network and thus to benefit indirectly other SADC member states. 82. Project outcomes include enhanced and improved statistics on economic growth, prices, labor market, employers, agriculture, fishing, and household welfare. Good quality data is expected to also contribute to improved governance and accountability. Strategic Relevance 83. The project activities are strategically relevant and timely. Moreover, experience from past projects in three of the five countries shows that investments have delivered stronger NSOs and more commitment from governments to invest in their own statistical capacity. The regional integration agenda is also a strong push factor for countries to adopt harmonization guidelines to monitor results and increase accountability at the SADC level. Both at the continent and regional levels, there is awareness of the necessity to produce a wide range of comparable, accurate and timely statistical information for policymakers and the public, as is apparent in SHaSA2 and the RSDS. As indicated in the World Development Report 2021, because of the non-rival character of data and the implications for their limitless reuse, it is inherently difficult to place an economic value on data, although many attempts have been made to do so. In view of today’s increasingly sophisticated application of machine learning and artificial intelligence to drive data-based innovations, it is quite conceivable that the economic value of unanticipated secondary uses of data may far exceed the value of the use for which they were originally collected. Furthermore, the project does not lend itself to conventional economic cost-benefit analysis due to the public good nature of its output. However, it is important to notice that improved quality and relevance of statistical production contribute to more informed evidence-based decision-making related to the different sectors covered by the project. This may also lead to the reduction of risks inherent in inadequate economic policy decisions. Official statistics are expected to be a public good, and as such data production does not generate any direct financial benefit. Therefore, only the public sector has an incentive to provide them. In fact, while the private sector can and does collect specific statistics, they are unlikely to invest in a complete statistical system. Left to the market there would be significant underinvestment in Page 45 of 120 The World Bank SADC Regional Statistics Project (P175731) statistics, thereby foregoing the benefits of better statistics. However, official statistics are key to evidence-based decision-making and can generate and leverage multiple economic and social benefits in and across countries. For this reason, the project is not amenable to financial analysis. 89. Furthermore, several studies show the importance for economies, particularly in developing countries, to promote open data and free access to micro and metadata of various sources, including national account data, price data, business survey data, social sectors data, etc. A 2019 study by the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD) titled “Enhancing Access to and Sharing of Data” presented the available evidence of the direct and indirect economic and social benefits of data access and sharing37. The diverse types of benefits include greater transparency and empowerment of users, new business opportunities, competition, and co-operation within and across sectors and nations, crowdsourcing, and user-driven innovation, and increasing efficiency. Evidence also shows that data access and sharing can generate positive social and economic benefits for data providers (direct impact), their suppliers and data users (indirect impact), and the wider economy (induced impact). However, even if quantifying the overall benefits of data access and sharing is difficult to assess or measure, studies by sector (public versus private sector) provide a rough estimate of the magnitude of the relative effects of data access and sharing. They suggest that data access and sharing can increase the value of data to holders (direct impact), but it can help create 10 to 20 times more value for data users (indirect impact) and 20 to 50 times more value for the wider economy (induced impact). Finally, these studies suggested that data access and sharing can help generate social and economic benefits worth between 0.1 percent and 1.5 percent of GDP in the case of public sector data, and between 1 percent and 2.5 percent of GDP (in few studies up to 4 percent of GDP) when also including private sector data. 90. In the case of the participating countries in this project, most of the advantages enumerated above apply. The project in contributing to the production of accurate, more reliable, and quality and shared data will help the countries to attain their goals and most particularly, availability of data can help, for instance, countries to have access to international financial markets (Eurobonds) at a better interest rate, as the project will induce, among others, better rating of these countries by agencies such as Standard’s and Poor (S&P), Moody, etc. Financial Management 91. Advancing gender equality in all spheres of society is therefore likely to accelerate progress toward shared prosperity and other development goals, including the SDGs. The lack of relevant data and statistics constrains the appropriate assessment of the extent of gender disparities in the participating countries - preventing adequate policy formulation to address those gaps. Accurate and timely gender disaggregated statistics are needed to measure the extent of gender inequality and formulate adequate policies to address the various gender gaps still prevailing in the subregion. The improvement of gender statistics will be achieved by introducing gender dimension in surveys and statistics from administrative sources, using the most recent data collection methodologies and tools. 114. A simple country-level GRM will be developed and implemented to receive and address grievances from affected households or other citizens and specific procedures shall be developed to receive and address grievances from all project workers (including consultants, PIU specialists, and field teams, as described in the Labor Management Procedure). The household and citizens GRM will contain procedures to address (i) grievances related to SEA/SH with provisions for confidentiality and support to survivors and (ii) address the cultural specificities of pre-identified vulnerable local communities. ANNEX 1: IMPLEMENTATION ARRANGEMENTS AND SUPPORT PLAN COUNTRY: Comoros, Madagascar, Malawi, São Tomé and Príncipe, Zambia, and Southern African Development community (SADC) SADC Regional Statistical Capacity Building Project This Annex provides an overview of institutional and implementation arrangements and articulates the World Bank’s approach to help borrowers achieve project results based on the requirements of the specific operation and the risk profile. This annex has sections on: (i) project institutional and implementation arrangements, (ii) financial management, (iii) disbursement, (iv) procurement, (v) strategy and approach for implementation support, and (vi) the implementation support plan and resource requirements. Project Institutional and Implementation Arrangements The Project will have two levels of intervention, at national, and regional levels, as outlined in the project description. Correspondingly, a two-layered project implementation approach is followed. The proposed implementation arrangement follows the successful model for the EAC Statistics Development and Harmonization Regional Project (P164243) and Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa (P169265) IPF projects and their lessons. Financial Management Overall Financial Management assessment. INE, INSTAT, ZamStats, SADC, NSO, AFAP and INSEED will be responsible for implementing the project under their respective components. Most of the agencies have prior experience in managing WB funded projects and others with experience in managing other donor-funded projects. A Financial Management Assessment (FMA) was undertaken to evaluate the adequacy of the proposed project financial management arrangements. The overall FM residual risk was assessed as Substantial. Even though implementing agencies are conversant with WB requirements, there is a need to be strengthened in terms of staffing, systems, and actual implementation of existing procedures; budget monitoring; strengthening internal audit function. Other weaknesses identified at the agencies are lack of control over census activities, particularly low control on cash transfer operations and poor assets’ management system in previous operations, lack of proper accounting software for transaction processing and reporting. The mitigation measures have been identified and recommended to ensure acceptable FM arrangements that will result in proper use of the proceeds of financing. Specific Financial Management arrangements.
