PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. This Restructuring Paper proposes to extend the closing date of the Catalyzing Enterpreneurship for Job Creation Project (CEJC) by 18 months from June 30, 2025 to December 31, 2026. This is the first restructuring of the Project. 2. Project Overview. The Project is a US$200 million IBRD loan. It was approved by the World Bank (WB) Executive Directors on April 17, 2019, and declared effective on January 15, 2020. The Project Implementing Agency is the Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprise Development Agency (MSMEDA). The current closing date is June 30, 2025. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to foster job creation and improve other economic opportunities for targeted beneficiaries. The Project comprises the following three components: a. Component 1: Financial Support to MSMEs (US$146 million) b. Component 2: Risk Capital for Innovative Startups and High Growth SMEs (US$50 million) c. Component 3: Business and Capacity Development (US$3.5 million) 3. The Project is complemented by the Accelerating High Growth Entrepreneurship (HGE) Eco-system in Egypt (Accelerate Egypt) Trust Fund (TF) Program with Euro 5 million from the Government of Italy through the Italian Agency for Development Cooperation and implemented by the WB. The objective of the Accelerate Egypt TF Program is to bridge the key gaps in the HGE eco-system in Egypt by building the capacity and linkages of key eco-system actors that enable the acceleration of high-growth entrepreneurship in the country. The Program is closely linked to Component 2 of MSMEDA’s US$50 million Fund-of-Funds program that aims to mobilize private sector participation in early-stage HGE financing. 4. Project Status. The PDO is measured by two main indicators: (i) the number of entrepreneurs receiving financing through the Project, and (ii) the number of jobs created through the Project. As of February 2025, the actual results for both PDO indicators have exceeded the end targets: For the first PDO indicator, 219,918 entrepreneurs have received financing through the Project against an end target of 85,200. For the second PDO indicator, 472,252 jobs have been created through the Project against an end target of 100,000, of which 37 percent are for women. MSMEDA’s Fund-of-Funds has invested in 11 early-stage VC and related funds (accelerators, seed funds, early-stage, and growth stage VC funds) in Egypt, and expects to invest in a total of 15 funds by June 2025. MSMEDA’s funds have invested in over 140 Egyptian startups and HGEs. Overall, the project has disbursed more than 99 percent of the loan. Pending commitments mainly pertain to Components 2 and 3. 5. The Project is generating catalytic effects. The Project initiated the Egypt’s first Fund of Funds program. It has thus far mobilized US$155 million from other commercial investors, including international development finance institutions (DFIs). This comprises US$99 million in private sector capital (exceeding the end target of US$ 89 million) which represents a leverage ratio of approximately 3.5x to commercial funding. Financial Management and Audit Status. Financial Management (FM) is rated Satisfactory. MSMEDA has consistently adhered to the agreed FM arrangements including the timely submission of the project’s quarterly Interim Financial Reports (IFRs) and annual project’s audited financial statements. Regarding this operation, currently, there are no overdue audits or IFRs. 8. Rationale for Restructuring. A closing date extension will allow additional time for MSMEDA to fully complete the implementation of activities under Component 3 and the Italy-funded Accelerate Egypt TF Program, as well as to finalize the Administrative Agreement (AA) with Qatar, a potential development partner, to raise additional financing (in the form of a grant) to support the institutional strengthening and sustainability plan for MSMEDA. The PDO remains achievable, performance is satisfactory, and an action plan has been agreed upon with the borrower. There are two reasons for the extension of closing date: (i) Allow the project to receive the potential grant from Qatar to support MSMEDA’s sustainability plan/efforts, in the form of additional financing; (ii) allow additional time for MSMEDA to complete some delayed activities under Component 3, as well as activities funded by the Italy-funded Accelerate Egypt TF Program. 9. While Component 3 constitutes a very small amount (1.75 percent) of the Project, MSMEDA has been slow in implementing fully this component, as well as developing the required comprehensive and timely work plans, budgets and procurement plans for Component 3. Several activities and actions under the work plan have not been initiated while others have been delayed. In addition, the Accelerate Egypt TF Program, which complements the Component 2 of the Project, also encountered delays in launching the program.1 A couple of initiatives under the Program are still in the early stages of implementation and need additional time to complete. So far, only 16 percent of the total TF resources have been disbursed and the additional implementation time will ensure full utilization of the resources. 10. Furthermore, Qatar (through Education Above All and Silatech Foundations) has expressed interest to establish a TF to support Egypt’s jobs agenda.
