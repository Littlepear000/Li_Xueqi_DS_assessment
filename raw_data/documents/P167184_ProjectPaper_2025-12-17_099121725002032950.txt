PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. This restructuring paper seeks the approval of the Division Director for a Level Two restructuring of the Improving Access to and Value from Health Services in PNG: Financing the Frontlines Project (IMPACT Health, P167184, IDA credit 64970). This restructuring will facilitate the completion of critical UN-implemented contracts, repurpose financing from non-performing disbursement-linked indicators (DLIs), and provide adequate implementation time to enable the project to fully disburse and achieve its project development objective (PDO) of “increasing the utilization of quality essential health services in Project supported provinces.” The proposed restructuring includes: (a) a no-cost extension of the closing date by 23 months from June 30, 2026 to May 31, 2028; (b) a reallocation of US$ 1.51 million equivalent from Component 2 to sub-component 1.1; and (c) a revision of the DLIs and results framework (RF) to reflect the changes made to components. The World Bank received a formal restructuring request from the Government of Papua New Guinea (GoPNG) on November 17, 2025. This is the second restructuring of the Project (previously on December 2023). A. PROJECT STATUS 2. IMPACT Health is financed with International Development Association (IDA) credit of US$30 million to the Government of Papua New Guinea and was approved by the World Bank Group Board of Directors on 22 April 2020. Previous restructuring: The Project was restructured in December 2023 to address implementation delays and limited progress by reallocating funds, revising project components, and updating targets and indicators to better support essential health services in selected provinces. Key adjustments include the following : (i) Reallocation of Funds: $4.275 million was reallocated from Component 2 (performance-based financing) to Component 1.1, increasing the budget for rehabilitating and equipping primary health care facilities in project provinces to address emerging needs and higher costs (ii) Component and Subcomponent Adjustments: Subcomponent 1.2 was renamed and refocused from digital innovations to “Strengthening community-based service delivery”, supporting maternal and child health incentives, and improving outreach planning; and component 2’s DLIs were revised, including shifting target dates by 12 months, canceling Year 5 DLIs, and updating the number of participating provinces (iii) Results Framework and Indicators: The results framework was updated to align with the new project structure, including new indicators, the removal of some indicators, and modifying the results framework and disbursement-linked indicators to reflect new timelines and project realities (iv) Timeline and Implementation: The mid-term review has been postponed from December 2023 to June 2024 to allow more time for implementation after restructuring; and the designated account ceiling has been increased from PGK 200,000 to PGK 800,000 to ease cash flow for project activities. Considerable progress has been made toward achieving the PDO.1 Four out of the six PDO indicators are on track to be achieved with service delivery interventions advancing at scale in all four target provinces.2 For PDO1,3 of the 156 health facilities assessed, 153 (98 percent) have demonstrated substantial improvement in quality scores compared to the baseline, with 85% now meeting the minimum quality threshold. Moreover, quality scorecard results have continued to improve despite increasingly stringent DLI targets set for Year 4. For PDO34 and PDO5,5 significant improvements in the number of antenatal care and pentavalent 3rd dose were observed, with 10–13 PHAs improving by 1–38% over baselines following the introduction of competitive DLIs in year 3. This initiative also provided other non-target provinces the opportunity to participate in improving key service delivery indices. 6 PDO47 recorded an 81% increase in outreach clinics (16,541 ) up from the baseline of 9158. PDO28 is expected to be fully achieved through implementation of the contract between National Department of Health (NDOH) and United Nations Office for Project Services (UNOPS) which is currently undergoing Government approval. PDO6,9 however, is off track due to unreliable national data and is proposed to be dropped through this restructuring. Consequently, DLI 11 on the ‘increase in the number of registered TB patients (all forms) on treatment who have been cured’ will also be dropped (further discussion below). 5. Coverage of essential health services is low, and coverage and utilization of many vital services is stagnant or declining with significant disparity in health service delivery especially between rural and urban areas. PNG’s coverage of essential health services is low for its level of income. Coverage of at least four antenatal care visits is only 29%, whilst that of health facility births (42%) and pentavalent-3 immunization (41%) remain below 50%. Low and declining rates are attributed to several challenges, including distance to facilities, cost of treatment, and lack of essential medical supplies. The rural terrain impedes effective distribution of medical supplies and access to health services and presents a challenge in attracting and retaining health personnel. Many access and utilization indicators have stagnated or declined in the last few years. Maternal and child mortality rates remain high for the country’s economic development status and immunization rates low, a tracer indicator for basic population health. Diphtheria, Tetanus and Pertussis vaccine for children under two for example is only at 42%.13 The challenging geographical terrain as well as lack of security undermines the coverage, affordability, and quality of vital health services, leading to poorer health outcomes, which in turn hamper 13 https://immunizationdata.who.int/dashboard/regions/western-pacific-region/PNG Official Use Only Page 4 The World Bank Improving Access to and Value from Health Services in PNG: Financing the Frontlines (P167184) human development, livelihoods (jobs or incomes), and economic growth. A strong primary health care system is critical for reversing poor health outcomes. 16. IMPACT Health remains highly relevant and is perceived by NDOH as a flagship health project that strengthens the country’s primary health care system. Aligned with the corporate indicator to reach 1.5 billion people with health, nutrition, and population services, the project provides the highly needed support to improve quality and access essential health services in PNG by ensuring health facilities are ready to delivery services and performance incentives are in place to drive service coverage and improvement in service delivery quality. Since the first restructuring in December 2023, disbursement has increased by 33% showcasing solid implementation progress. 17. Rehabilitating primary healthcare facilities under Component 1 directly advances a key government priority and aligns with the recently approved National Health Infrastructure Investment Plan 2025–2035. The condition of health facilities nationwide remains poor with 30% to 60% of health facilities from level 2 and level 4 in PNG requiring significant upgrades according to the National Health Plan 2021-2030. Under IMPACT Health, the project will introduce a model to rehabilitate health facilities and purchase and deliver medical equipment at scale. The initial World Bank technical assistance from the PNG Strategy Partnership Multi-Donor Trust Fund (US$1.386 million) which completed a comprehensive health facility audit in the 31 health facilities in East New Britain have shown that some health facilities will require a full knock down and rebuild. In addition to rolling out the health facility audit in 3 additional provinces and rehabilitating health facilities, Component 1 activities will include the possibility of rebuilding using a modular-type health facility within the same land footprint that can be scaled and replicated. Lessons learned will be used to inform future and partner investments in health infrastructure. 18. The NDOH is presently pursuing the utilization of existing IDA resources to expedite efforts aimed at increasing access to operational L2–L4 health facilities, thereby improving the quality of healthcare services. By repurposing financing, the Government intends to leverage project experience and local market dynamics to facilitate a rapid response and ensure swift disbursement of resources to address mounting challenges. Pursuant to an agreement with NDOH, unachieved and cancelled DLIs will be reallocated to Component 1 to support the two large-value UN contracts. To accommodate the contract implementation period, a no-cost extension of the project for 23 months is required. This proposal was discussed and formally endorsed by the special Project Steering Committee on October 8, 2025, following a request from NDOH to the Department of Treasury, which subsequently communicated its endorsement to the World Bank on November 17, 2025. This approach aims to capitalize on the established capacity of UNOPS operating in PNG to enable rapid deployment of solutions, while also unlocking further resources to support a broader initiative for healthcare workforce upskilling. A no-cost extension of the closing date by 23 months from June 30, 2026, to May 31, 2028. Revisions to Project Components and Costs 21. Reallocating US$1.51 million equivalent from Component 2 to sub-component 1.1 to support scalable investments in primary health care facilities in Project provinces. The sub-componnet 1.1 envelope will be increased to Official Use Only Page 5 The World Bank Improving Access to and Value from Health Services in PNG: Financing the Frontlines (P167184) US$ 10.785 million, bringing the total Component 1 envelop to US$ 18.185 million. The additional funds will help to bridge an emergent financing gap for the rehabilitation of primary health facilities given the anticipated needs and costs associated with the reaching remote, rural health facilities. The PCU will revise the existing ESMF to incorporate any additional or emerging environmental and social risks and corresponding mitigation measures. Furthermore, awareness training will be provided to UNOPS staff regarding the reconstruction of health facilities that require demolition and rebuilding.14 22. The US$ 1.51 million reallocation is facilitated by changes to the DLIs: (i) the cancellation of Year 3 and 4 targets for DLI 11 “increase in the number of registered TB patients (all forms) on treatment who have been cured’’ due to lack of reliable data (US$ 0.5 million equivalent); and (ii) reprogramming of funds allocated for unachieved year 3 DLIs (US$1.01 million equivalent) due to the implementation challenges mentioned above. The proposed changes will be reflected in the updated DLI matrix and verification protocol and subsequently the DLI Operations Manual. Reallocation between Disbursement Categories 23. Changes to the Results Framework 24. The RF is updated to align with the changes summarized above and the following: a. Adding baselines for the three Expanded Provinces (East Sepik, Simbu, and Central) which were added in year 3 of the Project for all PDO and intermediate indicators and aligned targets as necessary. b. Adding in the corporate scorecard indicator ‘People receiving quality health, nutrition, and population services (Number of people)’. c. Cancelling the PDO TB indicator ‘Increase in the number of registered TB patients (all forms) on treatment who have been cured’. The IMPACT Project has faced challenges due to its reliance on reliable and verifiable data to report against the DLI 11 “percentage coverage rates of the number of registered TB patients (all forms) on treatment who are cured” has proven to be a challenge given the data capturing and recording system for patient level data is not accurate and in many cases the numerator (those cured) is larger than the baseline cohort of registered TB patients on treatment (denominator).
