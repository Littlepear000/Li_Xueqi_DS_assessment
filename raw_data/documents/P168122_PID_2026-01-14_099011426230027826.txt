Introduction and Context 1. With small populations spread across vast areas, the Pacific Island countries (PICs) face unique challenges in maintaining essential statistical systems. The World Bank has 12 PICs members: Papua New Guinea (PNG), Fiji, Kiribati, the Marshall Islands, the Federated States of Micronesia, Nauru, Palau, Samoa, the Solomon Islands, Tonga, Tuvalu, and Vanuatu. Most national statistical offices (NSOs) in the region have fewer than ten staff responsible for producing a wide range of economic, social, and demographic statistics, requiring individual professionals to simultaneously manage a wide range of duties across technical and administrative domains. These small teams typically lack specialized expertise, leading to quality concerns. Operational limitations also constrain the ability of agencies to conduct consistent and regular data collection, with many efforts effectively “one-off�? episodic tasks that generate incomparable and volatile statistical indicators. The geographic Page 2 Official Use Only The World Bank Statistical Innovation and Capacity Building in the Pacific Islands (P168122) – Additional Financing Request 1 dispersion of many PICs further exacerbates these difficulties, requiring extensive logistical coordination to reach remote communities. 2. Given the shared challenges, a common regional approach is needed to strengthen statistics. While implementing harmonized methods and exploiting economies of scale have benefits in many parts of the world, the approach is particularly crucial in the Pacific context. Countries are small and NSO capacity is thin, limiting the opportunities for domestic initiatives. PICs also have many similarities, while being distinct compared to other contexts globally. This situation limits the applicability of importing international best practice without careful consideration and customization. However, it can provide immense opportunities for rapid adoption and scale-up within the Pacific region after local best practices are identified and tested. Considering these conditions, the most impactful approach to promoting regional and national data-driven policy is to empower a credible and established regional partner to coordinate the agenda. 3. Before the Pacstat-SPC project (P168122), the region’s NSOs lacked an established coordinating body for quality standards and guidelines. The absence of a mechanism to align approaches and a reservoir of specialized expertise led to a proliferation of inconsistent statistical measures, undermining comparability between countries and even within countries over time. Technical assistance for data collection was haphazard, with key tasks outsourced to consultants far removed from the region. Responding to these challenges, the Pacstat-SPC project successfully piloted a regional implementation model combining country-level execution of statistical activities with regional standards, oversight, and technical assistance. The pooling of expertise and shared services has benefited NSOs across the region and at varying levels of capacity, ensuring adherence to a common system collectively endorsed through the Pacific Statistics Methods Board (PSMB), a consultative body also supported by the project. This approach improved consistency of methods, generated economies of scale, and improved compliance with fiduciary, environmental, and social standards. 4. The project has significantly improved the quality, availability, and standardization of statistics across the region. To standardize methods and quality, the project commissioned and published guidance notes for key statistical issues (e.g. the Pacific HIES toolkit, collecting food data) for use in PICs. Each was reviewed and approved and are now applied in statistical activities across the region. The concentration of specialized technical capacity within SPC reduced the lag between data collection and publication, with most countries now producing reports within 12 months of the end of fieldwork. The availability of consistent data sets allows the Pacific region to better track its overall levels and patterns of poverty, inequality, and employment. These benefits are tracked in the results framework, including publication of guidance notes (PDOI 1.1), regionally comparable indicators (PDOI 2.1), harmonized microdata sets (PDOI 2.1), PSMB commissioned works on innovative experiments (IRI 1.2), and HIES reports produced within 12 months of data collection (IRI 2.2). 5. Pacstat-SPC is part of an interconnected Series of Projects (SOP) that is on track to achieve, and in several cases exceed, its initial objectives. The SOP includes two country-level operations in Tonga (P171377, “Statistical Innovation and Capacity Building in Tonga�?) and Kiribati (P171380, “Statistical Innovation and Capacity Building in Kiribati�?). Pacstat-SPC has directly supported the successful implementation of these companion projects. Regionally standardized survey methods and strengthened technical assistance directly contributed to successful Household Income and Expenditure Surveys (HIES) in Tonga and Kiribati, resulting in lower per-interview costs, improved data quality, and faster publication timelines. Both countries are on track to meet the household survey coverage target established in IDA21. (CAPSA, P180723), which completed a HIES without direct SPC technical assistance. In addition, an upcoming IPF in the Marshall Islands is expected to include a HIES sub-component that will benefit from Pacstat-SPC sponsored standards and methods. As of November 2025, funds in the Pacstat-SPC project are nearly fully disbursed, and all PDO and intermediate results indicators have been achieved or exceeded. Project ratings reflect strong implementation performance and effective project administration. Early challenges with procurement and contract management processes have been resolved following SPC leadership’s amendment of internal policies to allow project procurement to be undertaken solely through the Bank’s systems. These arrangements are expected to continue through the additional year of implementation and to sustain satisfactory procurement ratings going forward. 6. The project is a proving-ground for regionally relevant innovative experiments designed to lower costs, shorten production cycles, and raise the quality of Pacific statistical operations. These include: (i) Tuvalu’s Long-Form Census (LFC), which tested digitally enabled long-form enumeration and streamlined workflows to produce core statistics from a single field operation; (ii) Samoa’s Food-Away-From-Home (FAFH) experiment, which developed and field-tested a focused instrument for welfare statistics and price measurement; (iii) a Vanuatu “data science for climate change�? experiment, integrating emerging data sources such as satellite imagery and analytical methods to enhance the statistical system’s responsiveness to climate -linked shocks; and (iv) a pilot for a Statistical Population Register in Tuvalu to improve sampling frames and administrative data integration. These trials were identified and refined through structured consultations with National Statistics Offices (NSOs) and endorsed through the Pacific Statistics Methods Board (PSMB), ensuring technical rigor and relevance to scaling across the region. indicators confirm wide consultation, incorporation of country feedback, and progressive approval and reporting of the experiments. Collectively, the experiments demonstrate practical pathways to reduce per-survey costs (lighter modular instruments), improve coverage and accuracy in hard-to-reach geographies (digital capture and remote support), accelerate analysis (standardized syntax and templates), and strengthen interoperability with administrative and geospatial data. Their results are already translating into faster production of HIES and poverty outputs and more harmonized indicators and microdata across the region, laying the groundwork for a more efficient, comparable, and resilient regional statistical system managed through SPC’s coordinated support to NSOs 7. Improved methods have also been applied in countries without statistics IPFs—including Tuvalu, Samoa, Palau, and Nauru. The regional endorsement of new methodologies, the production of detailed technical guidance notes, and SPC’s improved capacity to provide technical assistance to countries has led to the application of the new methods in other PICs. Through the project, SPC has supported the publication of the poverty report in Nauru, Palau, Samoa and Tuvalu based on HIES datasets that were collected with improved methods and using a poverty measurement methodology that conforms to international best practices. The availability of new and consistent statistics on poverty, demographics, labor, and access to services directly benefits the countries in evidence-based policymaking and improves the quality of the Bank’s policy engagement and operations portfolio. 8. The operation and additional financing align strongly with GROW for regional organizations by empowering SPC to deliver regional public goods in statistics. The most notable project components contributing to GROW Page 4 Official Use Only The World Bank Statistical Innovation and Capacity Building in the Pacific Islands (P168122) – Additional Financing Request 1 objectives include governance and coordination via the Pacific Statistics Methods Board (PSMB), region‑wide harmonization and standards for household welfare surveys, pooled technical assistance to NSOs, and a proving-ground for innovations that lower costs and improve data quality across countries. These contributions translate into comparable, accessible welfare datasets and harmonized microdata that support evidence‑based policymaking at both national and regional levels. The project’s support to standardized HIES implementation (e.g., in Tonga, Tuvalu, and Kiribati) directly advances the IDA21 objective on welfare survey data coverage, and the additional financing sustains this momentum while laying the groundwork for a subsequent Pacstat‑II to broaden reach and deepen adoption. 9. This additional financing (AF) will extend project activities for one year, to sustain high priority activities and lay the groundwork for a planned Pacstat-II regional project under preparation for FY27. This AF proposes a scale-up of activities to enhance the project’s development impact. The proposal has two objectives: i) extend core activities from the ongoing Pacstat-I project for one year while maintaining the development objective, and ii) conduct preparatory activities to inform the design of a second phase of Pacstat. Pacstat-II will expand the geographic coverage of the SOP by engaging additional IDA countries (early indications of interest have been received from Kiribati, Tonga, Tuvalu, Solomon Islands and Vanuatu), while focusing on the next generation of efficiency improvements that can be implemented across the region to increase quality, timeliness, and coverage. The additional financing will sustain SPC’s increased capacity achieved during the project, which allows SPC to continue providing a standard package of technical assistance to all WB-member countries in the Pacific. Additional financing will also be used for pilot activities shaping the design of the upcoming project’s planned components. C. Under Component 1, the additional financing will maintain the activities of the Pacific Statistics Methods Board (PSMB) and commission suitability assessments of national administrative data systems for integration to be supported in the upcoming project. The project’s support to PSMB has been a success. PSMB meetings occurring twice a year as well as the research that feeds into them are vital regional public goods. The meetings are well- attended by National Statistics Offices (NSOs) across the region, and the meetings have led to the regional endorsement of desk research, experiments, and guidance notes for implementing and analyzing surveys. There is strong demand from NSOs and development partners, such as the Australian Bureau of Statistics and Statistics New Zealand, to maintain PSMB meetings to coordinate and standardize statistical activities across the region. 12. The additional financing will also support training and the production of commissioned assessments for PSMB endorsement. The new topics will cover assessments linked to two topics prioritized for the Pacstat-II upcoming regional project: 1) Prospects for integrating administrative data systems into statistical systems across the Pacific, and 2) Implementing continuous survey data collection modes in the Pacific. Conducting this research under the project and through the PSMB mechanism will build regional consensus on the methods that will be implemented in Pacstat-II. The project includes an additional US$100,000 to maintain the PSMB secretariat function, US$150,000 on administrative data research, and US$150,000 on researching survey design models. The component will support regional standardization of civil registry and vital statistics data using the OpenCRVS platform. Finally, regional workshops are important to build the knowledge and skills required at the country level to implement regionally endorsed new methods for data collection and analysis. This component will support two regional training workshops and Pacific NSOs, linked to the assessment topics (administrative data systems, and using continuously collected survey data). Results indicators linked to PSMB meetings and commissioned works will be updated in accordance with these proposed activities. Under Component 2, additional financing will support ongoing technical assistance to countries and project management. The project has increased SPC’s technical assistance capacity on the implementation and analysis of household surveys. This additional capacity was the key driver for SPC’s ability to achieve all the results indicators related to research output, technical assistance to countries, and overall improvements to the quality and availability of statistics in the Pacific. Additional financing under Component 3 includes a new pilot data collection initiative. The additional financing will also support improving price data collection to enable more countries in the region to participate in the current or next available cycle of the International Comparison Program (ICP), used for the calculation of Purchasing Price Parities (PPP). Currently, the only PIC that participates in the ICP is Fiji, with other countries having their PPP calculated based on the prices of goods in Fiji. Expanding ICP participation will enable more accurate PPP values to be estimated across the Pacific. The new price collections will be implemented by SPC in collaboration with participating countries. This will also include a regional workshop to train participating countries in the data collection methods. The project will allocate US$200,000 for these activities. Implementation The implementing agency for this project is SPC-SDD. SPC-SDD is responsible for implementation of all components of the SPC project, including procurement and financial management of the project funds. The activities under the project will be managed by a team led by the Director of SPC-SDD, with assistance for day-to-day activities from the Project Coordinator.
