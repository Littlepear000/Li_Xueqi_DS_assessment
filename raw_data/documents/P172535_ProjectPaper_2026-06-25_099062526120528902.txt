PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. The Colombia Resilient and Inclusive Housing Project (P172535) was approved on May 27, 2021, and declared effective on October 5, 2021. The project is implemented by the Ministry of Housing, City and Territory (MVCT) and the National Housing Fund (FONVIVIENDA). Total financing amounts to US$136.7 million, comprising a US$100 million International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) loan and two grants (US$21.7 million from the Global Concessional Financing Facility (GCFF) and US$15 million from the Global Public Goods (GPG)). 2. An Implementation Support Mission was conducted on April 28–29, 2026, by a joint team from the World Bank (WB) and the Government of Colombia (GoC). The mission assessed the current implementation status, reviewed financial and procurement and environmental and social (E&S) management performance, and evaluated the risks associated with project closure under the current timeline. The overall Project Development Objective (PDO) indicator—people provided with improved urban living conditions—currently shows 18,265 beneficiaries, representing 4,491 households. While project performance has improved considerably after many challenges during early years of implementation, reflecting prior restructuring, significant efforts to enhance capacity in the implementing unit, and stronger leadership commitment, it is not feasible to reach the original targets within the current project closing date of July 24, 2026. 3. The implementation of housing improvements continues to advance steadily. To date, 4,491 households have benefited from home improvement interventions. Of these, 2,905 are female-headed households; 119 households include at least one member with a disability; and 17 households include at least one member who identifies as Afro-descendant. In urban areas, 3,597 interventions have been completed as of April 2026. The 16 active contracts are expected to close within the project’s current closing date and will bring the total to 3,734 beneficiary households. Rural housing improvement, however, continues to present challenges: only 894 improvements have been completed. Of the nine active contracts currently underway, four are projected to close before the current closing date, adding 63 beneficiaries, while the remaining five are expected to extend beyond it, benefiting 503 households. Full completion of all contracts would ensure a total of 1,802 beneficiaries. 4. Rental subsidies for Venezuelan migrants are also advancing. The MVCT has assigned and initiated payments for 1,986 subsidies through 14 inter-administrative agreements with territorial entities and seven real estate managers. However, since the subsidy is granted for a period of 12 months, payments corresponding to approximately 1,700 households exceed the project's current closing date, representing an estimated expenditure of US$2.06 million. This situation also prevents the assignment of new rental subsidies. 5. Interventions to improve living conditions in selected neighborhoods continue to advance. However, there is no progress in terms of outcome indicators, as all contracted interventions are in the construction execution phase. Two informal settlements upgrading (Mejoramiento Integral de Barrios - MIB) projects and nine public space (PS) projects are currently under execution, which would benefit approximately 30,800 people. However, due to past delays in procurement, four of the PS works under execution exceed the Project's closing date, and the MVCT does not have the resources to cover the associated contracts. 6. Progress under Component 2 (Institutional Strengthening) is significantly below expectations. Of the five component indicators, only one has been achieved: the Climate Adaptation and Disaster Risk Management Guide has been formulated. Financial execution reaches only 3 percent of the allocated resources, which suggests that the targets will not be met, and the budget will not be executed as initially estimated. 7. Regarding Component 3, Project Administration, substantial improvements are evident in the strengthening of the Project Implementing Unit (PIU), with enhanced operational capacity performance. Within its sole indicator, 97.8 percent of complaints registered in the Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) system are addressed within a maximum of 15 Page 1 The World Bank Colombia: Resilient and Inclusive Housing Project (P172535) business days, and responses are communicated to the corresponding stakeholders. The mission recognized the progress in institutional coordination among the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit (MHCP), the National Planning Department (DNP), the MVCT, and the Presidential Agency for International Cooperation of Colombia (APC), which has contributed to improvements in the project's operational, procurement, and financial management. 8. Financial Management (FM) performance shows substantial improvements, allowing for the FM rating to be upgraded to Moderately Satisfactory (MS). The risk level remains Substantial, considering the challenges in budget execution and the financial closing actions at this stage. The mission recognizes the effort of the MVCT's financial team in the preparation and delivery of the semi-annual financial information report and audited financial statements. As of April 2026, overall disbursement stands at US$43.67 million. Of this amount, US$14.27 million has been executed and justified, with US$29.4 million held in trust accounts tied to active contracts. The remaining undisbursed balance is US$93.02 million (IBRD: US$66.53 million; GCFF: US$14.43 million; and GPG: US$12.06 million). 9. E&S performance continues to be Satisfactory (S). The PIU has effectively fulfilled the Environmental and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP) commitments across all components through the application of the Contractor Environmental and Social Management Plan/Contractor Environmental Management Plan (C-ESMP/C-EMP) and Stakeholder Engagement and Participation Plan (SEPP) instruments, with monthly monitoring and ongoing training processes, achieving 92 percent contractor appropriation of environmental and social requirements. On the environmental front, progress is evident in efficient resource management, comprehensive waste handling, and biodiversity protection. Regarding occupational health and safety (OHS), safe working conditions are maintained, with only 3 minor accident events in 2025. On the social component, participation is carried out in accordance with the SEPP, and the GRM operates efficiently, with 59 requests received in 2026, of which 81 percent have been closed and 92 percent addressed within the established timeframe, reflecting an improvement compared to the previous semester. 10. During an Implementation Support Mission held on April 28-29 , the risks associated with the Project's closure on July 24, 2026 were analyzed, concluding that this scenario would entail risks in legal, financial, environmental, social, and reputational terms. As described before, The MVCT reported that some activities under the rural housing improvement, rental subsidies, and MIB and PS subcomponents would not be completed within the current Project deadline. Additionally, it noted that the MVCT does not have its own resources to cover the associated budgetary requirements, which could lead to a widespread failure to meet acquired obligations. 11. Under the current Project closing date, significant legal and financial risks would arise, both through the forced termination of active procurement contracts and the suspension of ongoing rental subsidy payments. From a contractual standpoint, closing the Project on the current date would entail early termination, termination for convenience, or rescission of contracts currently under execution, particularly in the rural housing improvement, MIB, and PS subcomponents. This could give rise to legal claims by contractors, supervisors, territorial entities, and beneficiaries. Regarding the rental subsidies to Venezuelan migrants, some subsidy payments correspond to a 12-month period that extends beyond the current Project closing date and would therefore not be eligible as Project expenditures. The eventual suspension of these payments, given the MVCT's lack of budgetary availability to assume such obligations, could have serious consequences for the vulnerable migrant population, as well as claims and legal actions by affected landlords and other stakeholders involved. 12. To address the risks identified above, and in response to the extension request submitted by the (MHCP), the WB and the MVCT technical teams agreed that a minimum 11-month extension for the Project is required, changing the closing date from July 24, 2026, to June 24, 2027. This extension reflects the time needed to complete works, followed by verification by supervising entities (interventorías), financial closure, and contract liquidation with the fiduciary entities and autonomous patrimonies. In addition, this period will allow completion of all payments for the rental subsidies awarded to Venezuelan households. The Resilient and Inclusive Housing Project will be granted an 11-month extension, moving the closing date from July 24, 2026, to June 24, 2027. Following a technical analysis and as previously agreed with the counterpart, the WB agreed to grant an 11-month extension. The extension will provide the necessary time to conclude all activities with signed contracts, including completion of works, verification by supervisory entities (interventorías), financial closure, and contractual liquidation with fiduciary entities and autonomous patrimonies across housing improvement, MIB, and PS interventions. This has been the only extension granted to date. 14. No new disbursements are expected during the extension period. From the US$43.6 million already disbursed (including loan and grant resources), US$40.6 million (93.1 percent) is projected to be executed, and US$2.9 million in uncommitted funds are expected to be refunded to IBRD, GCFF, and GPG. The remaining undisbursed balance of US$93.02 million would be subject to cancellation, which must be formally requested to the WB by the MHCP and processed following the extension. 15. The extension will support continued progress toward the achievement of the PDO. As of April 2026, the Project has reached 18,265 beneficiaries. By the current closing date of June 24, 2026, this number is projected to reach 77,475 beneficiaries. With the 11-month extension, the total is expected to increase to 109,460 beneficiaries, driven primarily by beneficiaries from MIB and PS interventions. 16. Regarding FM, the extension would allow for the fulfillment of financial closure commitments prior to the closing date, by providing the necessary time to structure and implement a comprehensive financial closure plan. This would contribute to reducing the financial management risks identified, through strengthened controls over technical and financial validation processes, clearly defined internal deadlines, and well-assigned responsibilities, with particular emphasis on critical contracts. The extension would also help ensure that all payments are supported by complete, consistent, and duly validated documentation, through the application of checklists and robust document traceability. Additionally, it would provide the time needed to carry out periodic balance reconciliations, manage accounts payable and advances, process resource reimbursements, and verify the consistency of financial information — all essential steps toward an orderly and transparent project closure. The Borrower has no outstanding audit reports, and there are no audit reports deemed unsatisfactory by the Bank. 17. Regarding E&S performance, the extension would allow for the satisfactory fulfillment of all ESCP commitments prior to closing, by providing the necessary time to finalize the implementation of management instruments associated with ongoing works and activities. This would contribute to reducing risks related to unmitigated impacts and to ensuring full compliance with the Project's environmental and social standards. The extension would likewise help guarantee the continuity of benefits for vulnerable households, including completion of housing improvement, MIB, and PS interventions, as well as delivery of rental subsidies to migrant populations whose payment cycles extend beyond the current closing date—while preserving the expectations generated within the target territories.
