KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions Implementation and disbursement continued to accelerate during the reporting period, with the project reaching a disbursement rate of 56.98 percent as of June 2026 compared to 33.99 percent at the time of the previous ISR. The implementation support mission confirmed that the project is now operating at scale across all technical components, supported by strengthened coordination mechanisms, improved monitoring systems, and fully mobilized implementation partners. Overall implementation progress was assessed as Satisfactory. Under Component 1, significant progress was achieved in the business environment reform agenda. The revised Labor Code was adopted in December 2025, representing a major reform supported through the project, and expected to strengthen the country's performance under the Labor topic of the World Bank's B-READY assessment, where the Central African Republic currently scores 61.1 for the regulatory framework pillar. Progress was also recorded in the modernization and digitalization of the Guichet Unique de Formalisation des Entreprises (GUFE) - which is expected to significantly reduce the time required to register a business and improve the efficiency of business registration services, while the National Investment Commission (CNI) strengthened investment promotion and facilitation services significantly expanding its pipeline development, and investment approval functions. As of April 2026, the CNI had processed 25 investment applications representing an estimated investment volume of CFAF 113.5 billion, including 20 approved investment projects totaling approximately CFAF 99.8 billion (US$175 million). These investments are expected to generate approximately 8,000 jobs. The project also supported the preparation of a pipeline of 36 priority investment projects aligned with the National Development Plan. Under Component 2, major milestones were achieved toward operationalizing the National Guarantee and Investment Fund (FNGI). The first capitalization tranche of US$4 million was disbursed, governance structures were established, key operational and environmental and social instruments were finalized, and participation agreements were signed with Ecobank, BGFI Bank and BPMC - together representing approximately three-quarters of the country's banking market, providing the FNGI with broad market coverage from the outset. Three guarantee products were developed to support MSME financing and priority value chains. The immediate priority is now the completion of the COBAC licensing process and the launch of guarantee operations. Following the adoption of a new regional regulatory framework increasing the minimum capital requirements for guarantee institutions, the Government provided an additional capital contribution of XAF 300 million (approximately US$530,000) to meet the new regulatory threshold. The updated licensing application reflecting the revised requirements has now been submitted to COBAC. In parallel, agreement has been reached with COBAC to streamline the review process through closer technical exchanges during the appraisal phase, with the objective of expediting the issuance of the operating license. The project also initiated collaboration with IFC to strengthen participating financial institutions and expand SME access to finance. Under Component 3, support to MSMEs and workforce development continued to expand. More than 1,300 MSMEs have benefited from project-supported services delivered through implementation partners. The project strengthened beneficiary tracking systems, expanded business support services, and launched initiatives to improve MSME access to finance and markets. On the skills development side, PREPA-PRO was successfully completed with 1,984 youth receiving completion certificates, while FORMA-PRO was officially launched in June 2026. Fifteen priority skills standards Jun 27, 2026 Official Use Only Page 2 of 10 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report CAR Investment and Business Competitiveness for Employment (P176274) (referentiels) and 90 training manuals were developed, and assessments of training institutions confirmed a national training capacity of approximately 4,345 places. The next phase will focus on apprenticeship programs and labor market insertion through strengthened partnerships with private sector firms, for the first cohort by July 2026. Under Component 4, project management systems continued to improve through strengthened coordination, harmonized reporting mechanisms across implementation partners, enhanced monitoring tools, and improved data quality. Following the Government's formal request for a US$50 million Additional Financing to scale up the successful interventions of Phase I, the mission initiated discussions with the authorities on the strategic scope and priorities of the proposed operation, focusing on access to finance, SME development, productive value chains, skills development, and the economic inclusion of refugees and host communities. Key decisions agreed during the mission include: (i) completing the COBAC licensing process and launching FNGI guarantee operations by Q3 2026; (ii) accelerating the adoption and implementation of the remaining priority private sector reforms during by Q4 2026; (iii) transitioning MSME support toward market linkages, supplier development, and access to finance over the next implementation cycle by Q4 2026; (iv) rolling out apprenticeship placements and labor market insertion activities for FORMA-PRO beneficiaries beginning by end of Q2 2026; and (v) completing the remaining procurement activities, strengthening implementation quality and results monitoring, and advancing preparation of the proposed Additional Financing during FY27. 5.
