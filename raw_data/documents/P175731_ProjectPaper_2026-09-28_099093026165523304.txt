BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING A. Background 1. The SADC Regional Statistics Project (P175731), approved on June 8, 2023, seeks to modernize data ecosystems of participating countries by addressing gaps in data production, harmonization, and use. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to strengthen the institutional capacity of Southern African Development Community (SADC) and participating countries to produce, disseminate, and use quality statistics while increasing regional harmonization and collaboration. The project includes seven recipients: Comoros (US$8.5 million), Madagascar (US$45 million), Malawi (US$15 million), Mozambique (US$60 million), São Tomé and Príncipe (US$3 million), Zambia (US$30 million), and the SADC Secretariat (US$10 million), for a total amount of US$171.5 million. The closing date of the project is October 31, 2028. 2. Project description. The project comprises the following components: • Component 1: Improve harmonization, quality, and dissemination of core social and economic statistics. The regional component supports the implementation of continental and regional agreements on statistics – specifically, the SADC’s Regional Strategy for Development of Statistics (RSDS) 2020–2030 – by improving the capacity of the SADC Secretariat to help Member States improve harmonization, quality, and dissemination of core social and economic statistics. • Component 2: Country Component: Close gaps in data production, statistical capacity, and data use at country level. The country component finances key data collection and production (such as National Accounts, CPI, Household and Expenditure Surveys and Agriculture and Livestock Censuses), as well as statistical capacity building and data use activities. • Component 3: Project management ensures the implementation of the project in each participating entity. • Component 4: Contingency Emergency Response Component (CERC) for Madagascar and Malawi only. 3. Project activities for Sao Tome and Principe. The IDA Grant E1890 for the amount of SDR 2.3 million (US$3 million equivalent) became effective on February 13, 2024. Parent project activities include: (a) the final stage of the Population and Housing Census (notably thematic studies), (b) the Household Budget Survey (Inquérito aos Orçamentos Familiares, IOF), (c) labor force surveys, (d) business and production statistics, and (e) the production of other key economic indicators. The project also supports the upgrade of ICT infrastructure and operational systems to strengthen data collection, processing, analysis, dissemination, and archiving. Capacity building activities for INE staff are also financed under the project to strengthen technical and institutional capabilities. In São Tomé and Príncipe, the project is implemented by INE, with fiduciary and Environmental and Social Framework (ESF) responsibilities carried out by the Fiduciary Agency for Project Administration (AFAP). 4. Overall Project Implementation Status. The Project has yielded significant results across participating countries by strengthening the availability, quality, and dissemination of core economic and social statistics. In Mozambique, notable progress includes improved data coverage and dissemination, with the recent completion of the Agricultural Census expected to significantly enhance the evidence base for agricultural policy, alongside ongoing improvements in national accounts that have increased the timeliness and granularity of GDP statistics at national and provincial levels. In Madagascar, the Project has supported key data collection efforts, including the 2025 Household Budget The World Bank SADC Regional Statistics Project (P175731) Page 2 Official Use Only Survey, agricultural surveys, and ongoing high-frequency household surveys, contributing to the production of essential socioeconomic data. In Malawi, major statistical operations have been successfully implemented, improving the quality, availability, and timeliness of key datasets such as household, agricultural, labor force, and demographic and health surveys. In Zambia, several core statistical activities are either completed or underway—including the Census of Economic Activities, agricultural surveys, labor market statistics, and cross-border trade data—alongside support for foundational ICT systems, data architecture, and dissemination platforms such as the National Data Archive (NADA). In Comoros, the Project financed the 2024 Harmonized Survey on Household Living Conditions, supported the transition to SNA 2008 for national accounts, improved the consumer price index, and strengthened dissemination platforms, including INSEED’s website and NADA. At the regional level, the SADC Secretariat has reinforced capacity across Member States through workshops and technical assistance, enhancing statistical methodologies, standards, and the use of advanced tools for censuses, surveys, and administrative data. Progress toward achievement of the PDO is rated Satisfactory, despite delays in Madagascar and Mozambique. Implementation performance is rated Moderately Satisfactory 5. Status of Implementation in São Tomé and Príncipe. The disbursement rate in São Tomé and Príncipe is approximately 63 percent, consistent with the project's original implementation sequencing. Given INE's limited implementation capacity and the concurrent support provided by the STP Digital Project, including for census-related activities, the project initially focused on capacity building and preparatory activities, while major data collection operations were deliberately scheduled for later years to avoid overlapping implementation demands. The project has contributed to improvements in data production, harmonization, and use, including increases in the data collection and openness score, improvements in data harmonization, and rising demand for statistical outputs, as evidenced by significant data requests and downloads. However, implementation has been constrained by operational and financial challenges. The completion of the Population and Housing Census and the Household Budget Survey (IOF) required additional financial resources due to higher-than-expected implementation costs, including inflationary pressures, exchange rate movements, and increased project management costs. In addition, some census-related activities initially expected to be financed under the STP Digital Project had to be absorbed by the project following financing shortfalls under that operation. Weaknesses in procurement processes, delays in the preparation of technical documentation, and capacity limitations in fiduciary and project management functions have also contributed to implementation bottlenecks. The project’s performance in São Tomé and Príncipe in terms of Financial Management (FM) is rated satisfactory, with timely submission of financial reports and full compliance with audit requirements. Procurement is rated moderately satisfactory due to delays in the preparation of technical inputs and procurement processing. Environmental and Social performance is satisfactory, with risks classified as moderate and considered manageable within the existing framework. B. Rationale for Additional Financing 7. This Project Paper seeks approval for an Additional Financing (AF) in the amount of US$3 million to São Tomé and Príncipe for the SADC Regional Statistics Project to close a financing gap and scale up selected activities; as well as to restructure the project activities in other participating countries, post Mid-Term Review (MTR, completed in April 2026). 8. During implementation of the current project, the costs of key data collection activities, especially the Population and Housing Census and the Household Budget Survey, were revised upward due to operational and logistical challenges. These challenges include higher-than-expected inflation, exchange rate movements (notably the depreciation of the US dollar relative to the Euro, given the country’s reliance on imports), and increased costs of project management following institutional changes affecting AFAP. More specifically, for the Population and Housing Census, its completion has also been affected by financing shortfalls in the STP Digital Project, which had originally planned to support certain census activities. As a result, additional resources were required under the current project to ensure the completion of census-related activities, including post-enumeration work and thematic studies. 9. These combined factors have resulted in a cost overrun estimated at approximately US$1.25 million, affecting both already implemented activities and the remaining phases of ongoing operations. Without additional resources, the completion and quality of key statistical outputs—particularly the census and household survey—would be compromised. 10. Beyond addressing the cost overrun, in the letter dated November 20, 2025, Government has requested additional US$1.75 million for scale up of activities to address remaining data gaps in priority sectors. The Government has identified the need to expand statistical production to areas such as health, education, agriculture, and social conditions, including through the implementation of the Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) and one round of agricultural survey. These activities are critical for monitoring national development objectives and the Sustainable Development Goals, and for informing evidence-based policymaking. 11. The additional financing is aligned with the World Bank's Country Partnership Framework for São Tomé and Príncipe (FY24-29) and contributes to strengthening the capacity of public institutions to produce and use data for decision-making, building on the Bank's broader engagement in the country. The proposed scale-up builds on achievements under the parent project and addresses remaining gaps, thereby enhancing the overall impact of the The World Bank SADC Regional Statistics Project (P175731) Page 4 Official Use Only operation. The proposed two-year implementation period for the AF is considered feasible given the country context and the maturity of ongoing activities. No extension of the project closing date is proposed under this AF. To address the compressed implementation period, given the number of concurrent statistical operations and limited institutional capacity, the following actions will be taken: activity-level schedule with milestones and buffer, designated focal points, early procurement preparation and quality assurance, strengthened INE–AFAP coordination, close Bank supervision, and use of digital methods where feasible. Progress against the schedule and identified bottlenecks will be reviewed regularly, with timely adjustments to sequencing and implementation support. The implementation schedule will be maintained as a management tool and reflected in the Project Operational Manual. C. Rationale for Restructuring. 12. Following Mid-Term Review, several adjustments are proposed in the parent project, to realign country-specific work plans with priorities and implementation capacity, including the cancellation of some activities and the introduction of new ones, where justified by evolving needs. Adjustments to country work plans will require updates to Results Framework baselines and targets, as several indicators are defined as composite scores linked to the subset of activities supported by the project. These changes are limited in magnitude, given the broadly unchanged scope of activities. The restructuring reflects lessons from implementation and the Mid-Term Review, including the need to sequence activities more realistically in countries where national statistical offices are managing several large-scale data operations at the same time. In these cases, the proposed changes prioritize completion and quality of the most critical and policy-relevant activities over maintaining a broader set of surveys that would be difficult to execute within the remaining project period. II. DESCRIPTION OF ADDITIONAL FINANCING AND RESTRUCTURING 13. Project Development Objective. There are no changes to the PDO of the project. D. Proposed Additional Financing 14. The proposed additional financing will: (i) close the financing needs associated with cost overruns (Census and IOF) and revised budgets, to ensure completion of core statistical activities (business and labor force surveys among others); and (ii) introduce new activities aligned with the PDO, aimed at strengthening data availability, institutional capacity, and data use. The AF will also support institutional reforms, including strengthening the legal framework of INE and expanding the dissemination and use of statistical products through improved digital platforms. The AF will also revise selected results indicators and targets to reflect the expanded scope of activities. The implementation of the AF will build on current achievements and ensure the completion of core statistical operations while expanding statistical production to additional priority areas. Detailed description of the activities is presented below. Component 2: STP Country Component: Close gaps in data production, statistical capacity, and data use at the country level (Parent Project US$2.5 million, AF$US2.8 million) Subcomponent 2.1 Close data production gaps (Parent project $US2.1 million, AF $US1.6 million) 15. The AF will support both the cost overrun of activities originally planned in the parent project, and the implementation of new data collection activities aimed at addressing remaining data gaps in São Tomé and Príncipe. 16. Activities in the parent project include: The World Bank SADC Regional Statistics Project (P175731) Page 5 Official Use Only • Housing and Population Census (thematic studies and completion activities). The AF will finance the completion of the Population and Housing Census, including the preparation, analysis, and dissemination of the thematic reports. These activities faced a financing shortfall due to revised costs and the transfer of certain census-related activities from the STP Digital Project. • Household Budget Survey (IOF 2026). The IOF is a critical instrument for poverty measurement, consumption analysis, and CPI rebasing, and its successful completion is essential to improving the quality and relevance of economic statistics. • Business surveys and Labor Force Surveys. Additional resources will support the completion of planned survey rounds whose budgets were revised upward during implementation. • National Accounts. The AF will support the consolidation of recently rebased national accounts and strengthen INE’s capacity to update them. • Consumer Price Index (CPI). Building on price and consumption data collected under the IOF, the AF will support the development of a new CPI basket with an updated base year. This will improve the accuracy and representativeness of inflation measurement. 17. In addition to completing these ongoing activities, the AF will introduce the following new activities: • Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS). The AF will support the implementation of the MICS survey to generate comprehensive and internationally comparable data on key indicators related to health, education, child welfare, water and sanitation, and living conditions. The previous MICS was conducted in 2019. This new survey will strengthen the country’s ability to monitor development outcomes and track progress toward the Sustainable Development Goals. • Agricultural Survey. The AF will support the implementation of one thematic agricultural survey to complement the recently completed Agricultural Census in December 2025. Before that, the latest agricultural census followed by such a survey was in 1989/90. This survey will provide in-depth information for sectoral planning, agricultural policy formulation, and monitoring of climate-related risks and food security. Subcomponent 2.2 Strengthening institutional capacity (Parent project $US0.2 million, AF $US0.8 million) 18. The AF will support both new and ongoing activities from the parent project aimed at strengthening the institutional capacity of the national statistical system. 2.2.1 Enhance institutional frameworks and statistical capacity 19. DETAILED CHANGE(S) DEVELOPMENT OBJECTIVE (DO) Operation Abstract: Summary Description of Proposed Operation Operation Abstract (Approved as part of Approval Package on 12-Oct-2023) The SADC Regional Statistics Project will promote the production of high-quality statistics, regional harmonization of statistics, and timely dissemination to enhance their use. With support from the SADC secretariat, quality and harmonized statistics will be produced and disseminated by the National Statistical Offices (NSOs) of SADC Member The World Bank SADC Regional Statistics Project (P175731) Page 14 Official Use Only States and project beneficiary countries. The timely release of statistics and collaboration with policymakers and other stakeholders will lead to enhanced use of statistics, which – in turn – encourages more production of statistics creating a positive feedback loop enhancing the sustainability of the project objective. Specialized training and capacity-building activities will be implemented to enhance national monitoring and evaluation systems, involving NSOs and agencies from the governments of participants countries to promote evidence-based policymaking. At the country level, the project will support activities to raise the level of national statistical systems (NSS), allowing countries to align themselves to SADC standards, per the SADC Protocol on Statistics. The project will cover the following SADC IDA countries: Madagascar, Malawi, Zambia and Comoros, as well as a non- SADC state, Sao Tome and Principe. The project will also include the SADC secretariat. The estimated cost is tentatively US$ 104.5 million, to be provided through a combination of International Development Association (IDA) credits and grants from country-specific and regional windows. The project is planned for five years. The project has three components: Component 1: Improve harmonization, quality, and dissemination of core social and economic statistics. This component supports the implementation of the Regional Strategy for the Development of Statistics (RSDS) that the Southern African Development Community (SADC) started implementing in 2015 and that is reflected in the SADC Protocol on Statistics, the legal framework to enable and enhance statistical development and innovation in SADC member states. Under this component, SADC will primarily support NSOs to improve their ability to collect and produce statistical information by developing guidelines and delivering technical assistance for data collection from administrative records, expanding the use of specialized software, assisting development and maintenance of Statistical Business Registers, piloting processing Big Data for statistical purposes, delivering trainings for accessing and use of spatial information to collect census and survey data (GIS) through cartographic work, providing advanced training on sampling techniques, develop harmonized survey modules to collect household-level surveys to generate more data on informal sector, poverty, labor statistics, gender, migration, etc. The component will also deliver technical assistance and trainings to conduct of agricultural census and surveys. On macroeconomic data, the component will develop and implement harmonized Supply and Use Tables (SUTs) to generate regional value chains, will support country level harmonization of National Accounts (including rebasing of GDP), provide technical assistance to develop Integrated Economic Accounts (Environment) and Satellite Accounts, and develop and implement regional Producer Price Indices, Trade Indices and Index of Industrial Production. The component will also strengthen the capacity of the SADC Statistics Department in terms of expert training and equipment, generate partnership opportunities with academic institutions in the region on use of data for policy design, implementation, and evaluation, especially to monitor the implementation of the Protocol on Statistics. Component 2: Close gaps in data production, statistical capacity, and data use at country level seeks to extend the D4P package in beneficiary countries under the harmonizing guidelines, recognizing initial institutional and human resource capacities and building on existing core statistics, infrastructure and technical capacity. The activities planned under this component therefore aim to close some of the most important gaps in data production, equipment, materials, technical expertise, dissemination and use at a country level. These activities are mostly focused on country-level NSOs, however, to enhance use of data for policy design and monitoring, some countries will also undertake activities to strengthen analytical capacity at other public institutions, such as the Ministry of Finance. Component 3: Project Management will oversee, supervise and monitoring the first two components.
