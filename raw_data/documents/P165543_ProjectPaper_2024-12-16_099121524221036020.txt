PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING Background 1. The Institutional Strengthening for Improved Village Service Delivery project was approved by the World Bank on June 26, 2019 with the objective “to strengthen institutional capacity for improved quality of spending in participating villages”, financed by a US$300 million IBRD loan and US$50 million counterpart funding. At the time of approval, the project comprised four components: (a) Strengthening Village Government Institutions: Executed by the Ministry of Home Affairs (MoHA), this component financed the development and rollout of a Learning Management System (LMS) for village governments with content, tools, guidelines, and supporting instruments and a performance tracking system for capacity-building activities in villages; the upgrading and integration of MoHA’s village-level data systems and management information systems; and development of a village financial management and fiscal intelligence tool. (b) Promoting Participatory Village Development: Executed by Ministry of Villages, Disadvantaged Areas and Transmigration’s (MoVDA), this component financed the digitalization of oversight and management systems of village facilitation (the Village Digital Platform, GoDesa); the strengthening of community capacity and social accountability systems; and development and roll out of community learning systems and peer-to-peer knowledge exchanges. (c) Village Performance Awards: Executed by the National Development Planning Agency, this component financed the development and rollout of a system that rewards village governments for good governance and quality of spending through performance grants financed by counterpart funding. (d) National Coordination, Monitoring and Policy: Executed by the Coordinating Ministry for Human Development and Cultural Affairs, this component financed the establishment of a national coordination platform to coordinate and monitor the implementation of village level investments, harmonization of regulations, monitoring, and supervision of village development, as well as the establishment of an integrated information management system (InfoDesa) for real-time incorporation of key village development metrics. 2. During its first two and half years of implementation, the project experienced delays caused by the COVID-19 pandemic and repeated changes of senior government oversight officials that resulted in weak ownership. The project gained momentum in the third quarter of 2023, and implementation has accelerated impressively since then, as evidenced by the achievement of several project targets and improved disbursement performance. These improvements were supported by two Level 2 restructurings that improved alignment of the project’s design with the government’s evolving priorities and addressed implementation bottlenecks: (a) A June 2022 restructuring: (i) added to Component 1 the piloting of a program to improve frontline service delivery in participating village governments across 60 districts, implemented by the Directorate of Regional Administration under MoHA; (ii) replaced in Component 2 the GoDesa with the development of training modules related to social accountability, community empowerment, and participatory planning and their integration into the facilitator capacity building program, development of an LMS for the training program, and development of a digital marketplace platform; (iii) dropped Component 3; (iv) downscaled Component 4’s national coordination platform into strengthening the Coordinating Ministry for Human Development and Cultural Affairs’ coordination functions and supporting the development of a national strategy for the implementation of the Village Law; (v) canceled US$49.98 million of credit proceeds with corresponding changes to components’ costs and disbursement categories’ allocations; (vi) established separate project implementation units (PIUs) within MoHA to implement Page 1 The World Bank Institutional Strengthening for Improved Village Service Delivery (P165543) different Component 1 activities; (vii) removed the establishment of subnational implementation units as a legal covenant; and (viii) made results framework (RF) changes that reflected the revised, new, and dropped activities. (b) A November 2023 restructuring: (i) expanded Component 1 to financing capacity support to non-government village institutions and replaced the village financial management and fiscal intelligence tool with upgrades to MoHA’s village financial management system (SISKEUDES) and village financial audit and oversight system (SISWASKEUDES), with a small cost and disbursement category allocation reduction; (ii) further scaled down the new Component 3 to focus only on strengthening the capacity of the National Development Planning Agency and the Coordinating Ministry for Human Development and Cultural Affairs to implement the Village Law with reduced cost and disbursement category allocations; (iii) established a steering committee secretariat to support the steering committee’s oversight responsibilities; (iv) established Central Project Implementation Units (CPIU’s) under MoHA and MoVDA to coordinate the work of their PIUs and joint secretariats to coordinate sub-national activities; (v) made further adjustments to the RF by replacing two of the three PDO-level indicators and revising, adding, and removing intermediate result indicators (IRIs) to reflect the component changes. Project status 3. Progress toward PDO Achievement is currently rated as Moderately Satisfactory as several activities, such as the operationalization and rollout of the LMS and implementation of some training are still under way. Nonetheless, the PDO level outcome target of 70 percent increase in village governments submitting budget realization reports has already been exceeded (with a 72.7 percent increase) and the outcome target of 35 percent increase in villages with “village development status” stands at 28.4 percent and is expected to be achieved by the original closing date of December 31, 2024. Overall Implementation Progress is also rated Moderately Satisfactory to reflect disbursement delays and the partial achievement of several intermediate results, though these are also expected to be achieved in full by June 30, 2025. Component 1: Strengthening Village Institutions. Seventy percent of the 75,000 targeted villages are expected to complete basic capacity-building training by mid December 2024. The development and operationalization of the Learning Management System (LMS), along with its integration into the National Village Apparatus Strengthening Strategy, is scheduled for completion by April 30, 2025. Currently, activities related to strengthening and upgrading village institution systems, which include the Village Financial Management System (SISKEUDES), the Village Financial Audit and Oversight System (SISWASKEUDES), and improved coordination at the sub-district level for supervision, monitoring, and service delivery facilitation, are underway, though further acceleration is required. Training for women’s organizations (PKK) in the 6,000 target villages has been completed. Component 2: Strengthening Community Capacity for Village Development. The face-to-face training for 10,633 village facilitators, who assist eighty percent of the total villages, has been completed. The required training modules have been developed and integrated into the Learning Management System (LMS) platform for village facilitators. Improvements to the digital marketplace platform (P2KTD) are underway, and the implementation of the inclusion and social accountability scorecard is set to be completed by 2024. As part of the exit strategy, village facilitators and cadres will also be trained on the utilization of digital technology, including the Online Video Platform (OVP), under the smart village and village academy (Akademi desa) activities. Component 3: National Coordination, Monitoring, and Policy. This component involves the establishment and operationalization of a Steering Committee secretariat (by Bappenas and PMK) to assist the steering committee in coordinating projects along with related policies, studies, and assessments that will guide the future implementation of village law. The presidential decree regarding the facilitators has been finalized, and a model for rural and landscape Page 2 The World Bank Institutional Strengthening for Improved Village Service Delivery (P165543) development is currently under development, with plans for completion within the remaining project timeline. The supervision, monitoring, and evaluation of the implementation of the project is underway. 4. Disbursement stands at US$207.67 million (83.1 %) and is expected to increase to US$218.67 million (87.5 %) by December 31, 2024. The FM rating for the project is Moderately Satisfactory. The project is compliant with requirements for the timely submission of IFR and follow-up of audit findings. The FY 2022 audit findings are fully resolved. Weaknesses on internal control were noted in the FY2023 audit report, with potential ineligible expenditures equal to US$1 million / IDR 15 billion, which the client is in the process of rectifying. There are no overdue audits or financial reports. 5. Safeguards are rated Satisfactory, acknowledging progress has been made in (i) Complaint Handling guidelines that have been formally finalized by the CPMU (the system has been operational and been utilized based on draft guidelines); and (ii) the mainstreaming of E&S principles across all project components. The institutional arrangements for managing safeguards in the restructured Project will remain the same. The restructured Project will remain a Category B Project for environmental assessment and will not trigger any new safeguard policies. It will continue to adopt the Environmental and Social Safeguards Framework (ESMF) of the project, as well as the Project Operations Manuals. The Project reported 144 received cases through the complaint handling system SPAN LAPOR managed by Ministry of Home Affairs, of which 109 cases (76%) have been resolved and the remaining 35 cases are still under review. Most reported cases are related to allegations of misuse of funds (29%) and of authority (20%). The project’s legal covenants are complied with. Rationale for restructuring 6. Following discussions with the client, and in response to a letter from the Ministry of Finance, dated October 23, 2024, a third Level 2 restructuring is proposed to: (i) extend the closing date by six months (to June 30, 2025), to allow for a use of the remaining loan resources (US$29.35 million) to maximize the project’s impact, ensure the sustainability of its outcomes, and support relevant priorities of the new government. This will be achieved through institutionalization of the achieved results at the village, sub-district and district level. The undisbursed balance is the result of project cost savings attributable to implementation efficiencies and exchange rate fluctuations; (ii) reallocate the undisbursed loan proceeds among the disbursement categories and change the components’ costs accordingly; (iii) reflect the extended disbursement period in the project’s disbursement estimates; (iv) add indicators to the RF to monitor the additional activities; (v) reduce some of the project’s risk ratings to reflect a revised assessment; (vi) adjust the institutional arrangements to (A) include an additional PIU under the Ministry of Finance, Directorate General of Fiscal Balance to design policies for the Village Fund transfer and quality of its expenditure supporting climate resilient village development initiatives; (B) remove the CMHDCA as a PIU and SC co-chair as they no longer oversee village affairs in the new GoI administration; (C) provide more flexibility on the location of the CPMU and CPIUs within MOHA and MOVDA, in anticipation of potential administrative restructurings within these ministries; and (D) revise the implementation schedule to reflect the extension and new activities. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 7. The following changes are proposed: Page 3 The World Bank Institutional Strengthening for Improved Village Service Delivery (P165543) (a) Closing date extension. The Government of Indonesia has requested a project extension for twelve months until December 2025. The Bank is approving the extension of the project’s closing date by six months, from December 31, 2024, to June 30, 2025 to allow the GoI to complete the implementation of the project in an orderly manner, and to allow time for the preparation of a request for a follow up project that reflects the priorities of the new administration better. This will be the first closing date extension. (b) Components and costs. The additional activities will (i) support the implementation of the Government’s Medium term development plan (Rencana Pembangunan Jangka Menengah Nasional RPJMN) for 2025-2029 policies and programs related to improving village governance and development, (ii) strengthen the sustainability of project outcomes and results, including by adding a climate dimension to activities for the MoVDA and MoF to jointly set a framework for collaboration, design policies framework, initiate data sharing and studies to identify strategic entry points for village level climate action, (iii) support the operation of a new PIU under the Ministry of Finance, Directorate General of Fiscal Balance and (iv) considering cost savings incurred by the project, the cost of Component 1 will be reduced by 2.5 percent and the costs of components 2 and 3 will increase by 3.9 percent and 19.3 percent, respectively.
