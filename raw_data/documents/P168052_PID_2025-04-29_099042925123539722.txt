Introduction and Context 1. Background. The Socio-Economic Resilience Strengthening Project (SERSP) is one of three operations financed by the International Development Association (IDA) 18 Risk Mitigation Regime (RMR) to support Tajikistan’s Resilience Strengthening Program. SERSP was approved on June 20, 2019, for an IDA grant of US$37 million, and became effective July 8, 2020. The project is implemented by the National Social Investment Fund of Tajikistan (NSIFT). 2. Geographic coverage. The project operates in four districts in the Khatlon region (Kulyob, Panj, Hamadoni, and Farkhor) and in three districts in GBAO (Vanj, Ishkoshim, and Shugnan). The target districts were selected for having a shared border with Afghanistan, a relatively large population, a combination of rural and urban areas, and limited coverage by other donor interventions. Two additional districts, Shamsiddin Shohin in Khatlon province and Darvoz in GBAO province, were added at restructuring. 3. Project restructuring. The project was restructured once in May 2023. The restructuring involved: (i) reallocation Page 2 The World Bank Tajikistan Socio-Economic Resilience Strengthening Project (P168052) – Additional Financing Request 2 of US$4.7 million to Subcomponent 1a and US$0.2 million to Subcomponent 1b to expand the subcomponents’ activities to two new districts, Shamsiddin Shohin in Khatlon region, and Darvoz in Gorno-Badakhshan Autonomous Oblast (GBAO) region; (ii) revising the target for an intermediate-level indicator related to Subcomponent 2b; (iii) extending the project’s closing date by 18 months from June 30, 2024 to December 31, 2025; and (iv) aligning the results indicators’ end targets with the project’s new closing date. In addition, the restructuring removed a legal covenant for the establishment of a Project Steering Committee (SC). 4. PDO and implementation progress ratings. The project’s progress towards achievement of the PDO and it’s implementation progress are currently rated as Moderately Satisfactory, reflecting the steady and satisfactory progress in implementing project activities. The project is in its last year of implementation, with US$32.48 million (91%) disbursed and the remaining US$3.28 million is committed to be disbursed by December 31, 2025, based on current disbursement schedule. Three of the four PDO results indicators already exceed targets. PDO3 “percentage of youth beneficiaries who continue their livelihood activities after receiving livelihood tools and equipment�? will only be measured after the related livelihood support activities have been completed. 5. Implementation progress of SERSP is summarized below: a) Component 1: Inclusive Local Development Initiatives: Social mobilization for the original 7 districts, 44 jamoats in GBAO and Khatlon resulted in the prioritization of 193 subprojects. Of these, 153 are completed and the remaining 40 subprojects are scheduled to be completed by August 2025. In the two districts (Sh. Shohin and Darvoz) added during the restructuring, 15 subprojects are under implementation and are expected to be completed by September 2025. Around 75,000 community members (56% women) across 55 jamoats and 507 villages were mobilized, leading to formulation of Village and Jamoat Development Plans, as well as village-led prioritization of 208 social infrastructure subprojects. Other donors have supported 53 subprojects that communities identified in village and jamoat development plans, but were not selected for SERSP funding. Communities have expressed high levels of satisfaction with the project’s decision-making processes via the project’s social audits. Under sub-component 1c, NSIFT completed the renovation and upgrading of 17 youth facilities, which have been utilized to provide youth-inclusive services under Component 2 and handed the facilities over to the Ministry of Labor, Migration and Employment and the Ministry of Education and Science. b) Component 2: Youth Inclusion and Livelihoods: Under sub-component 2a, targets for all youth trainings have been met (digital literacy, Mind Masters, Upshift, healthy lifestyle, psychosocial support). Under subcomponent 2b: Youth Livelihoods, an initial cohort of 1,255 beneficiaries (47% women) received training, prepared basic business plans, and received tools and equipment, and are carrying out livelihood activities with the support of mentors. Monitoring indicates that beneficiaries are utilizing equipment and making value chain connections with bigger businesses and creating local jobs. An additional cohort of 1,145 beneficiaries to receive tools has been identified from youth who attended initial training, but did not receive equipment, and from youth in the two new districts. c) Component 3: Project Management, Monitoring and Evaluation: NSIFT’s SERSP project implementation unit is adequately managing its coordination and supervision, procurement, financial management, monitoring and evaluation, and communications activities, including oversight of its beneficiary feedback and grievance redress mechanisms. NSIFT has established a management information system (MIS) that is regularly updated and tracking project activities and results. It has also developed a publicly accessible, functional interactive map with information on SERSP subprojects across the target jamoats, plus information on top priority investments identified by participating jamoats and villages but not yet funded. The baseline survey was completed in September 2021 and the project endline survey is planned for 2025. 6. Rationale for Additional Financing. In August 2024, the Ministry of Finance (MoF) submitted a request to the World Bank for an Additional Financing (AF) to expand SERSP social infrastructure and livelihood opportunities. The proposed AF will extend the coverage of SERSP’s community-driven investment and livelihoods approach to additional districts and Page 3 The World Bank Tajikistan Socio-Economic Resilience Strengthening Project (P168052) – Additional Financing Request 2 jamoats in the Rogun Hydropower Plant (HPP)1 affected area and in other rural areas with higher shares of disadvantaged population. This will expand the Government’s successful experience with community-driven development (CDD), as SERSP and the CASA-1000 Community Support Project (CASA-1000 CSP),2 also implemented by NSIFT, have demonstrated significant results at the community level. 7. The AF is designed to complement the Sustainable Financing for Rogun Hydropower Project. First, it will complement the livelihood restoration activities included in component 3 of the Rogun Project by further enhancing livelihood opportunities in the project area communities. While the government is providing livelihood restoration support to project-affected persons (PAPs) who are resettled, livelihood restoration takes time and significant resources. Stakeholders have requested additional funding and technical support to set up small businesses as well as small local infrastructure investments. The AF will support Rogun HPP host villages and jamoats and other communities with additional infrastructure investments, livelihood training and technical support for individuals, groups and small businesses. Second, the Government can use SERSP AF resources to supplement the limited revenues for Rogun HPP benefit sharing that will be available in the early years of Rogun construction. The Rogun HPP design provides for the establishment of a Benefit Sharing Program (BSP) which will be financed through a portion of the revenue from the sale of electricity generated by the HPP. These revenue streams are limited in the initial years, and are expected to scale up gradually, as all turbines become operational and the reservoir reaches full supply level. A third rationale is that the AF project will strengthen the institutional capacity of target jamoats to use a participatory, community-led approach to plan, budget, and implement local investments and services. This in turn will help them effectively utilize revenues from future Rogun HPP benefit sharing as well as from other sources. It will also build a strong sense of ownership on the part of communities for the investments that they select for financing and demonstrate enhanced responsiveness on the part of government to the prioritized needs of communities. 8. An additional rationale is that the CDD approach is one of the top options emerging in the ongoing process to design the Rogun BSP – although a final decision on the BSP design will not be made until after the AF is approved. Globally, CDD approaches are used for benefit sharing for large infrastructure projects in multiple countries. This is in part due to built-in stakeholder engagement and feedback mechanisms that enhance participation, include women, youth and vulnerable groups, and in turn build local ownership and strengthen risk management. In Tajikistan, the CASA-1000 Community Support Project, also implemented by NSIFT, has successfully used a CDD approach to provide infrastructure and other services to communities in the CASA-1000 electricity transmission corridor before and during construction with 97% of beneficiaries reporting that they consider the CSP an effective response to the local impacts of CASA-1000. This CDD experience is one of the options being considered in the ongoing BSP design process. The design of AF is intended to be flexible, to be adapted to the BSP approach that will eventually be approved by the Government. 9. The AF will also align with priority Government objectives for improving the role of public institutions in economic development, alleviation of social and gender imbalances, and adaptation to climate change, and WB CPF objectives to enhance people’s knowledge and skills, address regressive gender norms, promote livelihood opportunities for women in rural settings, enhance public sector transparency and accountability, and ensure affordable, climate resilient energy. Finally, it will help to establish a platform for other financiers to support, via co-financing or other means, Rogun BSP and place- based, community-led infrastructure and livelihoods. 10. The AF will expand SERSP coverage to include Rogun-affected jamoats (70% of AF community investments) as well as jamoats with higher shares of disadvantaged population (the remaining 30%) in other parts of the country. In the Rogun-affected area, the AF will cover jamoats in the three districts and two cities (Faizobad, Nurobad, and Danghara districts, Rogun and Tursunzoda cities) that include resettlement villages under the second phase of resettlement in the Rogun HPP – 5 jamoats and Darband small town with 8 resettlement villages and an additional 27 jamoats across the five districts. In addition to the Rogun affected area, two additional districts will be selected from regions of the country (Khatlon and GBAO) that have lower income per capita, and selection of target districts will be based on criteria such as population, investments per capita and unemployment. These criteria, which will be weighted, will provide a mechanism to select districts with significant needs, but not exclusively the poorest or most remote. The selection criteria for these districts, including weighting scores for each criterion, developed by the Government will be described in the POM. 14. Component 1: Inclusive Local Development Initiatives (US$17.5 million IDA). The AF will provide subgrants to Rogun-affected and other jamoats to finance local-level, climate-resilient, socio-economic infrastructure that addresses priorities identified through two cycles of a participatory village needs assessment and decision-making process. It will strengthen the capacity of jamoat administrations and community-level institutions, including mahalla committees, to engage with citizens and deliver services efficiently, fairly, and in a way that responds to the needs of citizens, including youth and women. This component will finance public goods, civil works, community mobilization and capacity building support for community members and local government staff. 15. Component 2: Inclusive livelihoods (US$5.0 million IDA). This component is being revised and expanded to reflect lessons from the parent project and good practices identified from consultations with other livelihood support programs supported by the World Bank and other development partners in Tajikistan. Building on activities in the parent project, the Page 5 The World Bank Tajikistan Socio-Economic Resilience Strengthening Project (P168052) – Additional Financing Request 2 component will support individual and common interest groups to initiate or expand livelihood activities through training, finance and technical support. It will introduce a new subcomponent support existing and registered MSEs in target districts as business partners to improve local economic opportunities, create value chain linkages with downstream producers and generate new jobs for women, youth, returning migrants and other beneficiaries. These activities build on lessons from the parent project including need for: (a) expanding the livelihood beneficiaries beyond youth to also include women, Rogun affected communities, returning migrants and other vulnerable groups, (b) deepening support to livelihood groups supported through the project to enhance sustainability of enterprises set up by the beneficiaries, (c) continuing and deepening mentoring and coaching support to beneficiaries, (d) extending support to existing micro and small enterprises to create more economic activity and jobs in the selected districts and (e) providing enhanced implementation support to NSIFT and selected Implementing Partner(s) to address capacity gaps in the market and to ensure beneficiaries receive higher quality technical inputs (e.g. trainings, mentoring, coaching, monitoring of results) through the project. The activities under this component are designed to be flexible and to complement the Government of Tajikistan’s livelihood restoration support for Rogun-affected communities as this is detailed and rolled out. These activities will coincide with activities under a grant from the Japan Social Development Fund (JSDF) to support women’s economic empowerment in Tajikistan. The JSDF grant funded project and the AF have geographical overlap and are being designed in parallel, so that there is potential for JSDF piloting of alternative livelihood models, such as self-help-saving groups, to feed into AF implementation. 16. Component 3. Project Management, Monitoring and Evaluation (US$2.5 million IDA). This component will be expanded to cover new target areas. In addition to supporting overall project management, it will support NSIFT’s alignment with the BSP adopted by the government if applicable. This component will also support learning between component 2 and the JSDF on women’s economic empowerment being prepared for Tajikistan in FY 2025-26. This component will also cover repair and maintenance costs for the sanitary investments of the original project. 17. Change in disbursement estimates and schedule. Current Projects on International Waterways OP 7.50 Yes Projects in Disputed Area OP 7.60 No Summary of Screening of Environmental and Social Risks and Impacts The environmental and social risks are rated Moderate and Substantial, respectively, resulting in an overall Substantial' risk rating. Socially, the project is expected to have positive impacts, particularly for youth and women. Various risks exist, both external and internal, with key concerns around 'exclusion' due to geographic, socio-economic, and gender factors. Mitigation measures have been outlined, dependent on the performance of NGOs providing social intermediation services during implementation. For land acquisition, the project will use existing buildings or unused public land, with a Resettlement Policy Framework (RPF) in place as a backup. The environmental risk is Moderate as the impacts are expected to be small to medium scale and the associated environmental impacts are expected to be temporary, predictable, and easily mitigable. The types of work involve new construction, improvement, upgrading, rehabilitation, repair, retrofitting type of work. Additional Financing will expand financing to the Rogun-affected areas and beside the typical project activities, include new investments for nature-based tourism infrastructure or restoration of degraded landscapes. Reforestation will be done with local species from local nurseries. The Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMF), Stakeholder Engagement Plan (SEP), Resettlement Policy Framework (PRF), Labor Management Procedures (LMP) and Environmental and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP) have been developed to address these risks. E. Implementation 20. Implementation arrangements remain unchanged. The implementation arrangements for the SERSP AF will remain the same as for the parent project. The National Social Investment Fund of Tajikistan (NSIFT), the project’s implementing agency, will support communities in social mobilization with a competitively procured Training and Facilitation Partner (TFP). The MoF provides the proceeds of the IDA grant to the NSIFT through a subsidiary agreement.
