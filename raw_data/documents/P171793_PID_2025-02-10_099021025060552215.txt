Introduction and Context Country Context Niger’s economy has been dependent on agriculture, which is highly vulnerable to climate shocks, leading to volatile growth, and is projected to become more dependent on hydrocarbon, exposing it to even higher volatility. The agriculture sector contributes to about 40 percent of GDP. With limited improvements in productivity and high population growth, over half the population lives in extreme poverty, aggravated by gender disparities, with some of the weakest human capital development indicators globally. In 2023, Niger was marked by a political crisis, with an unconstitutional regime change, followed by sanctions and a large reduction in financing. After nearly 7 months, the sanctions were lifted on February 24, 2024; however, there are lingering effects: (i) external financing remains below pre-coup levels, contributing to lower government revenue, and (ii) political tensions with Benin have persisted, with the border remaining closed. Landlocked conditions and high costs of doing business obstruct Niger’s development prospects. Transport costs are high because of insufficient roads maintenance, common roadblocks, and limited competition in the trucking sector. In the most recent 2018 Logistics Performance Index (LPI), Niger scored 2 out of a maximum score of 5, ranking 157th out of 160 countries relative to a rank of 100 in 2016.1 This score is also well below the average LPI of 2.39 for sub-Saharan Africa. Sectoral and Institutional Context Road transport is by far the most dominant transport mode in Niger,2 and the RN 11 is the main transport axis connecting Agadez and Zinder. The Agadez–Zinder Road segment is a 425-km section of RN 11, one of Niger’s key transport axes and part of the Trans-Saharan corridor. RN 11 is a major national trade route through which the northeastern region of Agadez receives agricultural goods from the southern regions of Maradi and Zinder, and through which the southern regions receive livestock from the north. The dominant economic activity along the RN 11 axis is the primary sector (agriculture and livestock), followed by small-scale commerce. Niger is highly vulnerable to the impacts of climate change, which damage road infrastructure, affect transport connectivity, and contribute to food insecurity and fragility. Niger ranks 185 out of 187 countries in the 2022 ND-GAIN Country Vulnerability Index, indicating that it is one of the most vulnerable countries in the world and has low ability to adapt to climate change impacts.3 Niger is one of the hottest and driest countries in the world and is already experiencing severe droughts and heat waves that result in chronic food insecurity and social tensions.4 Niger’s arid climate is inducive to the movement of sand dunes and sandstorms, causing encroachment into farmland, transport infrastructure, and drainage 1 The most recent LPI was completed in 2023, but did not include Niger. Proposed New Development Objective The proposed new PDO is: To enhance climate resilient connectivity and road safety and to improve access to basic socio-economic infrastructure for selected communities in Northeastern Niger. D. Project Description 1. Revised Component 1: Road reconstruction, maintenance, and safety improvements along the Zinder– Agadez Road Section (Original project – US$133 million; Additional Financing [AF] allocation – US$58 million). Under the parent project, this component supports (i) reconstruction and rehabilitation of the 226-km national road (RN 11) connecting Tanout and Tiguidit; (ii) performance-based management and maintenance contracts (OPRC) for the 425-km national road (RN 11) section connecting Zinder and Agadez; and (iii) road safety along the Zinder-Agadez Road section. 2. Changes to component 1. The AF will finance rehabilitation of the 62-km road segment on the RN 11 connecting Agadez to Tiguidit to safe and climate-resilient standards5 using a Build and Design approach. This subcomponent will also address budget needs for an additional two years of climate-informed maintenance works6 and minor cost overruns for the road segments from Zinder to Tiguidit, along with road safety enhancements. To improve long-term maintenance, climate resilience, and environmental sustainability of the RN 11 corridor, the AF will support the installation of weighing stations and toll booths across the corridor. 3. Changes to component 2. The AF will finance the rehabilitation to climate-resilient standards of an additional 200 km of primary and secondary access rural roads in Northeastern Niger within the Zinder-Agadez region to support the development of nearby rural communities. The AF will also finance key socio-economic infrastructure in Northeastern Niger within the Zinder-Agadez region. Selection of infrastructure to be financed will emphasize enabling access to basic services and economic opportunities for women. This will be coupled with direct support to economically empower women-led associations and cooperatives. 5. These potential negative risks and impacts are related to: (i) resource use and pollution management (procurement of raw materials for civil works, water and energy use, GHG emissions , dust, noise, potential contamination of water sources due to spills and runoff of petroleum products, seepage into storage areas and improper disposal of fuels, solid and liquid waste management mainly due to management and disposal construction waste and hazards; (ii) Occupational and community health and safety; (iii) Potential loss of vegetation and biodiversity during civil works and site preparation, quarry areas for materials; and (iv) the potential impact of civil works and excavations on cultural heritage.
