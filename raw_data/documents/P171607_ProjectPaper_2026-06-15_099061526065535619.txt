PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. A. In response to the formal request received from the Government of Uganda (GoU) on May 6, 2026, this restructuring seeks approval for the cancellation of IDA Credit No. 70250, IDA Grant and IDA Window for Host Communities and Refugees (WHR) No. 9590, and Recipient-Executed Trust Fund Grant No. TF0B4751, committed to the GoU under the Investment for Industrial Transformation and Employment Project (INVITE, P171607). The total cancellation amounts to: (i) IDA resources of SDR 105,571,445.03 (US$ 144,046,2901) of which IDA Credit is SDR 68,600,000 (USD 93,645,860.00), IDA Grant is SDR 36,971,455.97 (USD 50,400,000) which includes SDR 32,583,011.72 from WHR. The reduction in the cancellation amount beyond what has been disbursed to date from the original US$200 million commitment is due to an incurred foreign exchange loss of US$ 4,927,210 (SDR 3,245,967.72); The cancellation also includes RETF resources amounting to US$ 4,451,228 undisbursed from the total RETF allocation of US$8,400,000. The GoU has committed to refunding the balances from disbursed amounts to the World Bank promptly, as stated in its cancellation letter. Project Status 2. The Investment for Industrial Transformation and Employment Project (INVITE, P171607) was approved to support Uganda in mitigating the effect of COVID-19 on private sector investment and employment, and to create new economic opportunities including in refugee and host communities. The project comprises four components: (i) Component 1 — Mitigating the Impact of COVID-19 with a Focus on the Manufacturing and Exporting Sectors, including in Refugee and Host Districts; (ii) Component 2 — Creating New Productive and Transformative Assets including in Refugee and Host Districts; (iii) Component 3 — Enhancing Capabilities in Public Institutions and Private Firms; and (iv) Component 4 — Implementation Support, Monitoring, and Evaluation. Financing is provided through IDA Credit No. 70250, IDA Grant and IDA Window for Host Communities and Refugees (WHR) No. 9590, and Recipient-Executed Trust Fund Grant No. TF0B4751. 3. Now in its fifth year of implementation, the project has an overall disbursement rate of ~26.4 percent — US$51.03 million under IDA and US$3.95 million under the MDTF — but an absorption rate of only 1.26 percent (US$2.58 million)2, entirely attributable to MDTF-financed activities. Progress towards achievement of the PDO is unsatisfactory. This reflects continued implementation delays under Components 1 and 2, which account for all IDA financing, resulting in no progress toward the project objective and zero absorption of disbursed IDA funds by the client. 4. Despite implementation arrangements being established under Component 4, several critical building blocks required for the operational readiness of the INVITE Trust remained incomplete at the time of the cancellation request. These gaps effectively impacted activities financed under the IDA resources. Persistent contract management weaknesses and inadequate implementation oversight at the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) added to these challenges. 5. Implementation under Component 3, financed by the MDTF, recorded substantive progress, especially in the past one year. The PIU signed grant agreements with 113 beneficiary exporting firms totaling approx. US$785,879, and prequalified 37 Business Development Consultants whose profiles were published on the INVITE portal. All 113 firms were engaged with consultants to address business development service needs spanning product upgrading, export market expansion, and market diversification. In a separate letter, the Bank will request the borrower to refund the disbursed IDA funds amounting to approx. US$51.026 million3. Rationale for Restructuring 7. During the March 2026 Implementation Support Mission, the Task Team and the Government of Uganda (GoU) concluded that the project design could no longer be satisfactorily implemented within the remaining project life ahead of the January 30, 2027 closing date. This assessment underpinned the downgrade of both PDO and Overall Implementation Progress ratings to Moderately Unsatisfactory in the March 2026 ISR. 8. The mission and the GoU initially agreed on a significant partial cancellation of the project with a streamlined scope retained for the remainder of the project. However, the GoU later reversed this position and requested full cancellation of all project financing instruments, committing to the immediate refund of any disbursed balances in the project Designated Account. The formal cancellation request was received by the World Bank on May 6, 2026. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 9.
