Overall progress toward achieving the Project Development Objective (PDO) and the implementation progress were assessed as Moderately Satisfactory. Three out of four PDO indicators have already been achieved, while one indicator on improved budget reliability is off target for Fiscal Year 2023 with the outturn of actual expenditure for the year against original budget has measured to 14% against a target of 7%. One of the Intermediate Results Indicators (IRIs), namely the FMIS coverage of performance information, is on track for achievement in the next FY. Key status of the components is summarized below: • Component 1- PFM IT and Systems Roll-Out: IFMIS roll out activities continue to progress well. The Government is enhancing IFMIS and reinforcing its capacity to support IPSAS implementation. The IFMIS department has been reenforced with experts who work in tandem with the IPSAS team for updating the system with the necessary requirements. Based on user requirements prepared by the IPSAS team, several modules 4/16/2024 Page 1 of 7 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Rwanda Public Finance Management Reform Project (P164807) in IFMIS have been configured (including the Debt, Financial Reporting and Consolidation and Asset Management modules). The revised Standard Chart of Accounts (SCoA) has been approved. • Component 2: Accounting and Financial Reporting: Posetive progress has been noted under this component. Key achievements include updated IPSAS implementation roadmap approved by the steering committee; 85% completion of accrual IPSAS accounting requirements; legacy asset requirements with IFMIS configuration completed; asset valuation procedures, processes and manuals approved and disseminated for MDAs to update their assets in IFMIS; chart of account approved and configured in IFMIS; existing financial reporting formats updated to capture all assets and debts in IFMIS for FY 2024 and IFMIS department fully sourced with experts to reinforce the implementation of the roadmap. Following through the full delivery of this component and preparing a capacity building strategy beyond the project life are priority areas. • Component 3: Performance-Based Budgeting (PBB) and Medium -Term expenditure Framework (MTEF): Satisfactory Progress continues to be made under this component. PBB has been rolled out to each sector in accordance with the PFM law and performance indicators. Monitoring and evaluation reports (based on these defined performance indicators) are fully generated from IFMIS with further link required for non-financial indicators. The use of MTEF base line costing has started in FY 23/24. The citizen’s guide to the budget is developed each year and MINECOFIN carries out a comprehensive citizen engagement process during the budget preparation and dissemination. To improve the data quality needed for PBB, continuous trainings will continue to be carried out to sector working groups, as well as targeted PFM staff. • Component 4-Professionalization of PFM Staff-PFM Capacity Development: Satisfactory progress continues to be made under this component as the target for FY 23 has already been achieved. Key achievements since the last mission include: (i) increased number of qualified Certified Public Accountants (36 students), Certified accounting technicians (50 students) and Certified risk managers (18 students) making the total number of qualifications to date in the various fields to 1, 022; (ii)results noted from the support provided to high performing university students, yielding 57% pass rate in the last two sittings; (iii) consultancy for procurement professionalization on-boarded, needs assessment completed and curriculum and training materials development underway for the four levels of qualification; (iv) TA for supporting the amendment of the procurement professionalization law has been on-boarded with anticipated draft law tabled for consultation by March 2024; and (v) support to Rwanda Public Procurement Authority and Institute of Chartered Certified Accountants of Rwanda (ICPAR). However, with the leadership of MINECOFIN, dialogue should be initiated within government to develop a strategy on sustainably carrying out the professionalization agenda after the project closure.
