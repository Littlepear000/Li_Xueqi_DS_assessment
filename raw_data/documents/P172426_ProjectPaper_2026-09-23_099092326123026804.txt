PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING INTRODUCTION 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the approval of the Regional Vice President for a proposed Level II restructuring of the Supporting Egypt’s Universal Health Insurance System (UHIS) Project (P172426), extending the Project closing date by twenty-three (23) months, from September 30, 2026, to August 31, 2028. This is in response to a request letter dated July 1, 2026, from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, International Cooperation and Expatriates of the Arab Republic of Egypt that the extension is required to sustain the Project’s development impact. The Project scope, development objective and financing amount remain unchanged. PROJECT BACKGROUND 2. The UHIS project, financed by a US$400 million loan (Loan Number 9132-EG) from the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD), was approved by the World Bank Board of Executive Directors on June 16, 2020, and became effective on August 29, 2021. It is an Investment Project Financing (IPF) operation with Performance- Based Conditions (PBCs). The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to (i) increase the coverage of Egypt’s UHIS in Phase I Governorates, (ii) strengthen UHIS-related governance and institutions, and (iii) provide temporary financial protection against high out-of-pocket health expenditures for vulnerable populations outside Phase I Governorates. 3. The Project has been restructured twice. The first restructuring, approved on June 26, 2024, extended the Project closing date by 23 months from October 31, 2024, to September 30, 2026, in response to a request letter dated February 8, 2026, from the Ministry of International Cooperation. The second restructuring, processed in response to a request letter dated September 5, 2024, from the Ministry of Planning, Economic Development and International Cooperation, revised select PBCs, their disbursement formulas and allocations, added new PBCs, and revised select PDO indicators, intermediate results indicators and their targets. It was approved and the corresponding Amendment Letter to the Loan Agreement was transmitted to the Government on December 4, 2024. However, the countersigning of the amendment letter was delayed by 21-months, and the restructuring could not be completed until September 4, 2026. PROJECT STATUS 4. Progress toward achieving the PDO and overall implementation progress are both rated Moderately Satisfactory in the latest Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR Sequence 12, May 21, 2026). The Project has made significant progress toward its development objective, particularly by rolling out UHIS across all six Phase I governorates; enrolling more than 5.2 million beneficiaries, representing over 85 percent of the total population of the six Phase I governorates; contracting more than 383 providers, including 86 private providers; near-doubling the utilization of primary health care services per capita; establishing national quality accreditation standards across different types of healthcare service provision; and establishing the legal framework for covering vulnerable populations. However, additional time and significant work are needed to complete, pilot, and fully deploy all UHIS IT system modules across the UHIS governorates. Moreover, although the legislative framework for enrolling vulnerable populations is now in place, it has not yet translated into their full enrollment in UHIS. In addition to this, while the second restructuring was pending completion, the task team worked proactively with the Project Management Unit and the Independent Verification Agent on 12 new/restructured Performance-Based Results (PBRs) introduced through the restructuring, representing approximately US$110 million in achieved results. Of these, 11 PBRs worth US$75 million have been proactively verified and can now be processed following completion of the restructuring, while verification of PBR 9, valued at US$35 million, is ongoing. However, because the Project received an unrecovered advance of US$97.3 million under Category 1 of the Loan Agreement at its outset, against future achievement of the PBCs, most proceeds associated with the 12 PBRs will likely be used to document and recover the advance rather than generate additional disbursements to the Government. 6. Fiduciary, safeguards, and INT compliance: As of September 2026, Procurement, Financial Management, and Environmental and Social Safeguards are all rated Moderately Satisfactory. There are no outstanding audit reports, and all previous Interim Financial Reports and audit reports have been received, reviewed and deemed acceptable. No change in procurement arrangements. There are no open Integrity Vice Presidency (INT) investigations related to the sector or Project, and the team confirms that no contract has been awarded to or is in the process of being awarded to a debarred firm(s). RATIONALE FOR RESTRUTURING 7. The proposed extension of the Project closing date responds directly to the Government’s request and is necessary to complete ongoing activities, including disbursements against the new and restructured PBRs introduced through the recent restructuring. During the extension period, the team expects to complete verification and disburse funds for the 12 Performance-Based Results (PBRs) already achieved and currently under review, representing approximately US$110 million in achieved results. In parallel, several new and restructured PBRs are also at various stages of implementation and would require time before they are completed and verified, to further strengthen the Project’s results and disbursement performance. The 21-month delay in completing the second restructuring, which was finalized only in September 2026, constrained Project performance. The extension is therefore critical to recover lost time and enable the Project to deliver its full results agenda and achieve the Project development objective. 8. The 23-month extension is equally important to complete two pivotal reforms that have reached critical stages and require sustained support through full implementation. The UHIS digital transformation is fundamental to the system’s operational efficiency and long-term sustainability. As UHIS expands nationwide, the IT system will become the operational backbone for insuring Egypt’s population of approximately 110 million. Because of the IT system’s complexity, its modules are advancing at different rates. The beneficiary enrollment and provider modules are already being piloted and deployed in some governorates and are expected to be completed within the first year of Project extension. By contrast, the claims processing module—essential for provider payment and financial protection—is only partly operational and will require more time to complete its remaining functions, conduct pilot testing, and achieve full deployment. Additional time is also required to implement the Prime Minister’s recent decree mandating the automatic enrollment of Takaful and Karama (T&K) beneficiaries in UHIS. This will require sustained inter-agency coordination, integration of government databases, and a nationwide outreach campaign to translate the legal The World Bank Supporting Egypt’s Universal Health Insurance System (P172426) Page 3 mandate into effective coverage. The foundational work supported by the Project will establish the systems and processes needed to extend coverage seamlessly to over 15 million T&K beneficiaries nationwide. II.
