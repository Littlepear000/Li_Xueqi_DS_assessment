Product Information Main: Lowlands Livelihood Resilience Project, Phase Two (LLRP II) (P180076) Operation ID Product/Financing Instrument P180076 Investment Project Financing (IPF) Beneficiary Country/Countries Geographical Identifier Ethiopia Ethiopia Practice Area (Lead) Farming and Agribusiness Borrower(s) Implementing Agency Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia Ministry of Irrigation and Lowlands Additional Financing Request 1 Estimated One Review Date Estimated Board Date 16-Nov-2026 07-Dec-2026 Current Status of the parent operation LLRP II is a five-year, US$424 million IPF operation supporting Ethiopia’s lowlands, comprising US$340 million IDA financing, US$80 million IFAD co-financing, and US$4 million in community contributions. Implemented by the Ministry of Irrigation and Lowlands since June 21, 2024, the project aims to improve the livelihoods and climate resilience of pastoral and agropastoral communities. As of August 2026, IFAD had confirmed US$69.24 million of its commitment and reaffirmed plans to mobilize the remaining balance. Progress toward the PDO is rated Satisfactory, Overall Implementation Progress Moderately Satisfactory, Overall Risk Substantial, and Environmental and Social Performance Moderately Satisfactory. The project has reached over one million direct beneficiaries (33%), while the sustainable land management indicator has achieved 67% of its end target. Cumulative disbursements stand at US$212.75 million (62.57%) under IDA and US$19.16 million (24%) under IFAD financing. Rationale for AF and proposed changes The AF will restore the US$50 million reallocated through the Rapid Response Option (RRO) under the Contingent Emergency Response Project (CERP), reinstating the project’s financing envelope and supporting implementation of its original design and development objectives Expanded investments in water resources, feed and forage production, and animal health services will strengthen drought response, livelihoods, and climate resilience in Ethiopia’s most climate-vulnerable lowlands. Total financing will rise from US$424 million to US$474 million. As part of the AF, a zero- budget Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC) will be added to address eligible crises. Therefore, the AF will be accompanied by restructuring to reflect the revised components. Components Changes to Project Components The proposed Additional Financing (AF) will not change the parent project’s development objective, implementation arrangements, geographic scope, targeting approach, results framework, investment priorities, or overall design. Its main purpose is to replenish the US$50 million disbursed by the Government of Ethiopia through the Rapid Response Option (RRO) under the Contingent Emergency Response Project (CERP), thereby restoring the project’s original financing envelope and enabling implementation to proceed as envisaged. The AF will also add a new zero-budget Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC). Therefore, the AF will be accompanied by restructuring. With the AF, the project will continue to finance the four existing components of the parent project as well as the newly added fifth, zero-budget component: (i) Pastoral Risk Management for Resilience (US$228 million): Supports disaster-risk profiling and planning and climate- resilient public economic infrastructure, including water-resource development, flood-control structures, irrigation schemes, all-weather feeder roads, livestock markets, and veterinary facilities. (ii) Integrated Rangeland Management (US$64.48 million): Promotes sustainable rangeland management and restoration through rangeland monitoring systems, investment planning, pasture rehabilitation, fodder and forage development, invasive-species control, and other nature-based solutions. (iii) Climate-Resilient and Sustainable Livelihoods (US$91.49 million): Expands access to financial services; supports micro and small enterprises; promotes climate-smart technologies; improves irrigation and drainage services; strengthens livestock and crop value chains; and enhances food security and nutrition outcomes. (iv) Project Management, Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning (US$40.03 million): Supports project coordination, fiduciary and safeguards management, monitoring and evaluation, knowledge management, policy engagement, and learning platforms. (v) Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC) (US$0.00): Provides rapid access to project resources following an eligible crisis or emergency affecting Ethiopia’s pastoral and agro-pastoral lowlands. The component may be activated following an emergency declaration by the Federal Government or the responsible sector authority. Activation and implementation will follow the Financing Agreement, an approved CERC Manual and Emergency Action Plan, and applicable fiduciary, procurement, environmental, and social requirements. The AF will enable full implementation of the priority investments originally planned under Components 1–3, particularly those designed to mitigate recurrent droughts, including impacts associated with the ongoing El Niño phenomenon. These investments include water-resource development, feed and forage production, animal-health services, and related resilience-building measures. The new CERC will also enable the project to respond rapidly to eligible crises and emergencies. Overall, the AF will preserve the project’s original scope and restore its capacity to improve livelihoods and strengthen the climate resilience of pastoral and agro-pastoral communities. It screened subprojects, applied E&S instruments, promoted resource efficiency, and avoided impacts on sensitive ecosystems. Its environmental performance is rated ‘Moderately Satisfactory’. As the Additional Financing (AF) mirrors the parent project’s scope, the environmental risk rating remains ‘Substantial’. Environmental, health, and safety (EHS) risks may arise under Component 1—including small-scale irrigation, rural access roads, solar energy, livestock clinics and laboratories, and flood-control structures—and Component 2, which covers smallholder irrigation and feed and forage production and management. Key risks include poor water quality in water-supply subprojects, particularly in lowland areas with high groundwater fluoride levels; inadequate management of effluent and sludge from water-treatment facilities; dam safety risks for schemes reliant on small dams, compounded by limited client capacity; land clearing, soil disturbance, and altered surface-water flows from new rural roads; resource depletion, soil salinization, and water contamination from agrochemical use in drought prone, water-scarce lowlands; hazardous and non- hazardous waste from construction and operations, including solar panels, batteries, and agrochemical containers; and occupational health and safety risks, highlighted by a project fatality. Given these varied risk pathways, including potentially long-term or irreversible impacts, the ‘Substantial’ rating is maintained. The rating also reflects limited capacity for E&S risk management, monitoring, and enforcement in Ethiopia’s lowland pastoral areas.
