No Rationale for Choice of Financing Instrument: PforR instrument supports the government’s reform program by linking disbursements directly to achievement of verified results, incentivizing outcomes and sustained sector reforms. It is well suited given three factors.1) It aligns disbursements with results that enhance Road Transport Corporations’ (RTCs) operational efficiency and financial sustainability. 2) Karnataka’s vision of fleet expansion, electrification, infrastructure development, and modernization, critical for improving connectivity and access to jobs , requires a holistic ecosystem including efficient planning, diversified financing, public private partnerships, and strong institutions. PforR enables this through technical, fiduciary, and environmental and social assessments that identify system gaps and embed them into performance- based DLIs. 3) It provides flexibility to implement a results-focused program across multiple RTCs while strengthening systems for long term sustainability and replication across states. Proposed Program Development Objective(s) To improve access to sustainable public transport services, leveraging private sector participation in the state of Karnataka. Sector & institutional context / problem statement Urban and regional mobility in Karnataka faces a persistent demand–supply gap in public bus services, driven by slow fleet expansion and uneven adoption of low emission and modern technologies. RTCs face financial and operational stress from rising costs, infrequent fare revisions, inconsistent state support. Weak creditworthiness and unattractive contracts limits private sector participation. State (GoK) identified 21 strategic areas to address the challenges, structured around 3 pillars- i) Improving operational efficiency and financial sustainability of RTCs, ii) expansion of urban and regional bus services, and ii) enhancing institutional framework and capacity. Summary Description (1) Context and Key Issues Karnataka, India’s ninth most populous state (69 million in 2026), and has above-average urbanization (38.7%), with urban growth highly concentrated in major centers, most notably in the State capital- Bengaluru. Private vehicle growth—particularly cars and two-wheelers at ~10%—has outpaced state-owned bus services, which expanded at only ~2% Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR). This divergence has created a widening gap between demand and formal public transport supply. The three regional RTCs - Karnataka State Road Transport Corporation (KSRTC), Northwestern Karnataka Road Transport Corporation (NWKRTC), and Kalyana Karnataka Road Transport Corporation (KKRTC) operate about 19,000 buses serving rural, semi-urban and intercity/ interstate routes, whereas in Bengaluru, the Bengaluru Metropolitan Transport Corporation (BMTC), urban RTC operates 7,000 buses. Buses account for 41% of all non-agricultural work trips and are particularly critical for women and low-income populations. However: • Access gaps persist in rural areas, with many villages lacking direct bus connectivity. • Urban congestion and emissions are rising due to rapid motorization, increasing travel times and costs. The challenge extends beyond fleet size to fleet composition and modernization: • Electric bus (e-bus) adoption is concentrated in Bengaluru (~25% of fleet), with minimal uptake in other regions. • Depot and charging infrastructure remain inadequate, and RTCs lack technical capacity for e-bus system management. • Shift to Gross Cost Contracts (GCC) requires RTCs to transition from operators to performance-oriented contract managers. Financial and institutional constraints have compounded these issues: • High input costs, below cost recovery fares, operational inefficiencies, and COVID-19 impacts have led to accumulated losses and declining creditworthiness. • RTCs rely on discretionary state support, limiting access to commercial finance and private sector participation. • Fragmented institutional systems and weak data integration hinder route rationalization, service optimization, and demand-responsive planning. (2) Program Description In 2022, the Government of Karnataka (GoK) adopted a strategic roadmap to transform RTCs into financially sustainable and operationally efficient public transport providers, identifying 21 reform areas. The program is anchored on three pillars: 1. Improving operational efficiency and financial sustainability 2. Expanding urban and regional bus services (with ~50% private participation) 3. Environmental and social risks of the Program are identified as “Substantial�? at this stage. For managing the risks & impacts, the Program will ensure E&S experts and focal points as appropriate at all levels, prepare and use E&S Screening and Audit tools, grievance mechanisms, develop appropriate designs and Standard Operating Protocols (SOPs) to manage EHS; in addition to continuous systematic training and capacity building through the proposed “Centre of Excellence�?. E&S Risk of the Investment Project Financing (IPF) component is rated as “Low�? at this stage, as it supports hiring of consultants, and capacity building measures and no civil works are included under IPF. Risks will further be clarified during the technical design and rating will be revisited if required. The Bank Environmental and Social Framework (ESF) will be applicable for the IPF component specifically the Standards on Environmental & Social Assessment, Labour and Working Conditions, Resource Efficiency and Pollution Prevention and Stakeholder Engagement (ESSs 1, 2, 3 and 10) are relevant at this stage, and the requirements will be ensured in TORs and other outputs. Gender and Citizen engagement: The Program will promote gender inclusion by integrating gender-responsive planning in transport services and infrastructure, strengthening existing schemes, and providing gender-sensitive training for RTC staff. It will establish citizen-centric service delivery through accessible and continuous feedback mechanisms, including a digital grievance redressal system (web and mobile). The program will also track outcomes, including the number of women benefiting from improved access to services and infrastructure. The Timing of ESSA Preparation, Consultation, and Disclosure As per the bank’s PforR Guidance, an Environmental and Social Systems Assessment (ESSA) will be conducted with stakeholder consultations to assess potential environmental and social risks and effects of activities under the operation, viz a viz the regulatory framework and capacities of the implementing agencies to manage these risks. Based on the findings, it will present measures to fill the gaps. Any high-risk activity, which may directly or indirectly result in significant and permanent risks to the environment, communities, cultural heritage and biodiversity will not be eligible under the operation.
