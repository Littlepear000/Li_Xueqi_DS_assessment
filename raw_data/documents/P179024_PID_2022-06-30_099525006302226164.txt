Introduction and Context Country Context 1. Despite strong economic growth pre-COVID-19, the Kyrgyz Republic remains one of the poorest countries in Europe and Central Asia. With a gross national income per capita of US$1,220 in 2018, the country has experienced strong but volatile economic growth, averaging 4 percent per year since 2010 (ranging from 0.5% in 2010 to 10.9% in 2013). With a population of about 6.2 million (over 60 percent living in rural areas), 64 percent are within the working age of 15-64 years and less than 40 percent (2.4 million) are employed. Given the limited absorbing capacity of domestic labor markets, it is estimated that over 15 percent of the population is working abroad. Despite progress in reducing poverty, more than 1.2 million people in the country (20.1 percent in 2019) were living below the national poverty line prior to COVID-19. Internationally comparable poverty measures based on the international $3.20-a-day poverty line (using 2011 PPP conversions rates[1]) – the measure most relevant for lower middle-income countries (LMIC) – also shows significant decline. 2. Economic growth has been largely driven by gold extraction and workers’ remittances from abroad and has not translated proportionally into labor force growth. Growth prior to the COVID-19 pandemic resulted in only modest improvements in welfare among the bottom 40 percent coupled with falling incomes for the upper 60 percent. Furthermore, jobs have not been created in the formal sector, and most of the employment creation took place in the informal sector, estimated to be around 50 percent of GDP and 60 percent of the total employment. The poverty gap at international measures ($3.20 per capita) has also stalled since 2010. Economic growth and poverty reduction are below the country’s potential, due to a business environment inconducive to private investment and job creation, weak public institutions and services, and constrained human capital that lacks essential skills required for a more productive and dynamic economy. 3. The COVID-19 crisis has partly undone the gains in poverty reduction achieved over the past Jun 15, 2022 Page 3 of 12 The World Bank Strengthening Social Assistance and Labor Market Programs in the Kyrgyz Republic Project (P179024) few years and a sizeable proportion of the population fell back into poverty . The COVID-19 crisis had adverse effects on labor income which have been the major contributors to poverty reduction prior to the pandemic. This, combined with the expected fall in the purchasing power of a substantial proportion of the population due to inflation and the lack of mitigating measures, left large segments of the population highly exposed to the economic consequences of the pandemic. Following the economic downturn due to the COVID pandemic the headline poverty rate increased by more than 5 percentage points in 2020, reversing the downward trend observed since 2013. As a result, an estimated 364 thousand people fell back into poverty between 2019 and 2020. The food insecurity prevalence rate based on direct responses from people about their experiences of constrained access to food also showed a sharp rise in 2020. 4. After a brief rebound in 2021 from the pandemic, the Kyrgyz economy is now hit by another major shock resulting from the ongoing conflict between Ukraine and Russia – through multiple channels. In the absence of mitigating measures, the large negative spillovers associated with the conflict are expected to reverse the progress made by the Kyrgyz economy in 2021 when annual GDP growth was 3.6 percent. The economy is now projected to contract by 2 percent in 2022, while average inflation is likely to exceed 15 percent, creating significant further pressure on fiscal and debt management as well as pushing more people into poverty. The fiscal deficit is expected to widen to about 5 percent of GDP in 2022 as the government increases spending to offset domestic spillovers from the war in Ukraine. Remittances, a major resource for poor and vulnerable, have started to significantly decline Recent data from the Listening to the Citizens of the Kyrgyz Republic shows that many migrants delayed remittances due to the unfavorable exchange rates: in the first two months of the conflict, the share of households receiving remittance fell by 16 percent, and the value of a typical transfer fell by 15 percent. In addition, the share of households with a member considering migration fell considerably from 13 percent before the crisis to 8 percent two months after. While massive returns have not been observed, they are expected to increase in case of a further deterioration of the Russian economy as a result of sanctions domestically, these effects are likely to be exacerbated by poor domestic labor market conditions including high rates of informality, self-employment (35 percent of total employment) and limited coverage of social insurance. Further, the decline in remittances (which were equivalent to 30 percent of GDP prior to the COVID-19 pandemic and mostly received by poor households) combined with higher inflation will erode households’ purchasing power. 5. The economic consequences of the downturn caused by the conflict are more severely affecting the poorest and vulnerable households, requiring the urgent support of the social protection system. The economic downturn in the Russian economy is severely affecting the poor and vulnerable groups and is expected to result in a significant increase and deepening of poverty. Households with labor migrants and receiving remittances are overrepresented among the poor and vulnerable: more than 20 percent of households from the poorest quintile receive some remittance income from international migrants, compared to 7 and 1 percent in the top quintiles. Like the drop in remittances, the increase in food prices is already disproportionately affecting the poor and vulnerable given the importance of food consumption in their consumption patterns. As a result of these effects, according to simulations using nationally representative household survey data, the poverty rate in the country is expected to jump to close to 40 percent compared to around 20 percent before the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic, in the absence of mitigating measures. In such a context, social protection systems have a crucial role to play both in providing emergency support to vulnerable households as well as to assist businesses and households during the period of economic recovery. Social Protection is an essential sphere of public policy in Kyrgyzstan and in the past few years, the GoK has undertaken serious reforms in the sector. Social protection is among the key focus areas of the National Development Program of the Kyrgyz Republic until 2026 which was recently adopted. It outlines the main priorities in the sector, such as better targeting of SP programs at the vulnerable and the poor, increased automatization of the SP system in order to improve transparency and efficiency of social spending, digital skills development, and a development of a comprehensive case management approach to SP and specifically social services. Based on this Development Program, the GoK, with the support of development partners, is currently developing a SP sectoral Development Framework to be adopted in 2023. 7. Existing programs in the country span the three pillars of the Social Protection system – Social Assistance, Labor Markets and Social Insurance, and the institutional setup is well placed to ensure effective service delivery. The priority groups targeted by existing Social Protection programs in the country include children and families in difficult life circumstances, people with disabilities, elderly and the unemployed. The main pillars of SP include: (ii) Social Assistance programs and social care services, at the core of which is the Uy-bulogo komok (or UBK, “Family Assistance for Children�?) program, a monthly child grant for poor families; (ii) Labor Market programs such as public works, vocational training, and unemployment assistance; and (iii) Social Insurance programs including contributory pensions to workers, the self-employed and farmers. The Ministry of Labor, Social Development and Migration (MLSDM) oversees the implementation of both social assistance as well as labor market programs and is therefore well placed to ensure efficient service delivery and support graduation of social assistance beneficiaries into the labor market. The Social Fund is responsible for implementation of social insurance programs. Social Assistance 8. The main social assistance program in the country—the UBK—had suffered over the years from chronic underfunding and as a result a significant exclusion of the poor. The current system of social assistance consists of several programs including, in addition to the UBK, a lump-sum birth grant, categorical monthly social benefits (targeting families with disabled members), energy subsidies and compensations for vulnerable groups. UBK is the largest social assistance program, providing monthly transfers to children living in poor families whose per capita income is below the Guaranteed Minimum Income (GMI) of KGS 1000 per month per household member. Even though the program has expanded coverage over the last few years, is effective in reaching the poor and distributing most of the benefits among the bottom 40 percent of the population and has increased coverage over the last few years, its contribution to poverty reduction and the poverty gap has been low (1.2 and 0.3 pp, respectively). The main reasons preventing a higher contribution of UBK to poverty reduction are related to low coverage of the poorest, significant exclusion errors, and until very recently, low benefit adequacy. Overall coverage remains low at 8.5 percent of the population (significantly less than the poverty rate) implying exclusion of a significant part of the poor population. This is in part due to limited budgetary resources dedicated to the program and a very low-income eligibility threshold. These characteristics leave the poorest, including those supported by UBK, not only in a situation in which leaving poverty is very difficult, but also highly exposed to future shocks. This is exacerbated by the fact that households have already been severely affected by the COVID-19 pandemic without a social protection response, which makes them particularly vulnerable to the negative economic spillovers of the Russian-Ukraine conflict. 9. To mitigate the impacts of the crisis on the poor and vulnerable, the Government recently Jun 15, 2022 Page 5 of 12 The World Bank Strengthening Social Assistance and Labor Market Programs in the Kyrgyz Republic Project (P179024) adopted measures to increase the benefit size of several programs since early 2022,1 and more recently the monthly benefit of the UBK in the context of the crisis,2 but requires urgent support to finance it and to also improve the performance of its targeting system. Given the currently very low incidence of the UBK benefits combined with the economic downturn the Kyrgyz Republic is currently facing, the government of the has recently adopted decree that would increase the monthly benefit amount of the UBK program by 50 percent from KGS 810 (approximately US$10) to KGS 1,200 (approximately US$15) starting June 1, 2022. The increase in the monthly benefit amount is expected to help the population currently eligible to the UBK program to cope with the adverse economic effects of the Russian-Ukrainian conflict. However, these efforts need to be accompanied by a strengthening of the targeting method to identify UBK beneficiaries to improve the coverage of the poor population currently not part of the UBK through a reduction in inclusion and exclusion errors. As the higher benefit is expected to increase UBK’s contribution to reduce extreme poverty, there is also a need to define a sustainable exit strategy for better off beneficiaries. Labor markets 10. The Kyrgyz Republic has a large working-age population but is faced with multiple challenges in labor markets, including high rates of unemployment among youth, informality, and vulnerabilities among workers who migrate overseas. The country’s population is young, with close to one-third of prime working age (29–59 years). However, official unemployment remains high and steadily close to 7.2 percent for the overall population, and over 18 percent for the youth. Job creation, especially in the formal sector, is limited and has not been able to keep pace with the new entries of youth into the labor market, who are frequently unemployed. For example, only 6 percent of the 88,000 jobs created in the country in 2016 were in the formal sector. This results in high rates of informal employment, characterized by low incomes and limited social insurance. Given the lack dynamism of domestic job creation, labor migration of youth is widespread with 15–20 percent of the population migrating to the Russian Federation. While this generates large amounts of remittances, which as a proportion of GDP is among the highest in the world, it also leaves migrants and recipient households very vulnerable to external shocks, as evidenced by the COVID-19 pandemic and now the Russian-Ukrainian conflict. As a result, there is a critical need for policies aimed at promoting inclusive job creation to absorb the productive potential of the workforce. 11. The GoK has introduced multiple programs as well as the necessary legislative, regulatory, and institutional frameworks for the implementation of the active labor market programs (ALMPs), but there remain significant challenges in design and delivery. The MLSDM is responsible for overseeing programs aimed at activating the workforce. Local offices of the MLSDM are responsible for implementation at the district level, including registering jobseekers, organizing public works, referring jobseekers to professional training or retraining courses, and so on. The main ALMPs are public works and small-scale vocational training programs which aim to activate vulnerable groups such as the low-skilled, youth, and people with special needs to promote graduation out of social assistance. However, the mix and scope of ALMPs is limited, with a shortage of support for self-employment and small-scale entrepreneurship development programs as well as limited support or absence of support with job counseling, apprenticeships, and wage subsidies and support for the hard-to-employ. The pension system is the main pillar of social insurance in the Kyrgyz Republic. The system is comprised of a basic pension (BP), a legacy pay-as-you-go (PAYG) pension, a notional defined contribution (NDC) and a funded defined contribution component (FDC). The Social Fund, which is the executive body of the system of the national social insurance and provision of pensions in the Kyrgyz Republic, manages the system. Those who do not qualify for an insurance pension receive a social pension. Overall, the system provides close to universal coverage for old age. Though about 90 percent of the old-age population is now receiving labor pensions, only about 30 percent of the working-age population is accruing pension rights at any given point of time, increasing the risk of poverty among the elderly in the future. At the same time, the Government is experimenting with mechanisms of expanding labor coverage, including recent unified collection of pension contributions with taxes under the Tax Administration. 13. In sum, the spillovers of the Russia-Ukraine conflict are expected to further depress domestic job creation as well as limiting employment opportunities in the Russian Federation in a context of limited capacity of public employment. Through a combination of factors including reduced demand and inflationary pressures, for many Kyrgyz citizens the conflict is expected to result in job losses and a drop of earnings of individuals that remain employed, especially among youth who are typically most vulnerable. In addition to the urgent increased support through social assistance to the poorest and most vulnerable, there is also an urgent need to develop, strengthen and scale up effective programs to provide support and assistance to workers most vulnerable to the negative impacts of the crisis and to strengthen public employment services, both as a crisis response and as a matter of capacity building to improve response to both idiosyncratic and future systemic shocks. There is also a need to support prospective international labor migrants who are expected to be adversely impacted by the economic situation in the Russian Federation, as well as current migrants who may return to the Kyrgyz Republic as a result of the negative shock. 14. There is need to urgent implement a set of measures to help affected households to offset the negative impact of the shock. The Government has requested the Bank tom process an emergency operation to provide support to poor and vulnerable families currently affected by the deterioration of the Russian economy. Accordingly, to ensure that funds to ensure adequate and timely support to poor and vulnerable households can be transferred to final beneficiaries on time, the proposed operation triggers the procedure under the World Bank Policy for Investment Project Financing, paragraph 12: Projects in Situations of Urgent Need of Assistance or Capacity Constraints due to conflict, impending natural disaster and capacity constraints. The operation is also supporting the foundation of effective activation measure to contribute to a rapid and sustained recovery process. C. Project Description Project Components 15. The project has three components that support achievement of the PDO. Component 1: Providing Emergency Support Through, and Enhancing the Effectiveness of the UBK Program ($19,8 million) 16. This component (i) provides emergency support and ensures that UBK social benefits are paid on time for the households whose incomes are particularly affected by the negative economic impacts caused by the economic crisis in Russia (Subcomponent 1.1: Enhanced Support to Extremely Poor Families, $17.5 million); and (ii) strengthens the effectiveness of the UBK program to support the poor and vulnerable during the shock and assist them to be better prepared for future shocks (Subcomponent 1.2: Improving the Effectiveness of the UBK Program, $2.3 million). To achieve these goals, this component will use the country’s existing country systems to deliver the increased benefit of the main anti-poverty program for families with children (UBK). The project will also support the Government in improving the effectiveness of the UBK program by developing, piloting, and evaluating the improved eligibility criteria and beneficiary selection methodology for the UBK program to reduce exclusion and inclusion errors. Component 2: Increasing the employability of vulnerable groups including labor migrants ($8.7 million) 17. The objective of this component is enhancing the employability and providing income generating opportunities to poor and vulnerable and difficult-to-employ individuals, as well as prospective and return labor migrants through the provision of more effective labor and employment services and strengthening of selected activation programs. Specifically, this component will achieve these objectives by supporting the following groups: (i) UBK beneficiaries to transition from social assistance to labor and employment opportunities, (ii) vulnerable youth and women to (re) enter the labor market, and (iii) returning and prospective labor migrants to better identify their needs, capacities, and aspirations and facilitate tailored support through three subcomponents. (SC) program. Subcomponent 2.2. (Enhancing Employability and Job Opportunities Among Youth Population, US$4 million) is to increase the employability and labor market insertion of poor and vulnerable youth who dropped out of the education system or completed only basic education and are currently not employed or employed in informal low earning activities. And Subcomponent 2.3 (Supporting temporary labor migrants in accessing better employment opportunities, $0.7 million) is to support the reintegration of labor migrants who returned to the Kyrgyz Republic from overseas (return migrants) and to strengthen the employability of workers who intend to migrate overseas (prospective migrants) who both have been affected by the spillovers of the recent conflict. Component 3: Strengthening the delivery systems of Social Protection ($1.5 million) 18. The proposed component has the medium- to long-term objective of improving the efficiency and effectiveness of the social protection delivery systems in the Kyrgyz Republic. the proposed project through this component will finance activities to ensure IT sustainability, additional data security, and expanded functionality of the social protection IT systems (Subcomponent 3.1: Strengthening Technical Capabilities and Increasing Data Protection of SP Data, $0.8 million). This component will also finance the project implementation unit for the duration of the operation (Subcomponent 3.2: Project Implementation Unit and Project Monitoring, $0.7 million). Overall, by enhancing IT systems in social protection and improving data exchanges, this Component will equip the Government of Kyrgyz Republic to be better prepared for future economic slowdown or other risks, including climate and natural risks, which may negatively impact the income of the poor and vulnerable households. Project Beneficiaries 19. The proposed project will support poor and vulnerable population if the country. The support to the MLSDM will ensure that the current beneficiaries of the UBK program receive the payments on time including the planned increase of the benefit amount. Therefore, the project will support approximately 110,000 families (350,000 children) who currently receive UBK monthly benefits with the additional payments. Developing and testing improved UBK targeting methodology is expected to cover additional beneficiary families. At around 8.5 percent of population covered by social assistance programs, a significant share of the poor population is currently excluded. The deterioration of the Russian economy can potentially contribute to a further increase in poverty. Regarding the activation programs in the country, the project will support about 3,000 families through the social contract program and about 3,000 youth at-risk through the youth employment program. The project will also support incoming labor migrants without job in Kyrgyzstan from several perspectives: First, lower remittances and therefore lower household income may take families of labor migrants to fall below UBK eligibility and as such they will be beneficiaries of the enhanced program benefit. Second, returning labor migrants may also be supported by activation programs, particularly the youth employment intervention. Finally, the registry of labor migrants will be a key operational tool to better identify the skills and aspirations of this group and provide tailored support. In the long term, improving delivery systems in social assistance will contribute to Government efforts to better target poor household under the UBK program, better support those households with improved social assistance as well as through the labor market programs. . The environmental risk rating is Low, and the social risk rating is Moderate. Accordingly, the project overall environmental and social framework (ESF) risk rating is Moderate. The following Environmental and Social Standards (ESS) are relevant to the Project: ESS 1, ESS 2, ESS 3, ESS 4, and ESS 10. 21. The project environmental risk is Low, since the project does not support civil works, and environmental risks are confined to the installation of new ICT equipment, disposal/recycling of electronic waste when equipment is replaced/repaired in the regional branches of the MLSDM. These risks are easily mitigable by the measures provided in electronic waste management plan (Checklist) prepared by the client. The electronic waste management plan (Checklist) will be prepared, reviewed and approved by the Bank, consulted, and disclosed prior to completion of project appraisal. 22. The project social risk is rated as Moderate. While the project activities per-se do are not risk- ridden, its implementation of the project activities will need to be crafted such as to ensure that errors of exclusion and inclusion are minimum. This would require making explicit eligibility criteria for participation, updating the national registry, firming up of the extent of assistance a particular household can expect and the duration thereof, ensuring the data privacy and delivery methods as well as transparency and accountability mechanisms. Further, it is critical to ensure that the project has an effective outreach program so that all the stakeholders and prospective beneficiaries are fully aware of the project's stipulations. All these can be addressed as a part of the project designing. The Environmental and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP) specifies the main responsibilities and actions to be undertaken by the Implementing Agency to ensure the project’s compliance with the Bank’s ESSs. The Stakeholder Engagement Plan (SEP) will be implemented throughout the project implementation. To mitigate labor management risks, the Labor Management Procedures (LMP) will be prepared, consulted upon, and disclosed prior to the Project effectiveness. E. Implementation Institutional and Implementation Arrangements 23. The MLSDM will be the implementing agency for the proposed project. The MLSDM is the central agency responsible for social assistance and labor program policies as well as the migration interventions in the Kyrgyz Republic. Direct implementation of social protection programs is carried out at district and local levels by the regional MLSDM branches. Therefore, the implementation of the UBK, the Social Contract, and the youth employment as well as other labor market programs are under its Jun 15, 2022 Page 10 of 12 The World Bank Strengthening Social Assistance and Labor Market Programs in the Kyrgyz Republic Project (P179024) domain. The MLSDM has implemented other World Bank-financed projects3 in the past and has been assessed by the World Bank fiduciary specialists as having the capacity to manage the proposed project with the support of a Project Implementation Unit (PIU). The PIU will be established in the MLSDM to support the MLSDM at central and subnational levels in implementing project activities and be responsible for the fiduciary as well as social and environmental aspects of the project. The PIU will comprise at least a project coordinator, a monitoring, procurement, FM specialist, and social development specialists responsible for the support of relevant activities under the three components of the project. .
