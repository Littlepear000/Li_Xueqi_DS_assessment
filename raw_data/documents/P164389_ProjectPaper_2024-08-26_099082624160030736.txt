PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the approval of the Country Director on a proposed restructuring for the Water Security and Resilience for the Valley of Mexico Project (PROSEGHIR) (P164389), to respond to the official requests from the Government of Mexico and the Project Executing Agencies to partially cancel the Project loan by US$90 million and to reduce the project implementation period by one year (official letter dated February 9, 2024). These changes, as well as others described below, are necessary to make the Project Development Objective (PDO) achievable, and will be reflected in the Project legal documents, the Loan Agreement, as well as in the environmental and social safeguards documents. A. Background and Project Status 2. The PROSEGHIR was approved by the World Bank Board of Executive Directors on February 27, 2020, with an International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) loan of US$120 million (Loan No. 9049-MX). The Project was signed on February 28, 2022, with an expected closing date of December 31, 2025, and the World Bank approved its first restructuring on May 5, 2022 to realign project resources with new government priorities and further strengthen the reliability of the Cutzamala Water System.1 3. The PDO is to improve the reliability of the Cutzamala System and strengthen the management of groundwater resources in the Valley of Mexico. Activities financed under the Project after the first restructuring are aimed at: (i) improving the energy efficiency and resilience of the Cutzamala System; (ii) enhancing information systems for better groundwater monitoring and surface water quality monitoring; and (iii) supporting the development of an investment portfolio to improve aquifer conditions and management. The PDO remains highly relevant to the priorities of Mexico as outlined in the 2019-2024 National Development Plan and in the World Bank Country Partnership Framework FY20-FY25, discussed by the Board of Executive Directors on February 27, 2020, specifically Objective 6, which aims to provide more inclusive and sustainable infrastructure services. 4. Progress towards achieving the PDO and implementation progress (IP) ratings were Moderately Unsatisfactory and Unsatisfactory in the last two Implementation Status and Results Reports (ISR). These ratings come from a lack of substantial progress and disbursements throughout 2022, 2023 and 2024, as well as inadequate planning and implementation conditions needed to execute priority activities within the current implementation period. Several challenges contributed to this situation, including complex administrative processes for federal budget allocation and project contracting – in particular, difficulties in securing sufficient budget labeled as External Credit, and the expiration of the current Valley of Mexico’s Water Basin Agency (OCAVM) investment portfolio, which limited and continues to limit the execution of multiannual activities (which originally made up most of the lending portfolio). Disbursements now stand at 8.4 percent of the total loan amount after a retroactive financing package (of an amount of US$9.8 million) was processed for disbursement on April 13, 2023. It is also to be noted that the project has yet to successfully complete a bidding process since project signing. Nevertheless, on July 2, 2024, OCAVM launched 8 processes of an estimated total of US$19.9 million that are set to be fully executed until the end of the calendar year. 1Urban water demands for the Valley of Mexico Metropolitan Area are currently being met through three principal sources: groundwater from the main aquifer below the Valley of Mexico, which accounts for approximately 68 percent of the total supply; and two water transfer schemes, the Cutzamala System (24 percent) and the Lerma System (8 percent). Water demands for the Toluca Metropolitan Area are being met through groundwater (84.6 percent) and the Cutzamala System (15.4 percent). Given the lack of progress and disbursements since project signing, the project mid-term review (MTR) which took place in November 2023 determined the need for a second project restructuring, which would involve key changes focused on the implementation period, the loan amount and the project scope. Following this, in February 2024, the Government of Mexico formally requested the Bank to: (i) partially cancel an amount of US$90 million from the total loan proceeds; and (ii) reduce the project implementation period by one year, i.e. to December 31, 2024. 6. Requirements for restructuring of the Bank Policy and Directive for Investment Project Financing have been met, namely: (i) there are no overdue audits; and (ii) the Project is not subject to suspension of disbursements. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 7. The proposed changes to the Project are as follows: in addition to cancelling an amount of US$90 million from the total loan proceeds, the Project will: (i) revise project components and costs, reflecting the requested cancellation of resources initially intended to strengthen the reliability of the Cutzamala System (Component 1), enhance groundwater management in the Valley of Mexico (Component 2), as well as strengthen institutional capacity and support project management (Component 3); (ii) revise project legal covenants, in particular those pertaining to the appointment of a Panel of Experts to oversee works related to the Cutzamala System Dams; (iii) update the Results Framework to accurately reflect the above-mentioned changes; and (iv) adjust the project closing date to December 31, 2024. A. Components and Costs 8. The following changes were introduced to the project components/sub-components: a) Subcomponent 1.1 Designing, Modernizing and Implementing Infrastructure Information Systems for Resilience of the Cutzamala System. This sub-component will be eliminated. b) Sub-component 1.2. Improving energy efficiency and reliability of the system’s infrastructure. The scope of this sub-component will be significantly reduced to include activities that have already been executed (and disbursed through retroactive financing) or launched during calendar year 2024. This sub-component will focus on carrying out minor civil works and maintenance activities in pumping stations, dams and the Cutzamala System’s water treatment plant. c) Component 2. Groundwater Management in the Valley of Mexico. Although no IBRD resources will be allocated to Component 2, it will be maintained to account for activities developed and results achieved under this component with fiscal resources (amounting to US$0.2 million, and supported by the World Bank technical team). Activities financed under this component primarily involve aquifer water quality monitoring in the Valley of Mexico and updating water balances. d) Component 3. Institutional Strengthening and Project Management. Although no IBRD resources will be allocated to Component 3, it will be maintained to account for activities developed and results achieved under this component with fiscal resources (amounting to US$0.1 million, and supported by the World Bank technical team). Activities financed under this component are related to gender and inclusion, as well as project auditing. E. Economic and Financial Analysis 15. The economic and financial analysis of the Project was not updated as part of this restructuring, and an analysis based on the activities effectively launched and completed until the revised closing date will be performed as part of the Implementation Completion and Results Report (ICR). Although the scope is significantly reduced, the Project will continue to generate environmental benefits such as reduced air pollution and reduced greenhouse gas emissions due to the energy efficiency focus of Subcomponent 1.2. In addition, the cost-effectiveness analysis conducted during preparation as part of a stress test of the Cutzamala System – using the Decision Tree Framework2 remains valid, as the activities to be financed under this subcomponent continue to be non-regret investments that would still need to be carried out in the case of selecting other solutions to improve water system yield. F. Technical Analysis 16. The Resiliency and Water Security Plan for the Sub-Basins of the Cutzamala System is based on a sound technical diagnostic carried out with the World Bank’s assistance during project preparation, including stakeholder involvement and technical inputs from various research institutions in Mexico. The diagnostic followed a multidisciplinary approach including an evaluation of the hydrology, water uses and water balances of the basin, as well as the challenges of the existing infrastructure, including eight dams and pumping/conveyance systems. This Plan included several infrastructure improvement measures that were thoroughly modeled and prioritized using the Decision Tree framework, allowing for the prioritization of investments and critical maintenance needs to be identified. In line with this methodology, and in light of the recent technical discussions between the World Bank, CONAGUA and OCAVM, the model was updated taking into account the last two years of the Cutzamala System operation (including planned and delivered water volumes) as well as the actions implemented since project signing. This allowed to establish the new baseline for the reliability PDO-level indicator, and to propose a new target taking into account the activities to be financed in 2024 under the Project, as well as activities implemented with counterpart funds which can impact the System’s reliability. The simulation of the impacts of these activities on the Cutzamala System’s reliability showed that maintenance of the system components included in the Project can help safeguard a certain level of reliability under climate change, i.e. that results contemplate avoided negative impacts rather than general improvements in reliability. For this reason, the same value of 95 percent was proposed for the baseline as well as for the target, that is, the actions to be financed are helping us maintain a 95 percent level of reliability, even under climate change.
