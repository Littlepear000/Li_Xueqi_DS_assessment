BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING A. Project objectives, design and scope 1. The Economic Transformation for Inclusive Growth Project (PIC3) is the fourth of a sequence of integrated growth pole support projects that the World Bank has been financing since 2005. PIC3 is the first phase of a Series of Projects (SoP) that was approved in June 2021 and became effective in November 2021. The project development objective (PDO) is to increase the growth of small and medium enterprises in target regions in Madagascar. It has a regional focus on nine regions in the north, east and south of Madagascar as well as a sectoral focus on tourism, agribusiness and digital. The original approved financing amount was US$150 million. Slow performance during 2023 led to a cancelation of US$20 million, and the current financing amount is US$129.8 million. 2. PIC3 is structured around mutually reinforcing components that support economic recovery after the COVID19 crisis: (1) Attracting and Retaining Private Investment and Removing Key Constraints to Support Economic Recovery from For Official Use Only the COVID-19 Crisis; (2) Removing Constraints to Private Investment and Enhancing Local Economic Competitiveness to Support Recovery in Target Regions and Target Sectors; (3) Supporting Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) and Entrepreneurship Recovery and Growth in Target Sectors and Target Regions; (4) Project implementation; and (5) A zero dollar contingent emergency response component (CERC). B. Project performance 3. The project is progressing well with a rating of Moderately Satisfactory. The Mid Term Review (MTR), conducted in November 2024, found that the project has achieved a number of important results – outlined in the paragraphs below. The project’s work continues to be well received by beneficiaries, including citizens in the project's intervention regions, the private sector and public sector authorities, at both local and national levels. 4. PIC3 is preceded by a series of Madagascar Integrated Growth Poles Projects (PIC 1, PIC2.1 and PIC2.2) that have contributed to the development of private sector in Madagascar in targeted regions in the tourism, agribusiness and digital sectors. More than US$40 million of private investments were enabled, a US$1 billion Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) mobilized, and more than 47,000 formal jobs were facilitated from previous PIC activities. In terms of direct support to firms, more than 700 startups and SMEs benefited from technical assistance and financial resources to support their growth and expansion. More than 35,000 agricultural producers working in selected cash crops or aquaculture have been supported by the projects to improve their productivity and market access. The projects also contributed to an increase in the value of exports from the main agribusiness value chains supported (including cocoa, pink pepper, sea cucumber, seaweed, pink pepper, vanilla, and honey). The smallholders supported by the projects in these value chains had a 45 percent increase in their revenues. 5. PIC 3 cross-cutting results: PIC3 has supported the improvement of the investment climate, particularly in the three key sectors. Across all components, PIC3 has so far supported the capacity-building of 38 governmental and partner institutions. In addition, the governance component has worked to strengthen the capacities of 162 communes in the PIC3 regions. PIC3 support enabled the adoption of 11 investment climate reforms, including a new investment law and three decrees to ensure its implementation, four regional decrees to guide the operation of major agri-food sectors such as vanilla, cloves and pink berries, and one interministerial decree on tourist investment zones in national parks. PIC3 supported the creation of 733 direct jobs, while facilitating the creation of over 10,000 indirect jobs in agribusiness and Official Use Only Page 1 The World Bank Economic Transformation for Inclusive Growth Project (P174684) tourism. PIC3's integrated approach has contributed to the creation of over 20,0001 new businesses across all sectors in PIC regions and facilitated new investments totaling US$56 million. To support financial inclusion, PIC3 supports the operationalization of the national payment SWITCH, as well as the digitization and expansion of microfinance institution branches. In terms of infrastructure, PIC3 is committed to rehabilitating 182 km of roads, 46 km of which have already been completed. 6. PIC3 results in the tourism sector: To date, PIC3 has supported maintenance and minor improvements, the preparation of development plans, improved management arrangements, or improved access at ten tourism sites. At some of the sites supported, the project's interventions have contributed to almost tripling the revenue generated by these sites. The project has also fostered a steady increase in the number of hotel rooms available in the target regions, with the addition of around 3,500 rooms since 2022. Thanks to the Miatrika Training Program, over 16,000 tourism workers and guides have benefited from training sessions, and a database of over 300 tourism trainers has been created. By supporting the control and formalization function of the Ministry of Tourism and Handicrafts (MTA) at national and regional level, the project has contributed to a 35 percent increase in the number of formal tourism entities registered in For Official Use Only the target regions. Through support for tourism promotion at national level, over 130 private operators in the tourism sector were able to take part in international tourism industry events, and more than 250 outbound operators took part in in-country educational tours. In 2024, Madagascar benefited from the addition of a new long-haul air route to the country, operated by Emirates. The Tourism Confederation of Madagascar (Confédération du Tourisme de Madagascar CTM), supported by the project, contributed to the dialogue and lobbying for the establishment of this new route with other stakeholders. Overall, Madagascar has seen a rebound in international tourist arrivals after COVID-19, with tourist arrivals in 2024 reaching the levels of 2019. The support provided by PIC3 to Madagascar Airlines has ensured the company's operation throughout 2024 and its contributions to the tourism sector in particular. 7. PIC3 results in the agribusiness sector: Project activities in the cocoa sector have consolidated the gains made in terms of structuring the sector through the National Cocoa Council (Conseil National Cacao CNC); the International Cocoa Organization (ICCO) has endorsed for the third consecutive time the 100 percent fine cocoa label for Malagasy cocoa in April 2024. The Project promotes the establishment of solid partnerships between public and private players; public- private dialogue platforms are beginning to be set up in the clove, pink pepper and essential oil sectors. These collaborations have, for example, helped to positively influence European regulations on nicotine residues in spices. Thanks to a stronger presence at international agri-food trade fairs, Malagasy SMEs have signed contracts worth US$25 million and attracted new investment, as witnessed by the opening of the first industrial chocolate factory in Ambanja. The value of targeted exports from intervention zones rose by over 50 percent. The incomes of direct beneficiary producers have risen by 45 percent for products targeted by the project. More than 15,000 producers benefited from capacity building and almost 2,000 of them were able to establish new direct commercial relationships with exporters, notably through the Madagascar Business Investment Facilitation (MBIF) Agribusiness Program. 8. PIC3 results in the digital sector: MIARY Digital, the program that supports digital entrepreneurs, has achieved good results so far. During the first cohort, three specialized private incubators supported 47 startups in the Analamanga region. 43 of them received a PIC3 grant of US$15,000. After a year of incubation, the 47 start-ups have created 283 jobs, 40 percent of which are for women. The second cohort of the Miary Digital Program has been launched in the project regions. The incubation of 45 digital entrepreneurs in the other regions of PIC will be carried out online by the three incubators, 15 of whom will benefit from a subsidy. Challenges. During 2023-2024, the project suffered from a delay of at least one year in most of its activities, particularly those related to Component 3 - that provides direct support to entrepreneurs and companies. Delays were mainly due to country-wide budget regularization problems that delayed payments and activities. Additionally, there were performance issues and processing delays at the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) level. The selection process for the new national coordinator took a long time, and the uncertainty associated with the change of management had a negative impact on the motivation of PIU staff. Project management issues were related to practices of (i) staff recruitment and management, (ii) procurement and contract management for staff and activities, (iii) financial management, (iv) Environment and Social (E&S) risk management, including the operationalization of the Grievance Mechanism (GM), and (v) project monitoring and evaluation. The issue related to the budget regularization was addressed at the country For Official Use Only portfolio level and resulted in simplification of disbursement procedures agreed with the Government. Enhanced hands- on support as well as a series of proactive actions, including weekly supervision of activities, have led to an increased implementation pace of project activities, mainly for components 1 and 2, and the disbursement rate improved from five percent in December 2023 to 66 percent currently. The March 2025 supervision mission concluded that significant efforts have been made to improve project management especially in the areas of human resources management and procurement and E&S risk management. A protocol for results measurements has also been finalized and a report on the functioning of the GM has been delivered providing information that the project has received a total of 221 complaints, 93 percent (205) of which are currently treated and closed. However, there is a need for close sensitization on GM related to the treatment of sensitive issues like Sexual Exploitation and Abuse / Sexual harassment (SEA/SH) cases. 10. The project has faced M&E challenges, reflected by declining ratings in this area since 2023. Results reporting have been inconsistent due particularly to a lack of M&E capacity in the PIU as well as the absence of a clear protocol of indicators. The PIU M&E specialist function has only been filled since January 2025. Since the MTR and as part of the restructuring, the World Bank team has worked with the PIU to clarify the methodology for each indicator and finalize the protocol. A final version of the protocol of indicators was agreed upon in April 2025. Results for 2024 as well as the adjusted targets for all project indicators are based on the protocol. The World Bank team will continue to closely supervise the M&E function, and an M&E consultant will be added to the World Bank supervision team as of July 2025 to ensure consistent implementation of the protocol. The DIANA Region, located in the northern part of the country, features vegetation formations that reflect the local environmental conditions, comprising four main types of forest: dense humid evergreen forest, dense humid mountain forest, dense dry forest, and mangroves. Although the region includes several official protected areas, these are located far from potential project intervention zones, which will be precisely identified through subsequent studies, with all necessary precautions taken to avoid impacting them. The DIANA Region is characterized by significant ethnic and cultural diversity, with a heterogeneous society where various religions and belief systems coexist peacefully. Approximately 60% of the population identifies as Christian, 30% as Muslim, while the remaining portion adheres to traditional beliefs rooted in ancestor worship. DIANA stands out as the region with the highest number of sacred cultural and spiritual For Official Use Only sites in Madagascar. These sites will be carefully identified during project-related studies, and all necessary precautions will be taken to ensure they are preserved and not impacted by project activities. Within the DIANA Region lies Nosy Be, an island recognized as a major growth hub with strong tourism potential. Its appeal has attracted a diverse influx of migrants, resulting in a vibrant cultural mosaic with a wide range of customs and traditions. The island hosts several nature reserves and marine protected areas, highlighting its rich biodiversity and ecological significance. The Anosy Region, located in southeastern Madagascar, is rich in natural resources and ecological diversity. Its landscapes range from dry shrubby forests to expansive, lush primary forests teeming with a wide variety of flora and fauna. With 194 kilometers of coastline, the region also features pristine beaches and vibrant marine ecosystems. Anosy is home to extensive forested areas and numerous conservation sites, encompassing both public and private protected zones recognized for their remarkable biodiversity. The region also boasts a range of natural attractions, including waterfalls, lakes, and geothermal springs, notably within the Andohahela National Park. The population is predominantly composed of the Antanosy ethnic group, whose cultural traditions are deeply tied to the land. Forests are considered sacred, as they serve as traditional burial grounds, further enhancing their cultural significance. The regional economy is primarily driven by mining, fisheries, agriculture, and tourism—sectors that are intrinsically linked to Anosy’s rich natural and cultural heritage. The Atsimo-Andrefana Region is the largest in Madagascar by surface area and is located in the arid southern part of the island. Despite its dry climate, the region holds a range of vital ecosystems and natural habitats, including dense dry forests, xerophytic thickets, mangroves, and marshlands. However, these ecosystems face significant anthropogenic pressures, notably land clearing, illegal logging of precious woods, charcoal production, and forest fire particularly in the ecological corridor. The region is mainly inhabited by the Antandroy, Bara, and Mahafaly ethnic groups. On the East Coast, Sainte-Marie is a renowned island destination and one of Madagascar's most outstanding tourism hubs. The island features rich coral reefs and a diverse underwater ecosystem, making it a premier diving site in the Indian Ocean. In addition to its ecological wealth, Sainte-Marie also offers a dozen cultural sites that serve as key tourist attractions. The Analamanga Region, situated in the central highlands and home to the capital city, Antananarivo, has been integrated into the PIC project for its potential in digital innovation and entrepreneurship. It is the core of Madagascar's ICT sector, hosting most the country’s tech companies, and waste management remains one of the principal issue to be considered in the capital. Finally, the East Coast is notable for the "Canal des Pangalanes," which connects various lagoons along the eastern coast, stretching from Toamasina to Vohipeno and crossing four districts. The ecosystems of the Eastern Corridor are rich in environmental and biodiversity resources, with many endemic species. This biological diversity makes the region a popular area for tourism, featuring national parks, natural reserves, and cultural sites. Additionally, the zone is known for its strong diversification of cash and industrial crops for export.
