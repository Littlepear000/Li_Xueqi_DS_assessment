Introduction and Context 1. The Human Capital Protection Project (HCPP) in Burkina Faso, approved on September 19, 2024, with an initial International Development Association (IDA) credit of US$100.4 million, was designed as a 15-month operation to support the government’s efforts to keep essential health and education services running amidst an unstable security situation and a difficult humanitarian landscape. The operation bridges the gap between closing IDA funded education and health projects and new ones, ensuring uninterrupted service delivery during transitional and vulnerable periods while achieving intended results, with 97 percent of funds disbursed and all Project Development Objective (PDO) indicators achieved or surpassed. Key outcomes supported include 16.2 million free health consultations, 1.13 million immunized children, 98 percent of students completing exams, and nearly all teachers certified with pedagogical competences. The fast financial execution and impactful performance of the operation provide a strong rationale to continue the support with a proposed Additional Financing (AF) to reinforce the protection of human capital in Burkina Faso and continue to bridge the gap until new IDA funded projects in education and health sectors become fully operational. Country Context Page 2 The World Bank Human Capital Protection Project (P506528) – Additional Financing Request 1 2. Burkina Faso is currently navigating a volatile security environment and a challenging humanitarian context. Ongoing insecurity has displaced over two million citizens1 and disrupted access to basic services such as healthcare and education. The HCPP mitigates adverse impacts by funding free healthcare, hospitals, health centers, and vaccinations. In education, the HCPP supports teacher training, exams, and materials. For instance, without HCPP activities in regions affected by the crisis, service delivery to displaced citizens would not have been covered solely by government resources. The security situation has seen a slow but gradual improvement, translating into economic growth projected at 4.7 percent in 2025. In the first half (H1) of 2025, fatalities as recorded by the Armed Conflict Location and Event Data (ACLED) fell 23 percent from H1 2024 and 26.6 percent from H1 2023, which saw a peak in violence (4,819 deaths). While the government has increased budget allocations for security and defense in previous years, recent improvements in the security situation have allowed for a recalibration of expenditures, with a renewed focus on health and education spending. Sectoral and Institutional Context 3. In the 2000s and 2010s, Burkina Faso registered notable gains in basic health services and education outcomes. However, since 2019, rising insecurity, mounting fiscal pressures, and the COVID-19 pandemic have undermined portions of this progress. Today, both sectors struggle with access and quality due to geographic disparities, imbalances in human resources, financial constraints, supply-chain challenges, and the surging needs of a young, rapidly growing population. Although the government has adopted sectoral strategic plans through the National Health Development Plan (PNDS 2021– 2030) and the Education and Training Sector Plan (PSEF 2017–2030), implementation remains constrained. Still, in 2025, the government increased the Ministry of Health’s budget by 11.4 percent and the education ministries’ budget by 8.7 percent, signaling its continued commitment to human capital development. Yet, persistent security instability, limited fiscal space, and systemic inefficiencies in public financial management impede the effective execution of these strategies and the scale- up of essential services. 4. The project remains fully aligned with the West and Central Africa (AFW) regional priority to strengthen core institutions for service delivery 2021-2025 (Report 159265), as well as selected priorities of the IDA Prevention and Resilience Allocation. It also aligns with the Country Partnership Framework FY18-24, which was adjusted in the Performance and Learning Review (Report 166080-BF). Furthermore, the Project aligns with the new Country Partnership Framework, which is currently under preparation. C. D. Project Description 5. The HCPP project in Burkina Faso is a 15-month initiative financed by a US$100.4 million credit from the International Development Association (IDA), with the primary goal of supporting the government in maintaining the provision of essential health and education services. The project has two main components: (1) US$100 million to deliver essential health services, incl. free healthcare, hospital and health center operations, and routine vaccinations, as well as key education services such as teacher training, exam organization, and provision of teaching materials; and (2) US$400,000 for project management and fiduciary oversight, covering audits and environmental and social safeguards. 6. Approved in September 2024 and effective since November 2024, the project has performed well with 97 percent disbursed by June 2025, with all PDO indicators already achieved or surpassed and nine out of twelve intermediate results indicators achieved. As of end-December 2024, in the education sector, 98 percent of students benefited from annual exams, 99.8 percent of teachers were certified through subject-specific exams, confirming their teaching competence, and 69,000 teachers were provided with pedagogical kits. In the health sector, the project has facilitated 16.2 million free health care consultations for women and children, 92.6 percent of children received the Pentavalent vaccine, and 1.13 million children were immunized. These efforts underscore the country’s commitment to protecting human capital. 7. The original financing is scheduled to close in December 2025. The proposed AF of US$100.4 million will close a year later and end the cycle of recurrent-cost financing in support of the budgeted rise in health and education spending. This will allow to smoothen the transition to other financing instruments and sources, including government resources and two new IDA-funded projects in the health and education sectors. These projects, the Burkina Faso Strengthening Health System Performance and Resilience Project (P180539) and the Restoring Education System Performance and Improving Resilience Project (P502472), were approved in early 2025 and are expected to become effective by November 2025. For the health project, the remaining effectiveness condition is currently being implemented; and for the education project, the Global Partnership for Education co-financing envelope has now been confirmed, and the project is currently being restructured to fit the envelope. The project is expected to become effective shortly after the completion of the restructuring. The adoption of the work plan for the two projects is scheduled for December 2025 and thereafter it typically takes several months for such project to start disbursing. 8. This AF will maintain and, in some areas, scale up essential health and education services by sustaining the free healthcare program for children under five and pregnant women, ensuring routine vaccine deployment, and maintaining critical education activities, such as teacher training and provision of learning materials. The AF will further strengthen project management and fiduciary oversight. The results framework will be updated to reflect new government priorities in health and education. For instance, the AF will consolidate the achievements of the parent project and will include the expansion of free healthcare activity to cover the elimination of malaria and the provision of dialysis products and micronutrients in the health sector, and the expansion of equipping teachers with pedagogical kits, along with the training and support provided for services during the vacation period. Current Projects on International Waterways OP 7.50 No Projects in Disputed Area OP 7.60 No Summary of Screening of Environmental and Social Risks and Impacts The implementation of the AF project activities could involve environmental and community health risks from inadequate storage, transportation, and disposal of infected medical waste and occupational and community health and safety including human security such as incidents and accidents during organization of exams and concours, trainings, and conferences. In addition, the risks of insecurity arising from (a) the grouping of pupils and teachers in the framework of the organization of exams and concours and (b) the training of education personnel and organization of conferences for teachers are also to be considered during the environmental and social assessments. Finally, social risks related to the AF project include the exclusion of certain categories of people due to the inadequacies of the information and selection systems; possible labor issues; vulnerabilities among the project beneficiaries, SEA risks; and collateral risks to education, social protection, and health workers delivering services in insecure areas. E&S's potential risks and impacts will be managed during the AF project’s implementation following the Environmental and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP) that will be part of the AF project financial agreement. The ESCP, SEP, SMP, LMP and ICMWMP of the parent project will be updated by appraisal to reflect the AF project design and the requirements of new templates. E. Implementation Institutional and Implementation Arrangements 9. The institutional and implementation arrangements for the AF will largely remain unchanged, building on the effective structures of the parent project. The current Project Implementing Unit will be preserved for the AF. Strategic oversight will be provided by an established steering committee and local consultation committees, ensuring alignment with government priorities and beneficiary needs. Financial oversight will remain robust, with ex-ante audits led by the General Inspectorate of Finance (Inspectorate Générale des Finances, IGF) and continued collaboration with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and sector ministries. Implementation will be guided by an updated Project Implementation Manual and annual work plans, with fiduciary and procurement arrangements following World Bank regulations. Environmental and social safeguards will be maintained, with updated documents and grievance mechanisms in place to address new activities under the AF.
