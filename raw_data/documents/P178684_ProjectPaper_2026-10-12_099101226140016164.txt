PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Introduction 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks management approval for a Level Two restructuring of the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) Girls Learning and Empowerment Project (GLEP) (P178684), financed through IDA Credit No. 7284-ZR. Following a request from the Recipient to cancel approximately US$50 million in remaining funds for reallocation towards other national priorities, the proposed restructuring entails: (i) changes in the scope of component activities; (ii) partial cancellation and reallocation of funds between components and disbursement categories; (iii) revisions to the overall disbursement forecast; and (iv) changes in the results framework. B. Background and Status 2. The DRC GLEP was approved on March 28, 2023, financed by an International Development Association (IDA) credit of US$400 million, with a closing date of December 29, 2028. Cumulative disbursements stand at US$73.29 million, representing 35.62 percent of total financing. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to improve safe and equitable access to education, particularly for girls, and teaching and learning conditions in secondary schools in selected provinces. The project comprises four components: (i) Improved Access to Quality and Girl-Friendly Schools (US$210 million); (ii) Improved Quality of Teaching and Learning for All (US$175 million); (iii) Project Management, Monitoring and Evaluation (US$15 million); and (iv) Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC) (US$0 million). Summary of Project performance by components 4. As noted above, the project has maintained a Moderately Satisfactory implementation progress rating since December 2024, despite facing significant challenges, many of which were outside of the project's control. During the early phase of implementation, progress was constrained by: delayed recruitment of the project coordinator and the consequent delay in establishing the Project Coordination Team (PCT); a subsequent change in project coordinator; procurement difficulties in contracting for textbooks and digital devices; the deteriorating security situation in eastern DRC; and challenges in recruiting NGOs and independent verification agencies (IVAs) for the girls' scholarship program. In addition, DRC has been affected by the increase in fuel prices due to the conflict in the strait of Hormuz, US dollar depreciation and the most recent shock, which hit the country in May 2026, Ebola epidemic, particularly affecting Ituri province. The initial project design assumed the simultaneous rollout of multiple contracting packages and verification arrangements, which proved overly complex given operating conditions and implementation capacity constraints. Despite these challenges, the PCT persevered, and while some activities experienced delays, implementation is now back on track. The World Bank DRC Girls Learning and Empowerment Project (P178684) Page 2 Official Use Only Component 1: Improved Access to Quality and Girl-Friendly Schools (US$210 million) 5. The objectives of this component are to improve access to schooling by creating quality learning environments, alleviating financial barriers to girls' enrollment, and expanding the proportion of female teachers. Under the original Project Appraisal Document (PAD), the component was structured around two subcomponents: (i) improving learning environments; and (ii) increasing female participation in education. 6. Subcomponent 1.1: Improving Learning Environments (US$140 million). This subcomponent financed the construction, furnishing, and equipping of classrooms — including science and technology laboratory rooms and information technology facilities for use across all subject areas — as well as water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) facilities. The construction is expected to start in the fall of 2026. To this date, the project conducted screening missions, identified and validated sites schools in 5 project provinces and WASH facilities (excluding South Kivu due to access constraints). Contracting for school construction in Ituri and for WASH facilities across 4 provinces, namely Kasai, Kasai Central, Kasai Oriental, and Ituri, through UNICEF is expected to be completed by end June, 2026. Implementation of this subcomponent faced the following challenges: (i) the conflict situation in eastern DRC, which prompted a strategic pause in North Kivu, South Kivu, and parts of Ituri until conditions improve; (ii) difficulties accessing certain rural areas; (iii) a significant increase in WASH construction costs; (iv) rising cost estimates for raw materials linked to disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz; (v) exchange rate fluctuations between the US dollar and the Congolese franc; and (vi) Ebola epidemic, which originated in Ituri province. Additionally, screening missions revealed that class sizes and school enrollments are considerably larger than projected during project preparation, due to population growth. 7. Subcomponent 1.2: Increasing Female Participation in Education (US$70 million). This subcomponent financed: (i) scholarships for girls attending public secondary schools in Kasai province, which has the lowest secondary school enrollment rate among the ten selected provinces; and (ii) performance-based conditions (PBCs) designed to increase the number of female teachers in government-paid teaching positions in secondary schools across the ten target provinces by 5,000. 8. The girls' scholarship program was structured across two levels: schools received financing to cover enrollment and examination fees for enrolled girls, while girls' households received a small transfer to incentivize families to send girls to school. During the pilot phase, 49,215 girls benefited from the scholarship program. The generalization phase is currently underway and is expected to be completed by July 2026. Preliminary results are encouraging: girls' enrollment across Kasai has increased significantly, with approximately 300,000 girls registered for the 2025–2026 school year. 9. The female teacher recruitment activity experienced initial delays; however, key interventions are now gaining momentum. A merit-based recruitment policy and manual have been drafted and are pending issuance of a General Secretary's circular to formalize this reform. The platform for merit-based teacher recruitment was launched in June 2026, the merit-based recruitment of the teachers using the new platform has started and the first set of teachers (approximately 2,500) is expected to be recruited during the first semester of the school year 2026-2027. Faced with fiscal constraints, the Government had initially considered limiting recruitment to replacing retiring male teachers and reducing the target to 2,500 female teachers. However, following extensive discussions and recognition of the transformative importance of this activity for the DRC's secondary education system, the original target of recruiting 5,000 female teachers has been maintained. Component 2: Improved Quality of Teaching-Learning for All (US$175 million) 10. The objective of this component is to improve the quality of education, particularly for girls, through improved availability and effectiveness of teaching and learning resources and practices, citizens engagement, and the creation of safe and inclusive school environments. The component is structured around three subcomponents: (i) strengthening the curriculum, textbooks, and teaching and learning materials; (ii) strengthening the quality of teaching; and (iii) promoting citizen engagement and safe and inclusive education environments. Subcomponent 2.1: Strengthening the Curriculum, Textbooks and Teaching and Learning Materials (US$90 million). The objective of this subcomponent is to increase the availability and use of quality, affordable, and gender-sensitive teaching and learning materials, including in digital format. This subcomponent financed: (i) the development, printing, and distribution of textbooks — in both paper and digital format, including a teacher's guide with structured lesson plans — for the common core in lower secondary schools and for the main streams in upper secondary schools (Scientific, Pedagogical, Literary, and Technical); and (ii) the procurement of IT equipment for multi-purpose Smart Labs in approximately 388 schools and Smart Classrooms in approximately 5,700 schools. 12. The project has contracted the development, printing, and distribution of paper textbooks (including a teacher's guide with structured lesson plans) for the common core in lower secondary schools and for the main streams in upper secondary schools (Scientific, Pedagogical, Literary, and Technical). Textbooks are expected to be delivered in early 2027. While the development of digital textbooks was originally planned under the same contract, the two have been separated due to the complexity of digital textbook development, and the digital textbook procurement is expected to be launched in the third quarter of 2026. The procurement of IT equipment has experienced delays for the following reasons: • The project team consolidated all equipment purchases into a single tender to achieve greater economies of scale and more competitive pricing. While the bid package was initially planned for launch in May 2025, an early market engagement process conducted in August 2025 provided valuable industry feedback that helped strengthen and refine the bid requirements. • Following these enhancements, the call for bids was launched in December 2025. The initial process did not attract sufficient competition; the bid will therefore be relaunched to encourage broader participation. The procurement process was relaunched in September 2026 and will be completed by November 2026. 13. Sub-component 2.2: Strengthening the Quality of Teaching (US$60 million). The objective of this subcomponent is to improve the quality of teaching and learning practices by strengthening systems for pre-service teacher training and continuous teacher professional development (CTPD). Pre-service teacher training activities (US$26 million) included: (i) an assessment of the pre-service training sector (covering policies, institutions, enrollments, and outcomes) and support for the development of the Government's reform strategy; (ii) civil works, equipment, and materials to strengthen teacher training colleges (ISPs) in five core provinces, including an online teacher training platform and Smart Labs in selected ISPs designated as Centers of Excellence (CoEs); (iii) staff training, curriculum updates aligned with tertiary and secondary reforms, and capacity building for delivery; and (iv) practicum costs for female students in targeted fields. Continuous teacher professional development activities (US$34 million) included: (i) establishment and capacity building of School Cluster Networks (REPs), including training of inspectors, school leaders, and CUP heads in classroom observation, mentoring, curriculum implementation, and use of pedagogical resources; (ii) support for ISP instructors' participation in training, provision of digital devices for inspectors and schools, and training on their use for classroom observation and feedback; and (iii) development of pedagogical resources and, in core provinces, training of selected teachers in digital skills and the use of IT and digital learning tools, with a view to cascading training to other teachers. 14. Pre-service teacher training activities are implemented in partnership with the Ministry of Higher Education. Progress under this subcomponent is underway, with varying degrees of advancement across activities. The in-depth evaluation of pre-service teacher training has been completed and is scheduled for validation in June 2026. Revision of ISP curricula has experienced delays due to alignment with the PTN project; recruitment of technical assistance is expected to be launched in June 2026, with completion anticipated by January 2027. In parallel, sites for the ISPs have been identified and screened, and civil works are currently expected to commence in mid-2027. Given the project's scheduled closure in 2028, the PCT, in collaboration with the Ministry of Higher Education, is exploring options to accelerate construction and ensure timely implementation. Remaining activities — including deployment of the digital platform, establishment of Smart Labs and a pedagogical studio, and support for female students' practicum costs — are at early stages of implementation. Sub-component 2.3: Promoting Citizen Engagement and Safe and Inclusive Education Environments (US$25 million). The objectives of this subcomponent are to strengthen beneficiaries' voice and participation throughout project implementation and to promote safe and inclusive education environments for girls through the establishment of an accountability and response framework and measures to prevent violence against children in schools. Activities included: (i) SEA/SH risk mitigation measures, including enforcing Codes of Conduct, developing accountability and survivor-centered response systems, strengthening GBV services and referral mechanisms, training focal points and service providers, conducting awareness campaigns, and supporting complaint handling systems; and (ii) school-based violence prevention through a whole-school model, capacity building, awareness activities, and girls' clubs with life skills programs in targeted schools. 17. Solid progress has been recorded across several interventions by mid-term. On capacity building, 36 of 37 Project Coordination Unit members (97 percent) were trained in GBV/SEA/SH prevention, 4 of 5 junior GBV specialists recruited (80 percent), and 8 of 10 planned technical assistance organizations are operational (80 percent). The Code of Conduct was fully adapted and signed by 35 of 37 staff (95 percent). Sensitization campaigns reached 233,498 teachers across 8 project provinces, exceeding the target of 227,000 (103 percent), while 4,859 of 7,493 targeted public officials were sensitized (65 percent). However, activities related to girls' clubs and the whole-school approach have been delayed due to procurement challenges and the need to relaunch several unsuccessful tenders. The conflict in some eastern provinces of DRC has also presented additional challenges for implementation in South Kivu and Ituri. Financial management (FM) performance was downgraded from Moderately Satisfactory to Moderately Unsatisfactory during the Mid-Term Review, due to persistent deficiencies and delays in implementing FM recommendations. The main shortcomings relate to: low budget credibility, with annual execution rates of the Annual Work Plan and Budget standing at 8 percent in 2024 and 18 percent in 2025; a low overall disbursement rate (3.3 percent as of April 2026); challenges in the payment and verification of girls' scholarships; the absence of an internal auditor since project effectiveness; and delays in revising the FM procedures manual. Procurement performance at the mid-term stage remains Moderately Satisfactory, with a substantial level of risk, notwithstanding ongoing implementation efforts and an overall compliant framework. Approximately 56.5 percent of Procurement Plan activities have been initiated, while nearly 30 percent of contracts have not yet started. Significant delays persist, particularly in works contracts, where implementation remains limited, 11 contracts have been launched, covering 52 of 176 planned schools, despite flexibility granted to the project. These delays are compounded by procurement timelines that consistently exceed initial estimates. Contract management is also characterized by frequent amendments, primarily for time extensions. The project team faced significant structural constraints in implementing the Procurement Plan. With many contracts now under implementation, the team has developed an acceleration timeline and progress will be closely monitored. Finally, while complaints have been handled in accordance with procedures, their volume and nature point to shortcomings in bidders' understanding of the requirements set out in the bidding documents. 22. The Mid-Term Review underscored the importance of sustaining fiduciary performance while strengthening procurement planning, internal controls, and budget execution tracking — particularly as disbursements are expected to increase sharply during the second half of the project. E. Rationale for Restructuring 28. The proposed restructuring of GLEP is based on the Government of the DRC's request to cancel US$50 million from the project. This cancellation, combined with rising global raw material prices, fluctuations in the US dollar exchange rate, and the conflict situation in eastern DRC, necessitates a revision of project targets and the restructuring of certain activities. 29. Almost three years into implementation, several core activities are at the early stages full operational delivery, including school construction, procurement of digital equipment, and the launch of the whole-school approach. While some areas have gained momentum over the past year — including the launch of school and WASH construction, finalization of the girls' scholarship pilot phase and launch of the generalization phase under Component 1, signing of the textbook contract, recruitment of girls' club NGOs, and signing of Codes of Conduct under Component 2 — overall progress has fallen short of original projections. As a result, several targets and sequencing assumptions are no longer feasible within the remaining implementation period. There is therefore a strong case for refocusing the project on high-impact activities that can be delivered within the remaining timeframe, while preserving alignment with the PDO and cancelling funding for activities that will no longer be pursued. 30. The restructuring proposes to revise targets, streamline underperforming or non-viable activities, reallocate financing across components and categories, update the results framework, finalize the partial cancellation, and adjust the disbursement forecast to reflect a simplified and consolidated delivery model. These changes are essential to accelerate disbursement, improve execution efficiency, and ensure the effective use of remaining resources. While the PDO remains valid and achievable, the restructuring provides an opportunity to recalibrate project ambition to match implementation capacity, strengthen delivery discipline, and maximize development impact over the remaining implementation period. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 31. To ensure the best use of available resources, the project restructuring will focus on realigning activities with realistic targets, reallocating budgets to high-impact areas, and refining indicators to reflect actual progress. There are five key changes proposed for this restructuring: (i) changes in the scope of component activities and costs; (ii) a partial The World Bank DRC Girls Learning and Empowerment Project (P178684) Page 7 Official Use Only cancellation and reallocation of funds between components and between categories; (iii) changes in the overall disbursement forecast; and (iv) changes in the results framework. Scope of component activities and costs Component 1: Improved Access to Quality and Girl-Friendly Schools [Original Allocation: US$210 million; Revised Allocation: US$180 million] 32. This component involves three types of revisions: (i) a reduction in component financing; (ii) a strategic pause of certain activities in North Kivu and South Kivu; and (iii) a revision of related intermediate indicators. The financing for subcomponent 1.1 is reduced from US$140 million to US$110 million as a result of the partial cancellation of project funds, and associated targets have been adjusted accordingly. Target revisions are further justified by the inaccessibility of certain areas due to conflict — prompting the strategic pause in North Kivu and South Kivu— as well as the depreciation of the US dollar estimated at 16%, and rising fuel prices linked to the conflict in the Strait of Hormuz. Additionally, technical standards for school infrastructure have been strengthened: sanitation blocks now average 11 cubicles instead of the original four, better addressing girls’ needs, inclusion and durability, with the planned 2,160 cubicles equivalent to approximately 196 blocks under the new standard. The indicators affected under subcomponent 1.1 include the number of classrooms and WASH facilities to be constructed or rehabilitated (see details in Section C). The overall allocation for subcomponent 1.2 remains unchanged; however, this restructuring proposes increasing the target for girls' scholarships. During the generalization phase, it became clear that the scholarship intervention has had a larger-than-expected impact on enrollment. The budget envelope remains the same because the increase in costs associated with higher-than- projected enrollment numbers was offset by savings resulting from the delayed start of the scholarship intervention, which required significant time to pilot and establish. Furthermore, additional cost-savings may be used on preventive measures to tackle Ebola pandemic, for example, equipping schools with handwashing stations, upon mutual agreement with the Bank. These activities are within the broad scope and PDO of the project to ensure safe environments for students, particularly girls. Component 2: Improved Quality of Teaching-Learning for All [Original Allocation: US$175 million; Revised Allocation: US$147.29 million] 33. Subcomponent 2.1: Strengthening the Curriculum, Textbooks and Teaching-Learning Materials (Original Allocation: US$90 million, Revised Allocation: US$79.77 million). This restructuring proposes three changes to this subcomponent: (i) a revision of the budget; (ii) a revision of targets; and (iii) a strategic pause of activities in certain provinces. The budget allocation is revised downward to reflect savings in textbook procurement and the strategic pause of IT equipment procurement in South Kivu and Ituri. These changes are also reflected in revised intermediate indicators (see Section C). Several indicators were additionally affected by demographic change: when the tender for paper textbooks was prepared, procurement volumes were based on available enrollment data, but actual demand has proved significantly higher than originally anticipated due to population growth. Furthermore, the Smart Classroom indicator has been revised based on findings from screening missions — a number of schools were found to lack electricity and the basic infrastructure required to store IT equipment. It has therefore been decided to focus equipment distribution on schools with a minimum acceptable level of infrastructure, and to adjust device specifications to ensure greater durability. 34. Sub-component 2.2: Strengthening the Quality of Teaching (Original Allocation: US$60 million; Revised Allocation: US$49.37 million). The proposed changes to this subcomponent relate to: (i) a revision of the budget; (ii) a strategic pause of implementation in North Kivu and South Kivu; and (iii) a revision of targets. Under the restructuring, activities related to the rehabilitation and establishment of SmartLabs at ISP Bukavu in South Kivu will be cancelled. In addition, activities related to continuous teacher training — including training of REP representatives, teachers, and inspectors, as well as the procurement of equipment for inspectors — in North Kivu and South Kivu will also be cancelled. 35.
