RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING The Government of the Republic of Rwanda submitted a formal request for restructuring dated June 6, 2022. This restructuring is for (a) project closing date extension; (b) adding new activities under existing components; (c) modification of outcome indicators and targets; and (d) reallocation of funds between components, for the following reasons: (i) To compensate for disruptions in implementation progress caused by COVID-19-related lock- down. COVID-19 restrictions imposed during the pandemic caused a downward trajectory in the number of professional qualifications achieved by staff as planned under Component 4 – Professionalization of PFM Staff -PFM Capacity Development. A number of challenges were experienced during this period including suspension of in person learning, limited internet connectivity to facilitate teaching and learning (once online-learning was introduced), lack of preparedness for the rapid shift to online learning (by tuition providers) and slow uptake of online learning by students. In addition to this, to better align with the PDO, starting from Y5, the ‘level of qualification’ will be mapped at full certification and intermediate achievements will be recognized under the PFM training Intermediate Result Indicator. An extension of the project closing date will enable catch up on delayed activities and achievement of planned/revised targets. (ii) To accommodate implementation of new activities that respond to emerging issues. The proposed new activities relate mainly to: (a) improving personnel and payroll-related IT systems controls and processes (under component 1); (b) expanding the scope of PFM professionalization courses available to PFM staff beyond accountancy e.g. corporate governance, audit etc. (under component 4); (c) extend opportunities for professionalization and capacity building to officers from government based entities and decentralized units (under component 4); (d) developing the pipeline of PFM professionals via a training/mentorship program under component 4; (e) development of a public sector PFM Certification Course (under component 4); (f) training opportunities on enhancing “Green” PFM (under component 4); (f) establishing a sustainable model to continue PFM professionalization (under component 4); (g) development of a strategic plan for effective oversight of State Owned Enterprises (under component 4); and (h) technical assistance to strengthen corporate governance in investment and portfolio oversight (through the Ministry of Public Investments and Privatization) under component 4. As detailed in Annex 1, all proposed activities fully align with the existing PDO and updated results. The Integrated Personnel and Payroll Information System (IPPIS) is a payroll related IT system that needs to be enhanced to better provide for reliable and accessible data for the better management of human resource and management of the wage bill. The project will also support related capacity building for its efficient roll out. Similarly, the proposed activities on expanding professionalization to more categories, developing a mentorship program and establishing a sustainable model for continuation of PFM professionalization (b)-(f) above, are targeted at addressing the current challenges facing professionalization of students as well as addressing issues round sustainability for future cohorts. Finally, to respond to government’s new priority focus on improving SOE governance and accountability, the project will also support to the development of a strategic plan for effective oversight of SOEs, TA to support improved SOE oversight, and capacity building to support strengthening corporate governance in SOEs, will respond to government’s new priority focus on improving SOE governance and accountability. 6 The World Bank Rwanda Public Finance Management Reform Project (P164807) (iii) The implementation of these new activities will be within the overall project resource allocation and proposed new activities will be financed from reallocation of funds from savings of other activities of the project. As detailed in Table 1 and Annex 1, despite the reallocation of resources between components , all original targeted results will be achieved (4 out of 5 of the current PDO indicators have been achieved already). Savings have been realized mainly through: (a) rationalizing the number of students that can achieve full certification during the remainder of the project period rather than a level of certification as originally targeted; (b) using actual costs of ongoing activities as the basis of budgeting rather than estimates that were previously used at the time of project design and were much higher); (c) removing sub activities that have since been funded by the PFM basket fund and the Government of Rwanda. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES The proposed changes are as follows: 1. Extension of project closing date: The proposed change is for a no cost extension of the project closing date by 22 months, from February 28, 2023, to December 31, 2024. This being the first extension of the project closing date, the cumulative extension period is 22 months. This period of time is necessary to ensure that: (i) at least four more professional examination sittings are available to students to respond to the same number of sittings interrupted by the suspension of online learning (as a result of the COVID 19 pandemic); (ii) the PFM mentorship scheme can be both designed and piloted; (iii) there is sufficient time to comprehensively discuss, agree and design a sustainable professionalization model with all actors in government; and (v) full roll out of SDMS to all secondary schools. 2. Recommendations from the IFMIS (Integrated Financial Management Information System) technical assessment were concluded and implementation of various recommendations are ongoing (e.g., of the conclusion technical review of the third data center and the commencement of procurement processes for the enhanced infrastrucuture). The rollout of the financial module of the school data management system (SDMS) to secondary schools is on track and expected to be completed by the end of this calendar year, December 2022. As the IFMIS usage expands, the IFMIS team requires advanced IT skills. The project will support these needs under different components of the project. Activities to support improving the functionalities of the Integrated Personnel and Payroll Information System (IPPIS) will be included and funded under the restructured project. The main objective of IPPIS is the provision of a central Payroll and Personnel database to provide reliable and accessible data for payroll processes, employee record management, and other information and process needs. This work will be underpinned by a quality assurance review and a payroll data cleaning exercise. To support these new activities, funds will be reallocated from savings made from other activities of the project. Component 2: Accounting and Financial Reporting: The International Public Sector Accounting Standards (IPSAS) Blueprint has been reviewed and priorities adjusted accordingly. Most activities under this component have been completed, with the remaining activities (data collection for the valuation of non-current assets and the development of the intangible assets and inventory management module) anticipated to be completed by the close of FY 23. The Government is enhancing IFMIS to support IPSAS implementation. User requirements for enhancing various IFMIS modules are being developed and upon the formal approval of policies and procedures that underscore the enhancements, the modules in IFMIS will be configured. Due to the highly technical nature of IPSAS, additional Technical Assistance (TA) will be provided through the restructured project to support the current IFMIS team. Funds will be reallocated from savings made from other activities of the project to finance this TA. Component 3: Performance-Based Budgeting (PBB) and Medium-Term Budgeting Activities under this component are on track. The National Budget Department successfully rolled out PBB in FY 21/22, the MTFF (Medium Term Fiscal Framework) manual and checklist for the preparation of budget ceilings have been prepared and endorsed., The baseline costing tool to update to inform the MTFF annually has also been approved. Further capacity building for budget agencies to accurately record actual outputs that are key for the PBB exercise will be prioritized. Relevant budget agencies will be trained in the use of the MTFF manual to prepare their medium-term expenditure framework. Component 4: Professionalization of PFM Staff – PFM Capacity Development 18 The World Bank Rwanda Public Finance Management Reform Project (P164807) Implementation progress under this component continues to be mixed. While all planned activities under the component are under implementation (i.e. service provider contracts for PFM professionalization courses, support the development of the draft Strategic Plan and Code of conduct for the Procurement Institute; TA to support the Institute of Certified Public Accountants of Rwanda (ICPAR) in the development of the PFM Certification Course; and the development of the Rwanda procurement professionalization program), there has been a significant reduction in uptake of professional examinations and only nominal improvement in pass rates (between the results of December 2021 and April 2022). This is despite the resumption of face-to-face learning (that was previously suspended due to COVID –19. The project has identified the challenges facing professionalization of students and has also prioritized addressing issues round sustainability for future cohorts. As part of the extended project period this will include support towards developing the pipeline of PFM professionals via a training/mentorship program; and establishing a sustainable model to continue PFM professionalization. The project will also support to the development of a strategic plan for effective oversight of SOEs, TA to support improved SOE oversight, and capacity building to support strengthening corporate governance in SOEs. 19
