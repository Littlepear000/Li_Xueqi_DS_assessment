PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Introduction 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the approval of the Country Director for a Level 2 Restructuring of the National Urban Cadaster and Municipal Support Project (P162278), pursuant to an official request from the Government of Peru (GoP) submitted to the World Bank (WB) on April 21, 2026. The proposal requests extending the Loan closing date from July 31, 2026, to January 31st, 2027, and reflects changes agreed upon during Implementation Support Missions and coordination meetings conducted between May 2025 and May 2026. This would constitute the project’s second restructuring. B. Background and Status 2. The project's total financing is US$ 80.90 million, of which US$ 50.00 million corresponds to the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) Loan and US$ 30.90 million to the Government of Peru’s counterpart funding. The project was approved by the Board of Executive Directors of the World Bank Group on January 10, 2020, and became effective as of August 19, 2020. The project’s original closing date was February 28, 2025. 3. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to improve the coverage of urban cadaster services in selected municipalities to enhance local government capacities for revenue generation and urban management. This PDO is being achieved through the following components: Component 1: Strengthening Municipal Systems, Services, and Capacities to Generate and Maintain Urban Cadasters in Project Municipalities (US$ 69.1 million, of which US$44.7 million is Bank-financed and US$ 24.4 is National Counterpart-financed), Component 2: Strengthening of the National Institutional Framework (US$ 6.3 million, of which US$ 5.3 million is Bank-financed and US$ 1.0 is National Counterpart-financed), and Component 3: Project Management (US$5.5 million, of which 100% is National Counterpart-financed). 4. First Project Restructuring. The COVID-19 pandemic, combined with a two-year delay in the preparation of Government’s required Project Documents, postponed the start of project implementation to February 2024. As outlined in the Project Appraisal Document (PAD), cadastral surveys were originally expected to be completed within a two-year period (February 2022–February 2024). Consequently, following the delayed implementation start, the remaining time under the original schedule was insufficient to complete the surveys in line with the two-phase implementation strategy established by the Project Implementation Unit (PIU), under which Phase 1 (Areas 1 and 2) was to be completed by March 2025 and Phase 2 (Areas 3 to 6) by July 2026. The Mid-Term Review (MTR) confirmed that the PDO would remain achievable only if additional implementation time were provided. Accordingly, the Government of Peru submitted an official restructuring request on May 20, 2024, proposing a 17- month extension of the Loan Closing Date. The World Bank approved the first restructuring on May 27, 2024, extending the Loan Closing Date to July 31, 2026. The PDO is rated Moderately Satisfactory, reflecting evidence-based progress toward PDO-Level and Intermediate Results Indicators. Progress includes: (i) 447,440 parcels/cadastral units having passed quality control for Areas 1, 3, 4, 5, and 6 (representing 53 percent of the target); (ii) ten municipal Disaster Risk Reduction and Prevention Plans (DRRPPs) and one Disaster Response Management Plan have been approved and/or adjusted to incorporate cadastral data, strengthening hazard exposure information for 1,960,709 people (representing 42% of the target), of which 44% are women; (iii) 175,503 validated cadastral units have been successfully linked to land use data (28% achieved); (iv) 22 Municipalities with property value maps have been generated (100% achieved); (v) the communication strategy has been implemented in all 22 project municipalities (100% achieved); (vi) 600 municipal officials have been trained (100% achieved), of whom 123 have been certified (21% achieved); (vii) 100 percent of the cadastral units have been updated, registering both men and women as property tax payers; and (viii) 89 percent of grievances/complaints are being resolved in 20 or less working days. 6. Implementation Progress is rated Moderately Satisfactory, reflecting sound implementation arrangements and strong institutional coordination. The Project is implemented under the leadership of the Agency for the Formalization of Informal Property (Órgano de Formalización de la Propiedad Informal, COFOPRI), in close coordination with key implementing agencies, including the Ministry of Economy and Finance (Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, MEF), the Ministry of Housing, Construction, and Sanitation (Ministerio de Vivienda, Construcción y Saneamiento, MVCS), and the Metropolitan Municipality of Lima. Following decisive actions by the PIU to adjudicate all cadastral areas, overall cadastral survey implementation has accelerated significantly and is on track. In particular: (i) all pending contracts and/or cooperation agreements for 5 Areas out of 6 are under active execution and progressing according to plan, except for Area 2, whose contract had to be canceled due to the vendor's unsatisfactory quality of work — a revised procurement strategy (direct contracting) will be proposed to deliver cadastral services to the 4 additional municipalities originally covered under that contract; (ii) cadastral supervision and quality control firms for Areas 1 to 6 are contracted and delivering results; (iii) IT development of Stages 1 and 2 of the National Urban Cadaster System (Sistema Informático de Catastro Urbano Nacional, SICUN) has been completed, with stage 3 expected to be finalized by May 2026; and (iv) all 22 project municipalities are receiving continuous, hands-on technical assistance focused on institutional strengthening and capacity building. In addition, the Project is rated Satisfactory for Environmental and Social Safeguards compliance and Project Management, and Moderately Satisfactory for Financial Management and Procurement. 7. Disbursements have accelerated significantly, increasing more than fivefold from US$5.5 million (11 percent) as of May 2024 to US$30.6 million (61 percent) as of May 2026, reflecting the strong implementation momentum described above. Cumulative commitments have reached US$46.94 million, representing 94 percent of the US$50 million loan, providing a solid basis for the Government's plan to reach full disbursement by January 2027. This acceleration has been underpinned by the completion of the PIU staffing, with all key project staff now recruited, and by strengthened institutional capacity across implementing agencies. There are no overdue audits; external audits have been received and considered acceptable to the Bank as of the date of this Restructuring Paper. Financial Management and Procurement are both rated Moderately Satisfactory. 8. Loan Repayment during the extended implementation period. The extension of the Loan Closing Date to January 31, 2027, implies that the Project will remain under implementation during the initial phase of loan repayment. In Page 2 The World Bank National Urban Cadaster and Municipal Support Project (P162278) accordance with Schedule 3 of the Loan Agreement, repayment of principal to IBRD is scheduled to commence on August 1, 2026, with a second installment due on February 1, 2027. These payment obligations are not affected by the extension of the Closing Date, as the amortization schedule is fixed at the time of loan approval and cannot be modified as part of this restructuring. The Borrower has been informed of this overlap and has confirmed its financial capacity to service the loan according to the existing repayment schedule while continuing to implement Project activities through the extended Closing Date. The Task Team has reviewed the disbursement projections and confirms that the remaining undisbursed balance of US$ 19.4 million is expected to be fully disbursed by the new Closing Date. 9. Final Evaluation. The Government's Final Evaluation Plan, comprising four studies, is being implemented in close coordination with the Bank, with Terms of Reference approved in November 2025, the market study completed in April 2026, and the procurement process expected to be launched in May 2026 (subject to the approval of this restructuring). 10. The overall risk profile of the Project remains manageable despite a complex operating environment. Recent political transitions — including successive changes in national leadership since October 2025, the April 2026 general elections, and the upcoming presidential runoff scheduled for June 2026 — have not adversely affected progress toward achieving the PDO, as evidenced by the significant acceleration in disbursements and continued execution of contracts across all cadastral areas during this period. The overall residual risk is assessed as Substantial, primarily due to the likelihood that activities related to Cadastral Area 2 — whose contract was canceled due to unsatisfactory vendor performance — would not be completed by the current Loan Closing Date, putting at risk the full achievement of Results Framework targets. The proposed extension, combined with the revised procurement strategy for Area 2 (direct contracting) and the near-complete commitment of loan proceeds (94 percent), would mitigate this risk. Accordingly, the overall risk rating is expected to be reassessed as Moderate in the subsequent ISR, reflecting the project's strengthened implementation capacity and a realistic pathway to full PDO achievement. D. Rationale for Restructuring 11. The rationale for the proposed extension is driven by a combination of external factors and implementation challenges that have directly affected the Project’s critical path, most of which have now been resolved. These include: (i) The cancellation of the cadastral survey contract for Area 2 due to the vendor's unsatisfactory quality of work, which required a revised procurement strategy (direct contracting) to deliver services to the 4 remaining municipalities; (ii) the cancellation of the first procurement process for cadastral Area 3 due to security risks and adverse market conditions, which required subdividing the Area into three packages and re-bidding them (currently under implementation); (iii) contractual delays in Areas 4 and 5 resulting from documentation requirements and extended technical negotiations; (iv) delays in formalizing the cooperation agreement with Instituto Catastral de Lima (ICL) for Area 6, as multiple operational adjustments were required prior to signature, and (v) significant delays in the procurement of the cadastral supervision and quality control contracts, where complaints raised during the evaluation process required Bank review and two rounds of reassessment, resulting in contract signature in October 2025. The Project has signed and committed contracts whose implementation periods extend beyond the current closing date of July 31, 2026, making it unfeasible to meet contractual obligations within the approved timeframe. The proposed extension is therefore required to ensure the full achievement of the PDO and Results Framework targets through the completion of remaining critical activities — including the finalization of cadastral surveys, the development, deployment, and operational consolidation of the National Urban Cadaster Information System, the completion of cadastral quality control processes, and the orderly transfer of capacities to local governments. Without the proposed extension, critical activities would remain incomplete, compromising the quality and sustainability of the national urban cadaster system and preventing the Government of Peru from fully meeting its commitments under the IBRD Loan Agreement. 13. The financial case for the proposed extension is compelling. A financial performance analysis conducted by the PIU indicates that, under the current closing date, a significant share of both Loan proceeds and national co-financing would remain undisbursed, directly affecting the Project’s ability to achieve its planned results. In a no-extension scenario, only US$34 million of the US$50 million IBRD Loan (68 percent) would be utilized, while execution of national counterpart resources would reach only US$21 million of US$30.83 million (68 percent). This would severely constrain both physical and financial execution and limit the delivery of key cadastral and technological outputs. By contrast, approval of the proposed extension would enable full utilization of the approved budget, support the completion of all technical activities essential for achieving the PDO, reduce the risk of incomplete or lower-quality deliverables, and set the stage for the institutional and financial sustainability of the National Urban Cadaster in Peru. 14. The proposed restructuring has no implications for the Project's financial arrangements. Financial reporting, funds flow, and disbursement arrangements under the Loan will remain unchanged following this restructuring. Consequently, no amendments to the Project’s Disbursement and Financial Information Letter (DFIL) are required. 15. The PDO remains fully relevant and aligned with Peru's national development priorities. It is aligned with the Government’s Strategic National Development Plan to 2050 and the FY23-27 World Bank Group Country Partnership Framework (CPF) for Peru. The relevance of promoting the exchange of cadastral information between national and local governments for urban governance and revenue generation remains unchanged, as does the Project's alignment with national and sectoral public policies. Through this restructuring, the World Bank will continue to support the implementation and sustainability of the National Urban Cadaster and land administration system in Peru. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 16. This restructuring proposes a six-month extension of the Loan Closing Date, from July 31st, 2026, to January 31st, 2027. The Government of Peru requested a 6.9-month extension to February 26th, 2027; however, in line with assessed implementation needs and the World Bank’s prevailing policies on cumulative loan closing date extensions, the proposed extension has been calibrated to six months — sufficient to complete all remaining critical activities while maintaining consistency with institutional requirements.
