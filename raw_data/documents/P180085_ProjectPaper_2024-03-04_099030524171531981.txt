BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING A. Introduction 1. This Project Paper seeks approval of the World Bank Board of Executive Directors to provide an Additional Financing (AF) in the amount of EUR 55.4 million (US$60 million equivalent) from the International Development Association (IDA) to the Republic of Congo and EUR 83.1 million (US$90 million equivalent) from the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) to the Gabonese Republic for the Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West and Central Africa – Series of Projects Two (HISWACA - SOP 2) Regional Statistics Project (P180085). 2. The HISWACA - SOP 2 Project was approved by the Board on September 28, 2023. It aims to improve the quality, accessibility, and production of regionally comparable statistics, with the goal to improve the statistical performance of participating countries and the extent to which their National Statistical Systems (NSS) meet user needs and contribute to better decision-making. The project will also support capacity building with a focus on organizing and modernizing statistical methods, physical infrastructure, and information and communication technology (ICT) of participating countries’ ministries, departments, and agencies (MDAs) responsible for statistics, including National Statistics Offices (NSOs). This will involve adoption of emerging alternative methods to data production to complement traditional statistics and enable NSOs to fulfill their respective mandates efficiently. Using a regional approach will promote coordination between relevant MDAs and NSOs and will create positive externalities by making available methods and tools that can be used beyond the participating countries. 3. The parent project includes four recipients, three countries—Cameroon (IDA Credit US$105 million equivalent), Chad (IDA Grant US$105 million equivalent), and the Central African Republic (IDA Grant US$60 million equivalent)—and a regional organization—the Central African Economic and Monetary Community (Communauté Économique et Monétaire de l’Afrique Centrale, CEMAC) (IDA Grant US$20 million equivalent)—for a total amount of US$290 million equivalent from National and Regional IDA windows. 4. The parent project includes the following four components:  Component 1: Harmonization and Production of Core Statistics using International Data Quality Standards . This component aims to support the regional statistical harmonization process and the production of quality core statistics to improve data sources and data infrastructure pillars of the Statistical Performance Indicator (SPI). It addresses the challenges related to the quality and harmonization of statistics to allow comparability of statistics in the region. Its main objective is to promote the adoption of international standards and bridge the gap in the production of a core set of economic, social, and demographic statistics that will improve data availability to meet the increasing demand for statistics, especially indicators needed for monitoring the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Through this component, the CEMAC will strengthen its capacity to effectively advocate for improved statistics in the region.  Component 2: Statistical Modernization, Institutional Reform, Human Capital, Data Accessibility and Use. This component aims to support statistical modernization; institutional reforms; improvement to data access, dissemination, and use; and human resources development in NSOs and selected MDAs of participating countries in their transition toward an Integrated National Data System. This component focuses on exploiting the potential of new data sources and leveraging advanced technologies to integrate data from diverse sources in a cost-effective manner. Furthermore, this component recognizes the importance of investing in human capital by providing academic training in statistics and on-the-job training Page 1 The World Bank AF - Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West and Central Africa - Series of Projects Two (HISWACA - SOP 2) (P180085) for staff. Finally, this component supports institutional reforms of statistical systems in participating countries, including statistics regulations and data protection frameworks.  Component 3: Construction, Upgrading, and Modernization of Physical Infrastructure. This component aims to strengthen the capacity of NSOs and statistical schools by providing them with the needed equipment and tools to adequately fulfil their mandates. This component will support the construction of new buildings or the rehabilitation of physical and ICT infrastructure for the NSOs and schools of statistics.  Component 4: Project Management, Monitoring and Evaluation: The goal of this component is to support project management and monitor project results and user satisfaction. B. Current Status of the Project 5. The HISWACA - SOP 2 Project was declared effective for Chad on December 22, 2023, and for the Central African Republic on January 12, 2024. The Financial Agreement (FA) for CEMAC was signed on November 11, 2023, and the FA is expected to be effective on May 30, 2024. The CEMAC commission is in the process of hiring Project Implementation Unit (PIU) staff, and the operation manual is being finalized. For Cameroon, the FA has not yet been signed. The National Institute of Statistics (Institut National de la Statistique, INS) of Cameroon is now working to get the remaining visas from (a) the office of the Prime Minister and (b) the Presidency. The first disbursement for Chad and the Central African Republic has been processed and the implementation progress and progress toward achieving the Project Development Objective (PDO) is rated Satisfactory. C. Rationale for Additional Financing 6. The HISWACA - SOP 2 Project was initially prepared with the Republic of Congo and the Gabonese Republic as project participating countries. All the necessary project preparation steps, from initiation to negotiations, were completed with the active participation of these countries along with Cameroon, Central African Republic, Chad, and CEMAC commission. Unfortunately, due to constraints unrelated to the project, the approval for these two countries could not take place in September 2023 and therefore the financing for Republic of Congo and the Gabonese Republic is now being processed as an AF. 7. The AF is proposed to include the Republic of Congo and the Gabonese Republic in the HISWACA - SOP 2 Project as originally planned, building on the progress made during the initial preparation phase and ensuring that complete harmonization of statistics is achieved in the region and the continuity of critical statistical activities implemented under previous World Bank-financed statistics operations in the Republic of Congo 1 and the Gabonese Republic. 2 More specifically, the activities include completing the Gabon 2024 population and housing census (PHC) (cartography, enumeration of the population, post census survey, data processing, and publication of results), equipping the newly built office complex for the Republic of Congo’s NSO and statistical school, and conducting a household budget survey in the Republic of Congo and the Gabonese Republic in 2025. 8. The project is in line with the countries’ Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs). It aims to support the production of harmonized quality data for comprehensive policy support of climate change mitigation and adaptation, and for achieving NDC targets in each of the participating countries. For example, the Republic of Congo 3 prepared its NDC 1 Republic of Congo Statistical Capacity Building Project (PSTAT-P133731) closed on September 30, 2023. 2 Gabon Statistical Development Project (PDS-P157473) closed on December 29, 2023. 3 Contribution Déterminée Au Niveau National (CDN) De La République Du Congo, 2021. The AF aligns with the Country Partnership Frameworks (CPFs) for the Republic of Congo (FY20-24 Report No. 166461-CG) and the Gabonese Republic (FY23-27 Report No. 174105-GA). In addition, the project will support the strategic priorities of the Western and Central Africa (AFW) region. Regarding statistics, it centers around bolstering statistical capacity to inform and drive development initiatives. The AFW strategy acknowledges the critical role of statistics in national development, disaster risk management, and environmental and natural resource management, with a special focus on climate change mitigation and adaptation. The AF will support production and dissemination of economic and socio-demographic statistics. The project will finance key statistical data collection operations: PHCs (finalization of the Republic of Congo 2023 PHC and the implementation of the Gabonese Republic 2024 PHC), enterprise census in the Republic of Congo, Harmonized Household Living Conditions Surveys (EGEP35 in the Gabonese Republic and EHCVM26 in the Republic of Congo), the Demographic and Health Survey (DHS) in the Gabonese Republic and the Republic of Congo (co-financed by the Global Fund), annual labor force surveys (LFSs) in both countries, and agriculture census and surveys. Because employment statistics are weak in both countries, the project will establish a system of regular employment statistics, starting with a national baseline survey followed by a quarterly employment survey. Additionally, the project aims to strengthen the routine data collection system in key sectors such as education, employment statistics monitoring, agricultural production including livestock and fisheries, and the establishment of an electronic platform for statistical and fiscal forms. Regarding data dissemination, the project will support the development of a national data dissemination policy and the establishment of a national data portal to disseminate socioeconomic data. Studies show the positive impacts for economies, particularly in developing countries, to promote open data and free access to microdata and metadata. Reports such as the 2019 study by the Organisation for Economic Co- operation and Development (OECD) and the World Bank’s World Development Report 7 show the direct and indirect economic and social benefits of data access and sharing. These include greater transparency and empowerment of users, new business opportunities, competition, and cooperation within and across sectors and countries, crowdsourcing, user- driven innovation, and increased efficiency. Evidence also shows that data access and sharing data can generate positive economic and social benefits for data providers (direct impact), their suppliers and data users (indirect impact), and the wider economy (induced impact). However, while it is difficult to assess and measure the overall benefits of data access and sharing, studies by sector (public versus private sector) provide rough estimates of the magnitude of the impact. For instance, in the European Union, the direct effect of open data on the economy was estimated at Euro 325 billion between 2016 and 2020, with a 36.9 percent increase in the share of gross domestic product (GDP) for the same period. 8 B. The Central Africa region’s common disasters include epidemics, droughts, floods, and storms. 12 The Congo Basin covers, among others, the Central African Republic, the Republic of Congo, and Gabon. These regions are characterized by significant social vulnerability, political instability, and poverty. The Congo Basin is naturally warm and humid and experiences only two seasons: the rainy season from March to November and the dry season from December to February. 2. Future climate trends in the Congo Basin. Under high emissions scenarios, average temperatures in the region are expected to increase by 2.5°C by 2050 while temperature increases are expected to be 3–5°C by 2100.13 Furthermore, regions within the Congo Basin that have a more semiarid climate are expected to experience higher average temperature increases compared with those of the tropical climates. 3. Key climate impacts include loss of habitat, biodiversity, and tourism revenue; increased carbon sequestration; reduced crop yields; increased reliance on forest products; food insecurity; increased hydropower production in some seasons and reduced production in others; reduced water quality; spread of vector-borne and waterborne diseases; and heat stress.14 Climate Vulnerabilities for Project Participating Countries 4. The World Bank’s Africa’s Pulse, October 2021 edition, states that the frequency of extreme weather events has increased in the region over the past four decades, and it has increased at a faster pace than in the rest of the world. It also says that most countries in West and Central Africa have been primarily affected by floods over the past two decades. It states that, “The rises in temperature and changes in rainfall patterns have led to the increase in frequency and intensity of extreme weather events across the continent.” In fact, natural disasters (including drought) have increased at a much faster pace than in the rest of the world. 5. Drought is defined as a deficiency of precipitation over an extended period (usually a season or more), resulting in a water shortage.15 Drought risk refers to the potential losses from hazard imposed by a drought event, and it is characterized as a function of vulnerability, hazard, and exposure. The drought risk ratio is found to be the highest in Central African countries as a consequence of vulnerability and population rise in that region. 6. Flood hazard is potential loss of life, injury, and economic loss caused by future flood events. Climate change, heavy rains, and floods take a toll on human life, property, land, and livestock. A UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs report from February 2023 states that in 2022, flooding affected over 5.8 million people in 20 countries of the West and Central Africa region, which experienced one of the worst flooding disasters on record that year. Subcomponent 1.2. Demographic and Socio-Economic Statistical Production 17. PHCs. The challenges caused by climate changes require an understanding of how the population size, structure, and dynamics influence, and are influenced by, changing climate. The PHCs are the only data source providing this crucial needed data at a granular level and their geo-localized distribution at this level. PHCs also play a fundamental role in providing needed data to assess population growth and the pace of urbanization in the foreseeable future that help a better understanding of the trajectory of global GHG emissions and developing and implementing adaptation plans and thus to global and national efforts to curtail this threat. The project will support the PHC in the Gabonese Republic whereas for the Republic of Congo where the PHC activities will already have been conducted, the project will support its analytical and reporting phases. 18. Support the implementation of an Integrated Household Surveys Program in each country. In each participating country, the Integrated Household Survey Program will include DHS/MICS, LFSs, and welfare and living condition monitoring surveys. The integrated nature of this survey program requires a unique and coordinated planning of operations in each country. The surveys will also be carried out based on harmonized concepts, variables, and classifications. Each survey will include a list of similar and standardized variables needed for nonmonetary poverty measurement. Standardization of coding, editing, and imputation process in the data processing of same modules will also be sought, to ensure consistency in the results. The program will also offer expansion of the range of data in addition to expanding the use of new technologies in data collection. The expansion of the range of data will consider the inclusion of new topics such as the modules on learning poverty, food insecurity, and the impact of high food prices. The program will also support the inclusion of longitudinal investigations to support evaluations of public policies or their designs, especially around climate changes. This subcomponent will support other data collection activities as necessary and aims to support all participating countries to advance in the collection and production of a set of core climate change statistics and indicators relevant to the country and the region. The UN Statistical Commission in March 2022 adopted a global set of climate change statistics and indicators as a framework to be used by countries to produce comparable climate change statistics. Indeed, this framework supports countries in preparing their own sets of climate change statistics and indicators according to their individual concerns, priorities, and resources. The framework covers the five policy areas of climate change, namely drivers, impacts, vulnerability, mitigation, and adaptation. Based on this global set of climate change statistics and indicators, the project will finance regional workshops to help identify a minimum set of indicators to be collected and produced by each participating country. In addition, the project will promote cooperation between NSOs and national authorities responsible for climate change, to strengthen the link between statistics and policy. Statistical capacity-building activities will also be done, in national and regional technical workshops on climate change statistics and indicators. 22. The project will encourage collaboration between NSO and other producers/users to make the most of this already available data. Subcomponent 3.1: Construction and Infrastructure Upgrading of selected National Statistical Offices (NSOs) and Statistical Schools 26. This activity aims to support the modernization of NSOs’ physical infrastructure and statistical schools by building or upgrading office complexes with modern facilities and providing needed office furniture and equipment for the entire statistical cycle from production through dissemination. The construction, rehabilitation, and upgrading of climate-resilient infrastructures for NSOs and statistics schools will include climate-resilient structures with resilience to flooding and extreme heat, and the buildings will be designed for lower energy consumptions, with the use of building materials with low embedded GHG emissions, construction of building structures for high energy efficiency, and addition of on-site renewable energy sources. 27. Examples of climate resilience investments for buildings. The project’s infrastructure activities could include optimized orientation of the buildings, with optimized window openings to improve cross- ventilation and decrease heating; the use of heavyweight structures to capture solar heat; and the use of green roofs and reflective surfaces to reduce temperatures in and around buildings. As part of the buildings’ design, energy and water loads applied to the buildings system could be reduced by including optimized window-to-wall ratios, low-flow appliances, high-efficiency appliances, energy recovery systems, LED lighting, and so on. The buildings could also have renewable energy systems installed to supplement or entirely replace the buildings’ grid energy demand. Additional adaptation to potential climate hazards could include harvesting rainwater and storing it for non-potable applications to adapt against drought; buildings could be designed carefully considering the level for new construction to adapt against flooding; and risk to extreme storms could be avoided by structurally designing higher wind loads and designing rainwater management for higher flows. The project can support recipients to follow IFC’s EDGE green building certification system focused on making buildings more resource efficient.
