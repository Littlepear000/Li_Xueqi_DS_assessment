Introduction and Context 1. The SADC Regional Statistics Project (P175731), approved on June 8, 2023, seeks to modernize data ecosystems of participating countries by addressing gaps in data production, harmonization, and use. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to strengthen the institutional capacity of Southern African Development Community (SADC) and participating countries to produce, disseminate, and use quality statistics while increasing regional harmonization and collaboration. The project includes seven recipients: Comoros (US$8.5 million), Madagascar (US$45 million), Malawi (US$15 million), Mozambique (US$60 million), São Tomé and Príncipe (US$3 million), Zambia (US$30 million), and the Page 4 Official Use Only The World Bank SADC Regional Statistics Project (P175731) – Additional Financing Request 3 SADC Secretariat (US$10 million), for a total amount of US$171.5 million. The closing date of the project is October 31, 2028. 2. STP is a small island state with a population of approximately 212,000 people in 2025. The country is rated as lowmiddle- income small island state with significant poverty: 55.5% of the population lives below the national poverty line, and 13% survives on less than US$3 a day (2021 PPP), the extreme poverty line. 3. Project activities for Sao Tome and Principe. The IDA Grant E1890 for the amount of SDR 2.3 million (US$3 million equivalent) became effective on February 13, 2024. Parent project activities include: (a) the final stage of the Population and Housing Census (notably thematic studies), (b) the Household Budget Survey (Inquérito aos Orçamentos Familiares, IOF), (c) labor force surveys, (d) business and production statistics, and (e) the production of other key economic indicators. The project also supports the upgrade of ICT infrastructure and operational systems to strengthen data collection, processing, analysis, dissemination, and archiving. Capacity building activities for INE staff are also financed under the project to strengthen technical and institutional capabilities. In São Tomé and Príncipe, the project is implemented by INE, with fiduciary and Environmental and Social Framework (ESF) responsibilities carried out by the Fiduciary Agency for Project Administration (AFAP). Sectoral and Institutional Context 4. This Project Paper seeks approval for an Additional Financing (AF) in the amount of US$3 million to São Tomé and Príncipe for the SADC Regional Statistics Project to close a financing gap and scale up selected activities; as well as to restructure the project activities in other participating countries, post Mid-Term Review (MTR, completed in April 2026). 5. During implementation of the current project, the costs of key data collection activities, especially the Population and Housing Census and the Household Budget Survey, were revised upward due to operational and logistical challenges. These challenges include higher-than-expected inflation, exchange rate movements (notably the depreciation of the US dollar relative to the Euro, given the country’s reliance on imports), and increased costs of project management following institutional changes affecting AFAP. More specifically, for the Population and Housing Census, its completion has also been affected by financing shortfalls in the STP Digital Project, which had originally planned to support certain census activities. As a result, additional resources were required under the current project to ensure the completion of census-related activities, including post-enumeration work and thematic studies. 6. These combined factors have resulted in a cost overrun estimated at approximately US$1.25 million, affecting both already implemented activities and the remaining phases of ongoing operations. Without additional resources, the completion and quality of key statistical outputs—particularly the census and household survey—would be compromised. 7. Beyond addressing the cost overrun, in the letter dated November 20, 2025, Government has requested additional US$1.75 million for scale up of activities to address remaining data gaps in priority sectors. The Government has identified the need to expand statistical production to areas such as health, education, agriculture, and social conditions, including through the implementation of the Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) and one round of Page 5 Official Use Only The World Bank SADC Regional Statistics Project (P175731) – Additional Financing Request 3 agricultural survey. These activities are critical for monitoring national development objectives and the Sustainable Development Goals, and for informing evidence-based policymaking. 8. The additional financing is aligned with the World Bank's Country Partnership Framework for São Tomé and Príncipe (FY24-29) and contributes to strengthening the capacity of public institutions to produce and use data for decision-making, building on the Bank's broader engagement in the country. The proposed scale-up builds on achievements under the parent project and addresses remaining gaps, thereby enhancing the overall impact of the operation. The proposed two-year implementation period for the AF is considered feasible given the country context and the maturity of ongoing activities. No extension of the project closing date is proposed. The project comprises the following components: • Component 1: Improve harmonization, quality, and dissemination of core social and economic statistics. The regional component supports the implementation of continental and regional agreements on statistics – specifically, the SADC’s Regional Strategy for Development of Statistics (RSDS) 2020–2030 – by improving the capacity of the SADC Secretariat to help Member States improve harmonization, quality, and dissemination of core social and economic statistics. • Component 2: Country Component: Close gaps in data production, statistical capacity, and data use at country level. The country component finances key data collection and production (such as National Accounts, CPI, Household and Expenditure Surveys and Agriculture and Livestock Censuses), as well as statistical capacity building and data use activities. Page 6 Official Use Only The World Bank SADC Regional Statistics Project (P175731) – Additional Financing Request 3 • Component 3: Project management ensures the implementation of the project in each participating entity. • Component 4: Contingency Emergency Response Component (CERC) for Madagascar and Malawi only. 13. The proposed Additional Financing (AF) will close financing gaps and support the completion of key statistical activities, including the Population and Housing Census, the 2026 Household Budget Survey (IOF), business and labor force surveys, national accounts, and CPI rebasing. It will also finance new activities, notably a Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) and an Agricultural Survey, while strengthening INE’s institutional capacity through legal reforms, training, scholarships, ICT investments, and purchase and installation of solar panels and air-conditioning equipment. In addition, the AF will improve data dissemination and use through modernized digital platforms, research partnerships, and enhanced use of statistics for policymaking, while financing the additional costs of project implementation under existing institutional arrangements. The AF will also revise selected results indicators and targets to reflect the expanded scope of activities. The overall Environmental and Social (E&S) risk rating is Moderate, consistent with the parent project as this is a regional project. Environmental impacts are inherently low given the absence of land acquisition, habitat conversion, large-scale construction, or use of hazardous substances. The primary environmental risks are localized and temporary: construction-phase noise, dust, and waste from the Príncipe rehabilitation works, and e-waste generation from ICT equipment upgrades and decommissioning. These risks manageable and a E&S checklist followed by waste management and site-specific environmental documentation will be requiered even for minor works. The most significant risks of the AF are social rather than environmental, arising from the large-scale field survey operations. The deployment of enumerators to households across both islands — particularly under the MICS, which engages women and children — carries an inherent risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Sexual Harassment (SEA/SH), compounded by limited supervision in remote areas and power imbalances between survey staff and respondents. Occupational health and safety (OHS) risks for field workers are also relevant. Additionally, the collection of sensitive household-level data on health, income, and agricultural assets raises data privacy concerns that have both social and community safety dimensions. The Environmental and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP), Stakeholder Engagement Plan (SEP), Labor Management Procedures (LMP), Code of Conduct (CoC), and a SEA/SH-sensitive Grievance Mechanism (GM) — provides an adequate foundation for managing the identified risks. The PIU at INE maintains a dedicated Environmental and Social Specialist to oversee implementation.
