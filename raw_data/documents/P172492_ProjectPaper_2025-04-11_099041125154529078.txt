PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING Project Status 1. This restructuring paper seeks management approval for restructuring of the Accountable Governance for Basic Service Delivery Project (AGBSDP). The Executive Board of Directors approved the $40 million Investment Project Financing Project on 11 June 2021 and the project became effective on 25 January 2022 (Table 1). The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to improve resource management, transparency and accountability of government systems for enabling the delivery of local development projects and basic services. The project comprises four components namely: Component 1: Systems and skills for local service delivery; Component 2: Local Development Financing; Component 3: Integrated data platforms for monitoring and accountability; and Component 4: Project management and implementation support. A Mid-Term Review (MTR) was conducted from June 3rd-14th, 2024, after two and a half years of implementation. Following the MTR the Government of Sierra Leone requested a restructuring in a letter dated 2nd August 2024. 2. Although progress has been made in several areas, overall implementation progress has remained slow, resulting in several downgrades across the various components. Progress towards achievement of the PDO was downgraded at the MTR to Moderately Satisfactory (MS) while the overall implementation progress was downgraded to Moderately Unsatisfactory (MU). While the risk of not achieving the PDO may have increased on account of slow implementation to date, the achievement of the PDO is still considered to be likely. The Disbursement rate is low driven by delays in procurement, especially for significant Information Technology systems like e-Procurement (e-GP) and the Audit Management Information System (AMIS). Consequently, the rating for Component 1 has been downgraded to MU during the last implementation support mission in June 2024. The Component 2 rating has been downgraded to MS due to implementation delays. Component 3, which focuses on the development and roll-out of an integrated data platform for local government monitoring and evaluation (M&E), was downgraded to Unsatisfactory (U) because of little progress in implementation since project effectiveness (Table 2) and will be dropped. 3. Inadequate project management and coordination continues to constrain implementation progress. Despite efforts made to streamline the Project Management Unit’s (PMU) review process of activity concept notes and preparation of the Annual Work Plan, there continues to be significant delays in implementation. The PMU faces significant coordination challenges Page 1 The World Bank Accountable Governance for Basic Service Delivery (P172492) given the large number (12) of implementing entities (IEs). There are also weaknesses in Monitoring and Evaluation as well as in Communication, mostly the result of capacity challenges in the PMU. As a result, the rating for Component 4 was also downgraded to MU in June 2024. The project rating related to Procurement has been downgraded to MS due to delays in the procurement process, lead times of big-ticket items and low disbursement rate at the mid-term of the project. Financial Management, and Environmental and Social Safeguards remain unchanged at MS and there are no significant social or environmental concerns reported. 4. The PMU forecasts an expected financing gap of around $5.9 million by project closing in July 2027, assuming full implementation of the existing project design and planned activities, mainly because of a $3.5m currency exchange variation (between SDR and USD). The costs of rolling out the public financial management information system (PFM Smart) to all LCs are also higher than expected (around $518k) largely due to the decision to invest more in the supporting infrastructure, including solar, to ensure optimum system functionality. Additionally, there are higher than expected costs to manage the Local Development Grants (LDGs) cycle anticipated (by around $1.0m) resulting from more extensive oversight by the PMU and the Fiscal Decentralization Department (FDD), with all 15 district councils qualifying for Round 1 grants (only 12 were expected). High inflation in Sierra Leone has also contributed to higher costs in most project sub- components. To stay within the project financial envelope, the government and the World Bank team propose to reallocate funds to prioritize areas critical to meeting the PDO as part of the restructuring. The reallocations will finance the impact of foreign exchange, the higher costs to implement activities, and the costs of new activities. 5. Despite the implementation challenges, the project has achieved impact in the project catchment areas: increasing school enrolment rates, attendance at health centers and local government’s own source revenues. The first round of Local Development Grants (LDGs) supported 29 sub-projects across 15 Local Councils (LCs) with US$1.6 million in financing for completion, expansion, and rehabilitation of 15 schools/learning facilities, 9 health centers, and 5 public facilities (markets and community halls). The second round of the local development grants has commenced expanding the support to an additional 33 sub-projects across the 15 target LCs1. The first tranche in the sum of $1.9 (60 per cent of the total round 2 grant) million has been disbursed to local councils with a plan to disburse the remaining 40 per cent in April 2025. To improve accountability for these funds District Development Coordination Committees (DDCCs) have been established in 16 districts to serve as hubs for coordinating district activities, and Audit Committees have been established in all 222 Local Councils to provide control measures and checks and balances in managing Local Councils’ funds. The delay in the rollout of the PFM SMART system to local councils is disappointing and has caused delays in full automation of the public financial management cycle at the local government level. 1There are 47 lots/contracts – consisting of 33 sub-projects and 14 covering fabrication of furniture and installation solar electric power in some of the sub-project facilities. 2 16 district councils and 6 city councils Page 2 The World Bank Accountable Governance for Basic Service Delivery (P172492) Rationale for Restructuring 6. The proposed restructuring aims to address implementation challenges and simplify the project to focus on core outcomes in line with the PDO that can be achieved by the end of July 2027. This is achieved by: (i) simplifying project components and related activities; (ii) reallocating resources to select priorities; (iii) improving implementation arrangements, (iv) enhancing social accountability and the role of the Interministerial Committee on Decentralization (IMC), and (v) simplifying the Results Framework (RF). Component 3 of the project is to be dropped to finance other technical activities and support project management. The proposed restructuring will also improve the effectiveness of the PMU by shifting the management of the project implementation support staff for the LDGs to the Fiscal Decentralization Department which will enable focus on core project management duties. Two new sub-components are proposed to be added to the project. Firstly, leveraging existing initiatives of civil society in Sierra Leone, the project will support the scale- up of the Building Accountability Systems through Empowered Communities (BASE) implemented by the Institute for Governance Reforms (IGR). Secondly, the project will work to support the IMC under the Vice President’s Office to strengthen the legal and institutional framework for fiscal decentralization, driving implementation of the fiscal decentralization strategy and efforts to boost revenue mobilization at LCs. Finally, the restructuring will amend the results framework to revise indicators whose targets are now unachievable due to implementation delays. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES A. Overview 7.
