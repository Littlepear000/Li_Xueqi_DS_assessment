KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions This is an updated ISR based on Implementation Support Mission conducted October 28 to November 8, 2024, and follow-up technical meetings in December 2024. Key results are summarized below: 1. Pillar A (Improving the business environment). This Pillar achieved tangible results in streamlining steps and procedures by combining enterprise and tax registrations into one process as well as supported a new electronic business registration system (eBRS) for the Department of Enterprise Registration for a live online business registration starting from December 9, 2024. New economic entries are now benefiting from a substantial reduction in time taken to start a business from from 173 days in 2018 to 23 days in 2024, exceeding the end target of 70 days. 2. Pillar B (Facilitating trade and connecting to markets). This Pillar achieved positive results in (i) a joint integration risk management framework for joint border inspections supported by a dashboard and alert mechanism to improve border compliance; and (ii) a strengthened institutional capacity of the National Trade and Transport Facilitation Committee (NTTFC) in resolving trade and transport issues and reform of non-tariff measures (NTMs) associated with the issuance of import and export permits, certification and licenses (PLC) to improve documentary compliance. Traders have benefited a reduction in cargo clearance time at borders by 64 percent from 9 hours and 10 minutes in 2018 (TRS 2017) to 3 hours and 19 minutes in 2024, exceeding the end target of 4.09 hours. 3. Pillar C (Improving Firm-level Competitiveness). The second Business Assistance Facility (BAF II) played a leading role in the development of a functioning BDS marketplace in Lao PDR, which hardly existed prior to BAF II, and demonstrated to the Lao private sector how BDS services can be used to develop and grow their businesses. According to both the BAF II’s Interim Impact Survey and the Final Survey, grant-supported firms increased their sales at a rate at least 1.85 or 85 percent, exceeding the end target of 1.2 or 20 percent higher than non-supported firms. In terms of impact, BAF II- supported firms had an average increase rate of employment by approximately 12.9 percent between 2021 and 2024, compared to that of firms that did not receive a BAF II grant which experienced around 3.4 percent over the same period. 4. Pillar D (Supporting Better and More Inclusive Policies). Public-Private Dialogue (PPD) delivered some good impact. The 15th Lao Business Forum (LBF) was successfully organized on April 2, 2024, in Vientiane. The 15th LBF has resolved six main issues including business operating licensing, drawn strong support from Prime Minister chairing the event. The success can be attributed to close follow-up and consultations on business issues during the quarterly inter-ministerial coordination meetings and the semi-annual steering committee meetings prior to the LBF. Of the 20 issues discussed, six were resolved, eleven are in progress, and three are under discussion. Project communications strategy has been implemented with significant progress made in upgrading of the Trade for Development Laos (T4DLaos) website at https://www.t4dlaos.org/, launching of LCTP’s Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) page at https://www.t4dlaos.org/lctgrm/. There has been growing visits and visitors to the T4DLaos webpage with tracking metrics showing almost 10,000 visitors and 20,000 visits over the past three months.
