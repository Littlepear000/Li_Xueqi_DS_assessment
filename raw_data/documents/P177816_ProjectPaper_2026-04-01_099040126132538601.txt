PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the approval of the Director for Regional Programs of a Level 2 Restructuring of the Food Systems Resilience Program - FSRP for Eastern and Southern Africa Project for Comoros (P177816) to address budget needs for the rehabilitation of feeder roads. The proposed restructuring seeks to reallocate funds between disbursement categories. The restructuring request from the Government of the Union of Comoros was received by the World Bank on September 9, 2025. This will be the second restructuring for the Comoros FSRP. The first restructuring was completed in July 2025 ; to extend the closing date of the PROBLUE Grant from the Global Program for the Blue Economy Multi-Donor Trust Fund, originally set for September 30, 2025, to August 31, 2029, aligning it with the closing date of the IDA grant for Comoros FSRP and the overall FSRP Project. Project Status 2. The FSRP-3 (Phase 3) was approved by the World Bank Board on May 31, 2023, with a closing date of August 31, 2029, and covers Kenya, Malawi, Somalia, and Comoros, plus the African Union Commission. The total Phase 3 allocation is US$754.6 million. The Comoros FSRP totals US$43.0 million, comprising an IDA grant of SDR 29.8 million (US$40.0 million equivalent) and a US$3.0 million PROBLUE grant from the Global Program for the Blue Economy Multi-Donor Trust Fund; both became effective on September 5, 2023. 3. As of March 26, 2026, FSRP-3 has disbursed 38.8% of allocated funds, with US$471.03 million undisbursed. For Comoros FSRP, the disbursement rate is 46.3% and undisbursed funds total US$23.36 million. In view of forthcoming Mid- Term Review in 2026, implementation needs to accelerate and particularly disbursements. 4. Results Framework (RF) progress is being recorded. Three PDO indicators are making progress: PDO 2, related to the number of farmers adopting resilience enhancing technologies and practices, is 37 (3.7% of 2025 target) with 24% of farmers being women (80% of 2025 target); PDO 3, on land area under sustainable land management practices, is 125 ha (125% of the 2025 target); and PDO 5, on policy products adopted, is 1 (50% of the 2025 target). Similarly, Intermediate results (IR) are making progress: IR4, on decrease in yield gap between program beneficiary female and male farmers, is 42.6% (284% of the 2025 target); IR5, on the number of agricultural landscape and natural resource management plans developed with Project, is 2 plans developed (20% of the 2025 target), with 1 implemented (100% of the 2025 target); IR6, on land area restored, reforested or afforested, is 34.5 ha (10.6% of the 2025 target); IR7, on Water users’ associations and farmer or community organizations established or strengthened for agricultural water and landscape management, is 3 associations/organizations (150% of the 2025 target); IR8, on area provided with new/improved irrigation or drainage services, is 33.83 ha (677% of the 2025 target); IR9, on the number of marketing infrastructure constructed or rehabilitated is 15 (700% of the 2025 target); and IR13, on the number of direct project beneficiaries reached, is 28,133 (281.3% of the 2025 target ), including 9,795 women (244.9% of the 2025 target). 5. Implementation is progressing across components. Under Component 1, cassava cuttings, orange-fleshed sweet potato vines and banana in vitro plants have been distributed; nutrition sensitization, culinary demonstrations and trainings have been delivered; IITA has been hired; the Livestock Code has been adopted and the training for veterinary students launched. Under Component 2, two watershed management plans have been implemented and eight more under preparation; 34.5 ha have been reforested with 11,480 fruit/forest plants, cisterns/impluviums for irrigation rehabilitated, and implementation of the octopus management plan has began. Under Component 3, 15 km of feeder roads have been rehabilitated, with the proposed restructuring aiming at financing additional prioritized road works. Under Component 4, collateral management has been expanded, enabling financing and delivery of 118 tons of wheat (KMF 50 million) and ordinary rice (KMF 150 million), with inclusion of animal feed planned and a market study completed. Under Component 5 (CERC)— the Food and Nutrition Security Crises Preparedness Plan (FSCPP) has been prepared and validated, with implementation as the next step. Under sub-component 3.2, the Project originally allocated US$10.3 million to rehabilitate 50 km of feeder roads (based on 2018 prices and projected inflation), split between 15 km inherited from the Integrated Development and Competitiveness Project (PIDC) and 42.67 km of new roads. Updated technical studies (2023–2024) indicate higher costs due to input prices and revised design parameters (e.g., widening to 4 m, inclusion of subsidence areas, and lessons learned from PIDC). As part of the restructuring and reallocation, 31.83 km have been prioritized from the 42.67 km, bringing the total expected rehabilitation to 46.83 km (including the 15 km completed), with the remaining 10.84 km to be addressed later, potentially through additional financing. Technical studies are complete and ready for implementation for the additional 42.67 km, and the STEP request for bids launched last year is near completion. Contractors are ready for selection, but contract signing is contingent on completion of the restructuring. The supervision firm (mission de contrôle) has been recruited and will mobilize after works contracts are signed. The supervision firm also conducted the technical studies. Rationale 7. Given the authorities reaffirmed priority for feeder roads and the selected roads totaling 31.83 km, the Project requires restructuring to reallocate funds, as the initially allocated budget for feeder roads is no longer sufficient. The restructuring will not affect project scope and no activities will be dropped. The Results Framework (RF) will not require adjustments (see below). The proposed reallocation supports efficiency and is expected to strengthen contributions to the PDO 8. Rehabilitating feeder roads improves access to production areas, which can increase agricultural productivity by reducing post-harvest losses and improving the availability of inputs and agricultural advisory services. It also supports higher marketed volumes through time savings and reduced transport costs. Feeder roads are enabling infrastructure with multiplier effects that can improve several Intermediate Results in the RF without changing the theory of change. 9. The proposed restructuring will focus exclusively on IDA E1830-KM. The Government will reallocate resources from other categories to finance the most critical feeder roads and support selected activities under sub-component 4.1 (strategies, standards, regulations, and the institutional framework) and Component 6 (project operations). The increase for Component 6 reflects underestimated appraisal costs and will support priority functions, strengthen the institutional framework, and ensure effective project management under the revised financing plan. 10. After careful analysis and exchanges with the PIU and support from an external consultant in charge of Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E), the reallocation of funds will not affect the RF. The proposed reallocation of US$13.88 million has been carefully planned to ensure that it will not affect the achievement of the PDO indicators and IR indicators with the following key points considered: (i) budget optimization (without dropping activities); (ii) optimization of resource allocation (rebalancing of activities, with priority given to most impactful multiplier effect ones); (iii) strengthened M&E mechanism on all activities (allowing to take corrective action as necessary). Therefore, no changes will be required as activities facing reduced funding because of the restructuring will not be penalized in their contribution to the RF. 11. Environmental and social risk management aspects have also been reviewed. Except for two lots on the island of Anjouan where resettlement action plans are pending from the PIU (and no works will begin until these are finalized) the feeder roads covered by this restructuring do not present major environmental and social issues, and the necessary requirements have been met.
