PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Project Status 1. This paper seeks approval from the Regional Vice-President (RVP) for a restructuring of the National Social Safety Net Program Scale-Up Project (NASSP SU, P176935, Credit No. 7019-NG), an XDR 776,524,345 (US$800 million equivalent at approval) Investment Project Financing (IPF). The proposed restructuring includes two key changes: (i) an extension of the project closing date by 12 months from June 30, 2026, to June 30, 2027; and (ii) a reallocation of resources to adequately finance essential activities under Component 3 (Delivery system strengthening and project management). This restructuring also enables the government to use the IDA resources towards providing emergency support in response to the current conflict in the Middle East. This restructuring represents the third amendment and extension of the project’s closing date. Regional Vice-President approval is required as the proposed closing date extends the original by three years (36 months). The Borrower’s request for the restructuring was received on May 25, 2026. 2. NASSP-SU was approved on December 16, 2021, and became effective on January 30, 2023. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to expand coverage of shock-responsive safety net support among the poor and vulnerable and strengthen the national safety net delivery system. The project has two components1: (i) Component 1: Economic Shock Responsive Cash Transfer (ESR-CT) that provides temporary cash transfers to provide relief against economic shocks to eligible beneficiaries, identified using the Social Registry2; and (ii) Component 3: Support to Project Management and Social Protection system strengthening activities. 3. Overall progress towards the PDO is rated Satisfactory. The project has achieved its PDO targets and almost all of its intermediate results target. As of May 2026, the program has reached 45.1 million beneficiaries, with 10 million poor and vulnerable households receiving shock-responsive cash transfers under Component 1 (ESR-CT). Over 57 percent of the primary beneficiaries are women, a significant increase from the target of 45 percent. Likewise, all program transfers have been done through safe, secure, and scalable digital mechanisms, with 98.9 percent of beneficiaries receiving payments within 10 days of payment approval. In addition, the project has facilitated financial inclusion of over 5.9 million households by opening fully transactional digital accounts (against a target of 2.9 million), out of which 56.5 percent are women. Over 87 percent of the beneficiaries surveyed in the post-distribution monitoring survey reported that they are satisfied with the program. 4. The project implementation progress is rated Moderately Satisfactory. Despite the results achieved, implementation progress towards the government’s target has faced bottlenecks. Implementation was paused from January to March 2024 during the government’s review of the National Social Investment Programs, including the cash transfer program supported under NASSP SU. The multi-ministerial Presidential Panel on Social Investment Program (SIP Panel), currently also the steering committee of the project, recommended resuming the program, conditional on biometric verification of all beneficiaries using either the National Identification Number (NIN) or Bank Verification Number (BVN). Upon resumption, the project initiated various approaches to link individuals in the Social Registry to their NIN/BVN system. The major initiative includes a ‘NIN Drive’ to collect and validate existing NINs of individuals in the Social Registry and to enroll adults from households without at least one verified NIN to obtain one. As of May 2026, only 7.6 million households from the Social Registry have a validated NIN. While PDO targets have been met, the government intends to use part of the remaining available resources towards vertical and horizontal expansion of the cash transfers under Component 1.3 5. The fiduciary performance is rated Moderately Satisfactory. The Project Implementing Units (PIUs), the National Social Safety Nets Coordinating Office (NASSCO), and the National Cash Transfer Office (NCTO), have addressed previously identified FM issues related to delays in documentation, weak internal controls, unrecovered advances to payment service providers, and lingering undocumented expenditures. Due to marked improvement in performance, the Financial Management (FM) rating was upgraded from Moderately Unsatisfactory to Moderately Satisfactory during the last Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) of March 2026. With the imminent completion of pending documentation for Category (3) expenditures, and continuation of good FM practices, such as adequate internal controls over consumables, timely submission of good quality unaudited interim financial reports, adequate supporting documents for financial transactions and timely retirement of advances, the team expects the performance rating would be further upgraded to Satisfactory. There are no overdue audit reports, and there will be no change in the audit requirements. The PIUs have also made appreciable progress on procurement compliance and documentation. The latest Procurement Post-Review revealed substantial improvement in compliance in procurement by NASSCO and substantial completion of all outstanding documentations in Systematic Tracking of Exchanges in Procurement (STEP) by both NCTO and NASSCO. Due to significant progress, the Procurement rating was upgraded from Moderately Unsatisfactory to Moderately Satisfactory during the last ISR. No large procurement is envisioned for the remaining project duration, so with the proper completion of ongoing contracts, the team expects the procurement performance ratings to be upgraded to Satisfactory in the coming months. World Bank team will continue monitoring performance through regular follow-ups to ensure that performance does not lag. 6. The Environmental and Social (E&S) performance is Moderately Satisfactory, and the overall risk rating remains Substantial. This reflects the key potential risks that may arise during implementation, which include electronic waste generation from disused digital devices, occupational health and safety risks associated with field enumeration activities, road travel, and security risks, as well as community health and safety concerns. Mitigation measures for these risks have been clearly defined in the Environmental and Social Management Plan, Stakeholder Engagement Plan, Labor Management Procedures, and the Security Risk Assessment and Management Plan, and implementation of the instruments is ongoing, and reporting is ongoing, although not as regularly as required. To address reporting irregularity arising from some lagging states, the PIUs are proactively engaging with states to ensure timely consolidation of reports, complemented by follow-ups during weekly Bank team check-ins. The proposed change of extension of closing date from June 2026 to June 2027 and the reallocation of resources to enhance the NIN drive will have no material E&S implications. Thus, all prepared E&S instruments are still considered adequate to support the project implementation. The project currently maintains functional E&S organizational structures at the national, state, and Local Government Area (LGA) levels. Environmental, Gender/Social, and Grievance Redress Mechanism Officers are in place at NASSCO and NCTO, providing the required technical support as well. The team has also undergone multiple capacity-building activities, including training on the Environmental and Social Framework. All Environmental and Social Incident Response Toolkit cases and incidents recorded to date have been fully resolved and closed. The project’s Grievance Mechanism is operational, with the NASSP-SU Call Center upgraded and functional. As of January 2026, all backlog grievances had been resolved, and grievance handling timelines are being met in line with the Project Implementation Manual (PIM). Continued implementation of corrective actions and strengthened monitoring are expected to further improve E&S performance over time. B. Rationale for Restructuring 7. The project sits at the core of World Bank's dialogue with the government on macro-fiscal reforms and social protection. The necessary but painful mid-2023 reforms—fuel subsidy removal and exchange rate unification—while critical to long-term fiscal sustainability, exacerbated the already worsening poverty scenario and pushed millions more into poverty. The number of poor had risen from 80 million in 2018/19 to 127 million in 2023. Due to reformdriven increase in inflation, peaking at 31.7 percent in 2024, 63 percent of the population—over 149 million individuals—is estimated to be poor. The World Bank has supported the broader reform agenda through the Reforms for Economic Stabilization to Enable Transformation (RESET) Development Policy Financing, with this project supporting the provision of safety nets to cushion against the adverse impacts. Project restructuring in June 2024 aligned it precisely with the government's cash transfer program, which targets 15 million of the poorest households with ₦75,000 (US$ 54.61) per household provided over three monthly tranches.4 The government has committed to covering the necessary financing gap to reach its target. 8. The current conflict in the Middle East has likely worsened the poverty and vulnerability situation in the country, primarily through the energy–inflation channel. Higher oil prices are boosting export earnings, strengthening the current account, and providing temporary fiscal relief. However, gains are partly offset by precommitments and tighter global financial conditions, which are raising Eurobond yields and increasing the risk of portfolio outflows. Domestically, the shock is transmitting strongly through fuel prices, which have surged among the fastest globally, with petrol and diesel prices rising by 48.7 percent and 65.5 percent, respectively, within March 2026.5 This is fueling inflation, as energy-linked items (10.1 percent of the consumer price index) raise transport and food costs. These impacts are weighing on household welfare by pushing up food and transportation costs, particularly in the context of a steep rise in domestic fuel prices that directly feeds into food and transportation inflation. With 27 percent of Nigerians already unable to meet basic food needs, households are highly exposed to further price pressures. A 1 percentage point increase in inflation is estimated to raise poverty by 0.45 percentage points, pushing an additional 1.1 million people into extreme poverty (US$3 per day).6 The poor and vulnerable in Nigeria need additional support, and the government is keen to use available IDA resources towards providing immediate support. 9. The project underwent key changes during implementation, most notably the integration of biometric identification, which needed the delivery systems and field processes to be adapted. The March 2024 introduction of biometric identification as a requirement for beneficiaries necessitated that the project adapt to the newer model of implementation than originally envisioned. First, NINs and BVNs already in the Social Registry were verified and validated before making cash transfers. But only a small share of households had reported NINs or BVNs, and many among them could not be credibly verified, which led to a sluggish pace of cash transfer rollout. To address the issue of low coverage, NASSCO, in partnership with the National Identity Management Commission (NIMC), launched a NIN Drive to collect and verify NINs from households in the Social Registry (NIN harvesting). Nonetheless, many households in the Social Registry still lack an adult with a verified and validated NIN. Adults in these households will have to be enrolled to obtain a NIN (NIN enrollment) and their NINs be integrated into the Social Registry for these households Exchange rate of US$1=1,373.25 as per the Central Bank of Nigeria for May 29, 2026. A partnership with NIMC-licensed private sector agencies operating as front-end-partners (FEPs) for NIN enrollment has been initiated, with field deployment preparations underway. 10. The NIN Drive had a promising start, but the pace has slowed down. The number of households in the Social Registry visited each month to collect and verify NIN has slowed down from over 2 million in the first months to less than 0.2 million since January 2026 (see figure 1, Annex I). The implementation and supervision mission of January 2026 and the multiple subsequent discussions with the government have identified a few key reasons for the reduced pace. Major reasons include: (i) delayed payments to the states for the households visited under the NIN Drive; (ii) lack of updated information on households yet to be visited; (iii) more effort needed to visit remote communities and harder-to-find households; and (iv) structural incentives for private FEPs to not fully engage in remote locations outside city centers. 11. The government has taken corrective actions to reignite the pace, but it will take time for those actions to translate to results. By April 30, 2026, all the states had been paid for the outputs produced. Detailed data on the households to be visited—disaggregated by LGA, Wards, and communities—have been shared with states and local teams. Communication and outreach prior to community visits have been strengthened, and field protocols have been updated to organize various stakeholders. Leveraging partnerships with NIMC, NASSCO has obtained and provided 1,000 NIN-enrollment devices to State Operations Coordination Units (SOCUs) with at least one device for each of the 774 LGAs. Over 4,000 agents, typically SOCU staff and seasoned data collection enumerators, have been trained to operate these devices. The process of testing and certifying these agents is ongoing. The in-house capacity will complement private sector FEPs, especially in areas that private FEPs may not want to go. The government has proposed additional financial incentives for the private sector FEPs for their efforts in linking the individuals being enrolled to receive a NIN with the Social Registry. The government has also proposed performance-based top-ups to the output-based-disbursement (OBD) amount received by the states for achieving clearly defined output milestones of households visited under the NIN Drive. In addition, state-level targets have been set to complete remaining NIN integration by December 2026 (table 1, Annex I). The government is preparing detailed action plans at the LGA level to achieve these targets. Data collected is also being monitored by the World Bank and the government in real time to gauge progress, with an uptick in performance already visible since mid-May 2026 (figure 1, Annex I). 12. To complement the in-person NIN Drive exercise, the government is exploring alternative technology-enabled ways to quickly harvest existing NINs. This includes: (i) extracting and verifying NINs associated with the phone numbers in the Social Registry; (ii) developing a mobile-based platform (e.g., USSD) where individuals in the Social Registry can securely provide their NINs; and (iii) developing automated or manual calls to the individuals in the Social Registry to provide their NINs. NINs extracted from these methods will then be validated and verified with NIMC. All of these options will be piloted simultaneously and rapidly scaled up. 13. The Social Registry system strengthening to develop a fully-fledged, automated, and integrated Social Registry also needs more time. To accommodate the decision of the government to integrate valid and verified NIN into the Social Registry through the NIN Drive, the data collection and processing system had to be quickly adapted. The adaptation and customization were done in-house through the World Bank technical assistance, with efficiency and project results delivery in mind. These adaptations, especially the linkages with NIMC for NIN integration and related data collection and processing, need to be further developed as a part of an integrated and fully automated Social Registry system so that they can be used by programs other than the project. Essential elements to be developed include: interoperability with the NIMC database for identity verification and management; automated day-to-day back-end operations; updated surveyor module for expansion and/or updating household information; user interfaces for various entities at federal and state levels; appropriate cybersecurity and data safeguard measures; and data sharing and exchange mechanisms between various stakeholders and users. The government, with World Bank Page 4 The World Bank National Social Safety Net Program-Scale Up (P176935) support, has already started putting together a Terms of Reference to procure a firm that will develop the system. A detailed assessment of the system, conducted by the World Bank and the government, will inform necessary features and improvements. This essential system strengthening and modernization can take up to 12 months to create a Social Registry system that can be used by other social programs within and outside the government as a credible and reliable targeting platform. 14. The Federal Ministry of Finance formally requested for an extension and restructuring of the NASSP-SU Project in a letter dated May 22, 2026. The letter specifically requests: (i) an extension to the closing date of the project by 12 months to provide more additional time for activities under NIN Drive and Social Registry modernization to be fully implemented and achieve government’s target; (ii) the reallocation of project resources to re-escalate the pace of the NIN Drive and for Social Registry modernization; and (iii) utilize the IDA resources, together with government cofinancing, towards an emergency shock-responsive support program for vulnerable Nigerians affected by the current conflict in the Middle East. The request follows extensive discussions during the January 2026 implementation and supervision mission and subsequent consultations and deliberations with the PIUs, the Federal Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Poverty Reduction, the SIP Panel Secretariat, and representatives of the Federal Ministry of Finance. The proposed restructuring accommodates the government’s request and updates project activities and the results framework accordingly. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 15. Extension of the closing date. The NASSP-SU Project’s closing date will be extended from June 30, 2026, to June 30, 2027. This extension is necessary for the NIN Drive to escalate so that the coverage of the shock-responsive cash transfers reaches the government's target of 15 million beneficiary households. The extension also allows for modernization of the safety net delivery systems, particularly the Social Registry, for current and future social programs. The proposed closing date extension meets the requirements of the World Bank Investment Project Financing (IPF) Directive regarding extension of closing dates, specifically: (i) the project objectives remains achievable; (ii) the performance of the Borrower remains satisfactory; (iii) the World Bank and the Borrower agree on actions that will be undertaken by the Borrower to complete project critical activities (see Annex I); and (iv) there are no outstanding audits and/or interim financial reports. 16. Emergency support in response to the current conflict in the Middle East. As per the government request, it can use available unspent resources under Component 1 (expenditure Category 1)—currently around US$175 million— towards providing immediate one-time emergency shock-responsive support to poor and vulnerable households from the Social Registry with a verified and validated NIN. The request is consistent with the objective of Component 1 and will be implemented through an amendment to the PIM in response to the government’s request. The eligibility and coverage of this support program and the exact transfer amount will be determined following subsequent discussions with the government and will be based on the available IDA resources along with complementary government cofinancing. 17. Introducing performance-based top-ups to accelerate the NIN Drive. The PIM will be revised to allow states to receive performance-based top-ups to the OBD financing of the NIN Drive activities. States will be eligible to receive a top-up if they can accelerate the coverage of NIN Drive towards a clearly defined and ambitious target within a set period. Only households covered within the period in which they meet their targets will be eligible for top-ups. The progress made in the NIN Drive will be closely supervised and monitored by the Federal Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Poverty Reduction and the World Bank. An indicator specific to the coverage of the NIN Drive has also been added to the results framework. Page 5 The World Bank National Social Safety Net Program-Scale Up (P176935) 18. Social Registry strengthening and modernization. The Social Registry will be strengthened and modernized to serve its function as a targeting platform for social programs beyond the current program. Key features will include end-to-end automation of all processes, seamless verification and exchange with NIMC on NIN integration to the Social Registry, data access and sharing mechanisms between federal and state levels, and safe and secure data sharing mechanisms with various programs and stakeholders. An indicator has been added to the results framework to capture the development of such a system. Likewise, the digital payment system will also undergo similar modernization to ensure end-to-end automation of processes with appropriate data safeguards in place. 19. Reallocation of project resources. Financing will be reallocated to further support the NIN Drive and system modernization as described above. Specific amounts of reallocations are detailed below: (a) Category (2) will be increased to XDR 16,551,050 (US$22.62 million equivalent) to provide additional resources for the NIN Drive, including the expected cost of performance-based top-ups. (b) Category (4) will be increased to XDR 19,478,390 (US$26.62 million equivalent) to provide additional resources for Social Registry systems modernization, cost of implementing technology-enabled harvesting of NINs, and increased operational costs. (c) Category (5) will be increased to XDR 17,128,470 (US$23.4 million equivalent) to provide additional resources for payment system modernization and increased operating costs. (d) The resource allocation under Category (1) will, consequently, be XDR 509,933,870 (US$696.79 million equivalent). 20. Updating the results framework. The restructuring will update the results framework to reflect the revised project closing date. Action Plan 1. There are three key activities to be completed by the end of June 2027: (a) Emergency support in response to conflict in the Middle East. (i) Finalize total resource outlay (including government co-financing), benefit size, benefit coverage together with the government: July 10, 2026. (ii) Prepare beneficiary payment lists and execute payments: July 31, 2026. (b) Complete NIN integration to the Social Registry: Currently, 9.5 million households in the Social Registry remain to be visited to harvest adult members’ NINs and enroll those who don’t have one to receive one. The government has agreed to the targets, as listed in Table (1), to complete the NIN integration by the end of December 2026. The targets will be met through a combination of in-person visits, and technology-enabled solutions to accelerate the pace of NIN harvesting. The World Bank team will continue to provide intensive technical assistance to across all avenues of the NIN integration Drive. Progress towards these targets will be monitored through: (i) Weekly progress review and update meeting with the World Bank task team. (ii) Fortnightly progress review and update meeting with Federal Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Poverty Reduction. (c) Social Registry modernization. Social Registry modernization is expected to be completed by the time of project closing in June 2027. The World Bank team will monitor implementation progress through regular check-ins.
