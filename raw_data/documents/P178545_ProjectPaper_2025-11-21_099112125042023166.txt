PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Background 1. The Community Livelihood Enhancement and Resilience Project (CLEAR, P178545) was approved on June 15, 2023, and became effective in October 2023. As of October 31, 2025, the project has disbursed USD 18.04 million, or 39.6 percent of the Project’s total estimated IDA financing of USD 45.84 million. Project implementation is progressing steadily as described in the last Implementation Status and Results (ISR) report.1 Component 2 on Community Livelihoods Enhancement and Component 3 on Community Nutrition Interventions are fully on track with high levels of community participation, ownership, and inclusion. More than 850 livelihood Self-Help Groups of about 12,000 members have been formed-- of which over 90 percent are women and from non- Lao-Tai ethnic groups—and are receiving seed grants to implement income generating activities. More than 800 model farmers (of which 83 percent are women) have been trained to share good practices with peer villagers on nutritious home-garden food preparation. Construction of small-scale climate resilient infrastructure subprojects under Component 1 has been somewhat delayed, however, Village Development Planning has been completed in all 230 Batch 1 villages, 304 subprojects have been selected and approved, and site surveys and designs are nearing completion. 2. The proposed restructuring seeks to enhance the impact of the CLEAR Project through the provision of Joint Co- Financing of USD 8,110,295 from the Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation (SDC).2 Specifically, the restructuring will increase overall project financing to an estimated USD 53.95 million to enhance resilience of vulnerable communities to economic and climate shocks, reduce poverty in rural and remote areas, reduce stunting, and enhance food and nutrition security. The restructuring will also extend the project closing date by one year and adjust the project’s implementation and disbursement schedules. 3. As a result of a series of assessments and consultations, SDC has found that CLEAR and its predecessor project (the Poverty Reduction Fund) have provided effective and critical support to targeted rural communities in Lao PDR. These reviews showed the projects achieved tangible impacts on the ground in terms of socio-economic infrastructure as well as improvements in livelihoods and nutritional status of target communities. They have also established high- quality fiduciary and environmental and social risk management systems. SDC therefore determined that CLEAR offers an ideal platform for the Swiss Government to help the Government of Lao PDR to enhance climate and disaster resilience of targeted rural communities. The provision of these additional resources by the SDC are timely as Lao PDR faces macroeconomic difficulties and mobilization of necessary funding to continue addressing persistent high levels of poverty, very limited livelihoods and job opportunities for rural populations especially among remote ethnic communities and women, and costly impacts of climate change. This proposed additional financing is also well aligned with the Rural Livelihood Resilience and Poverty Alleviation Program (RLRPAP) for 2026-2030 for the Poverty Reduction Fund, approved by the Minister for Agriculture and Environment on September 1, 2025.3 Experience gained 1 P178545 ISR of October 10, 2025: Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) - Community Livelihood Enhancement And Resilience - P178545 - Sequence No 5 2 SDC indicated its intent to jointly finance the CLEAR project in a letter to the Minister of Agriculture and Environment in July 2025, and the Joint Financing agreement between SDC and the Government of Lao PDR has been negotiated and is expected to be finalized in early November 2025. 3 The RLRPAP aims to intensify and accelerate the execution of its rural development program to address the emerging development needs of the most remote and vulnerable communities and the rural poor in Lao PDR. It also seeks, by providing technical assistance to the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment (MAE)’s line agencies and other relevant agencies of the Government of Lao PDR, to enhance the quality of life, food and nutrition security, climate resilience, and agricultural modernization in areas affected by frequent and intense climate change events. This restructuring paper includes a draft Amendment to the CLEAR Financing Agreement (FA), the amended Disbursement and Financial Information Letter (DFIL), an updated Environmental and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP), and the Government’s request letter for the additional Joint Financing and project restructuring. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 5. The main purpose of this first restructuring is to accommodate Joint Financing from the SDC of USD 8,110,295 to Lao PDR for the CLEAR Project. The SDC’s financing, through a grant provided directly to the Government, will help to further promote climate and disaster resilience of target communities. Specifically, this additional funding will support climate resilient community infrastructure (under Component 1) and livelihoods enhancement activities (under Component 2) in approximately 80 villages of CLEAR’s existing target villages, focusing on areas that are most vulnerable to the impacts of climate change.4 This Joint Financing would also support necessary management and operational costs (under Component 4). The specific changes to the project components proposed under this restructuring are described immediately below. 6. Component 1: Local Economic Development and Community Capacity Strengthening finances initial community mobilization and preparatory activities (Village Profiles and Village Development Planning (VDP)) and finances prioritized small-scale, climate resilient infrastructure (CRI). Building on existing CRI investments, the proposed SDC financing will support additional technical assistance, capacity building and delivery of additional small CRIs in target villages. Specifically, enhanced VDP processes supported by SDC financing will integrate land-use and geo-spatial data from the perspective of climate and disaster risks (such as landslides, flooding, drought, extreme temperatures) to more explicitly incorporate climate- and disaster-resilient land use planning to inform the selection, design, and location of CRI subprojects. A ‘landscape approach’ will also be applied to identify clusters of villages that are most vulnerable to the effects of climate change. Climate and disaster risk data and maps will be updated and adapted by the SDC’s regional thematic hub in Bangkok, which will then be customized to villages and presented in an accessible way for field staff and communities under the project. 7. SDC financing under Component 1 will also support financing of additional small-scale CRIs in the target villages, with emphasis on bioengineering, water resources management, and climate-smart productive assets to strengthen the CLEAR’s IDA-financed small CRIs and beyond.5 In accordance with the landscape approach, some of the infrastructure investments may benefit more than one village. CLEAR will engage two national-level technical specialists as well as four qualified district-level technical officers, to work closely with CLEAR’s existing engineering teams at national and district levels to support the design and implementation of additional small CRIs. Any small- scale CRI investments under the component will comply with the existing Environment and Social Framework (ESF) approach of the CLEAR, including Environmental and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP) and Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMP), and relevant sections of the Project Implementation Manual (PIM), that includes a 4 Preliminary areas have been identified based on recently available climate and disaster risks data for Lao PDR, supported by the SDC’s regional thematic hub in Bangkok, and agreed by the CLEAR project management team. Component 2: Community Livelihoods Enhancement provides capacity building as well as seed grants to target communities, through Self-Help Groups and Producer Groups, to enhance their livelihoods and income generating activities. The SDC funding will provide additional training and technical assistance in support of Component 2. This will be done by contracting qualified organization(s) to engage communities to plan for community-based and ecosystem-based adaptation in livelihoods activities and providing small block grants in support of such activities.6 Extension materials and training will be provided on themes such as weather forecasts, improved post-harvest storage, and sustainable use of Non-Timber Forest Products. As part of this group of activities and capacity building, hermetic bags will also be provided to households of the 220 Batch 2 villages to better preserve the quality and safety of dry grains. The provision of extension materials, training and pilot demonstrations related to sustainable water management will be carried out together with a specialized agency. Finally, seasonal review and planning will be organized twice a year in selected priority districts that face severe climate conditions and disaster risks. These meetings will serve to share information from the previous cropping season and plans for the next season. Topics will include weather, markets, road conditions, supplies of inputs, pests, technical problems, and new relevant government policies. 9. Component 3: Community Nutrition Interventions provides support for improvements in the dietary intake (both in quality and quantity) of mothers and children in the 1000-day window through the promotion of innovative nutrition practice. While the proposed SDC financing will not directly support this component, , it is expected that there will be spillover benefits to the activities under Component 3 through enhanced climate-smart and sustainable water management (such as small-scale irrigation and household use supported under Component 1 and 2. For example, home gardening and other nutrition-improvement related activities by communities, where hygiene and appropriate water management is key, can benefit from concrete measures under Component 1, such as improved water harvesting, shade housing and drip irrigation. Activities supported under Component 2, including the preparation of relevant extension materials, training for Young Graduates, and demonstration activities are also expected to benefit nutrition groups under Component 3. 10. Component 4: Project Management, Capacity Building and Monitoring and Evaluation finances incremental operating costs, staff costs, external audits and other related project management, social and environmental risk management, monitoring, evaluation and technical oversight costs. Incremental operating cost under the SDC funding will include the costs for the additional year of operations due to the extension of the Project Closing Date, audit- related costs, and additional staff cost to support activities under Component 1 and 2, both at the national and the district levels. SDC funding will also support recruitment and operating costs of two National Technical Officers, six District Technical Officers, and 20 Climate Resilience Young Graduates (CYGs), who will play a key role in enhancing the project’s capacity to deliver climate resilience interventions. These personnel will be fully embedded within the existing project structure to ensure seamless coordination and effective implementation. Annex 2 contains the organization chart of the CLEAR project that shows the existing staff (in blue boxes) and the additional personnel to be recruited under SDC funding (in red boxes). 11. The project’s original Results Framework (RF), including indicators, baselines, and targets will remain unchanged. The SDC-financed activities operate within a subset of CLEAR’s original target villages and contribute to the same PDO and therefore do not warrant modification of project-wide indicators. Several existing RF indicators such as the percentage of infrastructure audited as climate resilient and the number of producers adopting improved or climate-smart practices—are already well aligned with the results expected from SDC support. Progress on SDC-specific activities will be captured through the project’s established M&E arrangements, including the management information system, which already collects village-level and subproject-level data. This allows the team to disaggregate and report SDC-specific results without altering the RF. In addition, the M&E plan will be updated to clarify where SDC results map onto existing RF indicators. For example, CLEAR’s PDO indicator on total project beneficiaries will also be used to report the SDC indicator “number of persons benefiting from specific climate change adaptation and natural resource management measures,” ensuring consistency in methodology while avoiding duplication or double counting. 14. Financial Management. There will be no change in the financial management (FM) practices of the project. The funding from the SDC will be managed by the same FM team of CLEAR, following the Project’s FM manual, which will be updated to reflect specific disbursement requirements for the SDC funding. A second Designated Account (DA-B) in USD for the SDC funding will be opened at the Bank of Laos to receive the funds. The same arrangements for the existing Designated Account (DA-A) will be applied to DA-B, including: (a) a variable ceiling equivalent to a six-month cash forecast; (b) supporting documentation required for eligible expenditures paid from the DA will be the Statement of Expenditure; (c) the frequency of reporting expenditures paid from the DA shall be quarterly; and (d) the minimum application size for reimbursements, special commitments, and direct payments will be equivalent to US$200,000. 7These component cost estimates have been adjusted to reflect the appreciation of the SDR in relation to the USD since project approval. Procurement. World Bank procurement rules and systems — including the mandatory use of the Systematic Tracking of Exchanges in Procurement system — will apply to activities financed by SDC funding. Procurement will also be conducted according to the PIM, and the World Bank procurement team may review procurement and selection processes for all activities in line with the World Bank Procurement Regulations. The CLEAR Project Management Team will share bidding documents with both the World Bank and SDC for review and approval, as needed. 16. Environmental and Social Framework. The project’s Environmental and Social Risk classification remains Moderate, considering that the activities financed by the SDC will be of the same type and scale as those funded under the original project, which are selected by communities using the existing sub-project selection methodology as outlined in the PIM. The project’s ESMF and PIM, including a negative list and screening steps for subprojects, will be updated to ensure relevant risk mitigation measures are applied. The ESCP includes a relevant action to update the ESMF. 17. Risks. The overall residual risk rating of the project will continue to be Moderate. While fiduciary risks were considered Substantial at the time of the appraisal of CLEAR, fiduciary performance to date has been consistently Satisfactory during project implementation (Satisfactory for FM and Highly Satisfactory for Procurement). With SDC financing being integrated into the existing FM and procurement systems, and updates to the PIM and FM manual being made to accommodate the specific disbursement requirements under the SDC financing, this risk is considered Moderate. Risks associated with technical design and implementation capacity are also Moderate – the SDC technical team has been working closely with the CLEAR technical team to prepare Terms of Reference and updates for the PIM to kick start activities upon approval and effectiveness of the SDC financing. Additional project staff to be recruited at the central and district levels, as explained, will ensure supplemental capacity of the project technical team to deliver the proposed activities funded by the co-financing. While Macroeconomic risks remain Substantial, the fact that the SDC financing will be a grant (and therefore have a neutral impact on Lao PDR’s external debt difficulties) and that the same mitigating conditions as under the original CLEAR project continue to apply suggest that risks related to project implementation and the achievement of project objectives will not increase. 18. Extension of Project Closing Date. To ensure sufficient implementation time and effective management of the additional funding and related activities, the Project’s closing date will be extended from December 31, 2027, to December 29, 2028.8 The Project’s implementation schedule and disbursement projections will also be revised accordingly. 8 This closing date is proposed as December 31st falls on a weekend.
