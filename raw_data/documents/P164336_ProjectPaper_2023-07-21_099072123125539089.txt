No The World Bank Lowlands Livelihood Resilience Project (P164336) I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the Country Director’s approval for a level 2 restructuring of the Lowlands Livelihood Resilience Project (LLRP) to conduct the necessary adjustments following the cancellation in May 2020 of US$125 million to cope with the macro-economic impacts of COVID-19 pandemic in the country. The Project, approved on May 29, 2019, committed an initial allocation of US$350 million from IDA and a co-financing of US$90 million from the International Fund for Agriculture Development (IFAD). The latter was approved on May 20, 2020. The Project is currently expected to close on October 10, 2025. 2. LLRP has been under implementation since November 2019 in seven lowland and agro-pastoral areas. To date, it has benefited more than 2 million pastoralists and agro-pastoralists (PAP) (82 percent of the end-of-project target), of which 47 percent and 15 percent are female and youth beneficiaries respectively. The Project has, among others: a) successfully introduced the use of rangelands as entry point for development investments, identified 28 rangeland clusters and prepared comprehensive Rangeland Management and Investment Plans (RMIPs); b) created access to key natural resources through implementing rangeland improvement and sustainable land management practices on 248,179 hectares of land, thus improved the livelihood base of the PAPs and addressed the root cause of resource-related conflicts; c) increased production and productivity of selected livestock and crop commodities by 14 percent through strengthening public extension service delivery capacities and promoting improved technologies and practices among 86,147 PAPs; d) timely responded to an emergency drought crisis and provided emergency services to respond to the most vulnerable drought-affected populations with water supply, critical veterinary drugs, feed and forage worth US$24.9 million; e) improved access to basic social and economic services among the most drought prone, conflict-affected and marginalized communities through constructing and operationalizing 654 community identified subprojects and 81 strategic Public Economic Infrastructures; and f) improved access to rural financial services and created diversified livelihood opportunities for 41,918 poor PAP households through establishing and supporting 482 rural saving and credit cooperatives, thus enhancing their livelihoods and resilience to the impacts of climate change. 3. The Project has adopted the "Geo-Enabled Monitoring and Supervision System " tool, which enables monitoring and supervision of project activities in conflict areas and during COVID-19- related access and travel restrictions. However, the Project encountered multiple challenges over the past three years, including COVID-19 pandemic, drought, flood, dessert locust, the spillover effect of the armed conflict in Tigray and localized conflicts in Benishangul and Oromia regions, high inflation, and shortage of construction materials and inputs such as cement reinforcement bar and fuel. Nevertheless, good implementation progress has been made across most component activities. The midterm assessment conducted in January 2023 and concluded that the PDO and Implementation Progress has been “Moderately Satisfactory”. There are no outstanding issues related to procurement and all safeguard instruments have been cleared by the World Bank and are in place. The Grievance Redress Mechanism is operational. Grievance Redness Redressal Committees have been established in 98 of the 100 project woredas and are well-functioning. 4. The institutional arrangement has been changed, with the Project being moved from the Ministry of Peace (MoP) to the Ministry of Irrigation and Lowlands (MILLs). A letter received from the Ministry of Finance (MoF) on November 22, 2021, reference number IFRE 4/2/79, clearly indicated the new hosting ministry. Following this change, Financial Management assessment was conducted at Ministry of Irrigation and Lowlands (MILLs) in The World Bank Lowlands Livelihood Resilience Project (P164336) accordance with the Financial Management Manual for World Bank Investment Project Financing Operations issued on February 10, 2017 and supporting guidance note (February 28, 2017). The objective of the financial management assessment was to determine whether the new Ministry, MILLs have acceptable financial management arrangements which are capable of recording correctly all budgets, transactions, and balances, supporting the preparation of regular and reliable financial statements, safeguarding the entity’s assets, and are subject to auditing arrangements acceptable to the Bank. The arrangements include the entity’s system of planning and budgeting, accounting, internal controls, funds flow, financial reporting, and auditing. The conclusion of the assessment was that the project’s financial management arrangements meet the Bank's minimum requirements under Bank Policy and Bank Directive on IPF and FM Manual. 5. Likewise, The Bank team carried out procurement capacity assessment to get in-depth understanding of the procurement structure/function, policy framework/regulatory, oversight and integrity aspect, procurement process including contract management. The assessment confirmed that the ministry has the required procurement capacity to implement the project. The Environmental and Social Risk assessment also indicated that any environmental immediate risk is not anticipated which need to be considered as a legal covenant in the restructuring. The Ministry shall maintain the environmental and social risk management which is already established at federal level. Furthermore, notable progress has been made in establishing a project coordination office for the newly created Southwest Ethiopia’s People Regional State, with relevant staff recruited to steer the implementation of project activities within the region. On the other hand, implementation has been affected by insecurity and conflict in LLRP Woredas in Afar, Benishangul-Gumuz, and Oromia Regions. In Benishangul, 3 new woredas that are less prone to conflict site - Menge, Oundulu and URa were replaced. For both newly establish southwest region and the 3 new woredas in Benishangul Financial Management assessment, Procurement and E&S assessment was conducted. The Ministry assigned focal persons who can provide fiduciary and environmental and social risk management oversight at the operational. Following the change in the regional- woreda arrangements under the Project in 2022, the risk ratings were not changed. The project governance structure of federal and regional steering committees, regional technical working group and staff are maintained. no new staff is hired. The MILLs and MoP have signed MoUs during transition so as to ensure smooth project implementation. This MoU gives direction on activities that are commenced by the previous ministry to be completed by MoP. Following the change, the PIM is not updated but a deadline is set for July 31, 2023. 6. In May 2020, upon the request of the Government of Ethiopia (GoE), US$125 million (36 percent of the IDA funding) was cancelled from the original IDA allocations to help the country cope with the macro-economic impacts of COVID-19 pandemic in the country. The intent at the time was to replenish the cancelled amount through an additional financing by FY24 or earlier. Decision had therefore been made at the time not to make changes to the project design, budgets, or result framework as part of the cancellation. However, with the project being under the former safeguard regime, a replenishment of the cancelled amount was not possible. Given the budget gap, the possibility of fully achieving the original PDO and intermediate level result indicators with only 71.4 percent of the original allocation has become unrealistic. The GoE and the World Bank agreed to restructure the Project to reflect the previously cancelled amounts to the Results Framework and adjust the cost of each component. This paper therefore proposes that the Project be restructured by modifying (i) allocation amount per component budgets and (ii) Results Framework, in particular targets for PDO and intermediary indicators. 7. The Project is in compliance with all applicable World Bank policies. Quarterly Interim Financial Reports (IFRs) and audited financial statement are submitted on time. There are no outstanding issues related to procurement, and all safeguard instruments are in place and cleared by the World Bank. Furthermore, the Project is complying with all its Legal Covenants. The World Bank Lowlands Livelihood Resilience Project (P164336) II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES Changes to Component Costs 7. Component costs have been reviewed taking into consideration (i) the implementation of the project so far and what can be realistically achieved by the Project’s Closing Date, (ii) the total availability of funding and (iii) priority activities identified by the respective regions (particularly the strategic sub projects). The new component costs also reflect a reduction in the beneficiaries’ contribution given the COVID-19 pandemic and various natural disasters which have affected project beneficiaries.  Costs for component 1 is being reduced by 27.2 percent to reflect a decrease in the number of hectares which is planned to manage 700,000 ha of land, but eventually reduced to 400, 000 ha.  Cost for component 2 is being reduced by 54.5 percent and will cut the number of beneficiaries reached with agricultural assets or services and adopting improved technology  Cost for component 3 is being reduced by 10.3 percent, which corresponds to a decrease in beneficiary contribution. Indeed, despite the collection of community contributions which have been deposited into bank accounts being over 98 percent of target, the hardship from the COVID- 19 pandemic and various natural disasters has led the Project to decrease future contribution requirements from US$11 million to US$7.6 million. The contribution from Benishangul Gumuz and South Wester Ethiopia People regions fall behind a bit; hence, should expedite the mobilization of community contributions.  The only budget that is not being reduced is Program Management, to respond to high operational costs and current inflation.
