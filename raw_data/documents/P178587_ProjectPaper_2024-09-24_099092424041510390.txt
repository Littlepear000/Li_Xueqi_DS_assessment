PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Introduction 1. This restructuring paper seeks the approval of the Country Director for a proposed Level II restructuring of the US$29 million Strengthening Lebanon’s COVID-19 Response Project (SLCRP) at the request of the Government of Lebanon (GoL) through an official letter from the Ministry of Finance (MoF) dated June 26, 2024. The proposed first restructuring consists of: (a) reallocating funds across components and disbursement categories to support activities under component 2; (b) extending the Project closing date by 15 months, from September 30, 2024, to December 31, 2025; and (c) revising the results framework (RF). B. Project Background 2. The SLCRP (P178587) was approved by the World Bank on May 15, 2022, and became effective on December 20, 2022. The current closing date is September 30, 2024. The Project is financed by a loan consisting of a US$22.95 million non-concessional portion from the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD), a US$2.05 million concessional portion from the Global Concessional Financing Facility (GCFF), and a US$4 million grant from the Global Public Goods (GPG) Solutions. The Project was extended to the Lebanese Republic under the COVID-19 Strategic Preparedness and Response Program (SPRP) using the Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA) that was approved by the World Bank’s Board of Executive Directors on April 2, 2020 (PCBASIC0219761) with an overall Program financing envelope of up to US$6.00 billion, and the Additional Financing (AF) to the SPRP approved on October 13, 2020. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to prevent, detect and respond to the threat posed by COVID-19 and strengthen the national system for public health preparedness in Lebanon. The Project design comprises of the following components: (a) Component 1: Procurement of COVID-19 Vaccines and supplies; (b) Component 2: COVID-19 Prevention, Detection and Case Management; and (c) Component 3: System Strengthening, Monitoring and Management. 3. The Project’s progress towards achieving the PDO and the overall implementation progress were rated as Moderately Satisfactory in the last Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR). This rating reflects the successful submission of the Ministry of Public Health (MoPH) advance withdrawal application and the project’s Procurement Plan, which are the initial steps required to launch the implementation of project activities. The first disbursement from the Project amounts to US$5.41 million (18.65 percent of total financing) and was processed in November 2023. The delay in disbursement from the Project was caused by the delay in opening the Designated Accounts at the Central Bank of Lebanon. The reasons behind this were (i) the delay in issuing the decree pertaining to the loan by the Council of Ministers (this was resolved in February 2023); and (ii) delay in finalizing the authorized signatories’ letter (ASL). The Project Operations Manual (POM), which is a legal covenant, has been prepared by the Project Management Unit (PMU) and cleared by the World Bank on January 4, 2023. 4. The initiation of project activities has been delayed as a result of changing circumstances, including the global decline in COVID-19 vaccine demand, and national challenges related to the limited MoPH resources and staffing shortages. The delays in project implementation can be attributed to the following reasons; (i) Low demand for COVID-19 vaccines. The demand for COVID-19 vaccines in the country, as is the case worldwide, has significantly decreased. Lebanon also received COVID-19 vaccines as donations from several countries. Accordingly, the Ministry of Public Health (MoPH) decided not to purchase additional doses of COVID-19 vaccines. It is worth noting that the MoPH cancelled the procurement of COVID-19 vaccines (for a Page 1 The World Bank Strengthening Lebanon’s Covid-19 Response (P178587) value of US$5 million) for the World Bank-financed Lebanese Health Resilience Project (LHRP, P163476) for the same reasons. (ii) Increase in MoPH budget allocated for hospitalization. As per the national budget for 2024, the MoPH’s budget allocated for hospitalization increased. Accordingly, MoPH will not be using the Project’s funds for financing hospitalization claims for COVID-19 and related health conditions. (iii) Limited capacity at MoPH. Strengthening the technical capacity of public hospitals in the country through the procurement of equipment (under part 2.2 of the Project) has been a priority for the MoPH during the last period. However, this activity was delayed due to the limited capacity at MoPH (due to the absenteeism of civil servants as a result of the financial crisis) and the PMU (due to vacancies in several positions that have been filled as of December 2023). C. Country Context 5. Lebanon has faced multiple crises over the past four years and the economy has collapsed with devastating consequences for Lebanese households. The country has been besieged by multiple emergencies, beginning with a stark economic decline in 2019, compounded by the global COVID 19 pandemic, and a catastrophic Beirut port explosion in 2020 causing up to US$4.6 billion in damages1. Simultaneously, Lebanon is strained by a protracted humanitarian situation, with one of the highest refugee per capita ratios in the world due to the unprecedented influx of displaced Syrians since the onset of the conflict in Syria in 2011, which has strained the already overwhelmed public services. The country also hosts 492,946 Palestinian refugees (as of June 2024)2. In addition, there are growing concerns over the spillover effects of the recent Middle East conflict. The Lebanese economy has plummeted, shrinking by 37.3 percent from 2018 to 20223, resulting in a downgrade to lower-middle income status by the World Bank. Hyperinflation has ravaged the country, fueled by currency collapse and global price hikes, leading to dire shortages across all sectors4. World Bank estimations using the Lebanon Household survey indicate that, between 2012 and 2022, the share of individuals in Lebanon living under the poverty line more than tripled from 12 percent to 44 percent5. D. Sectoral and Institutional Context 6. The health system in Lebanon is based on a curative, hospital-centric and private service delivery model. The compounded country crisis has brought the system to the brink of collapse. Before 2020, the total health expenditure in Lebanon was higher than the average in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region (8.35 percent of Gross Domestic Product [GDP] as of 2019). Approximately, 55 percent of the health expenses were borne by households (33 percent out- of-pocket [OOP] and 22 percent for private insurance), whereas the GoL contributed 29 percent and private sector (also including households)13 percent. The health sector is skewed towards curative care, with the MoPH spending 73.3 percent of its budget on hospital care 6. Most of the remaining budget covered medications and operating costs. Less than 5 percent was spent on Primary Health Care (PHC) supplies. Rationale for Restructuring 10. The proposed restructuring aligns with the evolution of health priorities in Lebanon. The National Health Strategy vision 203012 outlines a key strategic direction: to enhance the resilience and adaptability of the health system by strengthening its core components. Similarly, one of the strategic objectives is to bolster preparedness and public health response to disease outbreaks, natural disasters, and other emergencies. Following these priorities, the MoPH aims to strengthen the public hospitals through several initiatives, one of which is providing them essential medical supplies and equipment. Another critical pillar in the strategy is digital health transformation for which the MoPH developed a National Digital vision in July 202313. The vision aims to leverage digital solutions to enhance healthcare outcomes through improving efficiency, enhancing healthcare quality and safety, improving care coordination, and strengthening preparedness for emerging health challenges. Project Procurement Strategy for Development (PPSD). Since the project was prepared under emergency procedures, the PPSD was deferred to be prepared during the implementation phase. Considering the limited capacity and vacancies at the PMU level, the PPSD was delayed. The draft PPSD was submitted by the PMU on July 11, 2024, and cleared by the Bank. - Systematic Tracking of Exchanges in Procurement (STEP). The Project is and will continue using the STEP to plan, record, and track procurement transactions. Any contract not uploaded in STEP, with award notification not being uploaded prior to the signing of contracts, may be considered not eligible for financing. - Procurement Plan. A Procurement Plan was developed and cleared by the Bank. - Project Management Unit. Procurement is being carried out by the MoPH through the established PMU for the active Lebanon Health Resilience Project. - Procurement Risks and Prior Review Threshold. The procurement risk rating remains high due to the instability of market availability, price fluctuations, and exchange rate volatility. Competition is narrow due to the survival of large firms and the collapse of small enterprises. Lengthy procurement transactions add to the lack of confidence in the public sector. Contractors are challenged due to the banking sector crisis and limited access to guarantees or loans for liquidity, if needed. The project will be subject to the high prior review threshold. - Key procurement under the project. The total value of the Project is US$29 Million. The Project has not launched yet any procurement activities since it became effective. The procurement and deployment of COVID-19 vaccines will be reduced to minimal intervention. The procurement of COVID-19 vaccines will be subject to prior review, regardless of the estimated cost. The restructuring will mainly finance medical equipment and supplies for public hospitals as per approved procurement and distribution plan, as well as financing goods and consultancies within the implementation of the digital health agenda of the MoPH. 20. Financial Management (FM). FM arrangements remain the same with the PMU at the MoPH handling all FM aspects of the Project. Summary of Performance of E&S Risk Compliance The Ministry of Public Health (MoPH) is the implementing agency for the COVID-19 health operation, which is financed by the IBRD, GCFF, and GPG. The MoPH has previously implemented the "Lebanon Health Resilience Project (LHRP)" under World Bank's Operational Policy (OP) 4.01. The performance of the Project Management Unit (PMU) in implementing environmental and social safeguards requirements under the LHRP has been satisfactory. They are expected to have a reasonable capacity for the World Bank's Environmental and Social Framework (ESF), especially after receiving several relevant trainings, the latest having taken place in May 2023. Although this is the MoPH's first operation under the ESF, they have a project management unit with an Environmental & Social (E&S) specialist/GRM officer who was recruited in January 2023 through a competitive selection process that followed the Bank-cleared Terms of Reference (TOR) and who will be responsible for ensuring satisfactory implementation, monitoring, and documentation of the E&S instruments throughout the project lifecycle, as per the provisions of the cleared and disclosed Environmental and Social Commitment Plan (ESCP). The World Bank task team has conducted an introductory session on the ESF requirements and the Environmental and Social Standards (ESSs) that apply as per the ESCP. All ESF instruments, including the ESCP, Stakeholder Engagement Plan (SEP), Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMF), and Labor Management Procedures (LMP), have been prepared, cleared, and disclosed on the MOPH website and the World Bank website. The E&S safeguards specialist and GM officer will monitor and document the material measures and actions stipulated in the ESCP. Currently the SEA/SH Action Plan is pending preparation and the WB team is following up on this action item as per the ESCP. The project was declared effective on December 20, 2022, however the initiation of project activities has been delayed for various reasons inter alia the delay in recruitment of specialists for the key PMU positions and the low demand for COVID-19 vaccines in Lebanon due to the evolving needs of the health sector in Lebanon, leading to lack of progress on vaccine procurement. As a result of discussions during the MTR mission held in May 2024, a level II restructuring has been proposed by the MoPH and will be submitted for World Bank approval to reflect the following proposed adjustments; (a) reallocating funds across components and disbursement categories due to the lower demand for COVID19 vaccines in Lebanon; (b) extending the Project closing date by 15 months, from September 30, 2024, to December 31, 2025; The current restructuring will not have any implications on the ESF instruments that have been prepared, nor on the agreed arrangements within the ESCP. Consequently, there is no current requirement to update the ESF instruments. It is worth noting that the implementation of this project has not yet started and the bank task team has not yet received a progress report.
