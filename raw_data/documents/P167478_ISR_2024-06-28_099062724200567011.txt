KEY ISSUES & STATUS Jun 28, 2024 Page 1 of 14 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Guinea Project for Results in Early Childhood and Basic Education (P167478) 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions Project Context. The Guinea Project for Results in Early Childhood and Basic Education (PRePEF) is a five-year operation financed by an International Development Association (IDA) loan in the amount of SDR 36.1 million (US$50 million equivalent). The PRePEF was approved by the World Bank’s Board of Executive Directors on July 9, 2019, and became effective on July 15, 2020. The project is scheduled to close on June 30, 2024. The project’s development objectives (PDOs) are to improve access to, and the quality of early childhood and basic education in the Project Areas and to strengthen the capacity of the education system to deliver better results and it specifically aims to increase access to pre- primary education and reinforce capacity of decentralized education units in selected management areas (such as teachers’ recruitment/deployment, provision of non-salary recurrent budget to schools, and information management). The PRePEF was originally designed to positively impact approximately 86,500 direct beneficiaries, including approximately 60,000 children, 1,000 Early Childhood Education (ECE) teachers, 25,000 primary school teachers (including 3,500 primary school directors who might be teaching) and 500 pedagogical support staff at the central and decentralized levels of the education system. The project was restructured on September 12, 2023, to revise, downward, some of the PDO indicator targets given the more limited availability of resources (due to inflationary pressures and exchange rate losses), as well the imminent closing date. Following the restructuring, the Project’s design was revised to positively impact approximately 16,500 children, 1,000 Early Childhood Education (ECE) teachers, and 3,000 primary schools. Relevance of Objectives. The project development objectives (PDO) were, and continue to be, Highly relevant, and strongly aligned with the Government of Guinea’s (GoG) priorities for the education sector. Specifically, the Project’s objectives are also strongly aligned with the GoG’s education sector program ProDEG (Programme Décennal de l’Education en Guinée) 2020-2029. These objectives also remain consistent with pillars 1 and 2 of the Guinea Country Partnership Framework (CPF) FY2018-2023. Based on the information above, the relevance of objectives is rated High under the original and revised project design. Efficacy (Achievement of the PDOs) Efficacy Pre-September 2023 Restructuring: Phase 1. Project implementation started slowly due to various factors, including delays in establishing the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) and launching several key project-supported activities. Between the project’s approval in July 2019 and March 2023, the Project was consistently rated Unsatisfactory or Moderately Unsatisfactory. In June 2023, the project’s overall performance was upgraded to Moderately Satisfactory, and this rating was maintained until the project was restructured in September 2023—though important delays and challenges with project implementation and the achievement of the envisaged outputs and outcomes remained. Objective 1: Improve Access to Early Childhood Education (ECE). Based on the available evidence, achievement of this objective during Phase 1 is judged to be modest. Activities linked to the achievement of this objective included the construction of ECE classrooms and the development of the Early Childhood Education (ECE) policy. Though the United Nations International Children's Emergency Fund (UNICEF) had been contracted to provide technical assistance (TA) to the Ministry of Pre-University Education and Literacy (MEPU-A) in the development of the ECE policy, and to subsequently support its implementation, the execution of this activity continued to face delays. In addition, although 361 contracts had been signed and an additional 404 were pending final sign-off/signature, construction activities were significant delayed during phase 1 of the project. Achievement of this objectives 2 and 3 is judged to be modest during Phase 1 of the project. A consulting firm was recruited to support the MEPU-A in developing a national policy on in-service teacher training for pre-school level. The consulting firm had initiated the process for the elaboration of a national policy on in-service training, but limited progress beyond that had been observed during Phase 1 of the project. No disbursement-linked results (DLR) under the disbursement-linked indicator (DLI) associated with Component 2 of the project had been achieved during Phase 1. Nonetheless, the development of a policy for in- service training and support was well-advanced and the 3 DLRs under this DLI were expected to be met in short order. In sum, though several important activities had been successfully launched during Phase 1, the increased pace of implementation in early 2023 was not sufficient to overcome the initial delays in implementation, as for this reason, the achievement of objectives 2 and 3 remained modest. Objective 4: Improve the Quality of Basic Education. Achievement of this objective is judged to be substantial during Phase 1 of the project. A consulting firm was successfully recruited to support the MEPU-A in developing a national policy on in-service teacher training for basic education level and enhancing the capacities of school directors and pedagogical support staff to conduct periodic in-service training and pedagogical support activities at school and sub-prefectural levels, using the mobile technologies. The main activities linked to the attainment of this objective were, therefore, well advance during Phase 1, thus justifying the rating of substantial. Objective 5: Strengthen the Capacity of the Education System to Deliver Better Results. Achievement of this objective is judged to be substantial. Measures to meet the two DLIs related to the decentralization of the teacher recruitment process and the provision of the non-salary recurrent budget to primary schools were well-advanced during Phase 1 of the project. Additional activities aiming to reinforce the capacity of the MEPU-A to measure student learning outcomes by carrying out standardized assessment had been strengthened and the Education Management and Information System (EMIS) had been launched with TA from a consulting firm and UNESCO. Based on the sub-ratings assigned to each of the Project’s overarching objectives—objective 1 (modest), objective 2 (modest), objective 3 (modest), objective 4 (substantial), and objective 5 (substantial)—the overall efficacy rating for Phase 1 is rated modest. Efficacy Post-September 2023 to Project Closure: Phase 2. The preliminary analysis presented herein summarizes the project’s performance in relation to its five overarching objectives during the period spanning from the September 2023 restructuring through project closure. Based on the data currently available, only 2 out of 6 of the project’s PDO-level indictors have been achieved thus far, and some of the project-supported activities will not be completed prior to the current closing date of June 30, 2024. However, it is important to highlight that some of the project’s data are still being analyzed and collated by the Government, and for this reason, cannot be fully captured in this final ISR. As such, the findings presented herein should be considered preliminary, and subject to be revised in the context of the forthcoming ICR. The project’s full dataset and complete set of studies and evaluations—with the most robust and up-to-date figures and results—will be examined in greater detail in the ICR. Objective 1: Improve access to Early Childhood Education (ECE). Achievement of this objective is judged to be modest mainly due to the non-completion of the pre-school classrooms. The activities linked to the attainment of this objective include the construction of pre-school classrooms and the development of an ECE policy, both financed under component 1. Progress towards the achievement of this objective was to be measured by the: (i) number of children Jun 28, 2024 Page 3 of 14 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Guinea Project for Results in Early Childhood and Basic Education (P167478) attending public ECE; (ii) status of standards-based, one-year ECE policy and plan; and (iii) number of pre-primary classrooms rehabilitated or constructed and supplied with furniture, equipment and play-teaching-learning materials. There are 372 pre-school classrooms completed under this project, and each classroom accommodates up to 35 students, for a total of 13,020 children. Though this result cannot be confirmed yet, upon completion of the construction activities by October 2024, a total of 565 pre-school classrooms will be built, accommodating a total of 19,775, therefore exceeding the target of 16,500. However, given the current progress to date, the achievement of this objective is rated modest, though it may be revised (upward) once updated figures are made available in October 2024. The contract with United Nations International Children's Emergency Fund (UNICEF) to provide technical assistance to the Ministry of Pre- University Education and Literacy (MEPU-A) to develop the Preschool is well implemented and the Disbursement-linked indicator (DLI) on Strengthened policy in Early Childhood Education (ECE) linked to Component 1 is achieved and disbursed. Objective 2: Improve the Quality of Early Childhood Education & Objective 3: Improve Access to Basic Education. Achievement of this objective 2 is judged to be substantial and was achieved through the training of early childhood education teachers under Component 2, while achievement of objective 3 is judged to be modest. Progress towards the achievement of objective 2 was to be measured by the following indicators: (i) early learning quality and outcomes as measured through the Monitoring Early Learning Quality and Outcomes (MELQO); (ii) number of teachers recruited; (iii) number of teachers trained; and (iv) number of students benefiting from direct interventions to enhance learning. To improve the quality of early childhood education, the project, under Component 2, developed and approved a national policy on in-service training. The targets for the number of teachers recruited or trained have been met, with 1,000 teachers have been recruited and trained. Therefore, the DLI on strengthened in-service teacher professional development with its three associated DLRs have been achieved and only DLR 2.3 remains to be disbursed. The number of students benefiting from direct interventions to enhance learning is 13,020, against a target of 16,500. Though this result cannot be confirmed at this time, once construction activities are finalized in October 2024, the target for the latter indicator is expected to be exceeded. The overall achievement of this objective during Phase 2 of the project, is deemed to be substantial. Finally, achievement of objective 3 during Phase 2 of the project is rated modest given the limited progress observed in improving access to basic education. Objective 4: Improve the quality of Basic Education. Achievement of this objective is judged to be substantial. Progress towards the achievement of objective 4 was to be measured by the following indicators: (i) early grade reading ability; (ii) status of standards-based policy on teacher professional development; and (iii) the number of teachers using mobile technologies in the classroom. Activities linked to the achievement of this objective have largely been successfully carried out, including by training of MEPUA staff in the Early Grade Reading Assessment (EGRA) to strengthen their capacity in assessing early learning outcomes. These trained agents have been deployed to various institutions to collect data on learning outcomes and provide a report on their findings by June 28, 2024. Teaching and learning materials for reading and mathematics have also been developed and approved. Tablets were successfully distributed to facilitate technology use in teaching pedagogy. Objective 5: Strengthen the Capacity of the Education System to Deliver Better Results. Achievement of this objective is judged to be modest. Progress towards the achievement of objective 5 was to be measured by the following indicators: (i) the percentage of stakeholders who use net-accessible dashboards for decision- making, advocacy or informational purposes, as measured by an end-line sample survey of decision-makers, which has not been met; (ii) the number of prefectures that recruit new teachers at decentralized level; (iii) the number of public primary schools to which a formula-based non-salary recurrent budget is transferred; (iv) the percentage of schools for Jun 28, 2024 Page 4 of 14 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Guinea Project for Results in Early Childhood and Basic Education (P167478) which current year education indicators are net-accessible to decisionmakers; and (iv) reported grievances addressed within stipulated time frame. UNESCO has delayed the implementation of the contract to reinforce the country’s EMIS. Therefore, the DLI on decision-makers and other stakeholders which have access to timely education system information has not been achieved and the target of having at least 20 percent of stakeholders use net-accessible dashboards for decision-making, advocacy or informational purposes has also not been met. However, the activities to meet DLI 3 on the recruitment of teachers is decentralized to the prefectural and regional levels with its four associated DLRs have been successfully completed and the money disbursed. The formula for non-salary budget transfer has been developed however, the recurrent transfer of these budgets to primary schools has not yet been implemented. Based on the sub-ratings assigned to each of the Project’s overarching objectives—objective 1 (modest), objective 2 (substantial), objective 3 (modest), objective 4 (substantial), and objective 5 (modest)—the overall efficacy rating for Phase 2 is, on balance, rated modest. Efficiency. Though several efficiency gains were realized under the project, overall efficiency is rated modest given the persistent delays in implementation and the higher-than-expected cost of key project activities, particularly those related to construction of pre-school classroom and ECE centers. A detailed economic and financial analysis for the project will be undertaken in the ICR evaluating both the external and internal efficiency gains realized under the Project. This detailed analysis will also draw on the findings of the various studies undertaken during the course project implementation. Overall Outcome. The Overall Outcome rating during both Phases 1 and 2 of the project is rated Moderately Unsatisfactory, given the sub-ratings of high for relevance of objectives, modest for efficacy, and modest for efficiency. Based on the detailed analysis which will be carried out during the forthcoming ICR, the WB’s task team will determine with the GoG if an upgrade in the Project Overall Outcome from Moderately Unsatisfactory to Moderately Satisfactory is warranted and supported by the evidence. 5.
