No I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Project Status: 1. The COVID-19 Response Project (Project) was approved on June 30, 2021, with a total financing of US$20 million (IBRD 92850-TM) and became effective on September 23, 2021. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to prevent, detect, and respond to the threat posed by COVID-19 and strengthen national systems for public health preparedness in Turkmenistan. Progress towards achievement of the PDO and the implementation progress have consistently been rated Satisfactory since the Project effectiveness. 2. Three of the four PDO indicators have reached or exceeded their end-target values. PDO Indicator 2: Number of COVID-19 designated laboratories with verified diagnostic equipment and test kits – end target: 5, actual: 18; and PDO Indicator 3: Number of designated beds for COVID-19 patients with access to continuous oxygen supply – end target: 600, actual: 1000. PDO Indicator 4: A National Pandemic Preparedness and Response Plan is regularly updated and tailored for each region. The Plan was prepared and finalized with the support of WHO, in the midst of the pandemic and is currently being implemented. It is applicable for all regions due to the centralized nature of the national pandemic preparedness and response and related measures. The Plan does not require an update considering the stabilized epidemiological situation in the country. This indicator is therefore considered as achieved. PDO indicator 1: Development and periodic update of a comprehensive national COVID-19 risk communication plan, including standardized, evidence-based information targeted to different population group – is on track to be achieved by June 2023. 3. The following Intermediate Results Indicators (IRI) have reached or exceeded their end-target values: (a) Number of health staff (physicians and nurses) trained in infection prevention and control in accordance with approved protocols (Number) – end target: 4000, actual: 4001; (b) Number of COVID-19 designated laboratories with staff trained to conduct COVID-19 diagnosis – end target: 13, actual: 18; (c) Number of focus groups conducted to engage communities, understand their needs and receive feedback on the project (Number) – end target: 16, actual: 16; The World Bank COVID-19 Response Project (P175131) (d) Number of COVID-19 designated laboratories with staff trained to conduct COVID-19 diagnosis – end target: 13, actual: 18; (e) Number of risk communication messages targeted to different population groups developed (Number) – end target: 4, actual: 4; (f) Number of gender-specific information campaigns delivered (Number) – end target: 2, actual: 2; (g) Percentage of participants in community meetings/events reporting that the local outreach and community engagement process is effective (disaggregated by gender) (Percentage) – end target: 75, actual: 75; (h) Percentage increase in risk awareness of COVID-19 among PHC nurses – end target: 50%, actual: 100%; and (i) Number of hospital and primary care physicians trained in the management of Severe Acute Respiratory Infection (SARI) patients and adherence to COVID-19 protocols (Number) – end target: 2,000, actual: 2,164. 4. The IRI on National testing strategy, national hospital surge capacity plan and national case management strategy developed is currently at the following stage: the activities under this IRI were implemented jointly with WHO through EU funds as confirmed by the Ministry of Health and Medical Industry (MoHMI) and WHO. The WHO used the freed resources to support the development of COVID-19 Preparedness Plans in five infectious hospitals in five districts and has conducted subsequent training activities under the Project. As such, it is proposed to replace this indicator by: Hospital preparedness plans on COVID-19 are prepared in five infectious disease hospitals in five districts and health workers trained, which has been achieved. 5. All other IRI are on track to be achieved by the Project current closing date of June 30, 2023. However, one IRI Electronic program for tracking and monitoring contacts developed and set up in Sanitary and Epidemiological Safety and Control (SESC) offices in Ashgabat and in five velayats (Text) is set to be achieved during the proposed extended period since it will allow time to procure off-the-shelf (or develop) relevant software application. 6. The project has fully disbursed the loan of US$20 million through two UN advances (first UN advance of US$14.95 million, the disbursement was processed on November 17, 2021, the second UN advance of US$4.99 million, the disbursement was processed on December 21, 2023). UNDP, acting as the Project Implementation Entity (PIE), is responsible for project management and day-to-day implementation under indirect financing arrangements, pursuant to a Standard Form of Agreement, Delivery of Outputs (Output Agreement) that was signed between the Government of Turkmenistan and UNDP on September 15, 2021, prior to effectiveness. As of February 28, 2023, contracts for US$15.41 million (77 percent of the total amount disbursed) have been signed and funds committed. Additionally, a contract for US$1.7 million for disinfecting chambers was recently signed (committed), which brings the total expenses and commitments to US$17.11 million (85 percent of the total amount disbursed). Remaining contracts to be finalized include a mobile PCR lab, antigen express test systems, spare parts for medical imaging equipment, contact tracing and surveillance application development/procurement, and payments to be disbursed to WHO and UNICEF as part of the UN- to-UN arrangements for their respective components, as well as contract management costs by UNDP for the project management activity. The World Bank COVID-19 Response Project (P175131) 7. Component 1: Improving COVID-19 Prevention, Detection and Response. Under this component, the project financed the procurement of PCR equipment and general laboratory equipment for laboratories in the capital city Ashgabat, as well as on regional and district levels, PCR tests, antibody tests, sprayers and disinfectant tanks, maintenance and repair of medical equipment, IT equipment, vehicles for sample transportation for a total of about US$8.3 million. As part of this component, financing was provided for carrying out a diagnostic evaluation of imaging equipment throughout the country by external engineers and a diagnostic report was prepared listing the damages and repair works to be carried out, and spare parts required. The Project has financed repair works and procurement of spare parts for the select number of medical facilities. 8. Component 1 also contains a sub-component focusing on strengthening Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE). UNICEF is responsible for implementing this sub-component and has made substantial progress and implemented most of the planned activities, including support in updating the National Emergency Risk Communication Plan; developing a comprehensive training curriculum and manual for medical and social services professionals on essentials of RCCE to address/minimize misinformation/bias and strengthen interpersonal communication and motivational interviewing; conducting awareness sessions among school children and youths, including vulnerable groups, about the prevention of infections and healthy lifestyle; procuring TV studio equipment to strengthen the capacity of the Health Information Center in working with the public on risk communication; discussing with women from velayats information needs in areas related to preventive behavior, early childhood development and nutrition during the pandemic; and carrying out three mass media briefings jointly with WHO and others. One important activity on collecting data on behavioral skills among the population (including focus groups, KAP - Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice study, etc.) is in progress and Terms of Reference were developed. However, subject to the proposed extension of the closing date, this KAP study could be included as an element of the upcoming Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS), which would yield a more in-depth data on behavioral skills. 9. Component 2: Improving Health System Preparedness for COVID-19. This component focuses on strengthening health system preparedness by expanding the capacity for treating COVID-19 and Severe Acute Respiratory Infection (SARI) cases, as well as enhancing infection prevention and control (IPC) measures in health facilities. As part of scheduled activities under this component, a list of equipment, consumables and medications for resuscitation and management of SARI patients with lung function disorders was defined and is being regularly updated; procurement and distribution to all identified medical facilities of essential medical equipment has taken place, which, among others, includes electrocardiogram (ECG) devices, bag valve bags with masks for children and adults, mobile digital X-ray equipment, cardio monitors, defibrillators, and similar items. Monitoring visits were undertaken by UNDP and MoHMI to health care facilities involved in the COVID-19 response across all regions of Turkmenistan where the goods were delivered. Through official communication, MoHMI and UNDP requested cancellation of the procurement of Oxygen generators and the mobile clinic due to 1) improvement of the epidemiological situation globally; 2) the easing of pandemic related restrictions in the country; and 3) the launching of necessary oxygen generating equipment by the state medical facilities and through other government-financed projects. In lieu of the oxygen generators and the mobile clinic, MoHMI suggested the financing of additional medical equipment and essential lab reagents, which are necessary for clinical diagnostic, treatment, and monitoring of patients with long lasting COVID-19 cases and complications. Considering the justification provided, and the fact that the reallocation of funds for procurement of other goods under the same Component 2 did not require a reallocation of the loan proceeds this request was approved by the Bank team. As a result of this, UNDP signed contracts for the additional medical equipment, which included ultrasound devices, spirographs, cardio monitors, autoclaves, and similar items. Contracts for delivery of the additional items were signed and deliveries are currently taking place or are scheduled to arrive before June 30, 2023. This component also financed the procurement of reagents and consumables for lab testing, and medical waste management systems, delivery of which is scheduled by June 2023 or earlier. Relevant documentation to address the hazardous risks for the medical waste management systems, including Environmental and Social Management Plan and Infection Control and Waste Management Plan (ICWMP), is being finalized with support of The World Bank COVID-19 Response Project (P175131) the Bank team. Finally, medicines for COVID-19 and SARI care were also procured. Total expensed and committed amounts for this component is around US$7.5 million. 10. Component 2 has a sub-component being implemented by WHO, which focuses on training and technical assistance to build local capacity. As part of its work plan, the WHO has developed the national laboratory strategy according to the latest WHO recommendations; hired national consultants and trainers to provide training and develop educational curriculum on COVID-19 in accordance with WHO recommendations; carried out multiple round tables on curriculum development with representatives of the MoHMI, Turkmen State Medical University, and newly hired consultants with further approval of the curriculum; strengthened the capacity of medical workers through relevant trainings. Most targets of related IRI under this component were achieved or overachieved or are on track to be achieved in Q2 2023. 11. Component 3: Project Management, Monitoring and Evaluation. Implementation capacity of the PIE under UNDP remains adequate and is staffed with all key project personnel hired as consultants. All relevant staff supporting the PIE and MoHMI on such aspects as fiduciary (financial management, procurement), environment and social, monitoring and evaluation, administration and logistics, communications are on board under the overall coordination and management of a Project Manager with support and oversight of Program officers and management in UNDP. Under this component a Project Board was established for general oversight and coordination of project implementation. According to UNDP requirements, all projects and programs implemented by UNDP must be governed by a multi-stakeholder board established to review performance and address implementation issues. The Project Board for this project, which has convened four times since the start of project implementation, includes the stakeholders representing the MoHMI, Ministry of Finance and Economy (MoFE), World Bank, UNDP, WHO, UNICEF, and the National Red Crescent Society. Additionally, regular working group meetings are conducted on average twice a month. 12. In accordance with the Output Agreement, UNDP has submitted quarterly progress reports demonstrating progress towards the outputs and activities specified for the project. MoHMI and UNDP teams also conduct regular visits to various healthcare facilities to verify delivery and installation of items procured under the project and to monitor implementation of capacity building activities. The proposed extension of the project closing date will require an amendment of the Output Agreement between the Government and UNDP to reflect the new closing date. This will be processed after the restructuring. 13. Financial management (FM) arrangements established by the UNDP Turkmenistan, including budgeting, accounting, financial reporting, staffing, flow of funds and external audit are adequate and satisfactory to the Bank. The World Bank relies on the UNDP arrangements for all aspects of the FM system. Currently there are no overdue audits for the Project. The FM review periodicity is risk-based with the next review being planned for May 2023. 14. Procurement arrangement remains as Satisfactory. Prior to effectiveness, the PIE had already initiated several critical activities, including negotiations with UNICEF and WHO to provide technical assistance services under UN-to-UN agreements. The UN-to-UN agreements with WHO and UNICEF were signed on September 21, 2021, and November 4, 2021, respectively. 15. Overall, most procurements are expected to be completed/delivered by the current closing date of June 30, 2023. However, procurement of the mobile PCR laboratory, spare parts for selected medical equipment, and installation of the application for contact tracing and epidemiological surveillance will require a six-month extension to complete delivery. One other activity that may benefit from the extension of the closing date is the planned KAP study by UNICEF, which could be included as an element of the upcoming Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) to be conducted by UNICEF. However, UNICEF will need to ensure that the field work for the proposed MICS survey takes place in Q3 2023 to allow for The World Bank COVID-19 Response Project (P175131) the KAP related results to be obtained before the proposed project closing date of December 31, 2023, otherwise UNICEF will need to conduct the KAP study as a separate activity (as initially planned). This has been discussed and agreed with UNICEF. As mentioned earlier, the extension of the closing date will also require an amendment of the Output Agreement between the Government and UNDP to reflect the new closing date. This will be a no-cost extension since the budgeted funds for project management costs have not been utilized in full due to delayed commencement of project activities at effectiveness. UNDP confirmed that the amendment will be processed through established procedures as soon as the restructuring is processed. 16. The environmental and social performance is rated as Moderately Satisfactory. Based on progress reports and virtual meetings held with the MoHMI and UNDP, the preparation of the Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMF) has been finalized and the final version has been disclosed in the UNDP website. In addition, UNDP organized a half-day introductory training on Environmental and Social Framework requirements and Environmental and Social Standards (ESSs) relevant to the project implementers. Monitoring visits of selected healthcare facilities were conducted in various provinces in July and August 2022 to verify the use of environmental and social screening checklists for project activities. Draft ESMP has been prepared for oxygen stations. 17. Although there is progress in finalizing and disclosing the ESMF, it was reported that there is still capacity limitation on the client side to enforce the ESMF. The Bank team has strongly recommended that UNDP comes up with a strategy on how to boost the capacity of the implementing agencies in the remaining project period to ensure that the environment and social screening checklists are properly used, and mitigation measures implemented. There is a need for more efforts for reaching out to communities, particularly to vulnerable groups such as elderly people, people with disabilities, unemployed, people with low incomes, women-headed households etc. as mentioned in the Stakeholder Enhancement Plan (SEP) to ensure that their needs are adequately addressed. B. Rationale for Restructuring: 18. MoHMI and UNDP have informed the Bank team of the extended terms of production and delivery of some medical goods that may take up to 8-9 months from contract signing, which will bring the completion of these contracts beyond the current project closing date. These include supply of mobile PCR laboratory, spare parts for medical equipment, and installation of the application for contact tracing and epidemiological surveillance, and other minor packages. As such, the signing of the contracts with delivery dates beyond the current closing can only take place after this proposed six-month extension is approved. Accordingly, and by letter of March 14, 2023, the MoFE has requested a six-month extension of the closing date until December 31, 2023, to allow for timely delivery of the goods and completion of the planned activities.
