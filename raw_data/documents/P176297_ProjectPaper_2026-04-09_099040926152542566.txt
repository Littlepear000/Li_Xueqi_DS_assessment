PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the Division Director’s approval for a restructuring of the project Strengthening Peru’s National Science, Technology and Innovation System - P176297. This is the first restructuring of the project. 2. This Restructuring Paper proposes the following: a. Re-allocating funds from Categories 1 (Grants) and 2 (Scholarships) to Category 3 (Goods, services, and operating costs), without any change to the total loan value. b. Replacing an R&D census with a survey. c. Expanding the eligibility for Phase 2 of the Academic Entrepreneurship program. d. Smoothing WB contributions across components, while maintaining total Project budget allocations therein. A. Project Background 3. The Strengthening Peru’s National Science, Technology and Innovation System (SINACTI) project is a US$125 million IPF (US$100 million financed by IBRD). The project was approved in February 2022 and will close in December 2027. The Project’s Development Objective (PDO) is to improve science, technology, and innovation services in targeted regions and three Strategic Areas: (a) low carbon and climate-resilient economy; (b) health; and (c) digital economy and ICT. 80 percent of the loan is allocated to providing scholarships and grants to beneficiaries. The project has remained as Satisfactory during the implementation period. This is a subsequent project of the Strengthening the Science, Technology and Innovation System in Peru (P156250), closed in 2022. 4. Component 1: Strengthening SINACTI’s institutions and governance to bolster innovation in Peru (US$1.3M). This component aims to strengthen the governance of SINACTI by improving the ability of its institutions to support the development of science, technology, and innovation capacities and enhance their contributions to sustainable development and climate change. Component 1 activities will enable the activities planned under components 2 and 3 by improving capacity to manage R&D and innovation support instruments. 5. Component 2: Capacity development for the generation of knowledge in Strategic Areas (US$72.5M). This component aims to improve the quality, quantity, and economic relevance of training at the doctoral and post-doctoral levels and promote the development of research, development, and innovation projects in scientific areas primarily related to the Strategic Areas, with at least 50% earmarked for the climate Strategic Area. The two sub-components are: i) Institutional alliances for strengthening research, science, and technology capacity; and ii) support for knowledge generation in strategic areas. These activities aim to directly contribute to the development of R&D and innovative approaches that result in climate change mitigation and adaptation, health solutions, and the design of a digital economy. 6. Component 3: Strengthening Industry-academia linkages to accelerate technology transfer and science-based business innovation (US$23M). This subcomponent aims to improve the relevance of R&D outputs to market demand primarily in the Strategic Areas, with at least 50% of overall component funding earmarked for the Climate Strategic Area. Key activities include: (i) building institutional capacity in CONCYTEC and universities to identify private sector technology and innovation needs and facilitate and accelerate technology transfers; and ii) closing critical innovation financing gaps for innovative research to reach investment readiness and/or meet the needs of innovation-seeking firms. These activities build on those in component 2 to directly contribute to the realization and operationalization of R&D and innovative approaches that result in climate change mitigation and adaptation, health solutions, and the design of a digital economy. This component finances project management by the team’s project coordination, technical, procurement, environmental and social, financial, and M&E specialists. B. Project Status 8. As of April 2026, approximately 73 percent of the US$100 million Loan had been disbursed. Both the Project’s “Progress towards achieving its development objectives” and “Overall implementation” are rated as Satisfactory. Financial management remains robust; as of the Project’s last ISR (November 2025), disbursements totaled US$63.08 million of which US$55.52 million had been documented, and bank reconciliations showed no discrepancies. The 2024 audit report was submitted in April 2025 and was deemed acceptable. Procurement performance is consistent and satisfactory, with low staff turnover and ongoing improvements in process manuals and sub-project audits. 9. The Mid-Term Review (MTR) confirmed that the project was progressing well overall while identifying several operational areas requiring stronger management discipline. The MTR - conducted in June 2025 - highlighted the need to better align project indicators, strengthen risk management, improve planning of critical path activities using structured scheduling tools, and streamline approval processes to reduce delays. In response, the implementation team has implemented corrective measures, including more structured activity planning, integration of risk monitoring into regular management processes, and updates to operational procedures to improve clarity and consistency in implementation. 10. Component 1 has advanced with the installation of equipment to support CONCYTEC’s digitalization, the continued roll- out of PerúCRIS Phase II, and implementation of the National Scientific Equipment Survey under the national indicators program. Progress has also been made in designing and awarding elements of the Competitive Funds Management Platform. At the same time, implementation has been slower than anticipated, leading to adjustments in strategy, including a shift to the use of individual consultants. A revised activity plan is now under preparation in close coordination with user areas, with the expectation that these measures will enable the component to meet its objectives within the project timeframe. 11. Component 2 is progressing satisfactorily, with substantial advances in doctoral training and research infrastructure. Under the “Institutional Alliances for strengthening doctoral programs in Strategic Areas” grant window (Alianzas Interinstitucionales para Programas de Doctorado), eight alliances remain active in five public universities, with resources transferred to ensure continuity of activities, and a total of 304 fellows are currently enrolled under the scholarship program. In parallel, 35 laboratories have been equipped to strengthen research capacity, and under the “Finance research grants: to support doctoral research projects in Strategic Areas” grant window (Proyectos de Necesidades De Áreas Estratégicas en Base a Desafíos), eight research projects have completed operational, safeguards, and procurement planning to enable full implementation. PROCIENCIA has also reported steps to enhance internationalization, strengthen mentorship, and improve monitoring systems, which will support sustainability and labor market impact. 12. Component 3 is advancing gradually, with a focus on initiatives that reinforce academia–industry linkages and innovation capacity. Activities are underway to update guidelines for the improvement of Technology Transfer Offices, alongside technical assistance to identify value-chain opportunities for regional interventions. Nine laboratories have received grants under the laboratory testing competition, and 85 percent of the allocated resources under the laboratory accreditation competition have been disbursed supporting 28 laboratories in obtaining or extending ISO 17025 accreditation. PROCIENCIA also plans to contract a firm to support Technology Transfer Offices starting in 2026, and beneficiaries of the accreditation competitions have identified key lessons to inform future initiatives, including standardization of protocols, improved advisory services, and stronger inter-laboratory networks. 13. Component 4 is proceeding as expected. The project is well managed, however the budget allocation has been found to be slightly underestimated. The Ministry of Finance submitted a formal request (dated 24th March 2026) to the World Bank to restructure the Project to introduce three principal changes. Specifically, MoF proposes (1) to replace the National R&D Census with a deterministic survey (3) to expand the eligibility for Academic Entrepreneurship Grants to attract a larger pool of high-potential innovations, by eliminating the restriction that limits sub-projects’ eligibility for phase two grants (which are for sub-projects that demonstrate strong commercial potential towards reaching investment readiness) to those that have already received phase one grants (which are support proving the relevant technology and estimating likely commercial demand); and (3) to reallocate Loan proceeds from the disbursement categories for Grants (Category 1) and Scholarships (Category 2) to that for other Project activities (Category 3). 15. A restructuring would ensure compliance with national institutional mandates while safeguarding timely delivery of critical data for the project. Under Peruvian law, only the national statistics agency (INEI) may conduct a census which has led to delays and uncertainties linked to INEI’s 2024–2027 census schedule. In place of the originally planned National R&D Census, the restructured Project would include a deterministic1 National Survey of Research Centers. The survey would generate the information required for Peru’s RICYT (Ibero-American and Inter-American Network on Science and Technology Indicators) reporting and for evidence-based STI policymaking within the project’s timeframe, thereby directly supporting the Project Development Objective. The survey is expected to be able to yield the same quality of information as would have been possible under a census. The proposed instrument will apply clear, rule-based coverage criteria—potentially encompassing the full universe of research centers—while incorporating quality-control measures, including stratification and data validation, to ensure reliability. This approach is consistent with the Frascati Manual’s guidance that R&D statistics may be compiled through surveys, and that a deterministic survey covering all identified units functions, in practice, as a census. Experience from the first Peruvian census (which reached only about 60 percent of units) and from OECD countries such as Chile demonstrates that well-designed deterministic surveys can achieve similar or higher completeness, while reducing costs and allowing results to be obtained in a more timely manner. These elements provide assurance that the revised methodology will enable the project to meet its objectives for producing robust, nationally representative indicators of research and development 16. A restructuring would leverage a wider pool of high potential innovations and maximize the Project’s impact on technology commercialization. The original design of the Academic Entrepreneurship program limits eligibility for Phase 2 grants to projects that received Phase 1 grants. This creates a timing problem: several Phase 1 projects will not conclude until mid- 2027, leaving insufficient time to evaluate their results, select successful initiatives for Phase 2, and allow them to carry out a Phase 2 project (up to 14 months) before the overall Project closes in December 2027. This restriction would leave available funding underutilized and reduce the number of eligible high-quality proposals. The proposed restructuring would address this issue by expanding the eligibility to include mature, investment-ready projects funded through other public STI programs which did not necessarily benefit from Phase 1. 17. A restructuring would reallocate funds across loan categories, without which core capacity-building and research activities would be interrupted before their intended outcomes are achieved. Through a restructuring, funds from Categories 1 (Grants) and 2 (Scholarships) would be re-allocated to Category 3 (Goods, non-consulting services, and operating costs). This would support two priorities: (i) extending the Elsevier scientific literature subscription from 18 to 24 months, ensuring that all researchers nationwide retain access to essential academic journals until the end of the Project; and (ii) securing continued financing for Senior Monitors who oversee the eight inter-institutional doctoral alliances and related scholarships through project completion. Crucially, this reallocation would not affect the goals of Categories 1 and 2 as funds would be drawn from unused grant balances and from an underrun revealed by the correction of a budgeting error. 1 A deterministic survey is one in which the outcome is completely fixed by the design and inputs. Currently, loan category allocations are based on disparate contribution ratios across Components 1 to 4 (8 percent, 97 percent, 97 percent, and 33 percent, respectively). The restructuring would harmonize the World Bank’s contribution at approximately 80 percent for each component, consistent with the overall project financing share. While total component allocations would not change significantly (apart from minor adjustments reflecting updated operational costs and re-budgeting) this alignment would aid financial tracking and enhance transparency in the use of project funds.
