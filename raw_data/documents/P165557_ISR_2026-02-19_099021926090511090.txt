KEY ISSUES & STATUS 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions The pace of project implementation has slowed down. Eight out of 65 planned activities under the parent GALOP, and three out of 71 activities under Additional Financing 2 have been completed under the various components of the project during 2025, resulting in downgrading of the implementation progress to moderately satisfactory. Also, this led to a marginal progress in the achievement of indicators targets. Component 1. Strengthen teaching and learning through support and resources for teachers is rated moderately satisfactory. A Differentiated Learning (DL) refresher training for master trainers across all the 261 districts was held in August 2025 to support district level DL training. The training included additional areas including the use of the DL+ dashboard, as the project prepares to expand the usage of the dashboard to all 16,500 primary schools. The harmonization of the teaching approaches for KG-play based learning is ongoing and will be validated against project guidelines in January 2026. Refresher training on coaching and mentoring support to schools was conducted for all SISOs across the 261 Districts. Component 2. Strengthen school support, management and resources is rated moderately satisfactory. All primary schools have now received their capitation grants (provided from the government budget) for the year as a result of engagements with the government to ensure that the PBC on timely releases of capitation grants is achieved. Even though schools are yet to receive their project base grants for the review period, the capitation grant will support the initial implementation of the School Performance Improvement Plans. Districts Strengthen grants amounting to GH₵8,310,000.00 has been budgeted to be disbursed to all 261 districts. Ghana Education Outcomes Project/Fund (GEOP). Sub-component 2.4 utilizes an outcomes-based funding modality and partners with social investors and service providers to improve learning outcomes of OOSC participating in accelerated learning programs before being integrated into schools. GEOP has successfully graduated over 52,000 learners through its Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) and provided interventions to more than 100,000 learners through the Mainstream School Improvement Program (MSIP) across all operational lots. For Cohort 1 (March 2023-September 2023), 17,720 ALP learners graduated, of whom 91% successfully transitioned into mainstream schools, with 79% retained after one year. Cohort 2 (October 2023-July 2024) enrolled 20,977 learners with a 96% completion rate, while Cohort 3 (October 2024-July 2025) had 14,399 learners enrolled and 14,137 graduated, achieving an 85% transition rate into mainstream schools. Lot 7A, targeting urban out-of-school children in Greater Accra and Greater Kumasi, commenced in April 2025 with 4,439 learners enrolled across 161 communities in five languages, achieving a 90% completion rate and 85% transition rate. Childcare and targeted after-school interventions for improved school readiness. Sub-component 2.5 support the implementation of after school learning interventions, afterschool childcare centers/clubs, targeting communities and low-income families with limited access to child-care. Key activities during the period include the development of the caregivers manual and handbook and the organization of a virtual orientation workshop for MMDAs in the 129 Communities of Excellence districts. The mission notes the significant progress made in the deployment of teachers to GALOP schools, including mechanizing 2275 newly recruited teachers into the government payroll and posting teachers to GALOP schools. Further the government has progressed with its policy announcement in the 2025 budget, for additional incentives for teachers deployed to rural areas (20% of basic pay), by submitting a cabinet memo for full implementation. Component 3. Strengthen accountability systems for learning. Progress made include National Council for Curriculum and Assessment (NaCCA) finalizing the analysis of the National Standardized Test (NST) 2024 assessment. The results for P4 suggest that there have been learning gains compared to the baseline conducted in 2021 in both Math and English. There has also been significant progress in the alignment of the NST to the Global Proficiency Framework which would enable reporting against SDG 4.1.1. One concern regarding the assessment is that only 33% of P4 test respondents were female which does not align with the overall composition of female enrollment in P4. Various reasons were given to explain this discrepancy including the incorrect shading by students of their sex (favoring the male option which was listed first). Accountability Dashboard. Overall, 16,200 schools have reported using the dashboard during cluster-level meetings. Thus far, about 64 indicators are captured in the revamped dashboard. Data from MoE EMIS data, GES mobile School Report Card (mSRC), the NaCCA National Standardized Test (NST), and National Schools Inspectorate Authority (NaSIA) inspection are currently linked to the dashboard. In addition, the MoE plans to add data from the GES’ Professional Learning Communities (PLC) and Differentiated Learning (DL) system, NaSIA School Licensing and Information Management System (SLIMs), GSS external data, and MGSP School Feeding, and other data such as SMC status, SPIPs, ADEOPs at the district level etc. It was agreed that a data glossary should be developed to include indicator name, data source, description, aggregation, and how indicator is calculated. Regarding the governance of the Accountability Dashboard, the mission team recommended that the World Bank should be represented on the Accountability Dashboard Steering Committee. The MoE also agreed that a workshop on the Accountability Dashboard should be held to receive feedback from internal stakeholders to ensure stronger coordination among partners. Education Management Information System (EMIS) In January and February 2025, EMIS organized regional stakeholder engagements with the national association of private schools. Challenges include the lack of sanctions if schools do not submit data, and the limited number of private schools that do not submit EMIS data. With the digitization of data collection for the annual EMIS census, data collection is now an administrative task with updates only in data variables that have changed. e.g. a teacher promotion, change of school etc. All learners have been assigned a unique identifier. Health and disability screening. Health screening is planned to be conducted on the medical history, physical examination, vision assessment, hearing assessment, intellectual impairment, oral health screening and nutrition assessment of learners enrolling in KG1 and primary 1. Orientation for National and Regional teams, District Teams, and in-school screening exercise is planned for early 2026. Component 4. Technical assistance, institutional strengthening, monitoring, and research. Component 4 is rated satisfactory. Significant progress has been made toward establishing the Ghana Education Evidence and Data Lab (GEEDLab) to strengthen data systems and reposition the MoE for evidence-informed decision-making. The GEEDLab aims to connect the MoE, its agencies, academia, and sector partners around a coherent research agenda while improving data quality and timeliness and supporting the evaluation of ongoing interventions such as Digital Learning, CoEX, and KG INSET programs. Progress to date includes the successful reconstitution of the Education Sector Research Feb 19, 2026 Page 3 of 32 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report Ghana Accountability for Learning Outcomes Project (P165557) Group (ERSG), which necessitated obtaining confirmation from the MoE. The team has developed a comprehensive Research and Learning Agenda (RLA) framework organized around five thematic areas: curriculum, teachers, and instruction; school improvement; system governance and accountability; data systems and evidence use; and community empowerment and system integration, with cross-cutting themes of quality, equity, inclusion, and EdTech & AI. The RLA development process has been guided by three core principles: alignment with national priorities and GALOP AF 2, co- creation and consensus-building with stakeholders, and strategic prioritization of learning enquiries for system impact. The next steps include circulating the revised RLA to ESRG for technical review, conducting validation workshops with key stakeholders including MoE leadership, and submitting the approved RLA for GALOP AF2 PBC 4.4.1 verification to unlock the associated disbursement. Two Project Oversight Committee (POC) meetings have been held in 2025 and three Education Sector Working Group meetings were held in 2025. 5.
