No I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. This Project Paper seeks approval of the Country Director to process a project restructuring for: (i) partial cancellation of funds; and (ii) revision of the results framework. This restructuring responds to a request made by the Government of Bangladesh (GoB) through letters dated June 26, 2023, and July 13, 2023, from the Economic Relations Division of the Ministry of Finance. 2. Project background: The Bangladesh COVID-19 Emergency Response and Pandemic Preparedness Project (P173757), in an amount of XDR 72.9 million (US$100 million equivalent) IDA credit (IDA 6605-BD) was approved in April 2020 under the COVID-19 Strategic Preparedness and Response Program (SPRP) using the Multiphase Programmatic 4 The World Bank COVID-19 Emergency Response and Pandemic Preparedness Project (P173757) Approach (MPA). In March 2021, an additional financing (AF) of XDR 347.1 million (US$500 million equivalent) IDA credit (IDA 6846-BD) was approved by the Board to enable affordable and equitable access to COVID-19 vaccines and help ensure effective vaccine deployment in Bangladesh through vaccination system strengthening, and to further strengthen preparedness and response activities under the parent project. Along with processing the AF, the project was restructured to incorporate US$100 million soft loan as co-financing from the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB). The financing percentage of the original IDA credit (IDA 6605-BD) and the AIIB loan (COFN-C1850) is set at 50:50. The AIIB soft loan is financing all project components as IDA except sub-component 1.5 (for COVID-19 vaccination related activities). 3. Project implementation status: The project has been instrumental in Bangladesh containing the COVID-19 epidemic by helping vaccinate people and strengthening health systems for better preparedness of future pandemics. More specifically, the project supported the following: i. Vaccinated 46.5% of the total population with 68 million doses of vaccines and 110 million pieces of auto-disable syringes procured, shipped, and deployed. ii. Liquid medical oxygen (LMO) systems established at 30 public hospitals that had previously not had centralized LMO systems. This marks a key milestone in strengthening the health system, as access to oxygen supplies is critical for the treatment of COVID-19, as well as other respiratory diseases. iii. Additional technical capacity ensured through hiring of around 1,200 staff (doctors, laboratory consultants, medical technologists, data operators) who have been placed at public health facilities dealing with COVID-19 cases. iv. Critical equipment and supplies ensured during the peak of COVID-19 outbreak: 220 beds for intensive care units (ICU); 300 ventilators supplied to various hospitals across the country; strengthening capacity of a field hospital in Dhaka; COVID-19 testing kits and reagents in large quantities; and large quantities of personal protective equipment, masks, body bags, gloves, goggles, coveralls, and aprons were supplied for the protection of frontline workers. 4. Achievement of the project development objective (PDO) is rated satisfactory and implementation progress as moderately satisfactory. The PDO is to support the government “to prevent, detect and respond to the threat posed by COVID-19 and strengthen national systems for public health preparedness”. Two out of the four PDO indicators have overachieved the end-of-project target, one is fully achieved, and the remaining one (fourth) indicator is in progress. At present, approximately 89% of total IDA allocation and 15% of AIIB funds have been disbursed. Besides, the project is rated satisfactory with respect to its performance in environmental and social safeguards, project management, and monitoring and evaluation, while the ratings for financial management and procurement are moderately satisfactory. There are no pending audit reports. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES The World Bank COVID-19 Emergency Response and Pandemic Preparedness Project (P173757) 5. Partial cancellation of funds: there were plans to construct three medical gas plants under the project. These require complicated engineering work and cannot be feasibly completed within the project closing of December 2024. Accordingly, the government has revised the project activity plan and proposing to cancel US$65 million from component 1 associated with this activity (50:50 from AIIB and IDA), as summarized below: i. AIIB loan (COFN C1850): US$32.5 million will be cancelled from disbursement category 1; ii. US$32.5 million equivalent will be cancelled from the two IDA credits: a. IDA additional credit (Cr. 6846-BD): XDR 21.57 million, which is the available balance under this credit, will be cancelled; b. IDA original credit (Cr. 6605-BD): XDR 2.78 million will be from disbursement category 1. 6. Revision of the results framework: in line with the project activities, one new intermediate outcome level indicator is proposed to be added and two indicators dropped, as summarized below. The end-of-project target date for all indicators have been updated to December 2024 (in line with the project closing date). i. Add new indicator: Epidemiological units functional with required inputs at the district level (number), baseline: 0 and target: 30 ii. Delete indicator: Emergency operations center functional with required inputs (number) iii. Delete indicator: Doctors and nurses at district-level facilities trained in infection, prevention, and control (IPC) per MoHFW guidelines (percentage). 7. No other changes are being proposed. III.
