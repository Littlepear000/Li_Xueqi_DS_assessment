PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. Pacstat–SPC (P168122) is part of an interconnected Series of Projects (SOP) that is on track to achieve, and in several cases exceed, its initial objectives. The SOP includes two country-level operations in Tonga (P171377, “Statistical Innovation and Capacity Building in Tonga”) and Kiribati (P171380, “Statistical Innovation and Capacity Building in Kiribati”). Pacstat–SPC has directly supported the successful implementation of these companion projects. Regionally standardized survey methods and strengthened technical assistance directly contributed to successful Household Income and Expenditure Surveys (HIES) in Tonga and Kiribati, resulting in lower per- interview costs, improved data quality, and faster publication timelines. Both countries are on track to meet the household survey coverage target established in IDA21. The standardization of methods has also supported the Solomon Islands “Building Capacity in Statistics and Audit Project” (CAPSA, P180723), which completed an HIES without direct SPC technical assistance. In addition, an upcoming IPF in the Marshall Islands is expected to include an HIES sub-component that will benefit from Pacstat–SPC sponsored standards and methods. As of November 2025, funds in the Pacstat–SPC project are nearly fully disbursed, and all PDO and intermediate results indicators have been achieved or exceeded. Project ratings reflect strong implementation performance and effective project administration. Early challenges with procurement and contract management processes have been resolved following SPC leadership’s amendment of internal policies to allow project procurement to be undertaken solely through the Bank’s systems. These arrangements are expected to continue through the additional year of implementation and to sustain satisfactory procurement ratings going forward. 2. The project has significantly improved the quality, availability, and standardization of statistics across the region. To standardize methods and quality, the project commissioned and published guidance notes for key statistical issues, such as sampling strategies and poverty measurement, for use in PICs. Each was reviewed and approved and are now applied in statistical activities across the region. The concentration of specialized technical capacity within SPC reduced the lag between data collection and publication, with most countries now producing reports within 12 months of the end of fieldwork. The availability of consistent data sets allows the Pacific region to better track its overall levels and patterns of poverty, inequality, and employment. These benefits are tracked in the results framework, including publication of guidance notes (PDOI 1.1), regionally comparable indicators (PDOI 2.1), harmonized microdata sets (PDOI 2.1), PSMB commissioned works on innovative experiments (IRI 1.2), and HIES reports produced within 12 months of data collection (IRI 2.2). 3. Improved methods have also been applied in countries without statistics IPFs—including Tuvalu, Samoa, Palau, and Nauru. The regional endorsement of new methodologies, the production of detailed technical guidance notes, and SPC’s improved capacity to provide technical assistance to countries has led to the application of the new methods in other PICs. Through the project, SPC has supported the publication of poverty report in Nauru, Palau, Samoa and Tuvalu based on HIES datasets that were collected with improved methods and using poverty measurement methodology that conforms to international best practices. The availability of new and consistent statistics on poverty, demographics, labor, and access to services directly benefits the countries in evidence-based policymaking and improves the quality of the Bank’s policy engagement and operations portfolio. SPC has requested additional financing to extend project activities for one year to sustain high priority activities and lay the groundwork for a planned Pacstat-II regional project under preparation for FY27. The proposed additional financing has two objectives: i) extend core activities from the ongoing Pacstat-I project for one year while maintaining the development objective, and ii) conduct preparatory activities to inform the design of a second phase of Pacstat. Pacstat-II will expand the geographic coverage of the SOP by engaging additional IDA countries (early indications of interest have been received from Kiribati, Tonga, Tuvalu, Solomon Islands and Vanuatu), while focusing on the next generation of efficiency improvements that can be implemented across the region to increase quality, timeliness, and coverage. The additional financing will sustain SPC’s increased capacity achieved during the project, which allows SPC to continue providing a standard package of technical assistance to all WB-member countries in the Pacific. Additional financing will also be used for pilot activities shaping the design of the upcoming project’s planned components. 5. The project’s overall FM arrangements are adequate and will be maintained throughout the remainder of the project life. FM arrangements are adequate and reliable to provide timely information required to manage and monitor project implementation. Project financial reports have been submitted on time and acceptable. The FY24 audit report with unqualified opinion included an opinion on RTSC and no issues were noted. PMU has been submitting withdrawal applications monthly ensuring timely documentation of project implementation progress. The project contract and fixed asset register are established and well maintained. There are no overdue financial reports or audits for the project. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 6. This paper proposes a project extension of two months to allow the task team to process the Additional Financing. The project is currently due to close on 31 December 2025, which does not allow enough time for the task team to undertake the due diligence in the processing of the additional financing. Thus, a short project extension is necessary to allow the project to stay open while the AF processing is underway. The team aims to complete the AF processing by February 2026, so a two month extension is requested. The request letter from SPC includes both a request for the additional financing and a request for a project extension to process and implement the additional activities. 7. No new activities or other changes are included in this restructuring paper.
