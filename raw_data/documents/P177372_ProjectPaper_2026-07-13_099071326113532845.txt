PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Project Description 1. The Gabon Urban Development Project (P177372) was approved on June 11, 2025, and became effective on January 13, 2026. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to improve access to climate-resilient urban infrastructure and public facilities, and reduce flood risk in selected cities. The selected cities are Franceville, Lambaréné, Oyem, Koulamoutou, Mouila, Ndendé, and Lebamba. The Project has four components: (i) Integrated urban development in selected cities (USD 90 million equivalent IBRD), (ii) Flood risk reduction infrastructure (USD 55 million equivalent IBRD), (iii) Project management, coordination, monitoring and evaluation (USD 5 million equivalent IBRD), and (iv) Contingent emergency response component (USD 0 million IBRD equivalent). B. Project Status 2. Since project effectiveness in January 2026, there has been solid progress in implementation readiness and compliance with legal covenants. Key milestones include the adoption of the Project Operations Manual, establishment of project governance structures, staffing of core PIU positions, and clearance of the 2026 Annual Work Plan and Budget. The first disbursement was made in April 2026, with a total disbursement of approximately USD 13 million currently. Component 1 - Integrated Urban Development in Selected Cities 3. Progress has been achieved in technical preparation of Component 1 activities. Environmental and social impact studies for the four priority investments have been completed and approved, and procurement for works contracts has been initiated for the first set of road investments. These initial works contracts are expected to be signed in July 2026, with civil works beginning in August 2026, conditional on a timely restructuring of the project and full implementation of the Resettlement Action Plans (RAPs) for the respective investments prior to the start of civil works. The final RAPs have been reviewed by the Bank and were publicly disclosed on July 1, 2026.1 Although the project carries a Substantial risk classification, the resettlement risks associated with these subprojects are assessed as moderate. 4. Besides the four priority investments, five feasibility study contracts have been consolidated to streamline preparation of the remaining Component 1 investments. Validation of all detailed design documents is expected by July 2026, with civil works contracts signed by October 2026 for these other investments. Component 2 - Flood Risk Reduction Infrastructure 5. Upstream flood risk studies have been completed across all target cities, enabling identification of 16 priority investments in Franceville, Lambaréné, Oyem, and Koulamoutou. The identification of drainage and flood protection infrastructure investments is anchored in the criteria agreed in the Project Appraisal Document (PAD)2 and the prioritization for investment readiness followed the approach to limit potential resettlement. Tender documents for the technical studies are being finalized with Bank support, and the first detailed designs are expected in March 2027. Component 3 - Project Management, Coordination, Monitoring and Evaluation 6. Good progress has been made on PIU staffing, a critical enabler for accelerating implementation. Fourteen staff were recruited between January and March 2026, filling key positions including the coordinator’s assistant, operations managers, and financial, procurement, and M&E specialists. The remaining core PIU staff, as defined per Financing Agreement schedule 2, paragraph 5 a and b, are the coordinator, the engineer/technical director, and the senior social specialist for which the recruitment process is underway. These positions are expected to be filled by September 2026. Component 4 - Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC) 7. The final CERC manual and CERC Environment and Social Addendum were approved by the Bank in March 2026 and are under validation by the project’s Steering Committee. C. Rationale for Restructuring 8. During project preparation, it was noted that some of the investments would result in involuntary resettlement, of varying scale and magnitude. At the point of loan negotiations, the project Financing Agreement (FA) did not include any category for resettlement expenditures because the Resettlement Action Plans (RAPs) were not yet prepared. It was agreed that a project restructuring could be undertaken to amend the FA once the first RAPs for the priority projects in Franceville, Koulamoutou, Oyem and Lambarene are ready. This would be to cover the cost of resettlement expenditures (including compensation for land acquisition related to implementation of a resettlement action plan) or cash compensation and other assistance paid in cash for involuntary resettlement. Accordingly, the Government of Gabon has now requested the Bank to consider using IBRD loan proceeds totaling USD 6 million equivalent for financing land acquisition and resettlement through an official request from the Ministry of Planning and Foresight on May 26, 2026. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES A. Reallocation: New disbursement category to reflect resettlement financing 9. On June 24, 2026, the Regional Vice President for Africa West and Central (AFW) approved the reallocation of USD 6 million3 from the IBRD loan proceeds for the Gabon Urban Development Project (P177372), to finance land expenditures and resettlement compensation to project affected persons (PAPs) for the implementation of the Resettlement Action Plan (RAP) for Component 1 and 2 investments in the seven cities. New Project description 10. Component 1 Integrated Urban Development in Selected Cities: (IBRD: USD 90 million equivalent). A new disbursement category for resettlement expenditures will be created in the Financing Agreement. The loan is denominated in EUR, and the USD 6 million allocated for the resettlement expenditures will also be expressed in EUR in the amendment to the Financing Agreement. 12. These changes have no impact on the PDO and results framework indicators as the funds reallocation to the new disbursement category will have minimal effects on the scope and extent of the planned investments. C. Results framework indicator adjustment 13. A minor adjustment to the measurement unit of the existing gender indicators is proposed to align with World Bank corporate scorecard guidance. While the definition of the indicators remains the same, it is proposed to change the measurement unit from a percentage-based (percentage) unit to a numeric count (number) to be consistent with corporate guidance.
