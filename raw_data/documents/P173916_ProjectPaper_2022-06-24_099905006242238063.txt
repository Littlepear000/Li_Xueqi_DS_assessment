Bank staff are operating in a hybrid environment with some days at the office and others at home. Notwithstanding the challenges, the MPA is on track to achieve its PrDO, which is to prevent, detect and respond to the threat posed by COVID-19 and strengthen national systems for public health preparedness. The significant level of disbursements attests to the strong implementation of the Bank' response thanks to the commitment of counterparts supported by Bank country teams. No I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Project Status The World Bank North Macedonia Emergency COVID-19 Response Project (P173916) 1. The North Macedonia Emergency COVID-19 Response Project (ECRP) was approved on April 30, 2020, became effective on October 29, 2020; and has a closing date of June 30, 2022. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is “to prevent, detect and respond to the threat posed by COVID-19 and strengthen national systems for public health preparedness in North Macedonia”. The project has three components: (i) emergency COVID-19 response, (ii) household support to enable social distancing, and (iii) project implementation, communications, community engagement, and monitoring. The Ministry of Health (MoH) and the Ministry of Labor and Social Policy (MoLSP), with the support of an existing Project Management Unit (PMU) under the MoLSP, are responsible for project implementation. As of May 31, 2022, the project has disbursed 84.8 percent1 of the total loan amount (USD 83.49 million of USD 98.50 million). 2. Overall progress towards the PDO is rated satisfactory. Three PDO indicators have already exceeded their end targets: the recovery rate from COVID-19 stands at 97 percent (end target: 90 percent); 99,073 new beneficiaries have been enrolled for financial support to enable social distancing (end target: 93,000 new beneficiaries); and 59,430 people have been tested for COVID-19 per MoH protocol (end target: 50,000 people tested). However, percentage of the population vaccinated has not been reported against since vaccines have not yet been procured through the project, which aimed to contribute toward this PDO-level indicator2. As discussed in the sections below, changes in the global vaccine market led the Government of North Macedonia (GoNM) to receive generous donations and grant funding for COVID-19 vaccine procurement, therefore, reducing the GoNM’s need to procure vaccines through the project, as initially planned. The proposed restructuring paper responds to this change in the need for vaccine procurement. 3. Implementation progress is rated satisfactory as described in the paragraphs that follow. 4. Component 1 implementation has progressed well, incorporating changes through the project restructuring and addressing emerging environmental and social (E&S) risks. Despite delays in the aftermath of the Tetovo Hospital fire (please see paragraph 10 below), implementation of Component 1 is proceeding well, with five intermediate results indicators already met (see Results Framework below). The project has supported the procurement and phased installation of 17 prefabricated modular facilities for COVID-19 treatment, with 16 of them (all but one in Tetovo) currently being operational and additional investments made in life and fire safety (LFS), as discussed under the E&S section below. Progress has also been made with the procurement of supplies for designated laboratories, intensive care unit (ICU) beds, and medical equipment. However, delays were encountered with the procurement of vaccines, given the rapidly changing context, and delays in the procurement of the communication campaign on vaccination hesitancy. The plan to refurbish selected public health institutions to be used for COVID-19 vaccinations did not advance as planned, as described in the rationale for the proposed restructuring section. 5. For a period of nine months from March to November 2021, the MoH also financed bonus payments—calculated as 5 percent of each eligible worker’s basic salary—for health workers in the public sector. Component 2 has also progressed well, reaching more beneficiaries than initially planned. The MoLSP financed nine rounds of monthly payments to Guaranteed Minimum Income (GMI) beneficiaries from the project covering the periods from August to December 2020 and from August to November 2021. The number of GMI beneficiaries paid each month has progressively increased from 29,480 in August 2020 to 35,720 in November 2021, reflecting the new eligibility criteria that were put in place as part of the emergency response that aimed to expand the GMI to households who were impoverished due to the COVID-19 pandemic. This has resulted in 99,073 beneficiaries having been paid by the project. The final payments under this Component are currently planned to be made to GMI beneficiaries in the second half of 2022, which the proposed restructuring will allow. The MoLSP has also addressed the significant delays in processing of some applications to the GMI, which were witnessed early in the pandemic. Additionally, the MoLSP is in the process of modifying legislation to enable online applications for all cash benefits in North Macedonia. The MoLSP aims to have this new online system in place by December 2022 as a complement to the current in-person application process. 7. The hygiene packages have also been fully procured and delivered, with the contract for this activity now closed. A total of 54,400 packages were distributed to beneficiaries of means-tested programs. The remaining 600 packages are currently stored by the MoLSP and are being distributed to new GMI beneficiaries as they enroll in the program. 8. As the pandemic has evolved—and given the response through other government channels—the impacts resulting in loss of employment leading to claims for unemployment benefits were less than originally anticipated, as documented in the restructuring paper for the first project restructuring. As a result, the MoLSP did not utilize the funds allocated to sub-component 2.2 (temporary unemployment insurance support); these funds were, instead, used to meet the sustained demand for GMI benefits provided under subcomponent 2.1. This flexibility was anticipated in the project design and was also reinforced through the project's first restructuring. End targets of all indicators under Component 2 have been met, except for one (adoption of an online application system), which is expected to be met by December 2022. 9. Component 3 has also progressed well. The PMU has been fully staffed with additional expertise required to manage the project. The grievance mechanism is up and running; the PMU hired a firm to carry out a telephone survey of GMI beneficiaries and health workers to gather information on their experience with the COVID-19 pandemic and the response provided through the project. 10. In September 2021, a severe incident occurred when a fatal fire broke out in the COVID-19 center of the public hospital in Tetovo; the center was housed in one of 17 modular facilities procured with funding from the project. While a formal investigation is still ongoing, a World Bank (WB) field mission was held in October 2021 to assess the incident, as well as the LFS risks for the remaining modular facilities. Actions have been taken to address the identified LFS risks related to the project, including: appointing Environment, Safety, Health, and Social Aspects Coordinators at all hospitals with modular facilities; updating all project E&S instruments, including the Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMF); monitoring compliance with the ESMF checklist by all facilities supported by the project; and planning for contracting a LFS company to ensure that project activities comply with North Macedonian LFS regulations and the WB’s Environmental, Health, and Safety General Guidelines. Overall progress on mitigating LFS risks, however, has been slow, as described in the E&S compliance section below. The MoH reconfirmed its commitment to advancing LFS measures in early 2022, with progress being reported since then on the actions agreed in October 2021. 11. The project’s procurement performance rating is ‘Satisfactory’. The PMU has managed project procurement in an efficient and timely manner and in accordance with procurement regulations and the procurement plan. The procurement plan has been updated regularly to reflect project needs. 12. The project’s financial management (FM) rating is ‘Satisfactory’. Following additional FM implementation support provided in December 2021, the Bank team confirmed that progress on FM-related aspects remains Satisfactory. The 2021 project audit is due to be submitted to the Bank by June 30, 2022, and will include confirmation that payments have appropriately reached GMI beneficiaries and healthcare workers. The World Bank North Macedonia Emergency COVID-19 Response Project (P173916) 13. The project’s E&S compliance rating is ‘Moderately Unsatisfactory’. Since the Tetovo Hospital fire and the October 2021 mission, progress on actions to address LFS has been slow, partially due to the political situation which led to a new government in early January 2022, hospitals’ limited capacity to finance LFS activities, and difficulties in procuring the required expertise on LFS issues into the PMU. However, since the accident occurred, the WB has intensified implementation support to the PMU to monitor the status of the action plan agreed upon in October 2021. During the most recent implementation support mission in May 2022, the WB E&S team conducted in-person site visits to four hospitals3 and confirmed that the MoH/PMU had advanced on nearly all LFS actions. All hospitals had appointed well- informed focal points; there was greater awareness of WB E&S instruments (i.e., the ESMF and the Environmental and Social Commitment Plan [ESCP]); and hospital staff were effectively trained on oxygen safety by the World Health Organization (WHO) in December 2021. The main barrier in ensuring compliance with the full checklist is the lack of funding for LFS interventions. The project will provide support to cover this gap through the proposed project restructuring so that a licensed company (or companies) can be contracted by the MoH/PMU to conduct the appropriate inspections and issue the relevant certifications, and LFS equiement can be procured and installed. B. Rationale for Restructuring 14. This would be the second restructuring of the project, and the first extension of the project closing date. The first restructuring, approved on September 9, 2021, was done to support the GoNM’s COVID-19 vaccination efforts. The restructuring included revisions to project components and component costs; the results framework; the overall risk rating; disbursement category allocations; and economic, environmental, financial, and social analyses. COVID-19 vaccination in North Macedonia began in February 2021; the following month, the GoNM requested a reallocation of project funds to support the country’s vaccine deployment plan. The WB accepted the reallocation request, as support for COVID-19 vaccination furthered efforts to achieve the PDO and improved the GoNM’s ability to acquire vaccines in a competitive global market with high vaccine demand. 15. This second restructuring seeks to: (i) extend the current closing date by 6 months; (ii) reallocate funds between the disbursement categories; and (ii) modify the results framework. An extension of the closing date is necessary to allow the GoNM to complete a set of critical activities under Component 1, which are either ongoing or are about to begin, and the implementation of which has been affected by the rapidly changing context of the pandemic. The completion of these activities will fulfill the project's objective of supporting North Macedonia in the fight against COVID-19 and strengthening its health system resilience. The Government requested a one year extension of the project; however, upon the Bank’s consideration and with mutual agreement between the parties, a decision was made to extend the project for a period of six months only. The proposed six-month extension maintains the emergency nature of the project, while also recognizing the evolving context of the pandemic and the ongoing need to support the GoNM in carrying out critical activities, including the purchase of medicines for COVID-19 treatment and a public communication campaign to increase vaccine acceptance. Reallocation of funds is required to reflect the request of the GoNM to finance additional cash transfers to GMI beneficiaries in response to the current, rapid food price inflation. Changes to the result framework are necessary to reflect the modifications in activities under Component 1 and the extended closing date. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 16. Implementation schedule: Through the proposed extension, the GoNM will be able to complete critical activities under Component 1 and disburse the final payments to GMI beneficiaries, while also providing an additional 1,000 MKD (US$17.36) per GMI beneficiary per month for three months to help them cope with ongoing inflation in food prices. The disbursement estimates are also revised to reflect these changes. 19. Under Component 1, the GoNM will carry out critical activities as detailed below, and as agreed during the last implementation mission in May 2022. These activities were initially envisioned under this component, and this restructuring modifies the amount and types of goods to be purchased. It also provides additional time for the GoMN to complete ongoing critical activities, such as improvements in LFS of the COVID centers. These activities are: a. Medical equipment. Additional medical equipment is necessary for the GoNM to provide high-quality health services for COVID-19 patients and patients with chronic conditions who are at higher risk of severe COVID-19 disease. A combination of procurement arrangements, including Bank-Facilitated Procurement (BFP) and procurement through United Nations Office for Project Service (UNOPS), will be utilized to procure additional items of medical equipment as quickly as possible. The PMU estimates it will take at least 120 days for the medical equipment to be imported and delivered, after which additional time will be needed for its installation and the training of healthcare workers on its use. b. Medications. Antiviral medications to treat COVID-19 patients are now available on the market and the GoNM intends to procure these medications for patients at highest risk and those who need them the most. The PMU has the technical specifications and the tender documentation for the following COVID- 19 medications: remdesivir, monoclonal antibodies, molnupiravir, and tocilizumab. The PMU is currently in discussions with Pfizer on pricing and quantities for the drug Paxlovid and anticipates additional resource from the project to be devoted to the procurement of Paxlovid once negotiations are complete. c. Vaccines. The first restructuring of the project was carried out to allow the GoNM to allocate project funding under Component 1 to finance the purchase of vaccines. However, considering recent grant funding made available by the European Union (EU) for the procurement of vaccines, donations of doses received from some European countries, and the current stock of vaccines already available in the country, the number of COVID-19 vaccines to be procured though the project will be reduced from 660,000 to 150,000. The vaccines to be procured through the project are expected to be delivered between September and December 2022 (this is by design, as the vaccines currently available in the country are set to expire between July and October 2022). d. Public vaccination campaign. To increase vaccine acceptance and ensure timely administration of the vaccines currently available in North Macedonia and those that will be procured through the project in the coming months, communication to the public about the benefits and potential risks of the COVID-19 vaccines needs to be strategic. The public vaccination campaign was programmed under the first project restructuring, and will begin shortly, with the procurement already initiated under the project. This is a key activity of the project and is essential for ensuring that the North Macedonian population understands the benefits of vaccination and utilizes currently available vaccine doses, as well as those to be procured through the project. e. LFS improvements for hospitals/modular facilities. Following the Tetovo hospital fire, activities began to improve the LFS of modular facilities and ensure their compliance with E&S standards. Procurement of smoke and fire detectors and alarms and of oxygen detectors has started. Through the proposed restructuring, the GoMN will support hospitals/modular facilities to carry out the necessary activities to ensure compliance with the ESMF checklist, including inspections on waste management systems and LFS measures and the issuing of appropriate certification by the designated and authorized companies. The World Bank North Macedonia Emergency COVID-19 Response Project (P173916) 20. Project support to the refurbishment of health institutions will not go forward as planned. The project had initially planned to support the refurbishment of prioritized public health institutions for use in providing COVID-19 immunization; however, this activity was deprioritized following the Tetovo hospital fire and was ultimately never launched. For this reason, this activity will no longer be carried-out, although some civil works have been completed under the project. 21. Under Component 2, the GoNM will complete the final payments to GMI beneficiaries in the third quarter of 2022 and provide an additional 1,000 MKD per GMI beneficiary per month for three months to help them to cope with ongoing food price inflation. The payments were to be made between January and June 2022; however, with the proposed restructuring, the GoNM has requested that these payments be made between July and December 2022. 22. Reallocation: The GoNM requested that an additional EUR 2.355 million be allocated to Category 2(b) “Cash Benefit and Cash Transfer” to finance the provision of the additional payments of 1,000 MKD per month for three months to GMI beneficiaries. While this request was approved by the task team on March 31, 2022, this restructuring proposes to formalize this request by reallocating EUR 1.2 million from Category 1 to Category 2(b) and 1.155 million from Category 2(a) to Category 2(b). 23.
