PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the approval of the Country Director for a Level 2 Restructuring of the Paraiba Improving Water Resources Management and Services Provision (P165683). This is the first Restructuring of the Project. The proposed restructuring would comprise: (a) an 18-month extension of the Closing Date until December 31, 2027; (b) excluding activities related to the rehabilitation and expansion of the largest wastewater treatment plant (WWTP) in the Metropolitan Region of Joao Pessoa city (JPMR), non-revenue water (NRW) control contracts in three areas of the JPMR water supply system, and pre-feasibility studies of proposed dams in the Piranhas-Açu River Basin; (c) adjusting the results framework; and (d) updating the disbursement schedule. The proposed changes are needed to adjust the Project scope and reflect priority activities to be implemented with available funds and ensure the PDO is duly achieved. The Government of Paraíba made a formal request to the Federal Government of Brazil and the World Bank on May 29, 2025 (Ofício GG 237). The Project restructuring request was approved by GTEC (Grupo Técnico da Comissão de Financiamentos Externos) in its Recommendation No. 22 of July 30, 2025; and concurred by STN in the Parecer SEI Nº 4057/2025/MF, dated on October 28, 2025; and by PGFN’s in the Parecer SEI Nº 4184/2025/MF, dated November 25, 2025. 2. The Project was approved by the World Bank Board of Executive Directors on February 28, 2019, and signed on December 1, 2020. The Project was declared effective on January 19, 2021. The total Project cost is US$207.09 million, to be financed through a US$126,886,000 million IBRD loan and counterpart financing of US$80,200,000 million from the State Government of Paraíba. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is “To (i) strengthen capacity for integrated water resources management in the State; (ii) improve reliability of water services in the Agreste and Borborema regions; and (iii) improve the operational efficiency of water and wastewater services in the Joao Pessoa Metropolitan Region”. The Project has three components: (i) Integrated Water Resources Management; (ii) Improved Water and Sanitation Services Reliability and Efficiency; (iii) Contingent Emergency Response (CERC). 3. As of November 2025, 47.3 percent of loan resources had been disbursed with a total amount of US$60.07 million. Counterpart activities have progressed well, with 100 percent disbursement of the US$80.2 million already attained. 4. PDO progress has consistently been rated Moderately Satisfactory along Project implementation. The PDO indicators "Annual operational water management plan for the Paraiba River, issued for the years 2020, 2021 and 2022" and "Hydromet stations operational and feeding into the water information system" have been achieved; and the client reports significant progress in the indicator "Non-revenue water reduced in the JPMR". Two other PDO indicators “Number of beneficiaries with reliable water services”, and “Number of days with piped water in the benefitted cities of the Agreste and Borborema regions” are related to the implementation of the Curimataú and Cariri water mains and will be achieved gradually as the infrastructure is progressively completed and put into operation, starting in December 2025 5. The Implementation Progress (IP) has been rated Moderately Satisfactory since December 2023, reflecting a steady improvement in the Project implementation rate over the past years. Most of the major works and contracts are under implementation, including the construction of the Cariri water main and a potable water treatment plant, the implementation of one NRW control contract and an automation system for the water distribution network in the JPMR, and the construction of a wastewater pumping station and outfall in the JPMR. In FY25, the Bank disbursed US$34 million — 57% of the total funds disbursed to date — illustrating a positive trend. By December 2025, the Bank is scheduled to disburse another US$30 million, which will represent 71% of the loan proceeds. Most of the funds have been committed, representing 89% of the loan. The Procurement progress rating has consistently been rated as Moderately Satisfactory since effectiveness. Procurement processes took longer than expected as the project implementing unit (PIU) had to deal with different procurement models, including the Design and Build FIDIC Yellow Book, performance-based contracts, and conventional design and build processes. Currently, the Client is managing the challenges posed by the FIDIC Yellow book contract. 7. Financial Management progress is currently rated as Moderately Satisfactory (MS). There is still a need to focus on capacity building to ensure improvements in the accounting processes and internal control procedures. The overall Project FM risk rating is maintained as Moderate, as there have been no significant changes throughout implementation. The Borrower has no outstanding audit reports, nor are there any audit reports deemed unsatisfactory by the Bank. 8. Compliance with Environmental and Social Safeguards is rated as Moderately Satisfactory (MS), mainly due to the delays in compliance with OP4.37 on dam safety. The Camalaú Dam Safety Plan indicated the need for complementary works to meet the technical and regulatory requirements for dam safety and local legislation. As the first bidding process for these works failed, a new process will be concluded by March 2026. The additional studies, technical investigations and complementary studies for the Boqueirão and Poções dams are at bidding process stage. The studies on the Boqueirão and Poções dams and their potential complementary works remain in the critical path. An action plan is closely monitored to ensure that activities will be completed within the proposed revised closing date. 9. To date, all expected environmental and social management instruments have been prepared by the Client and are under implementation: a) the Environmental and Social Management Framework (MGAS); b) the Resettlement Framework; c) the Civil Works Manual; d) two Environmental and Social Management Plans (PGAS) for the Curimataú pipeline; d) the PGAS for the João Pessoa sanitary sewerage system works (pumping station II and outfall); e) three Abbreviated Resettlement Action Plans (PAAR) for the Curimataú pipeline works; f) PAAR for the works on the João Pessoa sanitary sewerage system; g) PAAR for the Cariri pipeline and potable water treatment plant; h) the Inspection Report for the Dams in the Paraíba River Basin for water supply; i) the PGAS for the works on the Cariri pipeline; and j) Environmental and Social Management Plan (ESMP) for the NRW control contract in the cities of João Pessoa and Cabedelo. There is a continuous improvement in the overall environmental and social management of the Project, even with the increase in contracts and works. The main construction works, Cariri and Curimatau water pipelines, wastewater outfall and pumping station, are underway with no significant impacts identified. There is a Grievance Mechanism in place and accessible. 10. Status of completion rates of each Component. The ratings for Components 1 and 2 are Moderately Satisfactory (MS), while the rating for Component 3, CERC is Satisfactory, noting that it has not been triggered yet. 11. At the closure of the MTR in October 2023, the Client and the Bank concluded that a substantial increase in the Project implementation pace had been achieved, with most of the objectives of the Action Plan agreed in the November 2022 and May 2023 missions already attained. The bottlenecks identified in earlier missions, including lengthy construction contracting processes with underestimated budgets, low market response to bidding processes, no prior experience with ICBs, especially with design and build (D&B) FIDIC contracts and performance contracts, had mostly been overcome. 12. Rationale for the Proposed Restructuring. At the completion of the MTR the Client and the Bank also agreed to restructure the Project to adjust its scope and targets mainly due to funding shortfalls. These shortfalls were caused by underestimated budgets, for example, the Cariri pipeline contract was US$85 million versus the initial Project budget of US$66 million, the wastewater outfall and pumping station budget rose from US$15 million to US$21 million, and the WWTP budget rose from US$9 million to US$27 million. The Project Appraisal Document (PAD) included a detailed economic and financial analysis that confirmed the project’s viability at appraisal. While the restructuring reflects higher costs and a reduced scope -notably the exclusion of the WWTP and NRW control contracts-, the remaining investments Official Use Only Page 2 The World Bank Paraiba Improving Water Resources Management and Services Provision (P165683) continue to generate important social and economic benefits, particularly in strengthening water security for the JPMR. A full re-run of the original model is not feasible at this stage due to the absence of the appraisal spreadsheets; however, a qualitative review suggests that, even with higher unit costs and reduced benefits, the Project remains justified given the magnitude of the expected improvements in reliability of supply, service coverage, and resilience. 13. With the approval of the Federal Government of a new lending operation that includes the funding of activities that will be dropped in this Project -the Paraiba Water Security Project (Northeast MPA Phase I), P511557-, the conditions are all set to proceed to the Project restructuring. The MTR also agreed to assess the need for an extension of closing date once all activities were contracted. Therefore, the proposed extension intends to cope with initial delays in implementation, mainly caused by: (a) a significant lag between loan approval and effectiveness, (b) the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic in early years of implementation, and (c) the complexity and innovation of procurement processes, which took longer than anticipated. In the last ISM, of May 2025, the proposed changes were duly confirmed with the Client. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 14. The Level 2 Project restructuring will consist of the following: 15. Extension of the Closing Date from June 30, 2026 until December 31, 2027 (18 months), as requested by the State Government of Paraíba to the World Bank through official letter (Ofício No. GG 237, dated May 29, 2025, delivered on June 2, 2025). This extension will secure the conclusion of works, the achievement of the PDOs, and ensure full disbursement of the loan proceeds. The conclusion of dam safety activities, as noted in paragraph 8, remains in the critical path to making sure no environmental post-closure issues are left. 16. Exclude the following activities from the Project original scope. The following activities, as described under the Loan Agreement (LA), will be excluded: i) the rehabilitation and expansion of the largest WWTP in the Metropolitan Region of Joao Pessoa city (JPMR), ii) control of water losses in three areas of the JPMR water supply system to improve operational efficiency (NRW control); iii) pre-feasibility studies of proposed dams in the Piranhas-Açu River Basin. The first two activities will be included in a new World Bank financed operation as highlighted earlier. Furthermore, the Project will still fund NRW control activities outside the three areas previously mentioned. No expenditures have been incurred or financed with Loan proceeds for the activities proposed for deletion. 17. Adjustments to the Results Framework. The Results Framework will be updated to reflect the proposed changes in the Project scope, including the targets and definitions of two PDO indicators -reduction of water losses, number of beneficiaries of two water mains-, the change of one PDO indicator -reduction of the biological oxygen demand (DBO) load-, and the definitions and/or targets of four intermediate indicators. The proposed adjustments are depicted on the table below. 18. Project Risk ratings and Environmental and Social Performance. This Project is classified as Category B under environmental and social safeguard policies. The environmental risk is Moderate as the potential adverse environmental impacts are being prevented and minimized and are site-specific. Most activities are implemented in altered areas, and most impacts during the construction phase are being properly prevented and mitigated. The social risk was considered Substantial due to the impact on land acquisition and physical and economic displacement. However, with satisfactory progress in implementation, coupled with the borrower's technical capacity, instruments and mitigation measures in Official Use Only Page 3 The World Bank Paraiba Improving Water Resources Management and Services Provision (P165683) place, and impact reduction, the risk was reassessed to Moderate. Performance with OP 4.12 has remained Satisfactory since 2020. 19. The proposed restructuring does not result in any modification to the environmental and social policies applicable to the Project. These adjustments will reduce the scope of activities, as well as the requirements for social and environmental risk management. 20. The Fiduciary risk and Technical Design ratings have been lowered to ‘Moderate’ as the capacities of the institutions involved in Project implementation -the State Secretariat for Infrastructure and Water Resources (SEIRH), the State Water and Sanitation Company (CAGEPA), and the Paraiba State Water Resources Management Executive Agency (AESA)- have improved and have been consistently rated as Satisfactory or Moderately Satisfactory since the MTR.
