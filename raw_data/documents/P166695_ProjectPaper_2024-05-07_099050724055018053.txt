No The World Bank GAMBIA FISCAL MANAGEMENT DEVELOPMENT PROJECT (P166695) I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING Project Status: 1. The Gambia Fiscal Management Development Project (FMDP – P166695), financed through a US$35 million International Development Association (IDA) Grant, was approved by the Board of Executive Directors on June 4, 2020, and became effective on July 28, 2020. To respond to a number of fiscal management challenges in The Gambia at that time, the original Project Development Objective (PDO) was “to increase fiscal space and improve the effectiveness of public resources management.” To achieve this objective, the project was organized around three components: (i) strengthening revenue collection and public procurement processes; (ii) supporting fiscal management of State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs) and restructuring plans of telecoms SOEs; and (iii) project implementation support and reform coordination. At the time of appraisal, the PDO and project components were aligned with the strategic objectives of the Government’s National Development Plan (NDP) and the World Bank Group Country Engagement Note for the Republic of The Gambia for FY18-FY21 (CEN n° IDA/R 2018-0139). 2. Progress towards the achievement of the PDO has been Moderately Satisfactory since October 2023, and all three PDO indicators on track to meet the project’s end targets as well as all nine Intermediate Results Indicators (IRIs) having already met their final targets. The PDO-1 sub-indicator measuring the “percentage of on-time tax filing rates (Corporate Income Tax [CIT] and Private Income Tax [PIT]) by large taxpayers” achieved a performance of 91 percent against an end-target of 95 percent, while the PDO-1 sub-indicator measuring the “percentage of on-time tax filing rate by all Value Added Tax (VAT) registrants” achieved a performance of 48.8 percent, thus exceeding the end target of 30 percent. The performance of both sub-indicators related to PDO-1 is likely to improve further given the additional time available for CIT, PIT, and VAT collection rates before project closure. The PDO-2 indicator measuring the number of “performance audit reports of State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs) prepared by the National Audit Office (NAO), discussed with the Public Enterprises Committee (PEC) of the National Assembly, and published on the website of the Ministry of Finance and Economic Affairs (MoFEA)” achieved a performance of three against an end target of five. Two additional performance audits of the Gambia Telecommunication Company (GAMTEL) as well as the Gambia Civil Aviation Authority (GCAA), are in final review stages and these are expected to be achieved before project closure in June 2024. Finally, the PDO-3 indicator measuring the “percentage of government procurement tenders by value that are single sourced” achieved an end-December 2023 rate of 35.84 percent against a project end target of 22 percent, thus not achieving the end of year target. This is attributable to a number of recent emergency procurements in the electricity and health sectors; however, no additional emergency single source procurements are anticipated given the current drive to curb single source procurements in 2024. As of the end of March 2024, single source procurements stand at 6.98 percent against an end of project target of 22 percent, and further improvements to the performance of this indicator are expected before project closure. Supporting these efforts is the forthcoming The Gambia Second Boosting Resilience and Unlocking Productive Potential, Development Policy Financing (Boosting Resilience and Unlocking Productive Potential Development Operation [BRUPP] P-502012), which includes as a prior action the adoption of a legal framework and implementation regulations for e-procurement in line with the 2022 Procurement Act, thus encouraging competitive procurement processes by enhancing transparency and oversight as well as establishing and reinforcing emergency procurement rules. 3. The overall project Implementation Progress (IP) has also been Moderately Satisfactory since October 2023. Critical progress has continued to be recorded over the past year across all three project components, including, among others: (i) the completion of the Gambia Revenue Authority (GRA) business re-engineering, Integrated Tax Administration System (ITAS) contract negotiations with the selected vendor, as well as the completion of the Tax Compliance and Risk Assessment Policy; (ii) the passage of the 2022 GPPA Bill by the National Assembly and the training and certification of 80 The World Bank GAMBIA FISCAL MANAGEMENT DEVELOPMENT PROJECT (P166695) procurement officials on core principles of procurement; (iii) the passage of the 2023 SOE Bill by the National Assembly as well as the appointment of the SOE Oversight Authority Board of Directors; (iv) the acquisition of the Gambia Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA) quality of service monitoring tool for 4G, billing verification system, electromagnetic radiation measurement tool, as well as the completion of a Spectrum Assessment Report; (v) the development of an Electronic Government Procurement (e-GP) readiness report, a validated strategy and roadmap for implementing an e- GP system, procurement manual, and standard bidding documents in line with the 2022 The Gambia Public Procurement Authority Act; (vi) the organization of a market test for The Gambia Telecommunications Company (GAMTEL)/The Gambia Telecommunications Cellular Company (GAMCEL); (vii) the public disclosure of three SOE performance audit reports (e.g., The Gambia Ports Authority [GPA], the National Water and Electricity Company [NAWEC], and the Social Security & Housing Finance Corporation [SSHFC]) by NAO, with an additional two forthcoming (e.g., GAMTEL and GCAA); as well as (vii) the cabinet approval of the national cloud strategy and migration policy. Annex 1 summarizes key project outputs achieved to date. Rationale for Restructuring: 4. A level two project restructuring is proposed in order to cancel funds expected to remain unspent as of the project closing date of June 30, 2024. The cancelation of funds not anticipated to be utilized before closure, including the remainder of the Project Preparation Advance (PPA) balance that remains in Category 3, will allow for the Gambia to retain these International Development Association (IDA) resources beyond the closure of the present IDA20 cycle. This would enable these resources to be reallocated to other projects in the Gambia portfolio, including the forthcoming Public Administration Modernization Program (PAMP - P176924). 5. Reallocation between disbursement categories is required to facilitate disbursement of Performance-Based Conditions (PBCs) related to two additional performance audits for GAMTEL and GCAA that were not previously certain to be achieved. These are currently being conducted and planned to be finalized by end-April 2024. This would trigger an additional disbursement of US$500,000 (i.e., US$250,000 for each performance report). This necessitates reallocation of funds to category 2 which was previously overdrawn (i.e., within the allowable 15 percent threshold) to accommodate the disbursement of PBC 1 (“Establishment of a centralized and independent regulatory agency (SOE Commission), approved by law to monitor financial and operational performance of the SOEs in its portfolio”) in October 2023. Requisite funds would be reallocated from category 1 and category 3, which are expected to be in surplus based on projections included in the FMDP 2024 Annual Work Plan (AWP) (Annex 2). Reallocation of funds from category 1 and 3 to category 2 will also allow for disbursements for activities pertaining to Part 2(a) as per the Amended and Restated Financing Agreement, namely, newly added technical assistance and capacity building activities to support the preparation of the forthcoming PAMP operation. 6. A marginal redistribution of funds across project components would be needed. This would reflect the above explained reallocation between disbursement categories. II.
