PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Program Status Program Background 1. This Project Paper requests approval to restructure the Senegal Agriculture and Livestock Competitiveness Program for Results (PCAE - P164967, Cr.6616). The proposed changes include revising indicators and targets, reallocating resources among Disbursement Linked Results (DLRs), and extending International Development Association (IDA) funding by one year to complete activities and maximize outcomes. This would be the third restructuring, following a request from the Government of Senegal on October 24, 2025, and recommendations after the October 2025 implementation support mission. The PCAE was approved on May 12, 2020, became effective on September 2, 2020, and aims to improve productivity and market access in priority value chains and livestock sectors. Previous restructurings added US$20 million co-financing from the International Fund for Agriculture Development (IFAD) in October 2022 and revised the Results Framework following the Mid-Term Review (MTR) in June 2024. Program Implementation Status 2. The October 2025 implementation support mission confirmed that progress is on track. The Independent Verification Agent has completed five verifications, confirming several DLR achievements. As of October 25, 2025, IDA disbursement is at 69.7% (Euro 95.43M of Euro 137M), while IFAD is at 74.6% (Euro 12.92M of Euro 17.32M). Most PrDO indicator targets are also being met: a. Groundnut yields standing at 1.02 tons/ha in 2024 against an annual target of 1.3 tons/ha, and a final target of 1.5 tons/ha. Irregular rainfall marked by a dry spell during the groundnut flowering stage led to this drop in yield from 1.34 tons/hectare in 2023/2024 despite the Government's efforts in terms of seed and fertilizer subsidy program. Increased uptake of certified seeds with the Program’s support would help offset climate vagaries and meet the target of 1.5 tons per hectare for adopting farmers. b. Likewise, millet yields of 1.06 tons/ha against an annual target of 1.30 tons/ha in 2024. However, the final target of 1.5 tons/ha remains within reach for farming adopting the new set of varieties released. c. Maize yields of 3.00 tons/ha in 2024/2025 reaching the annual and final target of the project. This is the highest level reached in the region thanks to the Program’s support to mass adoption of new high-yielding climate resilient varieties generated by Senegal innovation system and namely the Regional Research Center of Excellence (RCE) in dry cereals1. d. number of farmers reached with agricultural assets or services estimated at 350,263 (including 158,862 women - 45 percent of the beneficiaries) in 2024 outpacing the final target of 300,000; and e. Mortality of small ruminant decreased by an estimated 13 percent in 2024, exceeding the annual target of 8 percent and close to the final target of 15 percent. The indicator will be updated as part of phase 2 of the National Livestock Census (RNE), scheduled to start by 2025. 3. Only two PrDO indicators have yet to be achieved. The increase in commercialized milk volume is still unmeasured due to pending data collection. Groundnut exports rose sharply during the Program's first year but declined afterwards, falling short of targets: 319,786 tons exported in 2021 (139% of target), but only around 105,400 tons each year in 2022/2023 and 2023/2024 (38% of target). This decrease reflects a shift in government priorities, now favoring seed supply and domestic oil processing. Consequently, unless policy changes, the final export goal of 350,000 tons for groundnut will be not easy to meet. 1Centre d’Etude Régional sur l’Amélioration de l’Adaptation à la Sécheresse (Regional Study Center on Improvement of Drought Adaptation - CERAAS) is the CRE leading research in dry cereals in West Africa Page 1 Official Use Only The World Bank AGRICULTURE AND LIVESTOCK COMPETITIVENESS PROGRAM FOR RESULTS (P164967) The producer price for groundnuts in 2025 was set at FCFA 305/kg, which was not competitive against the global price of FCFA 285/kg. Lower global prices and dollar depreciation reduced exports further, to just 1,210 tons in 2025, making achievement of the final target unlikely. 4. With regards to disbursements, the annual and final targets for 13 out of 15 DLRs have been fully achieved (5 DLRs) or are claimed to be achieved pending verification from the Independent Verification Agent (IVA – 4 DLRs) or almost achieved (4 DLRs): see Table 1 below. Progress has been made in the restructuring of the Interprofessional Groundnut Committee (CNIA - DLR 6) and the establishment of the M&E manual and digital platform for agriculture and livestock sectors (DLR 10), aligned with the Government new sectoral policy. Significant strides have also been made in the restructuring of the Interprofessional Groundnut Committee (CNIA - DLR 6) and in preparing the M&E manual and digital platform for agricultural and livestock subsectors (DLR 10), in alignment with the new agricultural sectoral policy letter. There has been steady progress in increasing the acquisition of certified groundnut seeds (DLR 1) and expanding operational milk collection centers (DLR 9). Notably, certified groundnut seed acquisition has grown from a baseline of 40,000 tons in 2019 to 69,427 tons in 2021/2022, rising further to 75,846 tons in 2023/2024 and reaching 57,897 tons in 2024/2025. While the final target of 120,000 tons was ambitious, the achievements thus far reflect ongoing commitment and growth, with promising potential to continue improving over years with the promotion of the adoption of the new high-yielding varieties generated by the CRE/CERAAS. Additionally, the construction and rehabilitation of milk collection centers have resulted in the completion of 42 units out of the planned 72, marking important progress and laying a strong foundation for future expansion. These advances illustrate the Program's positive momentum and readiness to build on current achievements for even greater impact. As per the October 2025 implementation support mission, all key performance ratings are noted Satisfactory to Moderately Satisfactory. The financial management risk was downgraded to Moderate owing to important steps taken by the Ministry of Agriculture, Food Sovereignty and Livestock (MASAE) to lower this risk such as strengthening the management system of the Programme fund. The budget planning mechanism necessary for the implementation of the Programme activities is adequate and the arrangements for collecting budgetary and financial information are in place. Reconciliation carried out during the mission indicates that the amount disbursed by IDA is less than the expenditures made by the implementing agencies. However, regarding procurement, the risk remains substantial with the delay in implementing some contracts. B. Rationale for Restructuring 7. Overall, despite changes in regime and policy direction, the Government has confirmed the structure and relevance of the Programme and its PrDO as it aligns with its Strategic Plan for Food Sovereignty (PSSA 2024-2029) and the country long term development strategy, Senegal Vision 2050. All three result areas remain relevant and aligned with the Government priorities2. The PrDO remains relevant and most of the targets have been achieved and many of the DLRs have already met. However, two targets are unlikely to be achieved by closing date due mainly to policy shifts introduced by the new government and require further alignment with the new policies. As a result, no modifications will be made to the Program’s design, PrDO, Program Action Plan (PAP), or implementation arrangements. 8. Significant progress has been made although the two structural reforms will not be completed by the original closing date of December 30, 2025. Those are related to the restructuring of CNIA, and the development of a digital M&E platform for agriculture and livestock sectors. The country-wide decentralized quality consultations with different groundnut value chain actors necessary to change the longstanding governance issues of CNIA required more time than initially anticipated. 2 Programme results areas are: 1) Improved Crop and Livestock Productivity and Resilience; 2) Improved Business Environment and Market Integration; and 3) Improved Governance Sector Coordination and Program Management Page 3 Official Use Only The World Bank AGRICULTURE AND LIVESTOCK COMPETITIVENESS PROGRAM FOR RESULTS (P164967) Following the government transition in April 2024, which introduced a unified ministry overseeing agriculture and livestock, additional time was needed to develop the M&E manual and adjust the design of the M&E digital platform to align with updated sectoral policies. 9. In response to the Government’s strategic shift towards promoting domestic processing and value addition—especially in agricultural products such as groundnut oil—and considering recent measures like export bans, fluctuations in global groundnut prices, and the natural variability of rain-dependent production, the original PrDO performance indicator “Increase exports of shelled groundnuts” is being adapted. The updated indicator will now capture the “volumes of groundnuts formally commercialized by farmers,” for both exports and domestic processing. 10. Additionally, the PrDO indicator related to the “Increase in volume of milk commercialized” has not been measured during the five years of Program implementation, primarily due to challenges in regular data collection. The Government acknowledges the difficulty in tracking this indicator owing to the absence of a suitable decentralized data system. Consequently, while there has been progress in increasing milk commercialization, these achievements cannot be accurately quantified or reported. 11. Furthermore, many progress was made but the final targets of the DLI concerning the “Quantity of certified groundnut seeds acquired annually” is unlikely to be achieved, because of insufficient pre-foundation and foundation seeds (a prerequisite to the development of certifiable seeds). Recognizing the significant Government initiatives that support and strengthen the national seed system, in a manner which follows the natural steps to boost productivity, it is proposed to focus on the steps required to increase the quantity of certified seeds instead. Similarly, the target of the DLI related to the “Number of milk collection centers supported and/or equipped and operational” will be not achieved given delay observed in the procurement process of these works. Out of a target of 72 milk collection centers from a base line of 12, only 30 new centers have been completed. It is proposed to reallocate the funds which will not be disbursed into more pressing sanitary activities, in response to looming pandemia. 12. Significant progress is being made in the small ruminants’ vaccination against plague and could be further sustained. Additionally, the Government needs to provide an emergency response to the Rift Valley Fever (RVF) affecting livestock. RVF has become a major public health concern due to its rapid spread and incidence both on (i) livestock with 291 confirmed cases out of 1,672 tested, and 1,844 abortions registered; and (ii) human population, with 28 deaths reported among 311 confirmed cases and 229 recoveries as of October 25, 2025. 13. Altogether, the proposed restructuring aims at addressing the challenges associated with these PrDO indicators and DLRs by: (a) optimizing the Programme development outcomes, particularly through strengthening the national seed system to boost agricultural productivity and improve animal health through sustained vaccination efforts; (b) implementing essential reforms, such as restructuring the groundnut inter-profession (CNIA), launching a digital M&E platform to evaluate agricultural performance, and repurposing the input subsidy program on high quality certified seeds of new improved varieties for increased development impact; and (c) directing IDA resources to align with evolving Government priorities, achieving development goals and maximizing impact more effectively. 14. The restructured program will create the basis for the implementation of the Agriconnect, a World Bank Group (WBG)- sponsored global initiative aiming at increasing smallholder farmers participation to agriculture value chains for jobs and food security, that Senegal has embraced as first mover country. The proposed reforms and investments under this Program lay the ground for focused interventions – leveraging the WBG portfolio and in coordination with other development partners Page 4 Official Use Only The World Bank AGRICULTURE AND LIVESTOCK COMPETITIVENESS PROGRAM FOR RESULTS (P164967) and the private sector- around government priority programs and selected value chains including livestock and maize supported under this Program, for impacts at scale. For example, IFC and WB collaborate to boost livestock competitiveness by supporting animal feed production within the maize value chain. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 15. The following changes are proposed: A. Result Framework 16. The changes proposed for the result framework are related to the following PrDO indicators: (a) Increase exports of shelled groundnuts (PrDO indicator): The change consists of the reformulation of this PrDO indicator to “Increase commercialized shelled groundnut for exports and oil processing” with annual target remaining unchanged to 350,000 tons in 20263. The change addresses the access to markets part of the PrDO, ensuring alignment with government policy while also taking into consideration the volatility of the export markets. (b) Increase in volume of milk commercialized (PrDO indicator): The proposal is to remove this indicator given the challenge related to data collection in the absence of a decentralized monitoring and evaluation system. The issue is being dealt with support from the WB-funded Sahel Regional Pastoralism Support Project (PRAPS). 17. Regarding the DLRs and amounts allocated, the proposed changes are as follows: (a) Quantity of Certified Groundnut Seeds Acquired Annually (DLI 1): The proposal recommends splitting DLI 1 into four sub-DLIs: (i) DLI 1.1 “Quantity of Certified Groundnut Seeds Acquired Annually” with a reduction of the amount allocated aligned with a reduced target of 75,000 tons, reflecting the results achieved over the past five years in spite of insufficient pre-foundation and foundation seeds to reach the initial target; (ii) DLI 1.2 “Quantity of Pre-Foundation Groundnut Seeds Produced in 2025/2026 campaign” with an end target of 150 tons to be achieved between July 2025 and June 2026, recognizing progress made by the agricultural innovation system led by the Institut Sénégalais de Recherche Agricole (Senegalese Agricultural Research Institute - ISRA), which is instrumental in rebuilding the groundnut seed system and targeting six new high-yielding varieties: Jambaar, Sunugal, Kom-kom (short cycle) and 55-437 (long cycle), tailored to diverse agro-ecological zones. The increase in pre-foundation seeds will enable Senegal to develop more foundation seeds leading eventually to increase volumes of certified seeds; (iii) DLI 1.3 “Quantity of Foundation Groundnut Seeds Produced in 2025/2026 campaign” with an end target of 800 tons by June 2026, targeting these new varieties and involving private sector seed suppliers -including farmers cooperatives and seed producers- to strengthen the country seed system and boost productivity with the focus on the new climate resilient and high-yielding varieties; and (iv) DLI 1.4 “Removal of Skimmed Seeds from Subsidy Program” Given the volume of certified seeds which has reached a critical level, it is now appropriate for MASAE to formally adopt the announced decree to eliminate definitively skimmed or any uncertified seeds from the subsidy program in alignment with the Programme, and implement it, further advancing the hard-won gains in agricultural productivity. 3These end targets were defined realistically based on actual values achieved.
