Introduction and Context Country Context Russia’s invasion of Ukraine continues to impose an immense human and economic toll. As of September 2025, the United Nations (UN) report that 14,116 civilians have been killed and more than 36,481 have been injured since February 24, 2022. To date, over 5.6 million people have fled abroad and 3.3 million remain internally displaced. After a rebound in 2023, growth has been decelerating to 2.9 percent in 2024 and is estimated at only 2 percent for 2025. The 2025 poverty rate is estimated at 36.9 percent, compared to 20 percent in 2021. Frequent attacks continue to impact households and industrial capacity, and add to reconstruction needs, which the latest Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment assesses at US$524 billion over the next decade. The last quarter of 2025 is an inflection point for Ukraine’s macroeconomic management. Early in 2025, authorities had worked under the assumption that a ceasefire would be in place by year-end. With this assumption increasingly unlikely to materialize, macroeconomic planning now incorporates sustained higher defense spending and active efforts to mitigate the impact of uncertainty on private businesses despite continued active hostilities. The draft budget for 2026, which is under consideration by Parliament, assumes that the war will continue at least through 2026, keeping military spending and financing needs elevated, and opening new fiscal and external new. The inflection occurs amid intensifying structural pressure on fundamental growth and jobs drivers. Investment rates of 19 percent are below estimates of rates needed to converge to per capita income to levels that would enable EU accession within 15 years. Labor market frictions due to skills and geographical mismatches that have arisen since 2022 are increasingly moving to the fore, as half of companies report that a lack of skilled labor limits their ability to expand production, while at the same time barriers to labor market entry for vulnerable groups are mounting. Limited business dynamism, driven by a large state footprint, constrained competition and weak access to finance, continues to constrain productivity. Relationship to CPF The DPO is part of the WBG effort to support the people of Ukraine which has mobilized over US$81 billion since February 2022. The WBG engagement is guided by the Ukraine Country Program Update, presented to the Board in December 2024, which identifies “(1) continuing to provide a platform for ensuring uninterrupted provision of core basic services, (2) supporting repairs in critical strategic sectors, and (3) sustaining economic activity and supporting businesses, also by bolstering resilience in the financial sector and enabling access to finance�? as priorities. The Country Program Update further emphasizes that the WBG will continue to support key market-oriented policy and institutional reforms. This operation implements the strategy’s focus on policy and institutional reforms, with a focus on providing enabling conditions for private sector growth and jobs. C. Proposed Development Objective(s) The proposed First Ukraine Jobs and Private Sector Growth Development Policy Operation supports the authorities efforts to (i) enable private sector financing and investment, (ii) attract skilled labor to jobs, and (iii) advance EU market integration. Key Results The operation is structured around three pillars that correspond to the three parts of the PDO. Intended results under the first pillar include an expansion of public-private partnership (PPP) opportunities, enhanced access to finance, especially for micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs), and advancements on Ukraine’s privatization agenda. To attract skilled labor to jobs, the operation supports reforms that strengthen the housing market and expand the supply Page 2 Official Use Only The World Bank First Ukraine Jobs and Private Sector Growth DPO(P510090) of skilled labor. Reforms under the third pillar intend to reduce the costs of cross-border payments and enhance integrity, strengthen public support in agriculture, enhance cross-border electricity trade, and strengthen emission monitoring. D. Concept Description The proposed operation, the first in a series of two, supports the authorities’ focus on jobs, investment, private sector growth, and EU market integration. Important supported reforms are proposed to include an update to Ukraine’s PPP framework and the preparation of state assets for an eventual privatization. In the housing sector, the operation proposes to support a reform of Ukraine’s overall policy framework. To attract skilled workers to jobs, supported reforms focus on overcoming labor market entry barriers for specific groups, including veterans and women. EU market integration encompasses proposed supported legislation on electricity markets, climate transparency, agriculture, and cross-border payments. In addition to supporting crucial reforms in the result areas outlined above, the operation will provide US$500 million in IBRD financing (guaranteed by bilateral partners) to Ukraine. The operation is closely coordinated within the World Bank Group (WBG) and with other development partners, especially the IMF and the EU. It forms part of a broader international support package to Ukraine that includes a US$15.6 billion IMF program, which passed its eight review in June 2025. The EU Commission’s support to Ukraine is centered around the accession agenda and the EUR50 billion four-year Ukraine Facility, guided by the ambition to align per capita incomes outline in the Ukraine Plan. E. Poverty and Social Impacts, and Environmental, Forests, and Other Natural Resource Aspects Poverty and Social Impacts The reforms supported by this DPO are expected to have mostly positive or neutral risks. Enabling PPPs could yield positive indirect effects over time by mobilizing private capital for reconstruction and improving public service delivery, provided projects are well targeted. Enhancing access to credit for MSMEs can have direct positive effects, supporting job retention and inclusive growth, with disproportionate benefits for poorer and more vulnerable groups, including self- employed individuals and micro-entrepreneurs. Housing reforms are expected to generate positive direct and indirect impacts by expanding access to affordable housing for vulnerable and displaced populations. Enhancing the the supply of skilled labor can directly reduce poverty and inequality by addressing barriers to labor market participation, particularly for women and veterans. Privatization and energy market reforms could generate adverse impacts in the short term due to risks of job losses or energy price impacts, but these can be mitigated through country systems.
