Introduction and Context Page 2 The World Bank Girls Empowerment and Quality Education for All Project (P169222) – Additional Financing Request 2 Country Context 1. Sao Tome and Principe (STP) is a small island nation with a population of 215,000, located in the Gulf of Guinea off the coast of Central Africa. Despite experiencing significant economic growth in recent years, the country has struggled with poverty reduction, with nearly a quarter of the population living on less than $1.90 per day before the COVID-19 pandemic. The population is young and increasingly educated, with half of the people under 18 years of age. However, the current economic model, which is heavily reliant on Overseas Development Assistance (ODA), fails to provide adequate opportunities for the young and fast-growing population. 2. The macro-fiscal situation in STP is challenging, characterized by weak economic growth, rising inflation, high debt levels, and a tightening of available foreign exchange. The economy suffers from weak domestic revenue mobilization, high debt, decreasing public capital expenditure, rigid public expenditure procedures, and a reduction in public resources due to declining ODA. There is an urgent need for reforms to stabilize the macro-economic framework and create the fiscal space necessary to implement measures that will spur growth and reduce poverty. 3. Despite some progress in human capital indicators, the public expenditure growth-led model has not resulted in prosperity for the population. It has failed to create private sector-led jobs, and the quality of public services remains low, with persistent poverty. The private sector is weak and not contributing significantly to growth and development. Poverty is most prevalent among households with more children and those headed by women, with the poorest households being the largest, reflecting higher dependency ratios. Sectoral and Institutional Context 4. The education sector faces significant challenges despite notable progress in recent years. While enrollment rates have increased significantly, inequalities persist based on location, special needs, and gender. The quality of education remains a concern, with outdated pedagogical content, insufficient teacher training, and inadequate school infrastructure contributing to low learning outcomes. The COVID-19 pandemic further exacerbated these issues, highlighting the digital divide and the need for more resilient and equitable learning opportunities. Although distance learning programs were introduced, many students from poorer households and remote areas could not benefit due to limited infrastructure, lack of internet access, and inadequate equipment. This led to learning gaps for many children, underscoring the need for measures to address digital divides and ensure equitable access to quality education for all. 5. Despite achieving universal basic education in 2010, a significant number of children, particularly in rural areas, from poorer households, and among girls, are out of school. Financial barriers, such as school fees, pose a significant burden for poor families, contributing to out-of-school rates. Gender stereotypes, teenage pregnancy, school-related gender- based violence (SRGBV), and early marriage contribute to girls dropping out of school. Additionally, limited secondary education schools and safety concerns about girls traveling long distances to school due to GBV risks further limit access. 6. Data shows low acquisition of basic skills across all levels of education, with students lacking proficiency in mathematics and Portuguese. Learning outcomes vary significantly by region, with rural areas consistently underperforming compared to urban areas. Ineffective teacher management, including lack of clear protocols for teacher recruitment, deployment, and transfers, leads to ad hoc hiring practices and inefficient allocation of teachers. Many teachers lack adequate qualifications, including pedagogical training and tertiary education, contributing to low learning outcomes. Overcrowded classrooms, dilapidated infrastructure, limited equipment, and outdated textbooks hinder effective learning. The internal efficiency is low and there is weak management and accountability. Repetition rates are significantly higher than in other Sub-Saharan African countries, particularly in higher grades, due to factors like low teaching quality and lack of support for struggling students. High repetition rates contribute to a large number of over-age students in basic education, leading to overcrowding and high student-to-teacher ratios. School principals are often political appointments, lacking experience in education and accountability for school management and performance. The supervision system needs strengthening to ensure frequent and effective feedback to schools, improving teaching and learning. Limited coordination between central, district, and school levels leads to inefficient management of resources and a lack of accountability. The national student learning assessment system needs improvement to provide timely feedback and guide decision-making. The majority of the education budget is allocated to teacher salaries, leaving limited resources for investments in infrastructure, materials, and quality improvement programs. While fees are charged for various services, their provision is not guaranteed, and the continuation of programs is uncertain due to funding constraints. 8. Addressing these challenges requires a comprehensive and multi-faceted approach. The country is committed to transforming its education system through a comprehensive partnership pact focused on three key areas: promoting equitable access and quality learning, strengthening teacher training and appreciation, and enhancing the efficiency and coordination of sector management. The pact outlines specific actions and strategies to address the identified challenges, including curriculum updates, improved teacher training, increased investment in infrastructure and materials, and a strengthened data-driven approach to decision-making. Implementing these solutions require a strong commitment from the government, development partners, and the community, to create a more equitable and effective education system that empowers its youth and contributes to the country's sustainable development. C. Proposed Development Objective(s) Original Development Objective The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to equip girls with life skills and improve student learning outcomes for all. The Proposed New Development Objective The PDO has not changed. D. Project Description 9. The proposed activities will expand and maximize the impact of the Parent Project while remaining consistent with the original PDO. Additional indicators will be added to the Results Framework to capture the targets related to increasing the reach of each component. The additional financing will support the continuation and/or implementation of the existing project activities (see below). Specifically, the Girls’ Education Accelerator Grant funds will finance the incorporation of anti-violence activities into the existing girls’ and boys’ clubs, as well as the WASH and related Menstrual Health Management (MHM) activities, all under Component 1. 10. Component 1: Participation in the girls’ and boys’ clubs under the contract with the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) exceeded expectations under the Parent project, thus the AF aims to support the continuity of the sexual and Page 4 The World Bank Girls Empowerment and Quality Education for All Project (P169222) – Additional Financing Request 2 reproductive health education program and adolescent clubs, particularly in schools that serve the poorest and most vulnerable communities and where early pregnancy rates are highest. To maximize the project’s ability to promote safe, healthy, and inclusive learning spaces, the AF will incorporate violence prevention curricula in the ongoing activities, helping schools design a response action plan with surrounding communities. After some issues with procurement, the rehabilitation of WASH facilities under the Parent project is well underway. The AF will support the rehabilitation of WASH facilities in two additional schools to improve girls’ attendance and completion. Associated activities relating to the distribution of menstrual health kits and behavior change communication activities around MHM will also be included and implemented in partnership with NGOs in STP. While the minor works will be carried out in the two schools, MECC will take the opportunity to upgrade kitchens to better enable future school feeding activities. 11. The educational projects pilots will focus on improving learning outcomes in a comprehensive way, seeking to respond to the specific contexts of the school communities in which they will operate. The main objective is to create a healthier and more collaborative environment within the educational community. This involves focusing on combating violence, sharing documents and relevant information that impact school life, and fostering greater constructive proximity between the school and the community. To this end, schools will be selected in the poorest areas and those with the highest rates of teenage pregnancy. The pilots will be implemented by the MECC in partnership with the district offices of the 6 districts and Principe Autonomous Region (RAP), in one selected school per locality. 12. The operational steps to carry out activities are, inter alia, to: (i) process an addendum to the contract with UNFPA for the scale up of the girls’ and boys’ clubs, (ii) begin the rollout of the school violence prevention pilot, and (iii) finaliz e procurement activities for the additional two schools for WASH rehabilitation, including supervision contracts. 13. Component 2: Activities under Component 2 will improve and further develop the digital platform tested within the National Digital Learning Program (PNAD in Portuguese). The additional funds will ensure monitoring visits to schools by the support team's implementing partners, strengthen the use of the system through training in different areas, and create more supporting materials and guidance. More work will also be carried out to complete quality standards of the preschool curriculum. 14. The operational steps to carry out activities are, inter alia, to: (i) work with UNICEF to identify the operation plan for the next steps in the PNAD, (ii) support the Preschool Directorate (through technical assistance and operational costs) in the implementation of the preschool strategy, and (iii) rollout the Human Resource Management strategy with the critical actors in the MECC. 15. Component 3: Enhancing M&E capacity within the project is critical for better data management and increased staff capacity. The Parent project has worked steadily with the MECC PIU to ensure a smooth project implementation and to help focus government’s attention on both girls’ education and second-chance and inclusive education. The AF will continue this work by complementing existing investments in the revamping of the EMIS, supporting project management, operating costs, and enhancing capacity of MECC and PEREQT’s staff involved in the implementation of activities of the Parent Project and the proposed AF. This includes dedicated support to the agenda linked to making schools safer for girls, under the leadership of the Gender-Based Violence expert based in the MECC’s Directorate of Planning. The component will also support the operationalization of the special education strategy, given that roughly 709 children in STP are disabled and/or at risk of development, and half of these children do not attend school due to Page 5 The World Bank Girls Empowerment and Quality Education for All Project (P169222) – Additional Financing Request 2 the shortage of services, marginalization, and discrimination. The existing strategy is being revised under the Parent project to ensure global standards of inclusivity and gender-sensitivity be deployed under the AF. In addition, teacher policies will also be reviewed and operationalized as part of the overall Human Resources Management strategy within the education system, which includes special education teachers. The review of these policies will take a gender- responsive approach, for example, the review will include a diverse group of stakeholders who will work together to identify concerns and opportunities for both male and female teachers, with the view of achieving gender parity in STP schools for students, teachers, and school leaders. It is also expected that the deployment of the Human Resource Management strategy will result in greater efficiencies of scarce resources, more equitable deployment of staff, and greater opportunities for young women in the education sector.
