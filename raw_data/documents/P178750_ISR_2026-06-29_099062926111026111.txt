KEY ISSUES & STATUS Jun 29, 2026 Official Use Only Page 1 of 13 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report SENEGAL HIGHER EDUCATION PROJECT - ESPOIR-JEUNES (P178750) 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions The Senegal Higher Education Project - ESPOIR-Jeunes was approved by the World Bank Board on June 29, 2023, became effective on December 21, 2023, and is expected to benefit over 400,000 young Senegalese over its lifetime. As the only higher education project in the World Bank's Senegal portfolio, it stands as a strategic flagship for the Government of Senegal in its ambition to transform access to quality vocational and technical tertiary education. Component 1 — Diversification and Expansion of Access to Technical and Vocational Higher Education. Component 1 aims to increase equitable access to short-cycle professional higher education through the construction, equipment and support of 8 new Institutes of Superior Professional Education (ISEPs - Instituts Supérieurs d'Enseignement Professionnel) across Senegal. On the infrastructure front, all 9 sites, including the Diamniadio extension are legally secured with full cadastral documentation, and boundary wall construction contracts for all 8 lots were signed on April 30, 2026. The Resettlement Action Plan and Livelihood Restoration Plan (PAR/PRMS - Plan d'Action de Réinstallation et Plan de Restauration des Moyens de Subsistance) is signed and under active execution. Twenty-two Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM - Mécanisme de Gestion des Plaintes) committees are operational across all project zones, with 660 community members trained. The Project Management Oversight Firm contract (AMO - Assistance à Maîtrise d'Ouvrage), the indispensable prerequisite for initiating civil works across all 8 ISEP sites and 2 pilot childcare centers, is expected to be signed by end of June 2026, at which point physical construction will be formally launched. On the demand side, enrollment in project-supported ISEPs has reached 20,198 students as of June 2026—nearly double the 2026 intermediate target of 11,000—with women representing 54% of total enrollment, a result that reflects both the sustained relevance of the project's educational offer and the effectiveness of its gender inclusion strategy. Youth trained through the Professional Training Financing Fund (3FPT - Fonds de Financement de la Formation Professionnelle et Technique) stand at 5,494, effectively meeting the annual target. On curriculum development, two strategic domain priorities are taking shape: the ISEP-Thiès has been designated as the lead institution for childcare work, with training curricula under development across three certification levels and an initial cohort of 500 to 1,000 beneficiaries planned for the first phase ; the ISEP-Diamniadio has been designated as the lead institution for pharmaceutical and biomedical training, in a multi-institutional partnership with the Institut Pasteur de Dakar (IPD) and the Ministry of Health and Social Action (MSHP - Ministère de la Santé et de l'Action Sociale), with an objective of training 20 to 50 senior technicians annually in bioproduction in support of Senegal's health manufacturing sovereignty agenda. A national workshop to validate training domains across all ISEP filières is scheduled for July 6–7, 2026. Component 2 — Improving the Management and Governance of the Higher Education System. Component 2 seeks to strengthen institutional performance, research and digital transformation across Senegal's higher education system. All 8 public universities have signed their Performance-Based Contracts (PbC) and are actively implementing their approved procurement plans, with monitoring missions scheduled for late July 2026 to assess first-year PbC results. A landmark achievement of this implementation period is the successful completion of the first competitive research fund cycle, one of the most innovative features of the project and a defining milestone of the past six months. Designed to stimulate high-impact research directly anchored in Senegal's development priorities, the fund introduced a deliberately differentiated architecture structured around three project categories: (i) "Solutions-Pays" projects (6 projects at 150 million FCFA each, targeting mature innovations ready for industrial deployment or real-world testing); (ii) "Écosystèmes" projects (10 projects at 50 million FCFA each, built on multi-stakeholder consortia bringing together academia, the private sector and public institutions to break down institutional silos); and (iii) "Pépinières" projects (24 projects at 25 million FCFA each, designed to support early-stage research and nurture the next generation of young researchers). Proposals Jun 29, 2026 Official Use Only Page 2 of 13 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report SENEGAL HIGHER EDUCATION PROJECT - ESPOIR-JEUNES (P178750) were evaluated across six strategic thematic areas, agriculture and food sovereignty, economy and industrialization, health and well-being, environment and natural resources, digital and emerging technologies, and society and governance with social science integration mandatory for all science, technology, engineering and mathematics (STEM) projects. A public call for proposals generated a record 425 eligible applications, reviewed by 5 independent expert juries comprising 36 evaluators, with scientific oversight provided by the Institut de Recherche pour le Développement (IRD) through a dedicated Technical Support and Monitoring Service (SAST - Service d'Appui et de Suivi Technique) specifically created to reduce administrative burdens on researchers and strengthen the quality of outputs. The Steering Committee (COPIL - Comité de Pilotage) validated the final selection of 50 research projects—five times the 2026 target of 10—with contracts signed in June 2026. Eighty percent of winning teams meet gender parity requirements, and all research outputs are required to be published in open access and translated into national languages, ensuring broad societal reach and knowledge dissemination beyond academic circles. This cycle represents a genuine and durable contribution to Senegal's research and innovation ecosystem. On digital transformation, the Integrated Higher Education and Research Management System (SIGESR - Système Intégré de Gestion de l'Enseignement Supérieur et de la Recherche) is gaining momentum, with 11 technical expert contracts signed and a cybersecurity and information systems diagnostic actively underway. Component 3 — Strengthening Project Management and Monitoring and Evaluation. The appointment of a new Project Coordinator has renewed leadership into the Project Implementation Unit (PIU - Unité de Coordination du Projet), with tangible improvements in team coordination, fiduciary documentation and reporting quality. Social safeguards are actively managed: 10 regional Gender-Based Violence (GBV - Violences Basées sur le Genre) prevention workshops have been conducted, a partnership agreement with a non-governmental organization (NGO) is effective for Gender-Based Violence (GBV) survivor support across all 10 project regions, and mandatory codes of conduct have been signed by all contractors. On the childcare infrastructure front, the feasibility study for the two pilot childcare centers at Diamniadio and Thiès is advancing. Overall disbursement stands at approximately 10%, a figure that reflects the pre-construction sequencing of the project rather than any structural underperformance, and one that is expected to accelerate substantially in the second half of 2026 as civil works are initiated across all 8 ISEP sites. 5.
