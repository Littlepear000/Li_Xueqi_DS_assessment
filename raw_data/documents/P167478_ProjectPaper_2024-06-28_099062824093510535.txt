PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. Purpose: This restructuring paper seeks your decision on a proposed restructuring of the Guinea Project for Results in Early Childhood and Basic Education (PRePEF), which entails: (a) cancellation of funds; (b) revisions to component description and costs; and (c) adjustment of disbursement projections. The request to cancel Special Drawing Rights (SDR) 2.891 million (US$3.8 million equivalent1) was received on June 27, 2024. Progress towards achievement of the Project Development Objective (PDO) is rated moderately unsatisfactory as progress towards meeting key PDO indicators by the current closing date is lagging. Implementation progress is rated moderately satisfactory mainly due to increase in implementation pace over the past 18 months, which are not sufficient to reach all expected results. No changes are proposed to implementation, fiduciary or safeguards arrangements/oversight, and there are no outstanding audits. A. PROJECT STATUS 2. Background: The PRePEF grant in the amount of SDR 36.1 million (US$50 million equivalent2) was approved on July 9, 2019 and became effective on July 15, 2020. The project’s development objective (PDO) is “to improve access to, and the quality of early childhood and basic education in the Project Areas and to strengthen the capacity of the education system to deliver better results.” The project specifically aims to increase access to pre-primary education and reinforce capacity of decentralized education units in selected management areas (such as teachers’ recruitment/deployment, provision of non-salary recurrent budget to school, and information management). 3. Progress towards achievement of the PDO. Progress towards PDO achievement is currently rated moderately unsatisfactory given that there is insufficient evidence available at this time to indicate that most key indicators will be met by the current closing date of June 30, 2024. Nevertheless, there remain data analysis activities as part of the Implementation and Completion and Results (ICR) process, which may provide additional results and could alter the final rating. This analysis and new data would be reflected in the ICR report. To date, the project: (a) had 13,020 children attending public pre-schools compared to a target of 16,500, of which 18 percent were girls (compared to a 49.8 percent target); (b) exceeded the end target of recruiting 1,000 teachers by recruiting 18,000 grassroots teachers, but only training 784 of them. On the other hand, the project exceeded the number of female teachers recruited or trained (412 recruited or trained compared to a 360 target); and (c) fell short of reaching 16,500 students with direct interventions to enhance learning (actual 13,020 beneficiary students), of which 8,000 female students (actual 1,474). In addition, results to determine the percentage of stakeholders using net-accessible dashboards for decision-making purposes are not available nor are results of the early learning quality and outcomes-related indicator. As mentioned, these results (once available) and any other updates will be incorporated as part of the ICR process while leaving the current PDO rating at moderately unsatisfactory. 4. In terms of classroom construction, 550 classrooms are under construction in the four regions and at various stages of completion, of which 360 classrooms have been completed, while 32 contracts which are significantly delayed, are in the process of being transferred to another Bank-financed project (the Guinea Support to Local Governance 2 Project – P177095; PAGL 2). Finally, all classroom furniture has been ordered and is expected to be delivered prior to the closing date of June 30, 2024. The project also undertook gender-sensitive awareness raising campaigns to highlight negative effects of stereotypes and exclusion while promoting the positive effects of pre-schooling for all children. 5. Component 2: Improving Pre-school and Basic Education Quality using Technological Innovation. The project successfully supported the in-service teacher training policy and its implementation strategy, which included an experience-sharing mission to Benin in November 2023 to learn from their successful experience in this field. Teacher training modules on early learning in French and mathematics have been developed and the respective modules are currently being tested, after which they will be uploaded onto tablets. Currently, there are 19,620 tablets and 4,000 chargers being distributed to the different beneficiary schools. Finally, the project is in the process of training 216 additional pre-school teachers in order to reach the envisaged 1,000 target by June 30, 2024 (and meet the respective disbursement-linked result – DLR 2.2), and validate the teacher training modules prior to uploading them onto tablets (and meeting the respective DLR 2.3). 6. Component 3: Strengthening education sector management capacity. Capacity-building for prefecture staff to implement deconcentrated teacher recruitment has been strengthened through the recruitment of teachers at grassroots level. In addition, a mechanism to transfer non-salary recurrent budgets to schools has been put in place, and capacity building activities provided. However, this activity is significantly delayed and the relevant DLRs regarding piloting and executing a formula-based non-salary recurrent school budget (DLRs 4.2, 4.3 and 4.4) will be cancelled. In terms of strengthening UNESCO's system for collecting and using education sector data, only 15 out of 34 deliverables have been produced. Given the delay in implementation of this activity, and in view of the project's closing date, the remaining deliverables related to regularly uploading school-level data and use web-accessible data for decision- making purposes will be dropped and funds related to DLRs 5.2 and 5.4 cancelled. Supply of information, communication and technology equipment (solar panels, servers, laptops, desktops, etc.), which was based on a UNESCO diagnosis, is underway and scheduled to be completed by June 30, 2024 at the latest. Finally, the project successfully supported ministry of education staff in the assessment of learning outcomes, which will benefit the production of the different learning assessments (early grade reading/math assessments, pre-school quality assessments). 7. Component 4: Project Management and Monitoring and Evaluation. Project staff has been in place, providing the necessary follow up and progress reporting. All quarterly activity reports as well as the annual activity report were provided on time and deemed acceptable. As mentioned, the project coordination unit is in the process of cancelling the delayed construction contracts and transfer them to PAGL 2 to ensure their completion. In addition, steps need to be taken to ensure the current PRePEF technical staff is being integrated into PAGL 2 to continue supervision of these construction contracts and provide the necessary quality control. Given significant delays, the government requested the cancellation of DLRs under component 3 related to “Non-salary, recurrent budgets are operationalized at school level – DLI 4” and “Decisionmakers and other stakeholders have access to timely education system information – DLI 5. Specifically, they are: (a) DLR 4.2. “A formula-based non-salary recurrent school budget is being piloted”, for an amount of SDR 0.723 million (US$0.95 million equivalent); (b) DLR 4.3. “The pilot is evaluated and a roll-out plan is approved” for an amount of SDR 0.361 million (US$0.47 million equivalent); (c) DLR 4.4. “A formula-based non-salary recurrent school budget is allocated and transferred to at least 3,000 public primary schools” for an amount of SDR 1.085 million (US$1.43 million equivalent) (d) DLR 5.2. “School-level information for current school year is inputted by March 31 using mobile technologies in at least 90 percent of pre-primary, primary and secondary education schools (public, community and private), and integrated into a national database” for an amount of SDR 0.361 million (US$0.47 million equivalent); and (e) DLR 5.4. “Web-accessed dashboards are used by at least 20 percent of decision-makers and other stakeholders, with key education indicators and query functions linked to national database with disaggregation to school level” for an amount of SDR 0.361 million (US$0.47 million). 9. As a result, component 3 description is also modified by removing these activities from its project- supported activities. 10. The government and the World Bank are in the process of making adjustments to ensure that pre- school related construction activities can be completed after PRePEF’s closing date by incorporating relevant activities into Bank-financed operations such as PAGL 2. Related indicators will however remain unchanged due to the fact that these changes are being introduced too close to the closing date to provide an effective way to measure the impact of having reduced or dropped such indicators on overall project performance. Nevertheless, the ICR will consider these changes and any associated impacts in its final assessment. C. Reduction in component costs 11. Based on the above, the combined value of DLRs being cancelled amounts to SDR 2.891 million (US$3.8 million equivalent). As a result, component 3 “Strengthening education sector management capacity” will be reduced from US$9.49 million to US$5.69 million, equivalent.
