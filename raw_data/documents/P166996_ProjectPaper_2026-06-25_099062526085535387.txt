PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Project Status 1. The Ghana Jobs and Skills Project was approved on June 29, 2020 (IDA credit of US$200 million) and is expected to close on June 30, 2026. It’s PDO is to support skills development and job creation in the Recipient’s territory. The project is considered as one of the main forms of support for the Government’s top-priority agenda of upgrading skills among the country’s population, creating more and better-quality jobs, and improving job outcomes for youth. It connects success in these areas to state stability, social cohesion, and economic inclusion. 2. The project comprises five components, as follows. Component 1: Provision of apprenticeship training for jobs (US$60 million). Component 2: Provision of entrepreneurship and micro and small enterprise support for jobs (US$100 million), comprising two sub-components — 2.1: Provision of entrepreneurship training and competitive business start-up grants to individuals for jobs (US$40 million), and 2.2: Provision of competitive grants to private enterprises for expanded employment (US$60 million). Component 3: Operationalization of the Ghana Labor Market Information System (GLMIS), upgrading of district Public Employment Centers (PECs) and services, and independent performance reviews of government youth employment and skills development programs (US$30 million). Component 4: Capacity development, technical assistance, and project management support for enhanced skills and jobs impact (US$10 million). Component 5: Contingency Emergency Response Component (US$0 million). 3. The project is implemented by the following agencies: the Project Coordinating Unit (PCU) under the Ministry of Finance; the Commission for Technical and Vocational Education and Training (COTVET/CTVET); the Ghana Enterprises Agency (GEA, formerly the National Board for Small Scale Industries/NBSSI); the Ministry of Labor, Jobs and Employment (MJLE, formerly the Ministry of Employment and Labour Relations/MELR); and the Ministry of Environment, Science, Technology and Innovation (MESTI). B. Rationale for Restructuring 4. The cancellation of US$55 million was requested by the Government of Ghana on April 1, 2026, in the context of its alignment of the Ghana Portfolio with Ghana’s fiscal consolidation and national priorities. a. The restructuring follows the application of a financial cap to the project in CY2025 and CY2026 resulting in very low disbursements and constraining the execution of planned activities. b. The Government of Ghana has determined that the undisbursed funds cannot be fully committed and disbursed prior to the project’s closing date of June 30, 2026. c. This cancellation is consistent with the GoG's request and reflects the Government’s decision to free up unutilized IDA funds for other priority investments. 5. The cancellation will result in a reduction in scope which will cause the project to reach fewer beneficiaries than originally envisaged. However, this restructuring paper confirms that (i) the PDO indicators, which measure the sustainability of interventions rather than their scope, remain achievable by project closing; (ii) the performance of Official Use Only Page 1 The World Bank Ghana Jobs and Skills Project (P166996) the Borrower and its implementing agencies remains satisfactory and (iii) all legal covenants under the project have been complied with. It is noted that the 2025 external audit report and accompanying management letter, together with the status of implementation of the 2024 external audit recommendations as verified by the Ghana Audit Service remain outstanding. These are expected to be submitted to the World Bank by June 30, 2026, and their findings will be taken into consideration in the final accounting of project funds, following the project’s closure. 6. The proposed restructuring will not result in changes to the Results Framework (RF), the PDO, safeguard policies triggered, the environmental category, legal covenants, institutional arrangements, financial management, or procurement arrangements. The project closing date remains June 30, 2026. II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES 7.
