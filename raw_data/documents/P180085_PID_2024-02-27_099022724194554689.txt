Introduction and Context 1. An Additional Financing (AF) in the amount of US$60 million (US$20 million from National PBA, and US$40 million from Regional IDA window) to the Republic of Congo and of US$90 million Loan from IBRD to the Gabonese Republic is proposed for the Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West and Central Africa Series of Projects 2 (HISWACA-SOP2). The AF will scale up this regional Series of Projects by integrating the Republic of Congo and the Gabonese Republic. The Project Development Objective (PDO) will remain the same. Feb 15, 2024 Page 3 of 12 The World Bank Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West and Central Africa - Series of Projects Two (HISWACA - SOP 2) (P180085) 2. The HISWACA-SOP2 Project (P180085) was approved by the Board of Executive Directors on September 28, 2023. The Project seeks to improve the quality, accessibility, and production of regionally comparable statistics, to improve the Statistical Performance of participating countries, the degree to which their National Statistical Systems meet user needs and contribute to better decisions. The Project also focuses on modernization of methods, organization, physical infrastructure and Information and Communication Technology (ICT) of participating countries’ Ministries, Departments, and Agencies (MDAs) responsible for statistics, including National Statistics Offices (NSOs), to support adoption of emerging alternative methods to complement traditional statistics, and to adequately fulfill their mandates. The use of regional approach fosters coordination among participating NSOs and MDAs to generate positive externalities by making available methods and tools that could be used beyond the participating countries. 3. The parent project includes four Recipients, three countries, namely, Cameroon (US$105 million IDA Credit), Chad (US$105 million IDA Grant), and Central African Republic (US$60 million IDA Grant), and a regional organization, namely the CEMAC Commission (US$20 million IDA Grant), for a total amount of US$290 million. It was declared effective for Chad on December 22, 2023, and for the Central African Republic on January 12, 2024. The Financial agreement (FA) for CEMAC was signed on November 11, 2023, and the declaration of effectiveness deadline is planned for March 18, 2024. For Cameroon, the FA is expected to be signed by end April 2024. The request for the first disbursement for Chad ($12.4 millions) and CAR ($4.9 millions) is under process. These amounts will be used to finance the preparation of the population census including, the pilot of the cartography, the pilot of the census, and the whole cartography. The acquisition of materials, equipment and architectural studies of the constructions will also be carried out under these first disbursements. The HISWACA-SOP2 Project is expected to be implemented over five years during 2024-2029. __ Country Context Republic of Congo 4. Republic of Congo is a low middle-income country located in central Africa. It has a population of 6.14 million people and a gross domestic product (GDP) per capita of US$ 2,6491. The Republic of Congo faces substantial development challenges, including widespread poverty and inequality, limited structural transformation and an undiversified oil dominated economy. Almost half of the population live in poverty and inequality remains high because growth and prosperity were not equitably shared in the country. The Gini index increased from 47.3 in 2005 to 48.9 in 2011. 5. The Republic of Congo recent economic growth and poverty reduction have been derailed by various factors including a seven-year recession that led to a dramatic drop in income per capita and has put the country at risk of falling back into low-income status. Between 2015 and 2021, Congo’s real GDP declined by 19 percent and GDP per capita by 31 percent. The 2014-16 collapse in oil prices plunged the economy into a prolonged recession resulting in massive cuts in public expenditures and investments, and the accumulation of domestic arrears to private sector firms, which has had a negative impact on private investment. The COVID-19 crisis further exacerbated the economic recession that began in 2016. The Gabonese Republic is an upper middle-income country located in central Africa. The country has an estimated population of 2.4 million inhabitants with a gross domestic product (GDP) per capita in 2022 of US$ 8,8203. The Gabonese Republic faces a challenge of reversing a decades-long trend of insufficient resource-based growth and persistent poverty, a third of the population live in poverty. Over-reliance on natural resources has left the Gabonese economy undiversified with growth, exports, and fiscal revenues all dependent on extractives. Despite the country’s abundant natural resource wealth, growth translated into slow poverty reduction. In the context of the multiple shocks over the last ten years including low oil prices, the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 and impact of the Ukraine war, GDP growth gradually dropped from 7.1 percent in 2010 to -1.8 percent in 20204. Inequality, measured by the Gini index, is at a moderate level of 38 percent, but wide geographic disparities in poverty, specifically between urban and rural areas, and significant social inequalities undermine prospects for shared prosperity and sustainable poverty reduction. 7. The Gabonese Republic’s economic recovery from the COVID-19 crisis intensified in 2022 driven by dynamic wood, manganese, agriculture, and services sectors. The economy grew by 3.0 percent in 2022, supported by strong demand from China and higher oil production. The rebound of the economy induced the decline of poverty from almost 36 percent in 2020 to 32.3 in 2022, but poverty is estimated to have increased in 2023. __ Sectoral and Institutional Context 8. Sub-Saharan Africa has made substantial progress in the production of statistics over the last 20 years. From the early 2000s, strategic planning through National Strategies for the Development of Statistics (NSDS) has been adopted, with most African countries having implemented one or more strategies by 2020. Several countries use the 2008 System of National Accounts (SNA). The geographic coverage and timeliness of the Consumer Price Index (CPI) has also improved. External trade statistics are harmonized in many countries. The number of household surveys that collect data on monetary and non-monetary dimensions of poverty has increased. Between 2016 and 2018, for instance, 45 countries in Sub-Saharan Africa conducted household surveys. The increase in the World Bank Statistical Capacity Indicator (SCI) confirms the overall progress in statistical capacity building in SSA, but the continent still lags other regions5. The SCI for SSA countries (excluding high-income countries) increased from 55.2 in 2004 to 59.0 in 2019. In the participating countries, one of the main challenges in their National Statistical System (NSS) is the availability of statistical data. This includes outdated data, particularly in terms of population census and household surveys, economic statistics, and lack of light data production system to monitor SDGs. For example, in the Gabonese Republic, the last population census was conducted in 2013 and the last household survey in 2017. According to UN guidance, population census should ideally be conducted within a ten-year period. The frequency of household surveys has also changed from 5 to 3 years, which poses difficulties for countries with limited or no funding for statistics; making it harder to produce and disseminate relevant statistics on a regular basis. In the case of the Republic of Congo, it took 11 years to implement a new household survey for poverty monitoring. However, with the support of a previous World Bank financed statistical projects, both countries have made some progress in addressing fundamental data gaps. This includes extending the coverage of the CPI, conducting living standard household surveys, national accounts, and enterprise census, among other improvements. 10. Building on these foundations, there is an opportunity and need to further improve the timeliness and quality of core statistical products and services, and to fill data gaps on critical emerging areas including climate change. The lack of such information hampers the design of effective poverty eradication programs and development policies. Having accurate and timely data is crucial for policymakers to make informed decisions and allocate resources effectively. Some sectoral statistics, such as public and private finance statistics, trade statistics, industrial statistics, and price statistics, are also insufficient and of low quality to assess the performance of the economy. Overall, the AF participating countries face significant challenges in terms of statistical data availability, which impacts the countries’ ability to accurately measure and monitor key socioeconomic indicators and design effective development policies. __ 11. Additionally, limited capacity, institutional weaknesses, poor workspace, and inadequate technical and ICT platforms further contribute to the challenges of the Gabonese Republic and the Republic of Congo NSSs. One of the critical issues is the lack of personnel with advanced degrees in statistics, demography, and economics in the statistical institutes of both countries. This shortage of qualified professionals hampers the ability of the NSSs to effectively analyze and interpret data. In addition to staffing and technical capacity shortages, the working conditions in Gabon's General Directorate of Statistics are not ideal. The statistical office is housed in old buildings that are space constrained, cannot be further upgraded structurally, and lack the technological infrastructure required to improve the performance of staff and the quality of statistical production. These shortcomings limit the ability of the NSOs to effectively communicate and share statistical information with the public and other stakeholders. C. The key aspects of the Project Development Objective (PDO) are the improvements of (i) the statistical performance of the NSSs; (ii) quality of data access and use; (iii) harmonization of statistics and (iv) modernization of NSOs. The project also aims to support the modernization process of the regional statistical systems. The focus area of the statistical performance component of the NSSs relates to their ability to generate data based on international standards, in a timely manner, and their wide availability and use. The harmonization component of the PDO aims to enhance statistical cooperation in the region to produce comparable and quality statistics which means that the NSOs (a) are provided with common regional guidelines and (b) have trained staff with adequate skills to implement them. Another requirement to produce these harmonized statistics is the availability of financial resources at country levels to undertake statistics operations needed to implement the provided regional guidelines. The data access component focuses on making widely available statistical products and reports and micro-data to users. 13. D. Project Description 14. The proposed additional financing is the scaling up of the HISWACA-SOP2 Regional Statistics Project (P180085) to include the Republic of Congo in the amount of US$60 million (National PBA US$20 million and IDA Credit US$40 million from Regional IDA window) and US$90 million loan from IBRD to the Gabonese Republic. 15. The parent project includes the following four components: • Component 1: Harmonization and Production of Core Statistics using International Data Quality Standards: This component aims to support the regional statistical harmonization process and the production of quality core statistics to improve data sources and data infrastructure pillars of the SPI. It addresses the challenges related to the quality and harmonization of statistics to allow comparability of statistics in the region. Its main objective is to promote the adoption of international standards and bridge the gap in the production of a core set of Feb 15, 2024 Page 7 of 12 The World Bank Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West and Central Africa - Series of Projects Two (HISWACA - SOP 2) (P180085) economic, social, and demographic statistics that will improve data availability to meet the increasing demand for statistics, especially indicators needed for monitoring the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Through this component, the CEMAC commission will strengthen its capacity to effectively advocate for improved statistics in the region. • Component 2: Statistical Modernization, Institutional Reform, Human Capital, Data Accessibility, and Use. This component aims to support statistical modernization; institutional reforms; improvement to data access, dissemination, and use; and human resources development in NSOs and selected ministries, departments, and agencies (MDAs) of participating countries in their transition towards an Integrated National Data System. This component focuses on exploiting the potential of new data sources and leveraging advanced technologies to integrate data from diverse sources in a cost-effective manner. Furthermore, this component recognizes the importance of investing in human capital by providing academic training in statistics and on-the-job training of staffs. Finally, this component supports institutional reforms of statistical systems in participating countries, including statistics regulations and data protection frameworks. • Component 3: Construction, Upgrading, and Modernization of Physical Infrastructure: This component aims to strengthen the capacity of National Statistics Offices and statistical schools by providing them with the needed equipment and tools to adequately fulfil their mandates. This component will support the construction of new buildings or the rehabilitation of physical and ICT infrastructure for the NSOs and schools of statistics. • Component 4: Project Management, Monitoring and Evaluation is responsible for overseeing the implementation of the project in each participating entity and ensuring effective and efficient execution of __ project activities. 16. The AF will support production and dissemination economic and socio-demographic and statistics. The project will finance key statistical data collection operations: population censuses (finalization of the Republic of Congo 2023 population census and the implementation of the Gabon 2024 population census); enterprise census in Republic of Congo; Harmonized Household Living Conditions Surveys (EGEP3 in the Gabonese Republic and EHCVM2 in Republic of Congo); the Demographic and Health Survey (DHS) in the Gabonese Republic and Republic of Congo (co-financed by the Global Fund); annual labor force surveys in both countries; agriculture census in the Republic of Congo and agriculture surveys in both countries. Because employment statistics are weak in both countries, the project will establish a system of regular employment statistics, starting with a national baseline survey followed by a quarterly employment survey. Additionally, the project aims to strengthen the routine data collection system in key sectors such as education; employment statistics monitoring; agricultural production including livestock and fisheries; and the establishment of an electronic platform for statistical and fiscal forms. Regarding data dissemination, the project will support the development of a national data dissemination policy and the establishment of a national data portal to disseminate socio-economic data. Additionally, economic, and public finance statistics are an important aspect of the project, in line with the Republic of Congo’s vision to comply with the IMF Enhanced General Data Dissemination Standard (e-GDDS). The project will support the rebasing of the national accounts and the production of quarterly accounts; improvement of economic monitoring; expansion of the CPI; and enhancement of macroeconomic forecasting models. The AF will inform crucial policy decisions related to climate change adaptation in the Republic of Congo and the Gabonese Republic. The agriculture and livestock census funded by this project will be a critical input into the design and implementation of agricultural surveys, an important source of data and evidence to understand performance in this sector, where most of the poor and vulnerable households earn their living. Conducting agricultural censuses and producing agricultural productivity statistics will also allow tracking of the use of agricultural inputs to improve crop resilience; reduce energy consumption and greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions; as well as climate risks and impacts. 18. The AF will support human capital, institutional reforms, and modernization of physical and ICT infrastructure in both countries. As part of the project, capacity building of human capital through academic and continuous training will provided, as well as sector coordination support to strengthen the capacity of statistical services of the sectoral ministries to produce statistics for their respective sectors. The project will look at the simplification and improvement of data collection systems; modernization of human resources management and the NSOs. Furthermore, reform of the national statistical system is ongoing, including the establishment of statistical training programs in a university or national school in the Gabonese Republic and support for the reform of the national statistics school of the Republic of Congo. In addition, the project will build on the infrastructure modernization efforts of the CG statistics capacity building project (PSTAT) in the Republic of Congo to expand the national institute of statistics and the statistics school (INS-CASP) complex and construct an office building for general directorate of statistics (DGS) in the Gabonese Republic. In the Republic of Congo, construction and rehabilitation activities will take place on the existing NSO premises. Rehabilitation in the Brazzaville site is related to the newly constructed NSO __ building where some finishing activities that remain will be finalized (including interior finishing, exterior signage, and air conditioner installation). The construction activities include extending the newly built statistics school (classrooms; lecture halls, conference rooms) and constructing a data lab and conference center for the NSO and the statistics school. In the Gabonese Republic, the project will construct and equipment an office building for the statistical office on the site provided by the Government.
