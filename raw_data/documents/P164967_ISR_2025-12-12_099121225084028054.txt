KEY ISSUES & STATUS 3.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions Significant progress has been noted in achieving the Program’s Development Objectives (PrDO), with four out of six annual indicator targets reached: (a) groundnut yields standing at 1.02 tons/ha in 2024 against an annual target of 1.3 tons/ha, and a final target of 1.5 tons/ha. Irregular rainfall marked by a dry spell during the groundnut flowering stage led to this drop in yield from 1.34 tons/hectare in 2023/2024 to 1.02 tons/hectare in 2024/2025 despite the Government's efforts in terms of seed and fertilizer subsidy program. Increased uptake of certified seeds and favorable rainfall suggests promising forecasts for the 2025/2026 agricultural season, with the target of 1.5 tons per hectare within reach; (b) maize yields of 3.00 tons/ha in 2024/2025 reaching the annual and final target of the project; (c) millet yields of 1.06 tons/ha against an annual target of 1.30 tons/ha in 2024, and a final target of 1.5 tons/ha that could be reached given the favorable rainfall; Dec 12, 2025 Page 1 of 18 The World Bank Implementation Status & Results Report AGRICULTURE AND LIVESTOCK COMPETITIVENESS PROGRAM FOR RESULTS (P164967) (d) number of farmers reached with agricultural assets or services estimated at 350,263 (including 158,862 women - 45 percent of the beneficiaries) in 2024 compared to a final target of 300,000; and (e) a decrease in small ruminant mortality estimated at 13 percent in 2024, exceeding the annual target of 8 percent and close to the final target of 15 percent. The update of this indicator is planned as part of phase 2 of the National Livestock Census (RNE), scheduled for November 2025. The Program made good progress in meeting the disbursement-linked indicators (DLIs), with 13 out of 15 DLIs whose targets have been met or nearly met. Regarding the overdue institutional reforms, the National Agro-Pastoral Development Fund (FNDAPS) have enabled significant progress in the restructuring of the Interprofessional Committee of Groundnut (CNIA), among other actions: the implementation of a restructuring action plan, the establishment of a standardization committee responsible for reviewing CNIA’s statutory documents; the preparation of the CNIA-State Framework Agreement by the inter-ministerial committee established. On the financial side, the Program’s expenditure framework for 2025 is largely covered, with 49 billion FCFA budgeted and a satisfactory execution rate of 72.74% as of October 9, 2025. Key decisions Considering achievements made, to ensure a satisfactory Program closure, two following key decisions were agreed with the MASAE: 1. Acceleration of Disbursements: Finalize the review by the Independent Verification Agent to unlock disbursements for the achieved DLIs (DLI 3, 4, and 5) by the end of December 2025. 2. Program Restructuring and Extension of closing date: Extend the Program’s closing date to December 31, 2026 to allow completion of critical activities, revise the results framework, and reallocate remaining resources to optimize the development outcomes of the Program and the disbursements. 4.
