Components Changes to Project Components Page 1 The World Bank CAR Investment and Business Competitiveness for Employment (P176274) – Additional Financing Request 1 The AF retains the three-component framework of the parent i-Compete project without structural change. Component 1 (Investment Reforms, Facilitation, and Coordination) continues to support the legal and regulatory reform agenda, investment promotion and facilitation capacity, and inter-agency coordination, extended to include reforms that strengthen the economic inclusion of refugees, including access to work, business registration, and financial services, alongside the analytical and validation work required to establish readiness of candidate economic zones. Component 2 (Access to Finance) scales existing financing instruments to reach a larger and more geographically dispersed set of firms. Component 3 (Supporting Businesses and Workers) continues through Subcomponent 3.1 on firm capabilities and value chain linkages and Subcomponent 3.2 on skills for employment, with delivery extended to new locations. The AF is a continuation and scaling of an operation whose institutional architecture is functional, whose reform milestones have been met ahead of schedule, and whose delivery systems are already operating. What changes is ambition, scale, and geographic reach. The principal dimension of scale-up is the extension of activities to displacement-affected areas where refugee and host community populations are concentrated alongside the country's productive economic zones. Under Component 1 this includes work on the regulatory conditions governing refugee economic participation, so that firms and workers in these areas can operate on a clearer legal footing. The geographical location of the applicant companies will be known after their selection, however, it can be assumed that the majority of Page 3 The World Bank CAR Investment and Business Competitiveness for Employment (P176274) – Additional Financing Request 1 companies in the targeted sectors will be located in urban areas, with agricultural and food companies being the exception and may be located in rural areas. Under Component 1, there may be limited investment in upgrading CAPMEA and the CMCAA's offices and developing additional physical presences in Bangui and 2-3 other cities. However, no new construction is envisaged under the project. Under Component 2 Partial Credit Guarantee Fund will be supported for which an ESMS will be developed including screening and monitoring of it’s sub-projects. The environmental risks will be limited, mitigable, and directly related to the small-scale development works envisaged. The environmental risk is considered moderate, especially considering that these activities will be carried out in a highly controlled environment. The social risk rating is Substantial at this stage of project preparation. Under Component 1, Provide Business Support Services to Strengthen MSMEs, activities to be developed include the capacity building/training of MSMEs including entrepreneurs especially women, grants and technical assistance to develop business support service providers, business associations, NGOs, training providers and private sector development organizations. The creation of e-services/e-commerce networks, through the digitalization of interfaces through some development of systems on payment of tax that focus on citizens and businesses(especially women entrepreneurs). In addition, this component will fund events, and information services. As these activities will target many MSMEs leading or belonging to women, support services provided will need to ensure that any risks of harassment both through online and face-to-face capacity building/training and grants are addressed. Moreover, the criteria for allocating grants should be clearly designed, fixed and publicly disclosed in a transparent process of selection of beneficiaries. An inappropriate or flawed selection process could create social tension among MSMEs or their employees/stakeholders. Same attention should be paid under Component 2 concerning the increase of credit provided to MSMEs. The SEA/SH is substantial. Due to the nature of the activities (especially technical assistance, training, grants, credit) that will be provided to MSMEs that are mostly owned by women and women entrepreneurs, there are risks of SEA/SH that may arise. Therefore, to mitigate this risk, procedures such as an SEA/HS assessment and action plan will be required and a preliminary version has been developed prior to appraisal. In addition, a code of conduct and awareness of sexual exploitation and abuse, especially among all service providers, will also be needed.
