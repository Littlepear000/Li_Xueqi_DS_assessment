No Page 1 of 56 The World Bank Morocco Green Generation Program-for-Results (P170419) I. PROGRAM STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING 1. Program overview. The US$250 million Morocco Green generation Program-for-Results (PforR) was approved on December 15, 2020, with a closing date of December 31, 2025, and became effective on February 25, 2021. The Program is co-financed by the World Bank (EUR 214.2 million) and the French Agency for Development (EUR 100 million). The advance of the World Bank loan (EUR 53.55 million) was disbursed on March 23, 2021. The Program Development Objective (PDO) is to increase the economic inclusion of youth in rural areas and the marketing efficiency and environmental sustainability of agri-food value chains in the Program area. The Green Generation Program covers three Results Areas (RAs): (i) Increased job opportunities and income generation for rural youth; (ii) Improved efficiency and sustainability of agri-food marketing systems; and (iii) Enhanced digitalization of agriculture and adoption of climate smart practices. . 2. Institutional arrangements. The Program Management Unit (PMU) was formally created on November 10, 2021. Four Steering Committees took place on February 14, 2022, November 16, 2022, June 21, 2023 and November 13, 2023. The fifth Steering Committee is scheduled for June 28, 2024. A consulting company has been providing technical assistance to the PMU since March 2023 and has contributed to enhancing the performance of program management to a level that is considered satisfactory. 3. Implementation progress. Despite the delays in some activities, as of June 2024, 11 Disbursement Linked Results (DLR) have been fully achieved and three partially achieved out of a total of 30 DLR as described below for each Result Area (RA): a. Result Area 1 (Increased Job Opportunities and income generation for rural youth) records 2 DLRs fully achieved and one partially achieved out of a total of 10 DLRs. Despite the delays in the achievement of some indicators, a solid operational set-up has been put in place with the Centres Régionaux des Jeunes Entrepreneurs Agricoles et Agroalimentaires (Regional Centers for Young Agricultural and Agri-Food Entrepreneurs, CRJEAA) established in the Program areas. The Centers support youth entrepreneurs in the preparation of business plans and to access financing. The first two years of the Program (calendar years 2021 and 2022) have been dedicated to the operationalization of 12 centers; it is now expected that progress towards the achievement of indicators will accelerate. b. Result Area 2 (Improved efficiency of agri-food marketing systems) records 7 DLRs and 2 DLIs fully achieved and 1 DLR partially achieved (out of a total of 13 DLRs); however, the DLI/DLR related to the four wholesale markets will need continuous attention. The first part of program implementation has been dedicated to feasibility studies, the selection of market development companies and the preparation of the key documents for the financing of the wholesale markets (agreements). The second part will focus now on creating the management structure, building the modernized wholesale markets and operationalizing them. c. Result Area 3 (Enhanced digitalization of agriculture and adoption of climate smart practices) is showing a satisfactory implementation with 2 DLRs fully achieved and 1 DLR partially achieved out of a total of 7 DLRs. 4. Key performance indicators. Based on the findings of the MTR and the supervision mission carried out in May 2024, the ratings measuring progress in the Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) of June 2024 were as follows: (i) Program Development Objectives (PDO) was downgraded to Moderately Unsatisfactory (MU) to reflect delays in RA#2 that requires an extension of the closing date to achieve the PDO; (ii) Implementation Progress (IP) and Fiduciary and Safeguards are maintained at Moderately Satisfactory rating; and (ii) the ratings for M&E, DLI and Technical are Satisfactory (S). 5. Disbursement. The disbursement rate is 57.4 percent and 57.1 percent respectively for the World Bank and AFD including advances, and 32.4 and 32.1 percent without the advances. The Program audit reports have all been satisfactory to the Bank, and no audit reports are currently outstanding. The PMU is fully staffed and supported by a team of technical assistants, the Monitoring and Evaluation system is operational; and the Program is fully compliant with fiduciary procedures and safeguards. 7. Main conclusions of the Mid-Term Review (MTR). The MTR mission was carried out in June 2023, followed by regular meetings between the Bank team, the PMU, the implementing agencies and the co-financier between June and end of October 2023 to discuss measures to improve the Program’s performance.1 The MTR did not identify a need for profound modification of the Program but, rather, for: (i) targeted readjustments of PDO 3 and DLI 9 (cf. details of quantitative changes at the end of the RP); (ii) adjustment of some indicators (Program Development Objective/PDO indicators, Disbursement Linked Indicator/DLI, Disbursement Linked Result/DLR and Intermediate Result Indicator/IRI); (iii) addition of indicators to explicitly monitor important intermediate actions; and (iv) simplifications of the Results Framework. Despite some delays in RA#1 and RA#2, there is no need to adjust the PDO or to change significantly the PDO indicators. Therefore, only a level 2 restructuring is necessary. 8. The delays of RA#1 and RA#3 can be explained by a combination of factors: (i) unfavorable external cyclical conditions for the agricultural sector (water shortage for irrigated agriculture, droughts for rainfed agriculture and increase in production costs in particular) which affect RA#1 by reducing the attractiveness of the sector for young people and RA#3 with regard to irrigated agriculture; (ii) the delay in the creation of the PMU (end of 2021), then the late mobilization of technical assistance to the PMU (beginning of 2023), i.e. two years after the effectiveness of the Program; (iii) insufficient financial resources deployed, particularly for RA#1 (6 percent of expenditure issued compared to initial estimates for the 2021-23 period); and (iv) an incomplete initial level of definition of the Program which was prepared during the final phase of development of the Green Generation Strategy. It therefore did not benefit from strong empirics to size Program targets and the design also had to anticipate the implementation mechanisms which would be decided after the approval of the PforR. 9. Additional changes posterior to the MTR. Subsequent to the endorsement of the changes proposed in the MTR by the Steering Committee in November 2023, new elements appeared modifying some conclusions of the MTR. This concerns in particular the fact that the legal modifications governing the modernized wholesale markets will no longer be the subject of a simple order but will require a law, which will delay considerably the operationalization of the four wholesale markets included in RA#2. This change is now reflected in the design by changing the previous DLR#4.2 by DLR#4.5 as described below. 10. Extension of the loan closing date. Considering the various issues described above (initial delays of implementation, effects of drought and shift from an Order to a Law for the modernization of wholesale markets), and to keep the original level of ambition of the PforR, the Government has asked formally2 for an extension by twenty-two months of the IBRD loan closing date3 and revision of some results indicators that are essential for achieving the PDO. New DLR#4.5:“The Law fixing the modalities regarding the creation and functioning of the modernized wholesale markets, has been published in the Official Gazette”. The Ministry of the Interior has decided to replace the Order with a law, due to the need to make all the necessary adjustments to the regulation of modernized wholesale markets (WSM). Indeed, an Order does not allow the tax system governing the WSMs to be repealed, nor the status of agents, nor the enactment of sanctions. The draft law is currently being prepared, based on the content of the latest version of the Order, and is structured into 3 chapters: (i) the definition of wholesale and the different actors and infrastructures; (ii) the organization of the WSMs, their spatial and operational distribution; and (iii) the technical and health standards governing their operation. The law must also repeal: (a) the current system of taxing sales of agricultural products at 7% (5% for the communes and 2% for the middlemen/mandataires) in the non-modernized wholesale markets to allow the creation of sustainable revenues for the new management structures, (b) the status of middlemen agents (mandataires) with the creation of a fund by the Ministry of Economy and Finance for the establishment of a fee compensating for the loss of income of agents resulting from the end of the taxation system, and finally, and (c) the establishment of a set of sanctions for violators of the new regulations governing the WSMs. The amount allocated to DLR#4.5 is 5,000,000 Euros for World Bank financing (50% for the transmission to the General Secretary of Government, a key step done after all ministries involved agreed on the text, and 50% for the publication In the Official Gazette). 11. Changes to PDO indicators. Changes to the PDO indicators are described below: PDO#1: Number of young people who have created businesses related to agriculture that have been operational for at least six months (number) 21. This PDO indicator records the final results of RA#1. The definition of the indicator has been discussed during the MTR to: “Number of young people with their agricultural projects and agricultural, para-agricultural and digital services operational and/or in the process of being implemented”. 22. The definition includes operational projects and those still being implemented in order to recognize the diversity of the nature of the projects; some agricultural projects, including in particular perennial crops such as arboriculture, may be implemented before closing date of the Program but cannot be considered operational because the plantations will not be productive. 23. The original final target is maintained at 13,000 youth but postponed to CY2027 to reflect the original delays of setting up the Regional Centers for Young Agricultural and Agri-Food Entrepreneurs (CRJEEA) and the legal framework providing new incentives to young rural entrepreneurs. PDO#2: Volume of local products marketed through modernized wholesale markets (Tons) 24. This PDO indicator measures the accomplishment of Result Area 2 activities linked to the development of four modernized wholesale markets for the cities of Agadir, Berkane, Meknes and Marrakech. The definition of PDO#2 remains unchanged. The World Bank Morocco Green Generation Program-for-Results (P170419) 25. The status of the indicator is zero as planned in the initial design (only calendar year 2025 had a non-zero target). The delays in the development of these four markets will lead to a time lag for the volumes marketed. The initial target of 865,000 T at the end of CY 2025 will be now reached at the end of CY 2027, the new proposed closing date. As per the findings of the supervision mission carried out in May 2024, the construction of two wholesale markets will start in early CY2025 (Berkane and Meknes). The design and budget for the other two (Agadir and Marrakech) still needs finetuning to get the endorsement of all stakeholders.
