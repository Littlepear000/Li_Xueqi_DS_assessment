BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING A. Introduction 1. This project paper seeks the approval of the Executive Directors to provide an additional financing (AF) in the amount of EUR 25 million (US$ 26.7 million equivalent) to the Serbia Accelerating Innovation and Growth Entrepreneurship (SAIGE) Additional Financing II (P181485), “Parent Project”. This is the second AF for the project and will be used to scale up the ongoing SAIGE activities and to further enhance client innovation capacity through better R&D and entrepreneurship in the areas of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and biotechnology, to increase the country’s competitiveness on global markets. 2. The Project Development Objective (PDO), and project design including component and sub-component structure and all implementation arrangements will remain the same. 3. In addition to the proposed AF, this project paper seeks the approval of a Level Two project restructuring to update the Project activities and extend the closing date of the original Loan (IBRD-9029-YF) by 24 months (second extension), to allow for implementation of new grant programs and capacity building activities.1 4. The Results Framework is revised to include new indicators, update targets upwards where they have been achieved, and reflect the new closing date, proposed for September 30, 2027. 5. Conditions of Effectiveness will include Subsidiary Agreements with Project Implementation Entities, executed in a manner acceptable to the Bank, and an updated and adopted Project Operational Manual, in a form and substance satisfactory to the Bank. Updated and adopted Innovation Fund and Science Fund Grant Manuals, acceptable to the Bank will serve as disbursement conditions. B. Original project objective, design, and scope 6. The SAIGE PDO is to improve (i) the relevance and excellence of scientific research, and (ii) innovative entrepreneurship and access to finance for enterprise growth, as a way of contributing to Serbia’s growth and competitiveness. Relevance of research refers to the potential of research results to be commercialized on the market, thus contributing to the economy, while excellence refers to international recognition of the quality of the research. The PDO will not change in the context of the proposed Additional Financing. 7. The Parent Project consists of three components. Component 1 (Research Sector Reforms) focused on the financing of grants and TA to the Science Fund (SF), institutional transformation of selected Research and Development Institutes (RDIs), and a Serbian Diaspora Facility, to transfer scientific knowledge back to the country. Component 2 (Enterprise Acceleration) builds on existing programs of the Innovation Fund (IF), combining the TA and the matching grants via co- investment fund to support early and growth-stage firms. Component 3 finances project implementation and monitoring, including PIU and part of CFU operational costs. 1 The approval Authority for the associated Level Two restructuring will be at Management level. 1 The World Bank Serbia Accelerating Innovation and Growth Entrepreneurship (P170185) C. Implementation of specific activities is described in Annex 1. D. Rationale for Additional Financing 9. Country context. While Serbia saw a growth rebound after the pandemic, the country’s rate of growth over the past two years has hovered around 2 percent, falling short of that needed to achieve meaningful income convergence with EU peers. The Serbian economy started to slow down in the second half of 2022, a trend that continued in the first half of 2023. However, in mid-2023 the construction sector has begun to show signs of a recovery, and some parts of the agriculture sector (wheat and corn production in particular) have performed better than expected. On the back of these developments, growth accelerated in the second half of 2023 resulting in a growth rate of 2.5 percent for the year as whole. Inflation started to decline since peak in March 2023 at 16.2 percent year on year and moderated to 7.6 percent by end of 2023. Inflation is expected to continue decline gradually and to return to within National Bank of Serbia’s target band by mid-2024. Budgetary revenues have overperformed significantly in 2023, primarily thanks to a higher-than- planned collection of contributions from social insurance, VAT, and corporate income tax, while public debt continued to decline and stood at around 52.9 percent of GDP at the end of December 2023. Over the medium term, the economy is projected to grow steadily at around 3-4 percent annually, supported by increases in consumption and investment, and strong exports. 10. Sector context. Compared to the EU, Serbia has lower per capita income, but a faster growing economy. Manufacturing takes up a larger share of the economy, with SMEs accounting for a larger share of turnover. Serbia’s innovation performance is at 63.2% of the EU average, making it an “emerging innovator” on the European Union’s Innovation Scoreboard for 2023. While Serbia lags behind EU levels of innovation, performance has been increasing at a rate higher than that of the EU, between 2016 and 2022, indicating the country’s potential to catch up to the EU average. Serbia has a number of strengths relative to comparator countries (other “emerging innovators), namely non-R&D expenditures, product innovation, employment in innovative enterprises, business process innovation, and innovation expenditures per employee, indicating a fairly robust private sector level of innovation. However, Serbia’s relative weaknesses have continued to persist, and these include resource productivity, government support for business R&D, patent applications, design applications and air emissions by fine articulate matter (the latter being an indicator of sustainability of innovation capacity). Since 2022, Serbia has experienced increases in performance related to enterprises. In comparison to EU member states, Serbia has higher shares of innovators that do not develop innovations themselves, as well as non- innovators. 2 The World Bank Serbia Accelerating Innovation and Growth Entrepreneurship (P170185) Figure 1: Serbia Innovation Index 2016-2023 (Source: European Innovation Scoreboard, 2023) 11. Since 2016, Serbia’s performance on the Innovation Index increased most strongly across indicators measuring research systems, digitalization, finance and support, innovators, linkages and employment impacts, while performance declined for information technologies, intellectual assets and environmental sustainability. 12. In terms of reforms supporting innovation, Serbia has made several important steps since the approval of the Parent Project. Specifically, Serbia has introduced the following: (a) A 200% deduction applicable to expenses directly related to R&D activities of enterprises; (b) A tax credit is available for investments in the share capital of new companies conducting innovative business activities (start-ups); (c) Taxpayers deriving income from the licensing of registered intellectual property may deduct 80% of qualified income from taxation; (d) Revenues from intellectual property developed in Serbia are taxed at 3%; (e) 70% lower tax and contributions for salaries within R&D projects (since March 1st 2022); (f) Startup founders pay no salary tax and contributions during the first three years; (g) Corporate income tax credit worth 30% of the investment in a startup; (h) 50% tax credit for private individuals for investing into a venture capital fund. 13. Serbia has also attracted international corporations to open R&D centers in the country. As a result of these, Microsoft, Siemens, Continental, Bosch, Cisco, Oracle, NCF, Rivian, Toyota, IBM, Epic Games, T2, Yandex, Ubisoft, Schneider Electric, Polygon, Cosmos, BGI and GoDaddy all have R&D teams based in Serbia. Since Parent Project approval, Serbia has also upgraded or opened new Science and Technology Parks (STPs) in Novi Sad, Nis, and Cacak, in addition to the STP Belgrade previously established. 14. Artificial Intelligence. Many high-income countries are increasing specialization in AI as a factor in national competitiveness. According to Chun et al. (2024), a nation's existing technological foundation strongly impacts its ability to specialize in AI. Research points to the crucial role of bridging the gap between scientific advancements and practical applications and, in middle or low income countries, the lack of advanced technological infrastructure needed to specialize in AI through traditional methods. 15. Since 2018, Serbia has introduced reforms to establish itself as a leader in AI within Southeast Europe. With EU-financed, World Bank managed technical assistance, Serbia developed a National Strategy for the Development of Artificial 3 The World Bank Serbia Accelerating Innovation and Growth Entrepreneurship (P170185) Intelligence for the period 2020-2025, outlining a roadmap for AI development and implementation across various sectors. Serbia is currently working on developing a new AI strategy. Serbia joined the AI Governance Alliance in 2023, stating commitment to responsible AI development and gaining access to expert support. Serbia is set to Chair the Global Partnership on Artificial Intelligence (GPAI) in 2025. Serbia has also founded an AI Institute, a beneficiary under SAIGE and set to benefit from intensive capacity building through this Additional Financing. 16. Bioeconomy. Innovation in bioeconomy, driven mainly by advancements in pharmaceuticals, is expected to grow by 8.7% between 2023 and 2030 globally. Governments around the world have recognized this potential by increasing investments in bioeconomy R&D and related sectors, including IT (AI). However, many common barriers still inhibit the growth of biotechnology. This includes the volume of funding required, the availability of sequencing equipment and the lack of IT resources for data storage and human capital, which absorb countries’ resources and reduce biotech’s positive impact. 17. Serbia has embarked on an ambitious project aiming to establish Serbia as a major hub for bioeconomy in Europe. Specifically, it is in the process of establishing a network of public and private institutions collaborating on topics in biotechnology, biomedicine, bioinformatics and biodiversity, which will also include a physical campus in the capital of Belgrade, BIO4. Construction of the campus is set to begin by September 2024 and is expected to be completed by the end of 2026. The completed campus is expected to accommodate 1400 professors, researchers, 4000 students, over 1000 PhDs, and house 300 cutting-edge laboratories. The Serbian government views BIO4 as a transformative project and has invested heavily in it. The Council of Europe Development Bank has also provided €200 million for the initial construction phase, while the EBRD has also provided €80 million for Science and Technology Park extension, of which €10 million is allocated to BIO4 campus infrastructure. A number of R&D Institutes that are current beneficiaries of the SAIGE Parent Project are expected to join the BIO4 initiative, along with several University of Belgrade faculties. 18. In August 2023, a government delegation led by the Minister of Science, Innovation and Technological Development (NITRA) requested additional support (IBRD lending) through the SAIGE project. Following this visit, in September 2023, the Bank received a written request for a second Additional Financing for: (a) further developing the field of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Serbia, (b) strengthening of R&D management capabilities related to BIO4 agenda, and (c) supporting the management and overall project implementation. 19. II. DESCRIPTION OF ADDITIONAL FINANCING 22. The second Additional Financing (AF) in the amount of €25 million (US$26.7 million equivalent) is proposed to the Serbia Accelerating Innovation and Growth Entrepreneurship (P170185) parent project. The SAIGE Project currently includes an IBRD loan in the amount of €43 million (US$48 million), signed on December 13, 2019, and declared effective on May 18, 2020. It also includes an EU IPA 19 funding, recipient-executed grant of €26.88 million (US$28.52 million) processed as Additional Financing in July 2023. This first AF was not processed at the same time with the original IBRD Loan due to the slightly later approval of EU IPA funds in December 2019, and negotiations on the EU-WB Financial Framework Partnership Agreement, finalized on July 8, 2021. The first AF enables scale-up of activities in the parent Project through the same implementing entities and programs. 23. Changes proposed through the AF entail expanding the scope of the activities financed by the Parent Project, adjusting its overall design, and extending the closing date of the Parent Project by 24 months. The relevant implementation documents, such as POM and GMs will be upgraded to include provisions related to improved risk impact assessment framework for safe, secure, responsible trustworthy and reliable AI (Trustworthy AI) ethics, environmental and social issues. Furthermore, the Bank proposes to utilize the Bank executed technical assistance within the overall SAIGE program to include a training program with a dedicated session on anticipating/managing AI risks for the implementing agencies and the beneficiaries. 24. Implementation arrangements have been reviewed by Legal and FM teams and no changes are envisaged . NITRA is responsible for the overall coordination and implementation, with SF and IF responsible for implementation of their respective components. Innovation Fund Activities – €8.4 million to be Added to Component 2: Enterprise Acceleration 27. The IF is the primary vehicle for enabling innovative Serbian products, technologies and services to enter the market. Borrower’s Institutional Capacity The AF II implementation arrangements remain unchanged. Thus, the Ministry of Science, Technological Development and Innovation (NITRA) will be responsible for the overall project coordination and implementation with specific agencies responsible for the implementation of their respective components, the same as for the parent SAIGE Project (the Project). The main recipients of technical assistance (TA) are AI Institute and the BIO4 campus management department. However, the roles of these entities in relation to the Science Fund (SF) and the Innovation Fund (IF) have not been clearly defined. Specifically, it remains uncertain what role they could play in supporting the institutionalization and oversight of the practices suggested by the TA. In a parent project, a single Project Implementation Unit (PIU) has been established within the NITRA reporting to the NITRA State Secretary (Research and Technology), responsible for the coordination of the Project with all project implementing entities (PIEs). It is expected that the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) will remain responsible for all project implementation-related activities, including technical, operational, environmental and social assessment and management of grant research components and all technical assistance (TA) under program, reporting, monitoring and evaluation, audits, studies, capacity building, etc. The Project Implementation Unit (PIU) is currently operational with two part-time environmental and social specialists tasked with assessing, preparing, and overseeing the implementation of mitigation measures, as well as reporting on environmental and social (E&S) risks at the sub-project level. Past E&S performance ratings of Project implementation were moderately satisfactory, primarily due to a persistent lack of technical capacity of the PIU to properly assess E&S risks and implement adequate mitigation measures, despite ongoing support provided by the WB. This shortfall contributes to an escalation in the overall social risk associated with the activities under AF II. Risk estimation and management of activities related to socially sensitive research domains such as clinical healthcare or e- mobility require strong expertise (in both technical and ethical aspects of AI). This is vital due to the potential cumulative impacts of these innovations on human life. Besides the lack of technical capacity in risk management, lessons learned from the parent project indicate that the part-time engagement of specialists has limitations in terms of commitment to fully integrate all ESF requirements and adequately report on all aspects of risk management. For the reasons mentioned above, for the AF II, the PIU will need to significantly improve its existing capacity for E&S risk management and to appoint one full time environmental specialist and one full time social specialist, instead of two part-time specialists, who will be fully devoted to all aspects of E&S risk management under AF II. Having full time specialists is essential for ensuring proactive risk management and the overall E&S compliance across all participants (Innovation Fund (IF), Science Fund (SF), AI Institute, and BIO4). The required training on ESF, Emergency Preparedness and Response planning are set out in the ESCP. Economic and Financial Analysis 1. An economic and financial analysis (EFA) has been conducted across the project components to determine the value of the anticipated benefits relative to the costs associated with this additional financing. The AF Net Present Value (NPV) is estimated at EUR 31.3 million at a 12 percent discount rate2, and the Economic Rate of Return (ERR) at 24 percent over a 15-year timeframe. The full project NPV and ERR including this second additional financing are EUR 54.5 million and 21 percent. 2. In reference to this project valuation, several points are worth noting. First, this calculation is indicative, using expected average parameters for the project beneficiaries and the resulting impact of the project components. While these assumptions are based on the project team’s discussions with potential beneficiaries along with results of other projects and economic literature, they are not precise representations of the impact that the project will have. The exercise of financially modelling the project helps to ensure that project funds are being allocated to investments and activities that will provide a return to the local population, along with helping to identify key risks and thresholds for the project to achieve its desired impact. Additionally, international development projects often target regions and sectors where the risk is high since these types of projects can specifically take on higher risk investments because of the social good that could result from them—as such, financial valuations of these investments may not be as high as those in other regions, but the social value of such investments could be considerably higher. 3. That said, our methodology (detailed below) accounts only for the project’s impact on direct beneficiaries rather than using a broader multiplier approach which would encompass positive externalities and spill over effects, along with overall growth of the innovation ecosystem in Serbia. Additionally, investments under this project are expected to facilitate further investments from EU and other donor funds, which could further amplify social returns. The value of such externalities is difficult to estimate; however, the social rate of return based on growth of the innovation ecosystem can be upwards of 60 percent. While this is not an exact estimate of the project’s value, this high value of broader social benefits from the project is worth underscoring. 4. Due to difficulties in estimating a precise return from public sector reforms and pure technical assistance activities, our economic analysis focuses on direct beneficiaries of the AI and BIO4 activities under the AF. That said, capacity building and institutional reforms under both activity-sets are likely to amplify the overall impact of the project by enabling growth of the wider research and innovation eco- system. The structure of these investments builds upon lessons learned from other projects in Serbia and the broader region, including those supported by other donors. 5. Background and Benchmarking of AI efforts 1. The Republic of Serbia selected Artificial Intelligence as a strategic spearhead in 2019, with the goals of creating: i. an education system that develops the competencies for future needs of the job market, referring to professions directly and indirectly influenced by artificial intelligence technologies; ii. a business sector that supports the development and integration of business entities based on the use of artificial intelligence; iii. scientific research that spurs the emergence of highly competent experts in the field of artificial intelligence and its implementations, with their research contributing to the development of the business sector; iv. legal regulation in all fields that is adapted to the new circumstances brought about by artificial intelligence, the requirements of new business models and product developments and services based on artificial intelligence, while taking into account the protection against potential negative effects; and v. comprehensive understanding of ethical aspects while planning, designing and implementing solutions in the field of artificial intelligence from a perspective of technical characteristics as well as from a perspective of effects of implementation, while taking into account the principles of preserving the freedom of individuals, fairness and equality, avoidance of damage, openness, transparency and sustainability. 2. Specifically, the AF seeks to strengthen economic growth, integration into global value chains, and job creation over the long term, through supporting the creation and growth of innovative companies based on artificial intelligence technologies, training students and researchers in topics directly and indirectly related to AI technologies, and stimulating collaboration between public research institutions (including institutes and faculties) and private companies, to enable creation of new AI-based products and services and their successful integration into markets. 3. For Serbia to respond as quickly as possible to today's challenges, and also to the challenges to come, Serbia will initiate the creation of development programs based on artificial intelligence and robotics in the following areas: • The development program for "green development based on IT" – this will deal with the development of technologies that can help solve essential problems of citizens and the economy, such as clean and alternative energy, environment (air, water and land pollution, biota), climate change and its impact on health care, agriculture and protection of forests and settlements. This program is multidisciplinary in every way and goes far beyond research and development in the field of artificial intelligence and robotics. Experts from all fields will be involved in these projects. • Centre for transport systems and the auto industry - Serbia has a significant increase in development centers and production in the field of the auto industry. This center will have tasks to develop and introduce solutions that, with the help of artificial intelligence and robotics, will enable new services and products in the auto industry, together with partners such as Continental, Stellantis, Siemens and others. This center is regionally important, because Serbia is a regional transit center for passenger and freight traffic, and implemented innovations will indirectly affect the whole of Southeast and Central Europe. 57 The World Bank Serbia Accelerating Innovation and Growth Entrepreneurship(P170185) PROJECT PAPER • Centre for the application of artificial intelligence in clinical medicine - The Institute for Artificial Intelligence already cooperates with various health institutions and research/scientific centers in Serbia on programs such as: developing a diagnostic system using AI in emergency medicine in the field of cardiology and neurology; implementation of AI in the analysis of ECG results; implementation of AI in X-ray, CT and magnetic resonance analysis for the detection of cancer and other malignant diseases; finding patients with rare diseases in the population of citizens of Serbia for which until now it was considered that there was no therapy. 4. In addition to the above, AI and robotics will be applied in other strategic areas of the economy and public administration of the Republic of Serbia. A growing number of companies are developing AI in Serbia, creating a critical mass of expertise in the domains of robotization, automation and predictive maintenance of the production process. Further initiatives will be directed towards promotion, government incentives and improvement of conditions for the growth and development of companies in this area. 5. In preparation for Serbia’s AI activities, two benchmark studies have been made with Finland (number 4 in the Artificial Intelligence Readiness Index), and with the Republic of Korea (number 10 in the Artificial Intelligence Readiness Index). With both countries, we found a similar path from government-initiated strategy, through funded action plan, to results. 6. Finland’s strategy in 2019 was to lift the country as the leading nation in the world for applied AI. The vision for 2025 is to prepare Finland for the age of artificial intelligence and make it into a nation that is competitive and able to attract talent, has the most relevantly educated population and where citizens are well-informed and independent. 7. The Finnish government is executing on the strategy by implementing a business program for startups and established companies with R&D funding, setting up an academic research group for solving scientific problems with the implementation of AI, launching a public tool for national and local government to recommend services to citizens, and by orchestrating a Massive Open Online Course (MOOC) for anyone to get educated on elements of AI. The duration of the Finnish project is 2019-2026 with a €496 million budget. 8. The Korean government is recognizing the demand for AI solutions expanding globally, yet the size and investments in Korea are low compared to the international market. The government is expanding its support throughout the entire lifecycle with a focus on creative startups. It plans to become the center of the global AI startup ecosystem by enhancing the technologies of homegrown domestic AI startups. 9. Korea is doing this by setting up a ‘Regional AI Innovation Cluster Project’. This project includes a construction of a new building in the city of Gwangju, constructing and operating a data center, testing equipment for automobile, energy, and health AI applications, support for Business Development, capacity Development, and direct funding for AI R&D. The duration of the Korean project is 2020-2024 with a US$328.2 million budget. 58
