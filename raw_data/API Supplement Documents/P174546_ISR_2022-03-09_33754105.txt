 MPA Table
 Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA) Status

 MPA Table Body
 The Emergency Locust Response Program is a regional integration project using the multi-phase programmatic approach (ELRP MPA), and it was
 approved by the World Bank Board of Executive Directors on May 20, 2020 with a potential funding envelope of US$500 million. The Phase 1
 projects, which committed US$160 million in the four first-mover countries of Djibouti, Ethiopia, Kenya, and Uganda, were approved along with the
 overall program document. A termination of the credit to the Republic of Uganda is in process based on a formal request from the Government of
 Uganda. The Bank is currently preparing a US$35 million additional finance to the Kenya project and a US$60 million to the Ethiopia project.

 ELRP MPA Phase 2 was approved on June 29, 2020, providing a US$40 million grant to Somalia. An additional financing of US$75 million was
 approved on June 17, 2021. Phase 3 was approved on June 8, 2021, with a US$50.7 million grant to South Sudan and a US$3.0 grant to the
 Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD). Phase 4 is a US$65 million grant to The Sudan has been negotiated and is on hold pending
 clarity on the security situation.

 ELRP-MPA Phase 1 has been implementing for almost two years in Ethiopia, Djibouti, and Kenya, and implementation progress and progress
 towards the program development objective is satisfactory. Disbursement for Phase 1 currently stands at 48%.

 Ethiopia has almost fully disbursed its funds and is preparing an additional finance. The project carried out control activities on 778,775 hectares, and
 has provided seed, fertilizer, and fodder seed packages to rehabilitate farmlands, pasturelands, and rangelands affected by the desert locust
 invasion. It has overachieved its seed distribution targets by 175% and continues to work on fertilizer distribution (currently 75% of target). To build
 national preparedness, the project has completed an evaluation on the strengths and weaknesses of Ethiopia’s current desert locust monitoring
 system; prepared a training plan based on identified capacity gaps; begun strengthening the government’s ability to collect analyze and communicate
 early warnings; and started work to establish a National Locust Control Unit (LCU) and three regional desert locust centers.

 The Djibouti locust project (PROLUC) has disbursed 73% of its credit and is on track to achieve its objectives. Of the 13 performance and
 intermediate indicators, 6 have reached or exceeded their targets, 4 are in progress, and 3 remain to be achieved. The project has provided
 awareness raising/training directly to 450 people from concerned communities on the risks from desert locust and pesticides and their impacts on
 human health and the environment. In partnership with FAO, it has trained 15 trainers and 30 technicians to improve locust monitoring and control.
 Cash transfers are being provided to 2,000 vulnerable households and the distribution of inputs to support production has been completed,
 exceeding the targets by reaching 1,106 farmers working 720 hectares and 3,077 pastoralists operating 14 ranges with a total area of 26,311
 hectares. The project continues to invest in the technology (eLocust3, Ramses) and training for desert locust surveillance, monitoring, and control in
 collaboration with FAO.

 In Kenya, ERLP supported the treatment of 226 swarms and 5 hopper bands affecting total area of 35,889 hectares across 25 counties. The project
 trained and deployed 240 National Youth Service (NYS) personnel across the eight control bases it helped establish to anchor surveillance and
 control operations. Eighty people across 16 control teams were trained on the Environment and Social risks and instruments for the project.
 Component 2 activities are commencing and will be expanded through the additional financing that is under preparation. The project has prioritized
 15 counties for livelihood protection and restoration interventions, and the Government has started community mobilization, stakeholder sensitization,
 and the rapid assessment, prioritizing 97 intervention wards across 15 counties. For national preparedness moving forward, the project has trained
 90 technical staff from National and County governments on desert locust management, engaged stakeholders on establishing an ICT Centre and
 Integrated Migratory Pest Management System (IMPMS), engaged with counties on establishing County Locust Control Units; procured equipment
 for DL monitoring, surveillance, and control; and, in collaboration with FAO, is developing a Migratory and Invasive Pests Management Strategy.
