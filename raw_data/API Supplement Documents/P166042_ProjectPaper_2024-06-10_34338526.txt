I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

   A. Project background

   1. The Republic of Guinea and the Republic of Mali are Recipients of an IDA financing in the amount of U$84 million
      to implement the Guinea-Mali interconnector Project (P166042). This includes the IDA credit 62610 (US$.37.5
     The World Bank
     Guinea – Mali Interconnection Project (P166042)


    million) and the IDA grant D3360 (US$.37.5 million) to the Republic of Guinea, and the IDA credit 62620 (US$.4.5
    million) and the IDA grant D3370 (US$.4.5 million) to the Republic of Mali, all approved by the World Bank’s
    Board of Executive Directors on July 13, 2018. Financing Agreements (FA) were signed on October 2 and
    November 8, 2018, and became effective on March 24 and May 29, 2019, respectively for Mali and Guinea, with
    the closing date set on June 30, 2024.

2. The IDA-financed project is part of a multi-donor operation co-financed with African Development Bank (AfDB
   UC60 million), European Investment Bank (EIB EUR 130 million), ECOWAS Bank for Investment and
   Development (EBID US$37.850 million) and West African Development Bank (WADB 18 billion FCFA) and
   involving: (i) the construction of a 714 km 225 kV double-circuit transmission line from the CLSG substation at
   Nzérékoré in Guinea to the Sanankoroba substation in Mali; (ii) the construction in Guinea of five new 225/30 kV
   substations at Siguiri, Fomi, Kankan, Kérouané and Beyla; and (ii) the extension of the 225/33 kV Sanankoroba
   substation in Mali and the 225/30 kV CLSG substation at Nzérékoré in Guinea. IDA financing covers the
   construction of 54 km of transmission line in Guinea and four substations; the remaining 660 km of the transmission
   line and three substations are financed by the other donors.The Project Development Objectives (PDO) are to (i)
   increase electricity supply to the Eastern part of Guinea; (ii) enable electricity trade between Guinea and Mali; and
   (iii) increase Guinea’s electricity export capability towards other West African Power Pool (WAPP) countries. In
   addition, the project is part of a network of interconnections that contribute to the realization of Guinea’s electricity
   export potential to the WAPP countries. The interconnector is complementary to the Côte d’Ivoire, Sierra Leone,
   Liberia, and Guinea Power System Re-Development (CLSG-P113266), the OMVS Transmission Expansion
   (P147921), and the OMVG Interconnection (P146830) projects financed by the World Bank.

3. Progress towards achievement of the PDO is currently rated Unsatisfactory and implementation progress is rated
   Moderately Unsatisfactory. The project has hardly reached a progress rate of 55% as of April 30, 2024, and the
   construction of the segments of the interconnection line is significantly behind schedule. As a result, the initial
   planned completion date of June 30, 2024, is no longer achievable.

4. As of April 30, 2024, the overall project disbursements stand at US$26.01 million, equivalent to about 33.35 percent
   of total financing. This is expected to increase by June 30, 2024. An additional amount of US$3 million, of which
   US$2 in Guinea and US$1 in Mali, is to be disbursed pending the shipment and delivery on site of part of the
   electrical equipment.

5. All legal covenants stipulated in the legal agreements, except the signing of a PPA between EDG and EDM, have
   been complied with. On April 30, 2024, EDG sent a letter to invite EDM to start negotiations of the PPA using the
   model enforced by the ECOWAS Regional Electricity Regulatory Authority (ARREC).


6. There are no overdue audits or interim financial reports at the time of the restructuring. The financial management
   performance is rated Satisfactory while the procurement is rated Moderately Satisfactory. Both environmental and
   social safeguards’ performances are rated Moderately Satisfactory and there are no unresolved issues. The
   Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) system is in place and functional in Guinea and Mali. With regards to the
   RAP, for the Guinean section of the transmission line financed by IDA, all the PAPs have been satisfactorily
   compensated. The GoG has just released GNF14 billion (US$1.6 million) out of the 18 billion (US$2 million)
   needed to finalize the compensations of PAPs along the Fomi-Siguiri line financed by EBID. In Mali, IDA-financed

       activities do not require compensations, as the Sanankoroba substation is placed at a site owned by EDM. However,
       the implementation of the RAP for the section of the transmission line financed by the AFDB and WADB requires
       the GoM to secure approximately US$3.5 million for the compensation of the PAPs.

   7. The detailed description of the IDA project components is provided below.


Component 1 - Power Transmission Infrastructure

   8. In Guinea, IDA financing supports the construction of the 53.7 km of transmission line between the Mali/Guinea
      border and Siguiri and the construction of three new substations in Siguiri, Kankan, and Kerouane. In Mali, the IDA
      financing supports the extension of the Sanankoroba substation.

   9. This component also finances the costs of the implementation of all the aspects of the ESMPs—other than the
      implementation of the RAPs. IDA finances the cost of the ESMP in Guinea. The cost of the ESMP in Mali is
      financed by WADB.

Component 2 – Implementation Support and Capacity Building

   10. This component supports project management, capacity building, technical studies, and technical assistance to the
       project implementation units (PIUs) established in the two countries and utilities. Specifically, financing was
       allocated to cover the following:

   (i) Setting up the fiduciary systems of the PIUs, such as procurement and financial management (FM); recruiting the
        needed staff; and covering the operational costs (per diems, acquisition of vehicles, office supplies, furniture, and
        hardware/software, etc.).
   (ii) Logistical expenses related to the organization of the Joint Implementation Steering Committee meetings.

   (iii) Supply of equipment and tools to strengthen the capacity of the two utilities to operate and maintain the
        interconnected grid.

   (iv) Analytical work to strengthen the two countries’ institutional, legal, and regulatory framework to facilitate the trade
        of electricity between the two utilities.

   IDA is financing US$12.2 million of the project implementation costs and all analytical work costs.




   B. Project Status

   11. The achievement of the PDO hinges upon the fact that the Guinea-Mali transmission line is completed and
       operational. This requires construction and commissioning of all 714 km of transmission line and the associated
       substations. With less than two (2) weeks left prior to project closure, 55% of the construction works have been
       completed. The indicators 1 and 2 of the PDO (increase electricity supply to the Eastern part of Guinea and enable
       electricity trade between Guinea and Mali) cannot be achieved at the current project closing date of June 30, 2024.
         The World Bank
         Guinea – Mali Interconnection Project (P166042)


       As for the PDO indicator 3 (increase Guinea’s electricity export capability towards other West African Power Pool
       countries), this is on track to be achieved as Guinea starts exporting energy to Guinea Bissau via the OMVG
       interconnector and will soon start exporting to The Gambia. Export to Sierra Leone via the CLSG is also expected,
       thanks to the additional 450 MW of the Souapiti hydro dam which was commissioned in 2021.

   12. The detailed status of progress of implementation by components are as follows:

Component 1: Power transmission infrastructure (transmission line and substations)

   13. For this component, the procurement process was completed for all contracts relating to the transmission line and
       associated substations. All contracts have been signed and are being executed. Advance payments have been made,
       and service orders issued. The total disbursement amount of US$26 million comes mainly from the project work
       contracts.

   14. However, there is a significant delay in the execution of works on the different lots financed by IDA including the
       substations of Kankan, Siguiri, Kérouané and the portion of the line in Guinea between Siguiri and the Malian
       border, as well as works to extend the Sanankoroba substation in Mali. The overall progress rate for IDA-financed
       works is 55%, against 33.35% of the financing disbursed. The progress rates of the components financed by IDA
       in Guinea are: 71% for the construction of the 225 kV line from the Siguiri substation to the Guinea-Mali border;
       43% for the construction of the substation of Siguiri; 52% for the construction of the Kankan substation; 60% for
       the construction of the Kérouané substation. For this latter substation, the electrical equipment was received in
       Conakry on March 11, 2024and has been delivered to the substation site; as a result, it is expected that the project
       disbursement rate will increase to 36%. In Mali, works for the extension of the Sanankoraba substation effectively
       started January 25, 2024, and have reached a 28% completion rate. As of April 30, 2024, the contractors’
       implementation schedules were revised and updated by the Owner’s Engineer. Overall, these schedules indicate a
       completion date of the IDA financed components as following:
      Transmission line from the Siguiri substation to the Guinea-Mali border: February 28, 2025
      Substation of Siguiri in Guinea: February 28, 2025
      Substation of Kankan in Guinea: February 28, 2025
      Substation of Kerouane in Guinea: December 31, 2024
      Extension of the substation of Sanankoroba in Mali: December 31, 2024


   15. The overall progress rates of transmission line constructed with non-IDA financing are as follow:


      16% for the transmission line Fomi-Siguiri, funded by the EBID in Guinea. The revised completion schedule is
       November 30, 2025, due to delays of the Government of Guinea (GoG) to pay compensations to PAPs;
      70% for the transmission line and substations funded by the European Investment Bank-EIB in Guinea. The revised
       completion date is June 30, 2025;
      57% for the section of line Fomi-Kankan and the substation of Fomi funded by the African Development Bank-
       AFDB in Guinea. The revised completion date is February 28, 2025;

      37% for the portion of the line Snanakoroba to Mali-Guinea border funded by the AFDB and WADB in Mali. The
       revised completion date is June 30, 2025, due to the delay for the Government of Mali (GoM) to secure the
       compensation funds of approximately U$3.5 million.


   16. Overall, based on the revised implementation schedule, IDA-financed activities are expected to be completed by
       February 28, 2025, and the construction of the entire transmission line by November 30, 2025. The commissioning
       of the Guinea-Mali transmission line is therefore scheduled by December 31, 2025. That justifies an extension of
       the project closing date until December 31, 2025, to achieve the PDO.

Price adjustment

   17. All the contractors working on the project (except the company in charge of extending the Sanankoroba substation
       in Mali) have requested price adjustments due to the increase of the cost of raw materials and freight following the
       COVID-19 pandemic and the disruptions due to the conflict between Ukraine and Russia. These requests were
       examined by the PIUs and the OE revised and provided its opinion on what is the reasonable and justified price
       increase for each contract. After negotiations with contractors, the agreed price increases are as follows:
      On the four IDA financed contracts in Guinea, the total price increase amounts to U$12.9 million. This amount can
       be absorbed by the U$15.7 million savings made at the signing of the contracts. Thus, there is a positive balance of
       U$2.8million on the Guinea side.

      On the two (2) contracts financed by EIB (Construction work on the 225 kV Kankan - Kérouané double terne line
       and the 225 kV Kérouné - Beyla and Beyla - Nzérékoré double terne lines; Construction work on the 225/30 kV
       Beyla substation and extension work on the 225/30 kV N'zérékoré substation), the total price increase is U$14.5
       million.

      On the contract financed by EBID (Construction work on the 225 kV double-terne line from the Fomi substation to
       the Siguiri substation), the total price increase is U$2.6 million.
      On the contracts financed by AFDB and WADB in Mali (Construction of the 225 kV double-terne line between
       Sanankoroba substations and Guinean border), and by AFDB in Guinea (Construction of the 225/30 kV Fomi
       substation; Construction of the 225 kV double-terne line between Fomi and Kankan substations), the total price
       increases is U$10 million.
   For all the above three (3) cases, the authorities are working with the donor to address the financing gap/cost over-run.


Safeguards

   18. In Guinea, the Resettlement Action Plan (RAP) is being implemented and the Grievance Redress Mechanism
       (GRM) is working properly. A total of 17 complaints concerning the PAPs along the EBID-funded Fomi-Siguiri
       line, for which the RAP is being implemented, still remains to be resolved. For Mali, the GoM has sent a request to
       the Bank to finance the FCFA 2.3 billion (about U$4 million) required for compensations along the portion of the
       line financed by the AfDB and WADB. The national evaluation commission, finally set up on March 6, 2024,
       performed its field mission during March 12-20, 2024, to consult and confirm with PAPs the compensation amounts.
       The reevaluation report prepared by the commission confirmed the number of PAPs but revised the total

       compensation amount. Accordingly, the RAP has been updated [consulted on and disclosed], with a total budget of
       FCFA1.82 billion (nearly U$3.5 million).

   19. Delays in securing full compensation funds in Mali represent a major risk of delaying completion and
       commissioning of the Guinea-Mali transmission line by December 31, 2025.

Component 2: Support for project implementation

   20. Study of the connection of Guinean mining companies to the national interconnected grid in Guinea: The
       recruited Consultant has initiated services and met with the mining companies. A focal point at the Ministry of
       Mines has been appointed to facilitate interactions with the Consultant. The consultant is expected to deliver the
       report by June 30, 2024.

   21. Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) between Guinea and Mali: EDG has sent invitation to start negotiations with
       EDM. The PPA will be in line with the ECOWAS Regional Electricity Regulatory Authority (ARREC) model
       contract. It’s expected that a draft PPA will be discussed during the end of year Steering Committee.

   22. Acquisition of special equipment for the electricity companies EDG (Guinea) and EDM (Mali): In Guinea: two
       (2) maintenance trucks are planned to be acquired for EDG. The procurement process has been launched. In Mali,
       six (6) special vehicles for the maintenance of HV lines were procured and delivered to EDM July 10, 2023.

   C. Rationale for restructuring and extension of the closing date

   23. The construction of the transmission line and substations has experienced significant delays due to: (i) the COVID-
       19 pandemic that impacted the pace of implementation of project activities; (ii) the challenging political context
       with the coup d’état in Guinea and in Mali that resulted in activation of the OP 7.30 and the suspension of
       disbursements; (iii) the lack of leadership within the PIUs after the coordinators were suspended in Guinea and
       dismissed in Mali; and (iv) the explosion of the oil storage facilities in Guinea in December 2023, which created a
       nationwide fuel shortage. The December 2023 incident in Conakry brought construction work to a standstill for
       almost two months. In addition, the contractors did not deliver the design studies and plans on time and did not
       mobilize sufficient material and personnel resources for the civil engineering work.

   24. During the implementation support mission conducted in April 2024, the work implementation schedules were
       reviewed and updated in agreement with the PIUs, the OE and the contractors. This resulted in a revised overall
       project schedule, with a completion date and commissioning of the Guinea-Mali transmission line on December 31,
       2025. IDA-financed work will be completed by February 28, 2025.

   25. Considering the revised implementation schedule, the IDA-financed activities cannot be completed by the project
       closing date on June 30, 2024. Therefore, an extension is required to enable the completion of all the components
       of the transmission line.

   26. In this context, the Government of Guinea has requested an extension of 12 months until June 30, 2025, and the
       Government of Mali has requested an extension of 18 months until December 31, 2025, in the letters dated July 3,
       The World Bank
       Guinea – Mali Interconnection Project (P166042)


      2023, and February 5, 2024, respectively and sent to donors. The project team supports an extension of the project
      until December 31, 2025 (i.e., an 18-month extension) in line with other donors and to allow the PDO completion.
      This additional time will enable all project components to be completed and the transmission line commissioned.
      The World Bank will be able also to measure the achievement of the PDO and capture concrete outcome of the
      project in its ICR. Moreover, an eighteen-month project extension would allow, among others, to complete the
      following:

       a. Component 1: complete the ongoing construction of all the sections of the transmission lines and the associated
          substations, and the commissioning of the transmission line.

       b. Component 2: finalize the activities related to capacity building and implementation support.

       c. Avoid any reputational risk to the World Bank following potential litigation with contractors related to
          unfinished work at different sites.

  27. Despite difficult environments, the GoG and the GoM are still committed to the project as the Guinea-Mali
      interconnector will allow to supply to their populations with clean and cheaper electricity from the Guinean
      hydroelectricity, particularly from the complex of Souapiti and Kaleta. The design of the Guinea-Mali
      interconnection project is in line with the WAPP master plan. The line will be connected to the IDA-financed
      OMVG and CLSG Interconnections, thus serving the WAPP’s objective of creating a subregional electricity market.

  28. The proposed closing date extension is in line with the World Bank Policy on Investment Project Financing (IPF).
      This request is based on the following: (i) PDO remains highly relevant and achievable subject to extension of
      closing date; (ii) the project remains likely to fully achieve its PDO within the extended period; (iii) the Recipients
      have prepared a revised implementation schedule with the support of the OE (agreed with the World Bank) to ensure
      implementation of all project activities and disbursement of all funds by the proposed revised closing date; (iv) the
      project is not subject to suspension; and (v) the audit reports are satisfactory to the World Bank and there are no
      outstanding ones. The project will continue to benefit from environmental and social provisions throughout the
      proposed extension. The World Bank’s team will continue to provide close follow-up on the IDA financed activities
      to ensure their completion by February 28, 2025. The revised implementation schedule is presented in Annex 1.



II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

  29. The proposed changes to the project include the following: (i) extension of the closing date of credits IDA-62610
      and IDA-62620 and the grants IDA-D3360 and IDA-D3370 by eighteen (18) months; (ii) changes to disbursement
      estimates; and (iii) changes to the implementation schedule.

  30. Closing date extension: It is proposed to extend the closing date of credits IDA-62610 and IDA-62620 and the
      grants IDA-D3360 and IDA-D3370 by eighteen (18) months, from the current closing date of June 30, 2024, to
      December 31, 2025. The proposed extension aims to allow time to fully implement all project activities and disburse
      all the project funds.
