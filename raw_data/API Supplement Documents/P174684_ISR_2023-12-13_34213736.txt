 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Project implementation and disbursements for the PIC3 project are still low due to continued challenges with "regularisation budgetaire",
  leading to another downgrading of the project performance to Moderately Unsatisfactory. Disbursement from the World Bank (WB) to the
  project designated account amounts to USD 12.8 million (equivalent to 9 percent of the total budget).
  Delays in meeting conditions for project effectiveness and governance issues with the Project's Steering Committee led to initial delays in
  implementation. Over the last 12 months cash flow problems at the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) level have added significant delays to the
  implementation of activities. These problems arise both from cumbersome procedures in processing budgetary regularization (and delays in

  Progress by components
  Component 1
  Business climate: The project continues to support activities that facilitate the business environment in the country. Notably, PIC is helping to draft
  the regulations that need to accompany the recently approved new Investment Law. The PIC also continues to support the Economic Development
  Board of Madagascar (EDBM), especially to promote sector focused investment promotion activities as well as to support the expansion of EDBM
  and its services into previously underserved regions of the country where the project PIC will now operate in.
  Financial inclusion: The transfer of two initiatives from the PIFM project (closed in December 2022) to the PIC3 is now complete. This support
  concerns the implementation of the National Payment Switch as well as specific support for microfinance institutions. A dedicated person was
  recruited by the PIU to supervise the implementation of these activities.
  National Payment Switch: The earlier delay in the procurement of the National Payment System (Switch) is being addressed, and the supplier of the
  system presented its new technical and financial proposals to the PIU. The signing of the contract is expected before the end of November 2023.
  Support for Microfinance Institutions: The PIC will be supporting the digitalization of front office services for 8 selected MFIs in the regions of PIC
  interventions.
  Agribusiness: The project continues to support the promotion of agribusiness exports through a number of activities, including Madagascar’s
  participation in trade fairs and other key sector events (SIRHA, SIAL, BioFach, Week of Excellence) to establish new commercial relationships and
  partnerships. The PIC is also supporting Government efforts to address and be able to meet the new Maximum Nicotine Residue Limits on spices
  imposed by the European Union. This affects several export products being supported by the project.
  Component 2
  Tourism: The Government, the WB, and the PIU are moving towards framing the majority of recurring activities that support the tourism sector into a
  “contract” or “MoU” framework whereby activities are reviewed and agreed upon upfront, to avoid recurrent and frequent individual requests for No
  Objection. Examples of activities that will be included in these MoUs are participation in trade fairs, and organization of promotional events, among
  others. These agreements are expected to increase efficiency in obtaining WB approval and implementing small-scale recurrent activities.
  The WB and the UGP discussed during the supervision mission held in October 2023 two new requests for road investments, in Diego (Joffreville/
  Montagne d'Ambre) and Sainte Marie (airport access road), as well as two new requests for airport investments (Diego and Tuléar) for the
  development of regional tourism. Before starting the studies that will be necessary to confirm the technical and financial needs of these activities,
  the World Bank requested that these activities be included in the formal process of preparation, discussion, and approval of the PTBA 2024. In
  addition, the WB team noted that the proposed Tuléar airport investment requires further and broader consultations with the private sector to assess
  demand for the initiative, and that the project should seek out more formal commitments to invest in the region from interested private sector
  operators. Finally, The WB team requested that a mapping of existing and proposed infrastructure activities be prepared by the PIU, to better
  visualize and ensure a balanced allocation of project resources across all regions supported by the project.
  Agribusiness: The PIC teams in the new regions (east coast) have started to scale up technical supervision and traceability support activities for
  selected value chains: distribution of “markers” to 4,400 producers is underway to support traceability, as well as the establishment of 50 organized
  markets to facilitate the selling of these products. Three agricultural access roads have been prioritized for project intervention in the new regions
  and the related technical studies will be launched at the beginning of 2024. Rehabilitation work is also planned during 2024 for three piers/landing
  structures along the Canal de Pangalanes to facilitate commerce in the region. Finally, the rehabilitation work on the Haut Sambirano road started
  under PIC2.2 will be finalized under PIC3 during the first semester of 2024.
  Digital: Discussions with the Ministry in charge of Digital Development (MNDPT) have led to the prioritization of the following activities to be pursued
  (or already being implemented) under PIC3: (i) preparation and launch of the “Miatrika Digital” program, which will help with specialized training for
  the BPO sector to facilitate further investments in the sector; (ii) support the review of the legal framework for the digital sector, mainly linked to the
  development of the private sector and entrepreneurship; (iii) continue to strengthen the implementation of MIARY digital. Further discussions
  between the Ministry, the DECIM project and the WB are needed to coordinate potential interventions in support of digital infrastructure.
  Local governance: 49 small projects are under implementation (OCAI grants) to improve productive infrastructures in selected communities in
  priority regions. These projects extend the works performed under previous PIC projects to the new regions and help to mobilize additional financial
  contribution from the municipalities for capital investments that facilitate economic activity at the community level.
  Component 3:
  The project launched in July 2023 the MIARY Digital, a digital incubation program in partnership with the 3 main incubators in Madagascar. 47
  entrepreneurs (18 women) have been selected for the first cohort and are currently going through a 12-month incubation program with one of the 3
  incubators selected. MBIF Agribusiness Program will be launched in December 2023 and has been adjusted to take into account feedback
  received during the first 2 waves of projects. Among the changes made, the program will now have a separate window for “start-up” projects, which
  will receive a smaller co-financing to address higher risks inherent in companies without a longer operation track-record. The program will be
  launched across all 9 regions supported by the project, and it aims again to improve the competitiveness of key value chains, by strengthening the
  commercial link between agricultural input providers and agribusinesses that transform primary products, for export or local markets. The
  Acceleration of SMEs program is under preparation, while the new wave of MIARY Agri and Tourism program is being prepared, with a structure
  adjusted to consider feedback received during evaluation of the first few cohorts of beneficiaries. Additional human resources will be needed to
  allow for the implementation of these additional programs. It is expected that these additional people will be hired by January-February 2024.
