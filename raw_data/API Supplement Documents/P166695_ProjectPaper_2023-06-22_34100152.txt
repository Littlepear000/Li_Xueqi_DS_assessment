 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

Project Status:

1.      The Gambia Fiscal Management Development Project (FMDP – P166695), financed through a
US$35 million International Development Association (IDA) Grant, was approved by the Board of
Executive Directors on June 4, 2020, and became effective on July 28, 2020. The Project Development
Objective (PDO) is “to increase fiscal space and improve the effectiveness of public resources
management.” To achieve this objective, the project comprises three components: (i) strengthening
revenue collection and public procurement processes; (ii) supporting fiscal management of State-Owned
Enterprises (SOEs) and restructuring plans of telecoms SOEs; and (iii) project implementation support and
reform coordination. At the time of appraisal, the PDO and project components were aligned with the
          The World Bank
          GAMBIA FISCAL MANAGEMENT DEVELOPMENT PROJECT (P166695)


strategic objectives of the Government’s National Development Plan (NDP) and the World Bank Group
Country Engagement Note for the Republic of The Gambia for FY18-FY21 (CEN n° IDA/R 2018-0139).

2.      After more than two years since effectiveness, progress toward achieving the PDO is
Moderately Unsatisfactory, with a cumulative disbursement rate of only 21 percent. Achievement of
the PDO has remained off track as a result of delays in key reform areas, including: (i) lack of a clear
strategy and roadmap to adopting an e-procurement system due to weak internal structures and
expertise, as well as delay in passage of the Gambia Public Procurement Authority (GPPA) bill; (ii) delay in
the passage of the SOE bill that provides the legal basis for the creation of the SOE oversight authority,
which the project activities and Performance-Based Conditions (PBCs) were specifically designed to
support; and (iii) delays in the Integrated Tax Administration System (ITAS) business process reengineering
and technical specifications, which impeded system acquisition and progress on key project indicators.

3.       As a result, annual targets have not been met for two of the three PDO indicators. The indicator
measuring Value Added Tax (VAT) Gross compliance ratio achieved an estimated end year performance
of 32.2% against an annual target of 30% (PDO 1); however, this performance is largely nonattributable
to project activities. Likewise, while the aggregate 2021 State-Owned Enterprises report has been
published by the SOE centralized oversight agency (PDO 2), it was only released in May 2023 and is based
on management, and not audited, figures. Finally, the share of competitive biddings for procuring
organizations using electronic government procurement remains at 0 against a target of 30 percent (PDO
3), not achieving its annual target. In addition to delays in key reform areas, this lack of demonstrable
results is also due to project design flaws, whereby an overly ambitious PDO formulation and misaligned
results framework do not allow for adequate attribution between project activities and intended results.

4.        Despite the initial delay pertaining to key activities, the overall project Implementation Progress
(IP) is currently rated Moderately Satisfactory, as recent improvements in progress have been recorded
over the past year.1 In particular, all three components have recorded Moderately Satisfactory
performance, and key milestones have been reached, including the completion of the Gambia Revenue
Authority (GRA) business re-engineering and ITAS tendering; the passage of the GPPA Bill by the National
Assembly; passage of the SOE Bill by the National Assembly; the acquisition of the Gambia Public Utilities
Regulatory Authority (PURA) Quality of Service monitoring tool for 4G and the completion of the Spectrum
assessment report; the development of an electronic government procurement (e-GP) readiness report;
the organization of the market test for Gamtel/Gamcel; as well as the finalization of the first SOE
performance audit report by the National Audit Office. Nevertheless, other key project milestones are
delayed, including Government decisions on Gamtel/Gamcel privatization options.

Rationale for Restructuring:

10.    Given the project status described above, a level two project restructuring is proposed in order
to address the following issues, which impact the implementation of project activities and the
achievement of the PDO:

      Reformulation of the PDO: Given the ambitious formulation of the original PDO to: (i) increase
       fiscal space; and (ii) improve the effectiveness of public resources management, adjustments are
       needed to increase PDO attribution, relevance, and realism relative to project scope. In particular,
       increasing fiscal space involves many factors outside of the project’s direct influence and project
       activities cannot be directly attributed to this result given numerous exogenous factors that can
       impact fiscal space in The Gambia—including recent macro fiscal developments related to
       inflation and Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. In addition, the objective of improving the overall
       effectiveness of public resource management is likewise beyond the direct control of project
       activities, and no indicators exist to effectively operationalize and measure effectiveness.
       Accordingly, the PDO needs to be revised to capture only outcomes directly attributable to project
       activities, thereby enhancing its relevance and likelihood of achievement.

      Reformulation of Project Indicators: A principal reason why majority of the PDO indicators and
       intermediate indicators have not reached their annual targets is because they are not adequately
       measurable and attributable to project activities. Accordingly, it will be necessary to devise new
       and/or reformulate existing indicators—including with realistic baselines and targets—so project
       outputs and outcomes can be adequately measured and results can be substantiated over the life
       of the project.

      Streamlining of Project Activities: In line with a revised PDO and results indicators, project
       activities need to be streamlined to ensure that well-performing activities are prioritized and
       continued, and poorly performing activity areas revised and/or cancelled. In addition, activities
       that are not directly linked to the PDO need to be reorganized and/or reassigned to other projects,
       which are better able to support their technical objectives.

      Adjustment of Implementation Arrangements: Given several organizational changes that have
       occurred since project appraisal, there is a need to update the implementation arrangements.
       This will ensure that the arrangements reflect the current structure and reporting lines within
       MoFEA as well as with respect to the technical focal points of the project within other beneficiary
       structures, for potentially improved implementation.

      Adjustment of Project Closing Date and Cancelation of Unused Funds: In line with the amended
       activities and PDO, adjustments will be made to the envelope of IDA resources needed to achieve
       the revised scope of activities, and accordingly, to the project’s timeline.

11.    Given these reasons, this Restructuring Paper (RP) seeks the approval of the Regional Vice
President (RVP) for the proposed restructuring of the Gambia Fiscal Management Development Project
(FMDP). This restructuring proposal is in response to the request from the Government of The Gambia

             GAMBIA FISCAL MANAGEMENT DEVELOPMENT PROJECT (P166695)


(GoTG) through a letter dated August 10, 20222. The following changes to the project were requested by
the Government: (i) early closure of the Gambia Fiscal Management Development Project and

    VII.     Implementation Arrangements:

18.      A dedicated Project Implementing Unit (PIU) comprised of a project manager, financial
management officer, procurement officer, monitoring and evaluation officer, governance officer, and
legal officer has been established within the Ministry of Finance and Economic Affairs (MoFEA) for the
day-to-day coordination and management of the project. To support coordination between the PIU and
the implementing partners, a network of designated focal-persons in each MDA involved (GRA, GPPA,
NAO, IAD, MoFEA, MOICI, PURA) with the implementation of the project has been established to facilitate
coordination. The identified focal persons of these MDAs are engaged in the implementation and
coordination of their respective components and facilitate decision making with their management
teams. The Permanent Secretary (PS) of MoFEA is responsible for the overarching strategic coordination
and oversight of the project supported by a Project Steering Committee (PSC) which is chaired by him/her.
To ensure representation from involved stakeholders, the membership of the PSC is drawn across key
implementing beneficiary institutions in order to ensure that they are duly represented in the strategic
decision-making process thus encouraging participation and ownership of project implementation
activities.

19.      Drawing lessons from current practice, there is a need to update the institutional framework to
enhance flow of information for strategic decision making. The diagram below illustrates the proposed
new structure which aims to enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of coordination of the project
activities between the PIU, project implementing partners, and project steering committee. As such: (i)
The Permanent Secretary of MoFEA will continue to chair the PSC with the PIU serving as a secretariat; (ii)
additional implementing partners will be added, including the National Audit Office (NAO), Directorate of
State-Owned Enterprises (DSOE), Internal Audit Directorate (IAD), and Ministry of Public Administration
(MPA); and (iii) a change in responsibility for the financial oversight of SOEs from The Directorate of Public
Private Partnership and Public Enterprises (DPPP&PE) to the newly created Directorate of SOE, which will
now serve as the Implementing Partner responsible for SOE reform related activities to be financed under
Sub-Component 2.1 and 2.2.

    VIII.    Economic Analysis:

20.      The economic analysis performed at project appraisal estimated an improvement in revenue
collection. In this regard, the projected economic impact based on a number of assumed project
outcomes, including: (i) Improved capacity in GRA to actively manage and improve tax compliance from a
low base; (ii) Improved performance in terms of timely payment of taxes and effective tax debt
management; and (iii) policy reforms in systemic areas that could be reformed relatively quickly and with
a positive impact on revenues. Based on these assumptions, the original economic analysis projected tax
revenues to reach 2.5 percent of GDP over ten years, with an overall net present value (NPV) of cash flows
expected to reach US$26 million over the period. However, since many of these anticipated activities have
not been completed, including the acquisition and roll-out of the Integrated Tax Administration System
(ITAS), it is not currently possible to quantify/ attribute financial and economic returns based on tax
administration.

21.      Given that it is unlikely that economic and financial benefits can be assigned to the tax
administration activities, the economic analysis of the restructured project will focus principally on
efficiency gains related to the SOE reform agenda. In this regard, several important activities have been
implemented, or will be implemented by the end of the project for this component, including: (i) Support
to the operationalization of the newly created Directorate of SOEs (DSOE); (ii) Audits of SOE by a private
accounting firm and support for audit function; (iii) SOE Bill approved by the National Assembly for
enactment; (iv) the Development of Performance Contracts between SOEs and Central Government and
developing the capacity of DSOE on performance monitoring of SOEs; as well as (v) a number of critical
reforms of GAMTEL and GAMCEL that are fundamental to the strengthening of their financial viability and
operational performance. Given these activities, the NPV and real rate of return (RRI) were re-calculated
based on the following: (i) SOEs net profits, from 2017 until the end of the project; (ii) Increased dividend
payments from SOEs to government as a result of increased profits; and (iii) a reduction of Government
subsidies to SOEs, from 2017 until the end of the project. The tables below summarize the calculations as
follows:

25.      Political and governance risk is reduced from high in the original project to substantial in the
restructured project. The Gambia continues to suffer from a complex political economy, weak
governance, and political tensions. The government has undergone political reshuffling, and political
opposition has increased, as noted during the recently completed presidential elections. Going forward,
political resistance is likely to be mitigated by the strong executive and legislative mandate held by the
current government. In particular, President Barrow—reelected for a second 5-year term in December
2021 – will be supported by a parliamentary majority also with a 5-year mandate. At the same time, the
restructured project will support on-going transparency and accountability reforms, for which there is
high political and popular support, and which complement the ongoing fiscal stabilization program and
reform measures supported by the recently approved Second Fiscal Management, Energy and Telecom
Reform DPF: Supplemental Financing: (P179543).

26.     Macroeconomic risk is maintained as high in the restructured project. While the macro-
economic outlook was uncertain at the time of appraisal, COVID-19 has dampened the short-term
economic outlook, exacerbated further by the spillover effects of Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. As a result,
reforms to reduce fiscal risks, improve domestic resource mobilization, and strengthen public
procurement and SOE oversight may be impacted. While the country has a track record of solid
macroeconomic performance, it is still fragile and relies heavily on external donor support. The
government is already facing spending pressures to support vulnerable households facing high energy and
food prices and agricultural input costs. These risks are partially mitigated by the authorities’ commitment
to fiscal and debt sustainability, including the Sustainable Development Finance Policy's (SDFP)
performance and policy actions (PPAs) as well as the IMF Extended Credit Facility (ECF), which are
expected to crowd in additional financial support from other development partners and help maintain a
sound macroeconomic environment.

27.      Sectoral strategies and policies risk is reduced from substantial in the original project to
moderate in the restructured project. Since the time of appraisal, the government has strengthened its
mandate and has been able to articulate a number of relevant sector reform strategies that support the
objectives of the restructured project. In particular, the Public Financial Management (PFM) Strategy
2021-2025 seeks to ensure better PFM through the strategic allocation of resources, aggregate fiscal
discipline, and efficient service delivery. The PFM Strategy equally seeks to create transparent,
accountable, and credible PFM systems to manage public resources for informed decision-making and
effective provision of public services. In this regard, the project's close alignment with these strategies
helps to mitigate risks related to sector strategies and policies. Moreover, the recently established
Ministry of Public Administration has been charged with monitoring the implementation progress of the
selected priority reforms, which can contribute improved monitoring of sectoral strategies, including
those supported by the restructured project.

28.     Technical design risk is reduced from substantial in the original project to moderate in the
restructured project. The original operation involved diverse areas of intervention, including tax
administration, procurement, audit, SOE oversight, and telecoms restructuring, which require the
involvement of a number of MDAs for successful implementation. In addition, the acquisition of new
information systems, including the ITAS and the e-Procurement system, have exposed the project to a
complex and delayed process for determining technical specifications and solution procurement. In the
restructured operation, these risks are mitigated by a streamlined scope of project activities, whereby
well-performing activities are scaled-up and continued, and poorly performing activity areas are revised
 The World Bank
 GAMBIA FISCAL MANAGEMENT DEVELOPMENT PROJECT (P166695)


and/or cancelled. In addition, as described above, activities that are not directly linked to the PDO will be
reorganized and/or reassigned to other projects, which are better able to support their technical
objectives. In particular, the well-performing activities under the project such as support for tax
administration and policy as well as internal and external audit capacity will be continued, while the
activities with weak performance such as e-procurement and SOE reform will be revised or cancelled as
necessary. For its part, the Gamtel/Gamcel privatization and telecommunication reform technical
assistance will be transferred to the Western Africa Regional Digital Integration Program (WARDIP-
P176932).

29.      Institutional capacity for implementation and sustainability risk is maintained as substantial in
the restructured project. Administrative and human resource capacities are weak across the public
service in The Gambia. These capacity constraints are particularly pertinent given the large procurements
involved in the project, including specialized technical assistance and complex IT systems, in a context
where it is difficult to timely and affordably attract and secure qualified vendors. To mitigate this risk, a
number of measures will be built into the restructured project, including: (i) reducing the scope of project
activities to be achieved under the original project, including removing the acquisition or the e-
procurement system; (ii) reprioritizing areas that were slow moving or where implementation challenges
exist, including delays in passing needed legislation; (iii) providing training and capacity building in key
areas beneficial to both the project as well as the PIU, which has experience implementing challenging
fiscal reform projects; and (iv) maintaining the oversight role of the Project Steering Committee (PSC),
chaired by the Permanent Secretary (PS) of MoFEA (the lead implementing agency), which has proven to
be very effective in managing multiple stakeholders in the implementation of complex technical activities.

30.      Fiduciary risk has been reduced from substantial in the original project to moderate in the
restructured project. The existing fiduciary risk is driven by ongoing systemically weak PFM systems and
capacity constraints, which have resulted in a relatively low disbursement rate after 2 years and 6 months
of implementing project activities. While financial management performance has been Moderately
Satisfactory as of the most recent Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) (archived on December
16, 2022), additional actions require follow-up, including: (i) the implementation of the internal auditor
recommendations; (ii) the submission of the Annual Work Plan each year at latest by November 30 in
accordance with the date stated in the financing agreement; and (iii) the regularization of the
overpayment from the designated account when processing final payment of spectrum management
contract. At the same time, procurement performance has been Satisfactory as a result of improvement
in the use of STEP and an adequate procurement arrangement. Since the last procurement risk
assessment conducted on April 10, 2022, STEP shows: (i) continued progress in implementing
procurement activities; and (ii) a regular update of the Procurement Plan (PP) has been made, the last of
which occurred on November 15, 2022. Any existing fiduciary issues will be mitigated by the timely filing
of interim financial reports and subsequent implementation support missions conducted by the Bank.

31.     Social and environmental risks are maintained as moderate in the restructured project. At
appraisal, the expected environmental impacts of the project were generally minor as the project will not
finance civil engineering works, which usually can induce risks and/or negative environmental and social
impacts. To mitigate these risks, the Government has prepared, consulted upon, and disclosed the
following instruments: Stakeholder Engagement Plan (SEP), Environmental and Social Commitment Plan
(ESCP), Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM), and Labor Management Procedures (LMP). An Environment
and Social Review Summary (ESRS) was likewise prepared and disclosed on March 2, 2020. While the
restructured project does not introduce any additional social or environmental risks, key actions will be
 The World Bank
 GAMBIA FISCAL MANAGEMENT DEVELOPMENT PROJECT (P166695)


taken to further improve the accessibility and usage of the Grievance Redress Mechanisms (GRM) in line
with the Stakeholder Engagement Plan, including: (i) the appointment of a focal point responsible for
managing and analyzing complaints and maintaining a database of complaints and the resolutions; (ii) the
establishment of a toll-free dedicated phone line, email address, and /or suggestions/complaints box; (iii)
test pilots of these mechanisms; and (iv) the development of a database for complaints.

32.      Stakeholder risk is maintained as substantial in the restructured project. The primary
stakeholders of this project include the Ministry of Finance and Economic Affairs (MoFEA), Gambia
Revenue Authority (GRA), Gambia Public Procurement Authority (GPPA), Ministry of Public Administration
(MPA), Gambia Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA), Ministry of Information, Communications,
and Infrastructure (MOICI), National Audit Office (NAO), and selected line/sectoral SOEs and MDAs. A key
risk relates to a potential lack of ownership by stakeholders and limited involvement of line ministries in
the implementation of the project, which may undermine the ability of the Government to complete
implementation on time. To mitigate this risk, the current Project Steering Committee (PSC) arrangement
will be continued and chaired by the Permanent Secretary of MoFEA and include representatives from
key stakeholders to provide strategic guidance and oversight of the project and ensure robust stakeholder
coordination.
