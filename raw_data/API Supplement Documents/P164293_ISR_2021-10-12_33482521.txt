 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Overview. The five-year Burkina-Faso Higher Education Support Project, approved by the World Bank Board of Executive Directors on July 10,
  2018, became effective on December 19, 2018, and it is expected to close on September 30, 2023. The Project’s development objectives are to:
  strengthen higher education institutions to increase access and deliver quality education in priority subject areas.

  Progress by Component: The Project has three main components. Progress by components is summarized below.

  Component 1. Enhancing Access to Higher Education: Establishment of the Virtual University of Burkina Faso

  The next project virtual supervision mission is expected to take place in mid-October 2021 and the information presented herein will be updated
  accordingly at that time. The implementation of Component 1 of the Project is underway, and the following significant results have been achieved:

  Regarding the Virtual University of Burkina Faso (VU-BF):

            The work to upgrade its headquarters—establishment of computer and telecommunication networks, finalization of civil work/infrastructure
             activities and provision of relevant equipment—has now been completed and the final handover to the Government is underway.

            The Virtual University of Burkina Faso has developed a strategic plan and a business that is expected to be validated by November 2021.
             A 2022 - 2024 triennial action plan and a draft financial resource mobilization strategy have also been developed and are currently
             pending validation by the Government.

            The architectural and technical studies of the digital open spaces have been completed and will be submitted to the World Bank for non-
             objection before the end of September for a launch of the calls for tenders by November 15th, 2021.

   Regarding the Project’s support to University Libraries all HERIs libraries have received equipment consisting of cupboards, trolleys, tables for
  computers and the market for books for upgrading libraries in the priority areas (agriculture, agri-food, logistics associated with the value chain,
  entrepreneurship training, trade and other economic activities) identified has been awarded and the receipt of 2,952 book titles is planned for this
  4th quarter.

  The recruitment of a firm to support the upgrading and strengthening of connectivity infrastructure within the HERIs targeted by the
  Project is also underway. The call for tender is being finalized and will be submitted to the World Bank for non-objection by the end of October
  2021 It should be noted that the cost of the activity has increased following the study carried out by the firm recruited by the Project,
  increasing from 500 million FCFA ($US 1 million) to around 3 billion ($US 6 million) . The call for tenders will be launched in the 4th quarter and the
  providers selected. The duration of the works could reach up to 10 months.
  The procurement contract for specific equipment for teaching and research laboratories in physics, chemistry and life sciences has been launched
  and the analysis has been underway since August 26th, 2021. The award will be completed with the signing of contracts in October 2021. Suppliers
  have 120 days to deliver the procured goods and/or services. The reception of this very important equipment will take place in the first quarter of
  2022.

  With regard to the “one student, one computer” program, more than 10,300 computers have been delivered to students to date. A new 7,000-
  computer agreement was signed on November 26, 2020. However, due to challenges stemming from the COVID 19 pandemic, providers were
  unable to deliver all procured computer. To date, a total of 700 computers were effectively delivered and the remaining 6,300 computers are
  scheduled for delivery in Q4 2021.


  Component 2. Strengthen higher education institutions and support innovations.

  Implementation of programmed activities are also somewhat delayed under Component 2 due to the competitive funds mechanism’s (sub-
  component 2.1) increased performance-based and merit-based funding modality recently adopted by the Project. In addition, delays have also
  resulted from the school closures stemming from the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic. Limited implementation progress has been made since
  the February 2020 mission due to the pandemic. However, to date, all the activities are under implementation and it is expected that for the next
  academic year (2021/2022), progress will be made in terms of improving teaching, building or revamping laboratories and libraries, and fostering
  innovation within the Campus Faso. The World Bank’s task team continues to work closely with the Government to devise creative ways to ensure
  the implementation progress improves in the coming months.


  Sub-component 2.1. Competitive Funds. A total of ten performance-based contracts (PBC), worth a total amount of approximately 11 billion CFA
  francs (around $US 22 million), were approved on March 19, 2021.

   On January 21, 2021 the performance contracts were signed, while the financing agreements were subsequently signed in March 2021. In August,
  the action plans for the year 2021, as well as the PPM, were validated and obtained the World Bank's non-objection, thus allowing the release of
  20% of the allocated amount. These contracts will be readjusted, and amendments made to the agreements to take into account the delay in the
  start of activities and the changes in the procurement process.


  Sub-component 2.2 - Developing the Faculty in Higher Education Institutions (HEI). This sub-component has now started to be implemented,
  despite the significant initial delays observed.

  The question of scholarships for doctoral students. One of the major activities of this sub-component is to grant excellence scholarships to
  doctoral students to strengthen the pool of higher education teachers. The selection documents have been validated by the World Bank, the call for
  applications has been published since July 15th, 2021 and the deadline for receiving applications has been set at September 15th, 2021. A
  technical committee has been set up and will select the 100 best thesis projects. Particular attention will also be paid to each student’s academic
  credentials and aptitude to pursue further doctoral studies.

  The activity related to the recruitment of qualified teachers for the priority areas is not yet completed.

  Sub-component 2.3 - Strengthen Quality Assurance in HEI. As already stated in previous ISRs, and with regards to the internal quality
  assurance units (IQAU), eight IQAU have been established in the public HEIs.

  Regarding the development of a national quality assurance strategy and a national qualification framework for higher education, the activity is
  almost completed (estimated completion rate of 88%) and the consultant in charge of this study will be in Ouagadougou from October 4 to 8, 2021
  to finalize the study and train the IQAU members.
  All eight established IQUA were provided with furniture and computer equipment.

  Sub-component 2.4 - Strengthen HEIs Management Capacity. Five activities are included in this sub-component within the framework of the
  implementation of the 2020 ABWP. As of November 15, 2020, four activities are under implementation, while an additional one has not yet been
  launched .

  The study of the organizational and HERIs functional diagnosis is still in progress. The interim report was submitted to the World Bank on July 29,
  2021. The restitution took place in early August before the Strategic Orientation Committee (SOC) from the Ministry of Higher Education, Scientific
  Research and Innovation (MHESRI). It should be noted that many shortcomings were noted in this report by both the World Bank and the SOC. The
  cabinet was instructed to take it back for consideration of the amendments proposed and resubmit a revised version of the report.

  As part of the implementation of DLI 6 relating to revenue generation, a subsidy of XOF 1,250,000,000 ($US 2,5 million) was granted to 4 HERI –
  University Joseph Ki Zerbo, University Thomas Sankara, University Nazi Boni and University Norbert Zongo). Action plans for the use of this grant
  were submitted by each HERI and received comments from the World Bank. These comments will be incorporated, and the plans resubmitted to
  the World Bank for non-objection.

   Component 3. Project coordination, management and implementation activities. The higher education program which oversees the Higher
  Education Support Project held its first review committee session of the year 2021 on July 23rd, 2021, roughly around the examination and adoption
  of the summary report of the second ordinary session of 2020; the presentation of the project's ABWP implementation report (on June 30, 2021);
  and the presentation of the revised 2021 ABWP and the 2021 revised procurement plan. The review committee adopted the revised ABWP and the
  2021 project procurement plan.

  Under Component 3, the recruitment of an individual consultant to develop a risk matrix and an internal control charter specific to the Project, has
  been postponed to the fourth quarter 2021. The task team will continue to work closely with the client to ensure this activity is successfully delivered
  in the coming months.
