I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Project Status
   1. This paper seeks approval from the Country Director for a Level II restructuring of the National Social Safety Net Program
   Scale-Up project (NASSP-SU, P176935, Credit No. 7019-NG), an US$800 million Investment Project Financing (IPF). The
   restructuring will extend the project closing date by 18 months from June 30, 2024 to December 31, 2025. The benefit size and
   duration of the cash transfers under component 1 will also be changed. Finally, the chairmanship of the project’s national
   steering committee will be changed from the Minister of Humanitarian Affairs and Poverty Alleviation to the Minister of Finance.
   This is the first restructuring and extension of the project’s closing date.

    2. The NASSP-SU was approved on December 16, 2021 and declared effective on January 30, 2023. The Project Development
    Objective (PDO) is to expand coverage of shock responsive safety net support among the poor and vulnerable and strengthen
    the national safety net delivery system in Nigeria. The project three components, (i) Economic Shock Responsive Cash Transfer
    (ESR-CT) that provides temporary cash transfers to provide relief against economic shocks to eligible beneficiaries in urban and
    rural areas, identified in the Social Registry; (ii) Extended Regular Cash Transfer (ER-CT) to continue supporting poor and
    vulnerable households identified under the National Social Safety Net Project (NASSP, P151488) with a regular cash transfer
    support for up to two years; (iii) Support to Project Management and Social Protection system strengthening activities.

    3. The overall progress towards achieving the PDO is moderately satisfactory. Progress has been slower than anticipated due
    to delays in declaring project effectiveness and the approval of the external borrowing plan by the National Assembly to
    commence project activities (October 2023). Since its start, about 30 million beneficiaries have been covered by social safety net
    programs, and about three million poor and vulnerable households have received shock responsive cash transfers as of May
    2024. Of these beneficiaries, 700 thousand households were from rural areas and about 2.5 million households were from urban
    areas. 1,652 urban wards have been covered through the targeting system developed under the project. Furthermore, the
    planned digital payment delivery mechanism has been put in place, using straight-through-processing to deliver transfers directly
    to beneficiaries’ accounts or wallets. Recently, the National Social Register (NSR) is being integrated with the National
    Identification Number (NIN) to further strengthen the targeting system.

    4. The Project implementation progress is moderately satisfactory. The Project Implementing Units (PIUs) at federal and state
    level are in place. Project implementation was paused from January 2024 to March 2024 due to the government’s review of the
    National Social Investment Programs, which includes the cash transfer program supported under NASSP SU. The multi-ministerial
    review Panel recommended the program to resume implementation with a focus on biometric verification of the beneficiaries.
    Despite earlier delays, the project remains central to the government’s ambitious plan to provide temporary cash transfer
    support to the population affected adversely by high inflation, particularly in the wake of the fuel subsidy removal and other
    macroeconomic reforms the government is undertaking. No financial or audit reports are pending, and there are no changes in
    the audit requirements. There have been some delays in procuring key service providers, and contract management practices
    are being improved by building the capacity of the PIU’s procurement team. The World Bank will keep providing embedded
    support to the project to improve the financial management and procurement practices.

    5. The Overall environmental and social performance is moderately satisfactory, and the overall risk rating remains
    substantial. The Environmental and Social Management Plan (ESMP) to mitigate the risks envisaged under the project has been
    prepared and disclosed in-country and on the World Bank external website. Two fatal incidents involving project staff were
    recorded. A project staff returning from conducting field activities was involved in a road accident and another project staff was

    B. Rationale for Restructuring
       6. Nigeria’s persistently high inflation has worsened since 2020 and reached a multi-decade high in early 2024, contributing
       to a rise in poverty. In 13 of the last 15 years, inflation was higher than nine percent, with sharper increases during and after
       COVID-19 pandemic. As of March 2024, annual inflation stood at 33.2 percent, driven by, among others, rising fuel prices
       following the removal of subsidy and exchange rate depreciation. High inflation, combined with the slow pace of economic
       growth, has pushed many people into poverty, increasing the number of poor from 80 million in 2018 to over 109 million in
       20231.

        7. Faced with a fragile economic situation, the Government of Nigeria undertook difficult but critical reforms. On its first day,
        May 29, 2023, the current government ended the gasoline subsidy and in June 2023, new foreign exchange policy was adopted
        to merge multiple exchange rate regime prevalent in the country. While necessary in the medium and longer term, these reforms
        further fueled the already high levels of inflation, exacerbating the poverty and vulnerability situation in the immediate term.

        8. The World Bank will support the government’s efforts to build fiscal space and protect the poor and economically insecure
        through a DPO. The Reforms for Economic Stabilization to Enable Transformation (RESET, P501661) DPF will provide a US$1.5
        billion budget support to the Nigerian government, to be delivered in two tranches of US$750 million each. Protecting the poor
        and economically insecure is a key pillar of the DPO, with a prior action directly linked to rolling out and sustaining targeted cash
        transfers supported under NASSP-SU. Extending the closing date for NASSP-SU will continue the cash transfers to poor and
        vulnerable households and support reforms aimed at strengthening the social registry proposed under the DPO.

        9. To cushion the impact on the poor and vulnerable population, the government announced support programs in October
        2023. The largest of such support programs is the temporary cash transfer program to poor and vulnerable households. Under
        this program, the government plans to reach 15 million households with N75,000 (US$ 53 equivalent) per household provided
        over three monthly payments of N25,000 (US$ 17.7 equivalent) each. The government decided to utilize the NASSP-SU project
        to finance the roll-out of the cash transfer program and strengthen the safety net delivery platform, while laying the foundation
        for future shock response programs. To ensure that the project can support the government’s program, the economic shock
        response cash transfer component must be adjusted to align with the design of the cash transfer program of the government.

        10. The Federal Ministry of Finance, through a letter dated March 4, 2024, formally requested that the NASSP-SU project be
        restructured to reflect the changes in the design of the cash transfer program. Furthermore, the letter requested an
        extension to the closing date of the project, to accommodate the implementation of project activities initially planned aimed at
        strengthening the safety net delivery systems and the project management. The restructuring request led to detailed
        discussions with the PIUs and representatives from the Federal Ministry of Finance to agree on the specific changes reflected in
        the restructuring.

   11. The government is also keen to strengthen the identity verification of program beneficiaries, and has increased the
   oversight of program implementation. A key recommendation of the multi-ministerial Review Panel on announcing the
   resumption of the cash transfer program in March 2024, is that cash transfers will go only to beneficiaries with a validated
   biometric NIN. The project will need to support collaboration between the Federal Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Poverty
   Alleviation (FMHAPA) and the National Identity Management Commission (NIMC) to ensure household members in the NSR have
   validated NINs. In addition, the Panel, under the chairmanship of the Honorable Minister of Finance and Coordinating Minister
   of the Economy, continues to provide oversight to the implementation of the cash transfer program.

   12.The initial implementation delays have also affected key system strengthening activities. The 22-month delay between
   approval and start of project activities has naturally delayed implementation of planned activities such as the rollout of the shock
   responsive and the regular cash transfers and activities to strengthen the delivery systems, the enhancements to the social
   registry and the upgrades to program management information systems. While implementation of these activities is now
   accelerating, completing these activities to achieve the PDO will require additional time. The cash transfers, for instance, will
   roll-out in monthly batches and requires time to reach sufficient beneficiary coverage.

   13. The restructuring will fully align the project parameters with the government’s program, accommodate the recent
   inclusion of the NIN, changes in the oversight function, and provide more time for the project implementation and system
   strengthening. In particular, the restructuring will: (i) update the benefit size and duration of support under the project to match
   the government’s program; (ii) update the results framework to accommodate for the change in benefit size and duration as
   well as the change in effective exchange rate since project approval; (iii) update the activities supported under component 3 in
   line with the recommendations of the Review Panel; (iv) update institutional arrangements to reflect the role of the Review
   Panel in project supervision and oversight; and (v) extend the project closing date to allow for the project activities to achieve
   their intended impacts.


II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

  14. Extension of the Closing date. The closing date of the NASSP-SU project will be extended from June 30, 2024 to December
  31, 2025, to compensate for initial delays. This will allow the shock-responsive cash transfers to reach sufficient coverage, and
  provide sufficient time for the completion of system strengthening activities including the registration and validation of NIN
  among program beneficiaries.

  15. Benefit size and duration. The ESR-CT will provide total benefit amounts of seventy-five thousand Naira (N75,000) to each
  beneficiary households, spread across three months. To be in line with the government’s announced program, and in response
  to increased inflation in the recent years, the benefit size for cash transfers will be increased from thirty thousand Naira (N30,000)
  per beneficiary household, spread over six months.

  16. Beneficiary targeting and selection. All project beneficiaries will be selected from the existing Social Registers, which includes
  the NSR and the Rapid Response Register (RRR). Existing households within the social registers, those from poorer wards, selected
  from the granular poverty map, will be prioritized. An updated proxy welfare score will be used to select the poor and vulnerable
  amongst those already in the Social Registers. Furthermore, the NIN will be integrated into the Social Registers as the NIN is
  required for beneficiary selection and account and wallet ownership.

                  17. The National Steering Committee shall be chaired by the Minister of Finance. Following the directive of the President of the

                  provided oversight to the implementation of the cash transfer program. Given the convening authority of the Honorable Minster
                  of Finance, the National Steering Committee will now be chaired by the Minister of Finance.

                  18. Results Framework. The restructuring updates the project results framework to reflect changes made to the project’s closing
                  date, project components and activities.

                  19. Other project design parameters remain unchanged and as described in the NASSP-SU project appraisal document.
