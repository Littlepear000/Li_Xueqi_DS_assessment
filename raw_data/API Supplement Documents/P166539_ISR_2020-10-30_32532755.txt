 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Effectiveness achieved: the project effectiveness date was extended to August 31, 2020, and the project was declared effective by this new
  deadline. The extension allowed the Ministry of Finance (MOF) to meet the remaining conditions that were still outstanding at the last ISR report, i.e.
  the establishment of the Project Coordination Unit (PCU) in accordance to agreed requirements with the World Bank and the approval of the Project
  Implementation Manual (PIM). The project was restructured prior to effectiveness to allow for key PCU staff to be either competitively recruited or
  assigned, allowing for the assignment of two accountants from the Controller and Accountant General’s Department to the PCU to fill the financial
  management specialist function. The restructuring also revised the requirement of having technical specialists in place before effectiveness, to allow
  for more time to recruit qualified candidates (specifically for Components 1 and 2), within three months of effectiveness.

  Project Coordination Unit (PCU): MOF has established the PCU, which is now almost fully staffed. Presently, the staffing of the PCU includes the
  Project Coordinator, Project Administrator, Procurement Specialist, Financial Management Specialist, Safeguards Specialist, and Component 3

  Technical specialist. Contracts have been signed with all the recruited and seconded specialists before the end of August 2020. Office space
  located near MOF and most Technical Implementation Agencies (TIAs) has been renovated to host the PCU; this should facilitate regular exchange
  between the PCU and the TIA focal persons. Also, procurement of essential goods and office supplies necessary for the running of the office has
  been completed. The PCU staff have started working in their offices and is now leading the day-to-day management of the project. More
  proactively, the PCU has taken the lead on organizing important workshops that brought together all the TIAs. These include a three-day offsite
  workshop for the PCU and TIA focal persons to unpack the PIM that would ensure a smooth implementation of activities and another three-day
  Safeguards training for the PCU and TIA focal persons.

  Dated covenants: With the project now effective, the focus has shifted towards meeting all the dated covenants. Two of the dated covenants were
  due one month after effectiveness and have already been met, and these are establishing the Project Oversight Committee (POC) and
  the Technical Committee. Four dated covenants are due within three months of effectiveness and are yet to be met. These include recruiting
  technical (i.e. Components 1 and 2) specialists, recruiting a Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) specialist, recruiting one or more organizations as
  “Independent Verification Agents”, and recruiting an independent external auditor. On recruiting an independent external auditor, country systems
  will be used, i.e. MOF will request from the Auditor General’s Department to second an auditor to the PCU.


  Project Workplan: the PCU and all the TIAs have submitted their initial workplans which have gone through several iterations. The World Bank has
  provided a few final comments which, once addressed, will enable the PCU to finalize the work plan and upload relevant activities into the
  procurement plan. MOF projects US$17m in disbursement during FY21, mainly driven by the two funds to be set up within the Venture Capital Trust
  Fund (VCTF) and for which the feasibility studies are underway. Other key consultancies for which the Terms of Reference have already been
  developed and reviewed will be launched soon, including the corporate strategies for both the Ghana Investment Promotion Center and Ghana
  Standards Authority, as well as the national Special Economic Zone policy and benchmarking and the launch of the Small and Medium Enterprise
  (SME) grants window to be implemented by the National Board for Small-Scale Industries (NBSSI).

  Implementation of project activities: a few critical activities have taken off, and these include consultancies for NBSSI and VCTF. Consultancy
  services for the revision of the strategic plan for NBSSI is underway. Similarly, VCTF also has two consultancies ongoing which include (i) an
  institutional reform roadmap and implementation support for the VCTF and (ii) the feasibility study for the establishment of two new funds (startup
  catalyst and strategic industries funds).

  Business Pulse Survey: the World Bank supported the implementation of the Ghana COVID-19 Business Tracker (also known as BPS/business
  pulse survey), results from which were released on August 3, 2020. The survey showed that even though the partial lockdown had been lifted at the
  time of the survey, firms were still severely affected, with 16.1 percent of firms still being closed, mostly in the education (65%), transport (34%),
  and accommodation and food (24%) sectors. Ninety one percent of firms that remained open reported that their sales had decreased in April 2020
  compared to the same period last year, with the average decrease in sales being about 61%. Firms also reported difficulties in accessing finance or
  sourcing inputs; highlighting how the pandemic affects firms in different ways. The results of the BPS would be useful information guiding the
  implementation of the first round of SME grants under the project to support firms affected by COVID19.
