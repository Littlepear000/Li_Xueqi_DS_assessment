 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The project performance towards the achievement of the Development Objective (DO) is progressing well. The project supported the government
  to control the outbreak of locust and restore affected land to production with 305 ha of locust affected pasture/rangeland restored to productivity
  against a target of 185 ha at the end of the project; 29,300 ha of locust-affected land area were restored to productivity against a target of 35,000
  ha; and 26,000 ha of locust-affected land were surveyed against the target of 45,000 ha at the end of the project. The project supported 45,929
  households with social safety nets against a target of 48,300 to restore their livelihood. An additional 162,197 locust affected farmers (against a
  target of 135,000) renewed with agricultural activities with the project support. More than 71 percent of those who benefited from social safety net

  and those who renewed with agriculture activities were female-headed households. Activities are being implemented across all components and
  subcomponents. The project has disbursed US$39.84 million (80 percent) of the total funding.
  Component 1 on desert locust control and surveillance supported the capacity of MAFS to strengthen pest control and surveillance with 10 staff of
  Agriculture and Food Security Directorate of Plant Protection and 14 staff from the national task force trained as trainers on pest surveillance. An
  additional 200 officials from state, county and community representatives were trained in pest surveillance. Five vehicles mounted with motorized
  sprayers were prepositioned in the frontline counties in addition to the provision of assorted surveillance and monitoring equipment to immediately
  respond in case of an outbreak. Three out of four pesticide facility storages were built according to appropriate international safety standards. The
  construction of the fourth facility will start soon.
  Component 2 on livelihood protection and rehabilitation supported investment to farm and livestock households to return to production for improved
  food security as well as restore the biodiversity of the affected areas with 23,799 (of whom 19,098 were female headed HH) households (HH) out of
  27,176 targeted HH provided with direct income support. An additional, 22,130 HH (of whom 13,762 were female head HH) out of 22,300 targeted
  HH earned income through Labor Intensive Public Works (LIPW). Furthermore, 120,109 farmers (of whom 84,718 were females) out of 50,000
  targeted farmers received input packets to grow and produce food. Moreover, the project supported agro-forestry for smallholder farming
  households with the establishment of 9 communities to do nurseries for various crops and trees.
  Component 3 on strengthening national preparedness systems supported the government to develop a locust control plan and established 9 desert
  locust control committees in each participating county. Capacity in entomology applied research was built for of 16 MAFS staff at the International
  Center of Insect Physiology and Ecology (ICIPE). An additional 45 MAFS staff were trained on pest surveillance, biocontrol, pest risk analysis, pest
  modelling and policy formulation.

 Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA) Status

 MPA Table Body
 For the component on IGAD financing. the project activities are mainly focused on providing technical assistance and capacity building, the
 environmental and social (E&S) risk rating for this operation is, therefore, low. IGAD is collaboration with its member states is working towards
 streamlining locust monitoring, control, early warning, and preparedness policies and systems and strengthening requisite capacity in the Horn of
 Africa to foster responsible pest management practices. It included development of policies and regulatory frameworks for transboundary pest
 management, and adoption of biopesticide policies and guidelines. It was agreed to expand collaboration and training with member states on areas of
 expired pesticide management and disposal and to engage the Bank' E&S team in the forthcoming training programs for plant protection officers,
 focusing on the Proper Use of Biopesticides and Insect Growth Regulators.
