 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Country Context
       The Republic of Yemen situation has not changed in any significant measurable manner since Board Approval
       in June 2022. Yemen continues to experience a severe humanitarian crisis. The country has been in a complex civil
       war since 2014, struggling due to a fall in global prices, weak public infrastructure, a limited ability to cope with
       extreme climate events, and the strains of COVID-19. Yemen also battles mass outbreaks of preventable diseases
       such as cholera, diphtheria, measles, and dengue fever. Yemen’s death rate is currently five times the global
       average, and the country has been designated a fragility, conflict, and violence (FCV) country by the World
       Bank. The PDO, to improve access to electricity in rural and peri-urban areas within Yemen and plan for the
       restoration of the Yemen power sector, remains highly relevant. The two primary groups of beneficiaries continue
       to be: (A) rural and peri-urban populations that are the poorest and most vulnerable groups in Yemen; and (B)
       critical public service providers (health facilities, schools, rural WUAs, and COVID-19 isolation and vaccine cold
       chain units).

B. Project Status

        The Financial Agreement was executed on 14 July 2022 and the Project became effective as of 7 October 2022.
        All effectiveness conditions for Environmental and Social Framework (ESF) have been met. Under subcomponent
        1.1, UNOPS is preparing the first call for Microfinance Institution (MFI) proposals and expects to award contracts
        by end of February 2023. Under subcomponent 1.2, the selection criteria for critical services facilities have been
        agreed with the UNOPS, Health, Water, and Education teams. The Environmental and Social Management Plan
        (ESMP) for the first batch of 89 critical service facilities (41 schools and 48 healthcare facilities) is currently under
        review by MNA ESF Operations team. The tendering processes for these facilities are expected to be completed
        by end of Q1 2023. UNOPS is preparing the tendering process for the project’s Third-Party Monitory Agency
        (TPMA), with a contract expected to be awarded not later than end-March 2023.

        As of January 23, 2023, the project has disbursed US$26.57 million (28% of the total project financing).

C. Rationale for Restructuring

        The RY EEAP II Financing Agreement contains a dated covenant that states UNOPS shall not later than three (3) months
        after the Effective Date contract a Third-Party Monitoring Agent(s) (TPM) - i.e., by 7 January 2023. This did not occur.


        UNOPS cited the reason for delay as mainly being the delay in the review of the terms of reference from both UNOPS
        and the Bank. The Bank’s review process has been completed, with comments shared back to UNOPS on 3 January
        2023. UNOPS has not yet finalized the TOR, nor did it issue a request for TPM Agent proposals by the required
        deadline.

        UNOPS’ delay in engaging the TPM agent and proposed extension of the dated covenant deadline is not anticipated
        to negatively impact the Project’s reporting processes as no field activities launched under RY EEAP II will take place

      until end-March 2023, at the earliest. TPM reporting would commence 3 months after the launch of field activities,
      i.e., July 2023, and continue quarterly thereafter.

II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

     The proposed restructuring is to allow for an extension of the deadline for one of the dated covenants in the RY
     EEAP II Financing Agreement. UNOPS requested a 3-month extension for completion of the TPM agency contract
     under Section I.E.,1 of Schedule 2 to the Financial Agreement (FA), which is to be met no later than three months
     after the Effectiveness Date - i.e., 7 January 2023 (UNOPS letter dated 5 January 2023, copy attached). If granted,
     the new deadline for satisfaction of the dated covenant would be 7 April 2023.

     The proposed provision in the FA would now read as follows: UNOPS shall not later than six (6) months after the
     Effective Date contract a Third-Party Monitoring Agent(s) (TPM).
