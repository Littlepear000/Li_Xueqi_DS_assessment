 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  This ISR is based on (i) the Mid-Term Review Mission for the LCT project (US$ 13 million) conducted 1-16 February 2021; (ii) the Appraisal mission
  for the COVID-19 Additional Financing (AF) to the LCT project (US$ 6.5 million) conducted 27-31 May 2021; as well as follow-up virtual meetings
  the Task Team conducts with the Project's National Implementation Unit (NIU).
        1. Covid-19 continues to constrain the project implementation while imposing tremendous stress on the private sector. Accelerating
            regulatory reforms and policy changes on the ground in business and trade regulations is even more urgent now to help viable firms
            survive and help them maintain and create jobs amid COVID-19. The project includes support for streamlining regulations like registration
            of a new business and a matching grant facility and is an important part of the government response to the pandemic.
        1. A scale-up Additional Financing became effective on 05 October 2021. The signed amount of the Multi-Donor Trust Fund (MDTF)
            Grant is US$ 2.72 million. The MDTF Grant is co-funded by DFAT (Australia), USAID and IrishAid. The total project financing is now US$
            15.72 million.

      2.      Despite COVID-19 the project remains on track to achieve its development objectives. AF activities are now included in the annual
              Work Plan and Budget. Policy reforms are slower in Pillar A and B while Pillar C has made some good progress in assisting firm
              competitiveness through the matching grants under the second Business Assistance Facility (BAF-2).
      3.      Pillar A (Improving the Business Environment) policy reform in improving business registration is progressing, the average time to
              obtain an Enterprise Registration Certificate nationwide was 2.18 days in early 2021. On operating licenses, two task forces (one within
              MOIC and one inter-ministerial) were set up. Four business licenses in MOIC were reviewed and the "to-be" process reform maps were
              produced.
      4.      Pillar B (Facilitating Trade and Connecting to Markets). Implementation of the TFA Challenge Facility (CF) Proposals needs to be
              accelerated. Progress was made in supporting the National Trade Facilitation Committee (NTFC) in leading the dialogue on trade
              facilitation issues and reform agenda. A Time Release Study (TRS) in 2020 was conducted and published in July 2021. The TRS finds the
              average time to clear imports from document submission to cargo release at border checkpoints dropped to 5 hours and 12 minutes in
              2020 from 8 hours and 10 minutes in 2019.
      5.      Under pillar C (Improving Firm-level Competitiveness) BAF-2 continues to help private enterprises access business development
              services including product designs, marketing, software development and training to improve management and innovation capacity. By
              October 2021, 213 matching grants had been approved to firms in Laos (98 since January 2021 alone) providing USD1.25 million
              excluding taxes in support to Lao businesses. Eight five percent of grants are to SMEs and 62 percent to women-led businesses.
      6.      Pillar D (Supporting Better and More Inclusive Policies). AF activities are being included in the annual Work Plan and Budget. The
              new safeguards instruments added with the AF and applicable to all project activities are being implemented. The 13th Lao Business
              Forum was organized on July 28, 2021.
