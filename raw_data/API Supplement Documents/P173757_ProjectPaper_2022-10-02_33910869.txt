The Additional Financing (MPA AF-V) to the COVID-19 Strategic Preparedness and Response Program utilizing
the Multiphase Programmatic Approach (“Global COVID-19 MPA”) was approved by the Board on October
13, 2021. The AF-V was approved with an envelope of US$12 billion ($6 billion from IDA and $6 billion from
IBRD) in financing. On June 30, 2021, President Malpass announced the expansion of financing available
for COVID-19 vaccine financing to $20 billion over the next 18 months, adding $8 billion to the previously
announced $12 billion. This financing is critical to expand affordable and equitable financing for vaccine
purchase and deployment. The AF-V is expected to enable vaccination for up to 750 million people, with
potential surge capacity for an additional 250 million people in the poorest countries (depending on the
delivered price of approved vaccines) while scaling support to strengthen immunization delivery, with design
flexibility at the country level. The AF-V is a scale-up of planned vaccination activities anticipated and
supported under the Global COVID-19 MPA and a key contribution to the WBG’s overall COVID-19 response.

 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

1.     This Project Paper seeks approval of the Country Director to restructure the Bangladesh COVID-19 Emergency
Response and Pandemic Preparedness Project (P173757) to document the following changes:
             a. Add the Public Works Department (PWD) of the Ministry of Works as a new implementing agency for the
                project in addition to the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW);
             b. Create a new disbursement category for civil works with a total allocation of BDT 6390.3 million
                (equivalent to US$67.3 million);
             c. Reallocate US$67.3 million from disbursement category 1 to the newly created category, 50:50 from IDA
                6605-BD and AIIB COFN-C1850;
             d. Update accordingly the financial management and disbursement arrangements of the project.

2.      This restructuring responds to a request made by the Government of Bangladesh (GoB) through a letter
September 14, 2022, from the Economic Relations Division of the Ministry of Finance. The proposed changes were
discussed during the mid-term review (MTR) of the project held in June 2022 and documented in the aide memoire.

3.      Project background: The Bangladesh COVID-19 Emergency Response and Pandemic Preparedness Project
(P173757), in an amount of XDR 72.9 million (US$100 million equivalent) IDA credit (IDA 6605-BD) was approved in April
2020 under the COVID-19 Strategic Preparedness and Response Program (SPRP) using the Multiphase Programmatic
Approach (MPA). In March 2021, an additional financing (AF) of US$500 million (IDA 6846-BD) was approved by the Board
to enable affordable and equitable access to COVID-19 vaccines and help ensure effective vaccine deployment in
Bangladesh through vaccination system strengthening, and to further strengthen preparedness and response activities
under the parent project. Along with processing the AF, the project was restructured to incorporate US$100 million co-
financing from the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB). The financing percentage of the original IDA credit (IDA
6605-BD) and the AIIB loan (COFN-C1850) is set at 50:50. The AIIB loan is financing all project components as IDA except
for sub-component 1.5 which includes COVID-19 vaccination related activities.

4.       Project implementation status: The MTR held in June 2022 documented impressive progress made with the roll-
out of COVID-19 vaccination as well as strengthening of health systems for future pandemic preparedness. The project
has helped vaccinate 46.5% of the population with 68 million doses of vaccines and 110 million pieces of syringes. In
addition, the project was instrumental in mobilizing critical equipment and supplies to deal with the emergency response
to detect and treat COVID-19 cases, which included ventilators, liquid medical oxygen systems, oxygen concentrators,
hospital beds, personal protective equipment, testing machines and kit, etc. Consequently, the disbursement rate of total
IDA credit is 96% (45% of the original credit 6605-BD and 87% of the AF credit 6846-BD) and AIIB loan is 9%. During the
remaining period of the project, activities to further strengthen health systems will be implemented to help the country
be better prepared to deal with future pandemics. Some of these initiatives include setting-up of additional intensive care
units, establishment of additional modern microbiology laboratories, additional isolation units and setting-up of medical
gas production plants. These will ensure utilization of unspent IDA and AIIB funds.

5.      The government has disclosed the Environment Social Commitment Plan (ESCP), the Stakeholder Engagement
Plan (SEP), the Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMF), and the Human and Occupational Resource
Management Procedures (HORMP) on the website of the Directorate General of Health Services. In addition, environment
and social consultants are onboard and working at the project implementation unit (PIU). Procurement of goods and
services is underway. Renovation of selected health facilities is underway for intensive care units, isolation units, and

 II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

6.      The following changes are being proposed:
             a. Adding PWD as a new implementing agency for the project in addition to the MoHFW: under the project,
                PWD is indicated as a procuring entity responsible for undertaking civil works under components 1 and
                2 of the project. With this project restructuring, PWD will be included as implementing agency to enable
                them to process payments to the contractors undertaking civil works across the country and to continue
                to function as a procuring entity to procure and supervise works related contracts.

             b. Creating a new disbursement category for civil works with a total allocation of BDT 6390.3 million
                (equivalent to US$67.3 million): a new disbursement category will be added to pay for expenditures
                incurred by PWD relating to civil works. So far there haven’t been any expenses reported under Works
                in the existing category (Category 1). Hence Works will be removed as an expense item from Category 1.
                All future expenses relating to Civil works would be charged under the newly created category 3.

             c. Reallocating funds from disbursement category 1 to the newly created disbursement category for civil
                works to be executed by PWD: as the disbursement ratio is 50:50 between IDA 6605-BD and AIIB COFN-
                C1850, the total allocation for civil works of BDT 6390.3 million (equivalent to US$67.3 million) will be as
                follows:
                      i. IDA credit 6605-BD: XDR25.7 million will be allocated to the new disbursement category for PWD
                         and allocation for disbursement category 1 will be revised to XDR47.2 million.
                     ii. AIIB loan COFN-C1850: US$33.6 million will be allocated to the new disbursement category for
                         PWD and allocation for disbursement category 1 will be revised to XDR47.2 million.

             d. Updating the financial management and disbursement arrangements of the project: A financial
                management assessment has been carried out for the PWD, which will be the new implementing agency.
                A dedicated financial management staff will be attached to PWD, either from the market (if adequate
                provisions are made in the government’s budget document) or engaging a deputed accountant from the
                government. The Additional Chief Engineer (ACE) and the Superintendent Engineer (SE) of the PWD
                Health Wing will be authorized signatories, with alternative arrangement, for requesting disbursement
                from the World Bank. A Designated Account for the project will be opened to receive IDA and AIIB
                financing. As per the standard requirement of PWD, the Executive Engineer (or a representative) and
                the ACE/SE of PWD Health Wing will be joint signatories to operate the Designated Account. Funds will
                flow to the cost centers across the district offices to implement the works related activities. Fund flow to
                the cost centers will be based on physical and financial progress reports. Individual operating accounts
                will be opened at each cost center where funds will flow from the designated account. Account officers
                of the cost centers will send monthly financial report to the PWD reporting expenditures for the months
                and bank reconciliation statements. The Foreign Aided Project Audit Directorate (FAPAD) will continue
                to be the independent auditor for the project. All the expenses till the date of counter signature of the
                amendment will be reported to the World Bank through statement of expenditures (SOE) and withdrawal
                applications by MoHFW. After the counter signature of the amendment MOHFW will submit withdrawal
                applications for expenses relating to category 1 and Health Wing of PWD would submit withdrawal

7.        Environmental and social (ES) arrangements: The overall responsibility for the required ES due diligence,
assessment and management in accordance with the ESF documents of the project remains with the existing PIU of the
MoHFW. PWD and the existing PIU will work in close coordination to ensure the ES screening is done before any civil work
is initiated and the requirements of ESF are adhered to. PWD will assign ES focal persons and assist and cooperate with ES
specialists of the existing PIU in preparing any Environmental Screening/Assessment along with Environmental and Social
Management Plans (ESMPs) required under the ESMF to address risks and impacts of the civil works of PWD (including
occupational health and safety issues). PWD will ensure incorporation of specific ESMPs and bill of quantities in the
respective bid document of the civil work, including provisions of safety measures, reporting, incidence response and
signing of code of conduct. The implementation of any required ESMPs during construction work will be the responsibility
of PWD’s contractor(s) with monitoring and guidance from relevant staff of PWD and the ES specialists of the existing PIU.
The PIU and PWD will ensure adequate training is provided to the field staff and contractors, since contractors play an
important role in executing the ESMP during construction.
