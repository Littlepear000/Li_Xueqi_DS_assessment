4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  Component 1:
  Economic Census (REGED): This activity is currently under implementation. The enumeration phase was completed in
  December 2025 and identified about 29,000 economic units both in the formal and informal sector. For enterprises
  operating in the free zone, INSTAD relies on administrative data, complemented by field operations. The enumeration
  questionnaire included additional variables, notably the formal/informal status of economic units, turnover, and number
  of employees, in order to strengthen the information base on the structure of the economy and employment. A technical
  workshop is planned to support the technical team in finalizing this first phase of the activity. REGED data collection is
  expected to start in September 2026, prior to the start of data processing, analysis, and dissemination of results.
  National Strategy for the Development of Statistics (SNDS, 2025–2029): The third National Strategy for the Development
  of Statistics has been prepared, finalized, and made available through publication on the INSTAD website
  (https://instad.dj/snds), providing a clear strategic framework for the development of the national statistical system. The
  SNDS highlights key institutional challenges, particularly related to human resources and working conditions, and defines
  strategic orientations aimed at strengthening career management and the attractiveness of statistical professions.
  Regular production of economic statistics is now operational, and tangible results have been produced and disseminated
  in the area of demographic and social statistics, notably through the Population Census (RGPH3), Household Budget
  Survey (EDAM5), and the labor force survey (EDST), contributing to improved data availability for the design, monitoring,
  and evaluation of public policies.
  Population and Housing Census (RGPH3): This activity is in the finalization stage. All planned thematic analyses have
  been published on the INSTAD website (https://instad.dj/rgph), with the exception of regionally disaggregated analyses
  and two additional thematic reports currently under validation. Published analyses cover key areas including population
  structure and dynamics, fertility and mortality, migration, education, employment, and housing characteristics. RGPH3
  data serve as the reference sampling frame for the main nationally representative household surveys implemented by
  INSTAD.
  Household Budget Survey (EDAM5): The activity is almost completed pending the publication of the results and micro-
  data sharing. The results of EDAM5, including new poverty and inequality estimates, were used to inform the preparation
  of the National Development Plan 2025–2030. A draft report was shared with sector ministries for feedback. Following
  incorporation of comments, the final report and associated datasets are expected to be published. A dissemination
  workshop is also planned to present the main findings of EDAM5 to government institutions, the World Bank, and other
  development partners.
  Labor Force Survey (EDST): Data collection covered the entire year 2025, with all four quarters completed between
  January and December 2025. The International Labour Organization (ILO) provided continuous technical support to
  INSTAD throughout survey preparation, data collection, processing, and validation, ensuring alignment with international
  standards. The report and results for the first quarter have been published on the INSTAD website. In addition, the World
  Bank collaborated closely with INSTAD on a joint labor market analytical note, based on data from the first two quarters,
  which has been incorporated into the forthcoming Djibouti Economic Monitor.

INSTAD Building. Progress is on track. The 2026 Finance Law includes a dedicated allocation of DJF 178 million
(USD 1 million), meeting IDA amendment conditions. Procurement steps have advanced, and construction is
scheduled through November 2027. To safeguard disbursements, works should be completed and formally
received by October 30, 2027.



Component 2:

Macroeconomic and Statistical Reforms. National accounts (2013–2023) have been finalized, 2024 provisional
accounts completed, and quarterly accounts are expected from June 2026. All 24 planned macro-modeling
missions have been delivered, and the upgraded quasi-macro model is now underpinning the 2025–2030 NDP
framework.

Debt Management. SYGADE 6 has been strengthened, but delays persist in operationalizing the DSA working
group. Adoption of the Medium-Term Debt Strategy (MTDS) by April 30, 2026 remains critical under SDFP
commitments.

Project Monitoring and Budget Reform. The GEMS tool has geo-referenced 575 infrastructure assets, improving
oversight. The MTEF has been successfully piloted in Health and Education for the 2026 budget cycle.
