 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  This ISR is based on the team’s ongoing discussions and implementation support, including the findings and agreed next steps from the
  latest mission (from March 6 to 27, 2024). The objectives of the mission were to assess progress towards achieving the Project Development
  objective (PDO) and Intermediate Result (IR) indicators in the parent project states, and to initiate implementation preparation in the eleven
  additional financing (AF) states. The AF was approved in September 2023, and is expected to be effective by the end of May 2024.
  The achievement of the PDO indicators is on track. The World Bank and Government teams are working to address several areas where
  immediate action is needed across each of the sub-components. Overall implementation progress remains moderately satisfactory, and funds
  disbursement to NPCU and SPIU accounts is currently $235 million (about 50% of the parent project allocation of $500 million). Although parent
  project effectiveness was delayed and only became effective on April 30, 2021, there has been notable achievement both on the PDO and the IR
  indicators.

  Progress towards the achievement of the PDO is being measured by 5 PDO indicators and 16 IR indicators. On PDO indicators, three of the
  five Year 3 targets defined in the results framework have been surpassed (girls currently enrolled in JSS and SSS in participating states, girls’
  completion rate in participating states, and girls trained who demonstrate digital literacy), and the remaining two objectives are at or above 90%
  achievement (students benefiting from direct interventions to enhance learning and girls completing life skills program). Based on project
  administrative data provided by the SPIUs and NPCU the project has reached 3,483,721 beneficiaries, of which 1,741,416 are girls.
  Critical implementation milestones achieved include: 20 schools have been constructed and 258 schools are currently under construction in
  four states, these schools will add 2,112 classrooms in the states. All phase 1 construction activities across the four states is anticipated to be
  completed in Q3 of 2024. 464,068 beneficiaries have received Financial Incentives (FI) to either transition to JSS, continue their education in JS2 or
  JS3, transition to SS1 or continue their education in SS2 or SS3 under sub-component 2.3. Full implementation of the life skills sessions across all
  parent project states, with 163,474 current beneficiaries under sub-component 2.2a and at least one cohort of girls having already completed the 9-
  month curriculum in Kano, Kaduna, and Katsina. Enrollment of new cohorts has been completed in these three states, and life skills activities are
  being extended to additional schools.
  Despite the significant progress made, there are key implementation challenges as described below: Overall project management: The
  slow implementation of critical project activities remains an obstacle for some states. The inefficient performance of lagging states could undermine
  project progress. These states have shown low capacity in implementing critical activities such as fiduciary, monitoring and evaluation, reporting,
  and project management. 2) Security challenges remain pervasive across some states, making it difficult to implement and supervise
  implementation of project activities in affected areas. 3) Bureaucratic processes and changes in SPIU staff continue to cause delays in the
  implementation of project activities in some of the project states.
  Moving forward: The World Bank team will continue to liaise with State Governors and Education Commissioners to minimize turnover at the SPIU
  level and ensure that qualified staff are recruited to support Project activities. States will need to introduce more enhanced security measures for
  schools. All SPIUs are now required to recruit a security consultant and appoint a security government focal person in the states The NPCU will
  also need to increase targeted support to participating states through technical assistance, training and refresher courses, and standardized
  reporting frameworks. The World Bank is also considering Hands-On Expanded Implementation Support (HEIS) for states in view of low capacity
  and high volume of future procurements
