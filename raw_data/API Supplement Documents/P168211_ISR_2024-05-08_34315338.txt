 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Despite good progress in the Program over the reporting period, the power sector continues to faces substantial financial performance issues. Key
  areas of progress include: (i) tariffs for 2024 increased by 16 percent for all customers (effective since January 2024); (ii) adequate electricity
  supplied by Sangtuda-1 power plant to BT; (iii) daily collection and distribution of the sector's revenue among the three entities maintained through
  the escrow account since its establishment in 2022; (iv) disclosure of 2022 audited financial statements and key financial, commercial and
  operational data for all three companies for 2022-23; (v) disbursement ratio of the parent project increased from 41% to 47% by December 2023.
  However, the sector continues to experience substantial financial performance issues with significant cash deficits due to below-cost recovery
  tariffs, continuous increase of the BT liabilities (debt and payables to IPPs), high levels of aggregate technical, commercial and collection (ATC&C)
  losses, high levels of taxation in the power sector including taxation of ATC&C losses, and burdensome penalties imposed on BT by the MOF due
  to missed debt payments. Therefore, despite significant progress achieved, the sector cash deficit as per the 2022 audited financial statements was
  estimated to over US$1.8 billion (approximately TJS 18.5 billion) in 2022.
