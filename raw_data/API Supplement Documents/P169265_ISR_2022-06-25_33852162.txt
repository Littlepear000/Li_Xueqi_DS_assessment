  Component 1: Regional Component: Harmonization, Data Collection, Quality Improvement, Dissemination, and Use of Core Social and Economic
  Statistics.
  1. African Union (AU) - STATAFRIC:
  A face-to-face implementation support mission (ISM) was conducted in Addis Ababa from March 14-18, 2022.
  Project management: The staffing of the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) -at both the AU HQ and Tunis STATAFRIC is almost complete. No
  objection was provided to the AU for the hiring of a Project Manager and Administrative Assistant who are expected to be on board by July
  2022. Previously agreed actions are largely achieved and implementation of project activities seems to be on track after some delays. The
  Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM), project operation manual, and the revised Annual Work Plan and Budget (AWPB) until December 31, 2022,
  were all approved by the World Bank. While the team acknowledged the progress made in opening Bank accounts, recruiting PIU staff, installing
  the SAP software, and submitting budget proposals, the team rated both FM and Procurement as Moderately Satisfactory for the delays in initiating
  key activities which led to slow disbursement.
  Technical aspects: The team found out that STATAFRIC organized four meetings since November 2021 as per the agreement in the previous
  mission. These included: (1) a hybrid meeting for the Specialized Technical Group (STG) of SHaSA 2 on National Accounts and Price Statistics
  (African Group on National Account (AGNA)); (2) a hybrid meeting for the Specialized Technical Group on External Sector (STG-ES); (3) a hybrid
  meeting with the executive committee of SHaSA 2 to assess the progress made by the African Statistics Systems (AfSS) on the implementation of
  SHaSA 2; and (4) the 15th AU Committee of Director Generals (CoDG) meeting of NSOs. The HISWA project is now supporting the STATAFRIC in
  recruiting several consultants to (1) develop the code of conduct on security and confidentiality; (2) develop a statistics database and dashboard on
  SHaSA 2 indicators; (3) update the AFRICA TRADE (continental database on trade statistics) and produce the 2021 edition of Africa Trade
  yearbook; (4) prepare the SHaSA 2 Consolidated Activity Report 2021; and (5) support African Countries in the process of revising the 2008 System
  of National Account (2008 SNA).
  Recommendations: the team recommends: i) recruitment of a Project Manager, Administrative Assistant as soon as possible; and ii) expediting
  the recruitment of an external auditor to enable submission of the Audit Report by June 30, 2022.
  2. Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS):
  A face-to-face implementation support mission (ISM) was conducted in Abuja from March 28 to April 1st, 2022.
  Project Management: The mission noted that the overall implementation of activities has been slow. Out of the actions agreed on the last mission
  in July 2021; only two main activities have been completed, few are in progress while others are delayed. Key staff hiring ((Project Manager,
  Finance Specialist, and Procurement Specialist), is almost completed with ECOWAS awaiting internal approval prior to signing the contracts. The
  incumbents are expected to start by early June 2022. ECOWAS made effort to produce timely IFR. However, the training on Client-Connection has
  not been provided yet and ECOWAS will remain in contact with the World Bank focal point to have this training done. In disbursement, the team
  was advised to submit partial SOEs in order to proceed with new disbursements. ECOWAS has to accelerate the audit firm recruitment to enable
  audit submission by the end of June 2022 and fully comply with legal covenants. The ECOWAS team attended a training organized for Togo PIU
  Procurement Specialists to help solve issues on access to STEP. In addition, the Bank mission team identified a champion from another project to
  assist the ECOWAS team. The GRM is not yet finalized. The mission shared example templates from AU and Ghana with the ECOWAS team. It
  was agreed to have this document before June 30th.
  Technical Aspects: The diagnostic of the national mechanisms and data processing tools for the production of national accounts (Annual &
  Quarterly) and assessment of the compilation of Consumer Price Index (CPI) in the ECOWAS member states on the regional harmonization side
  has been initiated. The diagnostic missions were carried out in thirteen (13) ECOWAS Member States. The missions in Praia (Cabo Verde) and
  Dakar (Senegal) were not carried out due to logistical issues and are now scheduled to take place in early June 2022. The completion rate for this
  activity is approximately 86.7%. The deliverables obtained are (i) a report on Annual national accounts and Quarterly national accounts compilation
  status in the ECOWAS member states, specifically presenting the common & divergence points in the different methodologies used by member
  states in the compilation of those official statistics; ii) a report on CPI compilation status in the ECOWAS Member States, specifically presenting the
  common & divergence points in the different methodologies used by member states in the compilation of CPI, specifically the divergence between
  the West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU) States and others member States.
  Recommendations: The mission provided several recommendations to the ECOWAS project team. These include: i) holding regular
  implementation status meetings (2nd Tuesday and last Tuesday) of every month between the World Bank and ECOWAS, to better follow up on the
  issues and difficulties the project is facing at ECOWAS; ii) holding a meeting with all the project participating counties and STATAFRIC at the AU;
  iii) submission of HISWA annual work plan to be validated by the Steering Committee (Heads of NSO) before the end of each October so that it
  could be sent to the Bank for no objection by December 31st; and iv) finalizing the GRM, and recruitment of the Audit firm.
  3. Burkina Faso:
  This ISR is based on a virtual technical mission conducted from March 23-25, 2022.
  Project management: The recruitment of the project team is now completed and consists of a Project Coordinator (the Director-General of the
  National Statistics Office (NSO) (institut national de la statistique et la démographie, INSD), a Financial Management Specialist, a Procurement
  Specialist, a Statistics Specialist who works as Monitoring and Evaluation Expert, and an Administrative Assistant. These have terms of reference,
  experience, and qualifications satisfactory to the Bank. The recruitment of the External Auditor is ongoing and will be completed as soon as
  possible. Thus, the project complied with all the Legal Covenants. The 2022 Annual Work Plan and Budget were adopted by the Steering
  Committee and the Bank issued a no objection. The monitoring and evaluation of the project are rated satisfactory. The FM rating remains
  satisfactory as all the interim financial reports have been submitted and accepted by the Bank. But the financial risk remains substantial and the
  project team has proposed to hire an Assistant Accountant to support FM activities. The procurement management is rated moderately satisfactory
  and the risk is substantial as many activities are not fully filed in STEP.
  Technical aspects: The project has advanced key activities and milestones. Statistical reforms: The new statistical law was adopted in December
  2021 and its implementing regulations will be prepared in 2022. Population and agriculture census: Preliminary estimates of population, including in
  security-challenged areas, have been published and the updating of enumeration areas is underway. The data collection for the inventories of
  irrigated land was carried out in 2021 and the data processing is underway. Macroeconomic statistics: The technical documents of the Harmonized
  Index of Consumer Prices have been developed and the data collection team was trained. The project documents to rebase National Accounts are

  Product (GDP) is 2019. Business census: The project document and technical documents will be finalized in the next months. A World Bank expert

  visits to learn from countries that demonstrate relevant experiences on some of the key activities including enterprises census and electronic

  4. Cabo Verde:
  An ISM was conducted from May 4 to May 10, 2022.
  Project management: Except for the Project Manager who only works on the HISWA project, the Account, Procurement, M&E, and GRM
  specialists also manage the portfolio of other World Bank-funded projects. The Project Manager was on board since May 3rd. The updated annual
  work plan (AWP) of the project for 2022 was approved by the World Bank. Overall, the PIU is functioning well in implementing and monitoring
  project activities. TORs for implementing activities are well written and submitted without delay to the Bank for clearance. More importantly, the PIU
  regularly participates in field supervision missions. However, there is a need to improve the activities coordination by setting up a functional steering
  committee and ad-hoc technical committees. Regarding the diversity of implementation by the stakeholders, these committees should ensure the
  consistency of the annual working plan (AWP) by improving the programming, budgeting, and monitoring of the activities. The project's financial
  management performance is satisfactory. The project bookkeeping is up to date and transactions are adequately documented. The interim financial
  reports' quality is satisfactory. The internal control environment is also adequate; the external auditor has issued an unqualified opinion and the
  annual work program (AWP) has been submitted on time. However, the project disbursement rate of 5% after 20 months of implementation is
  relatively Low. The procurement plan has already been updated by the special project management unit (A Unidade de Gestão de Projetos
  Especiais, UGPE) and cleared by the World Bank and the clients were advised that future proposals for change to the Project Procurement
  Strategies for Development (PPSD) should be cleared by the Bank. The GRM is operational and regular awareness-raising events are organized
  for the beneficiaries.
  Technical aspects: Household survey: The preparation of the household survey is well advanced. However, the technical discussions between the
  Mission and the NSO reveal limitations for measuring poverty according to the best standards. In addition, aspects related to the impact of COVID-
  19 and the targeting of social safety net programs, among others, are not well covered in the questionnaire. The sampling plan results in a survey
  budget that exceeds the available resources. The Bank agreed to provide hands-on technical assistance to resolve the above issues. Fisheries and
  the sport censuses: the data collection at the household level is completed and the administrative data collection is ongoing. In relation to the
  administrative data system for the National Statistics System (NSS), the national institute of statistics (Instituto Nacional de Estatística, INE) has
  recently organized the official launch workshop with the participation of the NSS member. The workshop focused on the administrative data register
  and the quality data system, which is expected, to be operational with the upcoming IDRF survey and the existing data (fisheries and sports data).
  Regarding the master data activity, INE’s experts are developing the application and the work is well progressing. Discussions with the Department
  of Domestic Revenue Collection (DNRE) also highlighted significant progress in using the electronic platform with the issuing of over five million
  electronic invoices.
  Recommendation: The team reiterated the importance of previous mission recommendations related to the valorization of existing data. INE has
  started well by launching the data quality system activity. However, there is a need to do more by exploring the way to showcase the potential use
  that could be done on the data already collected by INGT in some islands. In project management, the mission recommends establishing a
  functional steering committee and ad-hoc technical committee to improve coordination; similarly, to improve the financial management system the
  team recommends requesting the auditor to elaborate a mapping risk of the activities and link the internal auditor function to the various institutions
  that oversee the projects. In procurement, the team advised setting up the MOU between INE and UGPE for the implementation of the fishery
  census and the IDRF; and finalization of the TOR for the recruitment of the firms and consultants.
  5. Cote d'Ivoire:
  An ISM was conducted from May 7 to 30, 2022
  Project management: The project team is now completed and consists of a Project Coordinator (the Deputy Director-General of the National
  Institute of Statistics (INS)), a Financial Management Specialist, a Procurement Specialist, an M&E Expert, and an Administrative Assistant. All
  these team members have terms of reference, experience, and qualifications satisfactory to the Bank. The mission considered that the
  procurement performance is Satisfactory and fiduciary risk in terms of procurement remains Moderate. However, the overall financial management
  performance of the project is rated moderately unsatisfactory due to the delay in setting up multi-site accounting software, and the hiring of an
  external auditor to audit the project's accounts. Financial management risks remain substantial, the legal covenants are partially met, the
  outstanding covenants which include the signing of a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between the Project Implementing Entity (PIE) and the
  Ministry of Finance, and the hiring of an administrative assistant, the hiring of an internal auditor, and the hiring of an external auditor are all
  ongoing. The team has also received the draft GRM of Togo as a reference to prepare one for Cote d’Ivoire.
  Technical aspects: The population census (cartography, enumeration, and post-census survey) is completed, and the team is cleaning the data;
  the preparation of the enterprise census and the labor force survey are progressing well. Regarding capacity building activities, the project is
  supporting the regional statistical school (Ecole Nationale Supérieure de Statistique et d’Economie Appliquée, ENSEA) to develop a specialized

  6. Ghana:
  An ISM was conducted from March 02 - 03, 2022.
  Project management: The updated annual work plan (AWP) for 2022 was approved by the World Bank. The plan was developed in collaboration
  with the implementing Ministries, Departments, and Agencies (MDAs) and was also discussed with the project’s technical advisory committee. The
  project also submitted all IFRs due for the year 2021. Overall, the performance of financial management and procurement is satisfactory.
  Technical aspects: 2021 population and housing census: Enumeration and post-enumeration surveys were successfully conducted. Based on the
  census, the Residential Proximity to Essential Service Facility report and volumes including the populations of regions and districts; age and sex
  profile; background characteristics; literacy and education; economic activity, and difficulty in performing activity were released end-2021. Volumes
  including information and communication technology; fertility and mortality; housing, characteristics; water and sanitation; and structures were
  released in February 2022 and additional statistical products including 23 thematic reports, 20 analytical reports, a census atlas, and policy briefs
  are planned to be released until 2024. Annual Household Income and Expenditure Survey (AHIES): The first data collection cycle was conducted
  and completed in March 2022. Compilation of Physical Asset Accounts for Forest and Other Wooded Land Using the System of Environmental-
  Economic Accounting on Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (SEEA AFF) The GSS is collaborating with different agencies to implement this activity
  and already prepared a concept note and a data needs assessment. Re-basing of the Producer Price Index (PPI) and the Index of Industrial
  Production (IIP): These started in the last quarter of 2020, and have now captured, edited, cleaned, and coded the data; the processing of data is in
  progress with technical support from an International Monetary Fund (IMF) team. Enhancing Consumer Price Index geographical coverage: Price
  data collectors received training and eight months of price data were collected from the six new regions in addition to the former ten regions.
  Computation results and publication of CPI are expected to be published by the second quarter of 2022.
  Other activities: To improve administrative data sources, the Ghana Statistical Services (GSS) is working with the Ministry of education, the Ghana
  Police Service, Ministry of Health, Ministry of Gender Children and Social Protection, Ghana Prison Service, Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture,
  Ministry of Employment and Labor Relations, and Ghana Health Service. The project is also supporting the improvement of data accessibility and
  dissemination. For example, the 2021 Population and Housing Census reports are disseminated on the website and other platforms, and, the
  Ministry of employment and labor relations published its 2020 statistical data reports. The Ministry of Food and Agriculture is planning to validate
  and disseminate its annual production estimates.
  Recommendations: The team recommends: i) submission of the sample contract and code of conduct for enumerators for Bank’s review; ii) meet
  with the Ghana Federation of Disability organizations and translation of census and associated technical reports in Braille an audio; and iii) conduct
  stakeholders satisfaction survey.
  7. Liberia:
  An ISM was conducted from February 21 - 22, 2022.Technical aspects:
  Population and housing census: The cartographic work for the census is 99% completed, with over 18,300 enumeration areas mapped. The
  census pretest fieldwork has been completed, and analysis of the data and report writing is underway. The population census financing gap of over
  US$10 million, census management structures not being in place and the increased number of enumeration areas resulting from the cartographic
  mapping in turn increasing estimated costs for the main enumeration remain a challenge. National Accounts: A two-day stakeholder workshop on
  National Accounts was held. Liberia Institute of Statistics and Geo-Information Services (LISGIS) continues working with AFRITAC to produce GDP
  numbers for 2018, these are expected by March. Under the project, computers were distributed to eight Government of Liberia (GoL) departments'
  main data providers for National Accounts and are now in use. LISGIS will conduct a National Establishment Census this year. The LISGIS has
  supported the Liberia Revenue Authority with data entry for pre-2019 records that only existed in hard copies and have analyzed the data with the
  assistance of AFRITAC. Consumer Price Index: The collection of data and publication of the CPI is happening on a regular and punctual basis with
  the support of HISWA. For now, Excel is used for the CPI compilation. and LISGIS will need new software. Under the HISWA project, ECOWAS is
  working on the development of regional software for the production of the CPI. As LISGIS is also planning on rebasing the CPI and extending the
  geographic coverage, the mission recommends that this be combined with the use of the new software. The LISGIS has a CAPI application and
  server for CPI data collection which they are currently piloting before launching fully. Statistics degree program: Over 50 students have been
  registered, and the refurbishment of 3 classrooms and the computer lab will be completed soon, followed by the setting up of the network
  connection. LISGIS is also developing a strategic plan for capacity building and staff training within the institution, identifying needs and looking for
  national and international training opportunities. Improving physical and statistical infrastructure: LISGIS will conduct an infrastructure needs
  assessment on its overall physical, and IT facilities. The mission recommends sharing the needs assessment report as soon as it is prepared to
  help plan the feasibility of the activity including its scope and budget.
  Recommendations: The team advised that the project consider reallocating or reusing resources and HISWA project funds to support the census
  activities. For increased enumeration areas, the LISGIS GIS team will investigate and merge some Enumeration Areas once the mapping is
  completed, and share them with the World Bank team, so that its technical experts can review the data and provide suggestions on the way
  forward.

  8. Sierra Leone:
  There has been limited implementation progress since December 2021 following the Bank’s withdrawal of technical and financial assistance of the
  mid-term census. However, the Bank team has had discussions with Government on the overall planned activities under the HISWA project and a
  draft Annual Work Plan for 2022 has been shared with the Bank for review. A set of activities in the action plan has been discussed and agreed for

  9. Togo:
  An ISM was conducted from March 7-12, 2022.
  Project management: The activities approved in the 2021 Action Plan were implemented and have reached acceptable levels of completion. The
  Project Coordination Committee (PCC) meets regularly and its meetings are documented. The World Bank project team and the Project Coordinator
  hold regular weekly meetings. The Procurement risk is moderately satisfactory and procurement performance is rated moderately satisfactory.
  Financial management risk is Moderate and FM performance is rated Moderately Satisfactory (MS). The project's accounting is still kept in an Excel
  spreadsheet. The project accounts for the project preparation advance were audited and certified without qualification. The reports were acceptable
  to the World Bank. The report of the audit of the 2021 accounts is expected to be submitted to the World Bank by June 30, 2022. The mission also
  reviewed the issue of social safeguards. The risk of the Safeguards is Moderate and the performance of the Safeguards is rated as Moderately
  Satisfactory. A draft GRM is produced and INSEED was advised to forward the draft to the World Bank for approval before proceeding with its
  implementation. The mission also reviewed incidents including cases of accidents that occurred, especially during the implementation of census
  mapping. In total, 23 cases of accidents were recorded, including the one involving a vehicle that caused the death of the pedestrian as a result of
  injuries. It should be recalled that the risks of accidents and occupational diseases were taken into account in the planning of the mapping operation
  by taking out an insurance policy.
  Technical aspects: The activities included in the 2022 Work plan have been revised; in particular the activities related to the 5th General
  Population and Housing Census (RGPH5). The estimated enumeration budget was re-estimated taking into account the probable number of
  Enumeration Areas (EAs) that will result from the census mapping work. This has objectively increased the estimated budget for the operations from
  2,650,000,000 CFA francs (provided for in the 2021-2025 action plan) to 3,958,830,707 CFA francs, i.e., an increase of 49.4% of the initial budget,
  indicating the importance that the World Bank attaches to this major activity. The Population and housing census cartography has been delayed,
  due to logistics and financing difficulties for about three months and a half behind schedule, as activities ended around April 09, 2022. Preparations
  for the main enumeration have begun with the recruitment process for enumerators. Similarly, (Institut National de la Statistique et des Etudes
  Economiques et Démographiques, INSEED) has begun negotiations with the Société des Postes du Togo (SPT) to ensure the logistical transport of
  sensitive equipment (tablets) throughout the country and to ensure the payment of field agents. Economic statistics are almost on track. In terms of
  improving INSEED visibility and communication, the development of INSEED's graphic charter has been completed and popularized, both at the
  central level and in the regional offices. On working conditions, the mission noted the lack of office space for INSEED staff, particularly for staff
  recruited for the project. Some of these staff squat in cubicles provided for other staff and this may pose a huge risk to the confidentiality of certain
  fiduciary files. The mission recommends that the supervisory authority requisition the offices unoccupied by the National Center for Study and
  Computer Processing (Centre National d'Etude et de Traitements Informatiques CENETI), available on the first floor of the building.
  Recommendations:
  The mission recommended several actions to improve the management of procurement, finance, safeguards, and communication. In procurement,
  the mission recommended immediate submission of an updated procurement plan to the World Bank; entering and archiving of relevant information
  in STEP in real-time for procurement activities, and improving evaluation of estimated costs and better development of technical specifications to
  ensure value-for-money procurement processes. In financial management, the mission recommended that INSEED: (i) accelerate the recruitment of
  the accounting secretary to strengthen the project's accounting and financial management team; (ii) take the necessary steps to effectively involve
  the registrar and the accountant in the project's activities, with a clear description of their respective tasks to be transmitted to the World Bank for its
  opinion; (iii) accelerate the process of switching the project's accounting to the newly acquired and installed accounting software; (iv) comply with
  the provisions of the financing agreement by transmitting the Annual work plan to the World Bank no later than November 30 of the year preceding
  the year in which it is executed, and (iv) attach the internal audit reports to the quarterly IFRs to be transmitted to the World Bank. The mission
  noted that there is a need to improve the visibility and communication of official statistics activities under the coordination of INSEED, in general, but
  also of the project in particular. The mission recommends that INSEED present a more elaborate draft communication plan accompanied by
  structures with proven expertise in this area.
