                            I.       BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING

                        A. Introduction
                                 1. This Project Paper seeks the approval of World Bank management to provide Additional Financing (AF) in the
                                 amount of US$40 million equivalent and an extension of the parent project’s closing date until December 31, 2027,
                                 for the Log-in Georgia Project (the Parent Project). To achieve the Parent Project’s Project Development Objective
                                 (PDO), the Government of Georgia (GOG) requested AF to the Parent Project (letter June 13, 2025), to address the cost
                                 overrun incurred in the deployment of the middle mile fiber optic infrastructure under Component 1.
                                 2. Digital connectivity is the backbone of modern economies, driving both inclusive development and sustained
                                 economic growth. As such, Georgia is strategically investing in digitalization efforts, including the digitalization of the
                                 ‘Middle Corridor’ between Europe and Asia via the South Caucasus— and aims to position itself as a regional
                                 connectivity (physical, digital, and energy) hub given its geostrategic role in bridging Europe and Asia. To realize these
For Official Use Only




                                 ambitions, a robust and dense fiber-optic infrastructure across the country is essential, to ensure multiple routes of
                                 interconnection with digital connectivity networks in neighboring countries, in turn forming reliable international
                                 regional connectivity networks. Recent initiatives supported by the World Bank such as the report on the Middle
                                 Corridor1 and the ongoing Enhancing Energy Security through Power Interconnection and Renewable Energy (ESPIRE)
                                 Program (P179950) approved by the World Bank on May 21, 2024, underscore and support the critical role of digital
                                 infrastructure in enhancing regional trade, energy flows, and digital services in Georgia.
                                 3. The Log-In Georgia Project is supporting the implementation of key actions under Georgia’s National Broadband
                                 Development Strategy (NBDS) 2020-20252 and is aligned with the draft Strategy for the Development of the Digital
                                 Economy and Information Society of Georgia for 2025-2030.3 Both recognize the importance of digital infrastructure
                                 and universal access to broadband to support economic development, while also advancing digital inclusion and
                                 fostering the skills and demand necessary for meaningful internet use, particularly in rural areas. Specifically,
                                 component 1 (on increasing access to high-speed broadband) is being implemented as defined in Government
                                 Resolution No. 3754 with the policy oversight of the Ministry of Economy and Sustainable Development (MOESD), and
                                 with the involvement of the regulator Communications Commission (ComCom).
                        B. Parent Project Description and Current Status
                                 4. The Parent Project was approved on August 28, 2020, for a total amount equivalent to US$40 million in IBRD
                                 financing. The Project became effective on December 28, 2020, and is currently set to close on December 31, 2025.
                                 The Parent Project is co-financed with a loan from the European Investment Bank (EIB), amounting to EUR 34 million
                                 (approximately US$ 38.1 million equivalent), which became effective on October 24, 2023. Additionally, the Project
                                 benefits from counterpart financing of US$ 3.75 million equivalent, ensuring a robust and well-supported financial
                                 foundation.
                                 5. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to increase access to affordable broadband internet, and to
                                 promote its use by individuals and enterprises, in selected rural settlements. The Project supports achievement of
                                 these objectives through both supply and demand side interventions, as well as enabling environment improvements.
                                 On the supply side, it aims to address a gap in middle mile broadband infrastructure deployment in rural areas by the

                            11. The implementation of the proposed AF will be enhanced by drawing from lessons learned from the Parent
                            Project implementation.
                                 (a) Where possible, underground construction will continue to be the preferred option for passive infrastructure
                                     deployment, as it mitigates the impacts of climate and weather-related hazards in Project areas – see
                                     paragraph 40 for details on climate considerations. During network construction, Open Net has encountered
                                     the middle mile infrastructure of other telecom operators at depths lower than that used in the deployment
                                     of the Open Net network7 and often aerially deployed. Maintaining international good practices for the Open
                                     Net network’s infrastructure will ensure resilience of national broadband networks.

                                     a few contracts being delayed. As a result, the PIE has adjusted its approach in risky areas to deploy the

                              III. KEY RISKS

                             22. The AF does not affect the overall risk rating of the Parent Project, which continues to be assessed as Moderate,
                             and the overall risk of the AF is also rated as Moderate. The environmental and the social risk ratings for this AF remain
                             Moderate as the AF financed activities are of the same nature, including small and medium works, and the PIE’s
                             performance in implementing the ESF has been Satisfactory throughout the Parent Project’s implementation. The
                             Political and Governance risk has been upgraded from Moderate to a Substantial rating given that achieving the PDO
                             the Project’s closing date is dependent on timely ratification of the amended Loan Agreement in the Parliament’s
                             Autumn session scheduled for September 2025.14 Furthermore, municipal elections are scheduled for October 4, 2025,
                             which may result in disruptions that impact Parliamentary proceedings. As a mitigation measure, the project has
                             ensured strong support from the Ministry of Finance and MOESD to be able to launch procurements in advance of
                             ratification, availability of retroactive financing, and strong engagement and commitment from the PIE. Furthermore,
                             the Stakeholder risk has been downgraded to Moderate from Substantial due to the Parent Project’s stronger
                             performance, demonstrated government commitment through the AF request, and growing private sector
                             engagement, reflected in the rising number of internet providers and mobile operators connecting to the network.

                                ANNEX 1. ECONOMIC AND FINANCIAL ANALYSIS

1. The Additional Financing economic and financial analysis undertaken follows a standard Cost-Benefit
Analysis (CBA) approach and reveals a positive Net Present Value (NPV) and Internal Rate of Return for
Subcomponent 1.1. The model relies on available secondary data and reasonable assumptions, based on prior
experience as well as additional evidence sourced both from the PIE (Open Net), ComCom and Ookla broadband
speed test data. The analysis also leverages the updated business model prepared by Open Net in 2025 – though a
consultancy financed by the Project.
2. The model adopts a social discount rate of 11.65%, reflecting the opportunity cost of capital from an
intertemporal choice perspective for society as a whole. Under constant relative risk aversion, the social discount
rate can be expressed as r=β+ε*σ. The pure time preference rate (β) was estimated at 1, the elasticity of marginal
utility of consumption (ε) is estimated to be 1.5 on average and the expected growth rate of per capita consumption
(σ) was 7.10% according to IMF forecasts (World Economic Outlook Database for 2023-2027).
3. Key assumptions to derive the total benefits resulting from GDP impact from increase household penetration
with the Project include:
    (a) Total incremental GDP due to increased broadband penetration. The analysis relied on the assumption
        that an increase of 10 percentage-point in fixed broadband penetration yields an increase of 1.38 percent
        in GDP growth rate (World Development Report 2016 – Digital Dividends, World Bank Group). As such, the
        analysis considers the current and expected percentage increase in fixed broadband penetration in project
        areas as a result of Project financed activities to calculate the impact on national fixed broadband
        penetration from 2024 until 2030.
    (b) Total incremental GDP due to possible new employment on account of improved fixed broadband speed
        in project areas. An analysis of broadband speed changes on the likelihood of being active in the labor
