I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

  A. Project status

     1. Progress towards achievement of the Project Development Objective (PDO) is Satisfactory with no
        material risks that would undermine achievement of the PDO.

     2. Project implementation is progressing well, with significant progress in the procurement and contracting
        of the main activities of the Project, namely the telecoms infrastructure (middle-mile fiber optic network)
        deployments. Four contracts have been signed, representing around 600km of infrastructure to be
        deployed (total value of around US$8.4 million). The works in Racha and Lechkumi regions have been
        completed, while the works in Samtredia-Chokhatauri and Kobuleti-Khelvachauri regions will be
        completed in the first half of 2023. Three additional tenders have been announced for the following
        regions: Jvari-Mestia, Zugdidi-Khobi and Abasha-Senaki, representing around 670km of infrastructure to
        be built and a budget of around US$11 million. Concerning the active equipment to operate this
        infrastructure, the contract for a first lot (value around US$1.9 million) was signed in September 2022.
        This first lot will allow OpenNet (ON) to test the solution from a technical and a business perspective, so
        that the larger contract(s) can be adjusted accordingly.

     3. The policy and regulatory studies are progressing without specific issue or delay.

     4. Activities related to digital awareness and training programs have been proceeding broadly in line with
        the Project’s implementation plan since May 2022, now that challenges posed by the COVID-19 pandemic
        are no longer relevant.

  B. Summary of Performance of E&S Risk Compliance

     5. ON completed public consultations on the Environmental and Social Management Plans (ESMPs) for five
        locations. For locations where contractors have been selected, the updated ESMPs, including records of
        consultation process, were re-disclosed through ON’s webpage. Environmental and social requirements
        for contractors were duly incorporated into the standard procurement documents for the provision of
        civil works as well as into the Terms of Reference for the technical supervisor of works. ON continues
        stakeholder engagement activities in the Project areas, specifically in the municipalities of Tsageri,
        Lentekhi, Ambrolauri, Oni, Kobuleti and Chokhatauri. Specific groups of stakeholders are targeted for wide
        reach, as detailed in the Stakeholder Engagement Plan. This includes civil society representatives,
        schoolteachers and young people, among others. The questions and concerns that ON usually receives
        during meetings are about specific details of the Project implementation and timelines. A Grievance
        Redress Mechanism (GRM) was set up for the Project on the ON’s website since early 2021. It allows for
        the submission of grievances either email and telephone. There is evidence that Open Net receives and
        handles complaints. Prior to commencement of works, ON provides training for contractors on setting up
        contactor’s GRM for workers and its integration with the overall GRM arrangement.

     6. ON has furnished the Bank two semi-annual Project Progress Reports, including an account on the
        environmental and social performance. Reporting needs quality enhancement as advised by the World
        Bank. The last implementation support mission applied due diligence to identifying any recorded or,

  C. Rationale for restructuring

     7. The Loan Agreement between the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) and
        Georgia (Loan number 9161-GE) was signed on August 31, 2020, structured as a EUR35.7 million (US$40
        million equivalent) IBRD loan plus US$40 million counterpart financing. Subsequently, Georgia finalized
        discussions with the European Investment Bank (EIB) regarding the financing of the part of the Project
        covered by the counterpart financing. This resulted in a Finance Contract signed between Georgia and the
        EIB on November 25, 2021 to provide co-financing for activities planned under Part A.1, Schedule 1 of the
        Loan Agreement: “Support Georgia’s Open Net Program to develop national infrastructure offering open
        access, wholesale, broadband-telecommunications services” (in lieu of the US$40 million counterpart
        funding initially considered). Consequently, the EIB becomes a joint co-financier in this Project, in which
        the World Bank and the EIB have agreed that the World Bank will act as the lead finance institution. This
        change needs to be reflected in legal documents related to the Project.

     8. As a first step, the Principles of Collaboration between the World Bank and the EIB that will apply for the
        co-financing of the Project were signed between the two institutions in July 2022. These principles define
        and specify the terms and the sharing of the implementation and monitoring tasks (covering compliance
        with the minimum requirements for environmental and social safeguards as well as disbursement
        arrangements).

     9. As a second step, a restructuring is now required to reflect this joint co-financing with the EIB, which is
        not yet reflected in the original Loan Agreement. A restructuring request to that effect was communicated
        to the World Bank on December 19th, 2022. The restructuring will include an amendment to the Loan
        Agreement.
