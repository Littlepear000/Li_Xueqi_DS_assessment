 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Component 1: PforR
  Results Area 1:Revenue Mobilization. Collection of the province’s taxes has improved significantly from a baseline of PKR 14.30 billion in
  FY2018/19 to PKR 25.41 billion in FY2019/20, and PKR 26.48 billion in FY2020/21 (preliminary estimates), exceeding the project target. As of May
  2021, the registered Services Sales taxpayers who filed returns stood at 82 percent, exceeding the target of the Disbursement-linked Result
  (DLR) 1.2 (60 percent). There has been some progress in the integration of databases of the provincial tax authorities (DLI 2), and simplification and
  harmonization of their tax administration processes albeit many delays. The data system integration plan was approved by the provincial
  Cabinet and, therefore, DLR 2.1 has been achieved. However, DLR 2.2 has not been achieved as the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Revenue Authority
  (KPRA) has signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with only one out of the expected three withholding agents. KPRA has signed an MoU
  with KP Information Technology Board and is expected to sign MOUs with the Water and Sanitation Services Peshawar and KP AG office, in due
  course. There are ongoing discussions with relevant authorities to expedite this activity and therefore, this DLR is expected to be achieved soon.
  DLRs 3.1 and 3.2 have not been achieved as these required extensive field-based coordination, review and digitization of field records, and survey
  work for property tax—which was delayed because of COVID-19 restrictions. The KP Excise, Taxation and Narcotics Control Department (ETNCD)
  has now initiated the work in two districts of Abbottabad and Nowshera (which includes five cities), with the objective of achieving the DLR targets
  before the end of the calendar year.
  Results Area 2: Public Resource Management. Overall, the Government is making satisfactory efforts to assess the volume of cash deposits in
  commercial banks, as a first step to reduce the amount of cash deposits held by Government in commercial banks. The stock take of
  existing number of accounts and deposits has been initiated with the State Bank Pakistan. The Cash Management Policy was approved by the
  Provincial Cabinet, and therefore DLR 4.1 has been achieved. However, monthly consolidation of cash balances takes place in only three
  (Provincial Account 1 and 2 and District Account 4) out of nine government accounts/departments. As a result, DLR target 4.2 has not been
  achieved, but will likely be achieved before the end of the calendar year given the current progress. There is satisfactory progress in reducing the
  share of Annual Development Program funds allocated to unapproved projects (excluding Foreign Project Assistance). The FY20/21 budget
  allocates 30 percent of the budget to unapproved projects compared to the 49 percent for FY 18/19, the baseline year. Therefore, DLR 5.2 has
  been achieved. Similarly, the Government has prepared education and health sector plans to improve the National Financial Management
  Information System (NFMIS) coverage. A NFMIS decentralization plan has been approved by the provincial Cabinet, and DLR 6.1 has also been
  achieved as the system has been developed, tested and fully functional. Around four Tehsil Municipal Administrations have started using NFMIS to
  record payments and there are plans to roll out NFMIS in ten cities by the end of this fiscal year, which would help achieve DLR 6.2. The Output-
  Based Budgeting departments now include measurable gender key performance indicators (KPIs) and report on number of females reached by
  their services. At least eight departments have KPI disaggregated by gender. Satisfactory progress has also been made on strengthening
  government capacity for management of dedicated funds.
  Component 2: Technical Assistance and Capacity Building (Investment Project Financing/IPF)
  Component 2.1: Providing Foundational Support for e-Government Functionality
  Progress in this component is satisfactory. The KP team has completed all activities required and held several consultations with relevant
  stakeholders. Twelve macro processes with around 170 procedures have been reviewed in 8-10 key government departments. Standard bidding
  documents and implementation road map are currently under review.
