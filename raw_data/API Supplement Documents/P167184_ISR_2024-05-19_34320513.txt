4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  The Project became effective in December 2020. Despite challenges over the last few years, the Government of Papua
  New Guinea remains committed to achieving the Project Development Objectives and the National Department of Health
  sees the Project as essential towards achieving the national health plan. The Project was Restructured on December 12,
  2023 to implement changes required because of these delays and to streamline implementation, particularly for the
  verification of results. Since that time positive progress has been made with three new Expansion provinces being
  selected including Central, East Sepik and Chimbu in addition to the Early Adopter Province, East New Britain. A total of
  USD 2.1 million has been disbursed this year. A large part of the disbursements (USD 1.4 million equivalent) is from the
  Year 1 disbursement linked indicators, which are reimbursement to government against the achievement of key targets.
  Year 2 DLI verification has been initiated and a second tranche of funding is expected to be released by June 2024.

  The National Department of Health has successfully launched a Competitive DLI process with 22 Provincial Health
  Authorities who have signed Memorandums of Understanding to participate. The competitive DLIs will reward the top
  two performing Provincial Health Authorities who improve their respective results for each of the following indicators:
  increase in women who receive four or more antenatal care check-ups (C-DLI 1); increase in the number of children who
  receive a third dose of pentavalent vaccine (C-DLI 2); and increase in the number of TB patients who have been cured (C-
  DLI 3). Once Year 3 concludes on June 30, 2024, a third tranche of performance payments will be released when the
  achieved targets are verified.

  The Bank has contracted the United Nations Office for Project Services (UNOPS) to develop a standard health facility
  audit tool to assess primary health care facilities against the National Health Services Standards (activity financed by the
  Papua New Guinea (PNG) Strategic Partnership Multi-Donor Trust Fund). The tool is in an advanced stage and will be
  rolled out in East New Britain to inform the investment needs to be financed under the Project and develop a model that
  can be replicated across IMPACT Health project provinces. The tool is aimed at systematically identifying the
  refurbishments and equipment requirements needed to bring health facilities back up to standard.

  The next implementation support mission is scheduled for June 17-24, 2024.
