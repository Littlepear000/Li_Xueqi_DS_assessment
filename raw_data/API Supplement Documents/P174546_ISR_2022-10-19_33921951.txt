 MPA Table
 Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA) Status

 MPA Table Body
 The Emergency Locust Response Program (ELRP) is an emergency regional integration project under the multi-phase programmatic approach
 (MPA). The program provides funding across 3 technical components:

         Component 1. Surveillance and Control Measures to address the immediate threat posed by the upsurge; and

         Component 2. Livelihoods Protection and Rehabilitation to safeguard food security of affected households and restore farmers and
          livestock herder to productivity in the wake of an invasion; and

         Component 3. Coordination and Early Warning Preparedness to strengthen national systems and regional cooperation to address future
          threats.

 The ELRP-MPA program, with its Phase 1 first-moving countries, was approved by the World Bank's Board of Executive Directors on May 20, 2020,
 with an overall IDA financing envelope of US$500 million equivalent. Overall, ELRP MPA has committed XDR$315 million equivalent to 5
 countries and one regional organization. Across all 3 phases disbursement is 43%.

         PHASE 1 committed XDR$164.8 million equivalent in grants and credits to Djibouti, Ethiopia, Kenya. A minor restructuring of Phase 1
          that reallocated funds from component 1 to component 2 was completed in December 2021. An AF to Phase 1 of €$31.4 million
          equivalent to Kenya was approved by the World Bank Board in March 2022. A second AF to Phase 1 of XDR$43.5 million equivalent to
          Ethiopia was approved by the World Bank Board in June 2022. It is 44% disbursed after the additional financings to Kenya and Ethiopia.

         PHASE 2 was approved on June 29, 2020, providing a XDR$29.3 million equivalent grant to Somalia. An additional financing (AF) to
          Phase 2 of XDR$53.2 million to Somalia was approved on June 17, 2021. The Phase is 48.2% disbursed.

         PHASE 3 was approved on June 8, 2021, with XDR$37.9 million in grants to South Sudan and the Intergovernmental Authority on
          Development (IGAD). It is 26% disbursed.

 COMPONENT 1. The end of the desert locust upsurge affecting East Africa was declared as of March 2022, and the ELRP MPA contributed to
 swarm surveillance on over 10 million hectares of land and locust control (ground and aerial) on over 530,000 hectares across Djibouti, Ethiopia, and
 Kenya. This achievement required training over 700 frontline staff on desert locust management and providing awareness raising activities directly
 with over 40,000 community members. ELRP has financed, inter alia, the purchase of over 24,000 pieces of personal protective equipment, ultra-low
 volume spraying equipment for back-pack and vehicle-based control teams, the lease of 6 airplanes, the purchase of one spray plane and drones,
 and the lease of helicopters. Moving forward, Ethiopia will continue with heightened surveillance and control activities because it is a frontline country

 with an endemic locust population, swarms have still been sighted as recently as March, and the level of precipitation in the spring and summer could
 trigger more breeding.

 COMPONENT 2. Safety net support has been provided to about 550,000 families under Subcomponent 2.1, the vast majority being in Somalia,
 which exclusively finances livelihood protection through a shock-responsive safety net and will target over 1.5 million people by project end. Djibouti
 is currently distributing safety net payments to 2000 locust affected vulnerable families. Ethiopia and Kenya have safety net programs already
 operating in their countries, which ELRP cooperates with but does not finance. South Sudan will commence safety net support as soon as third-party
 contracts are finalized. Subcomponent 2.2 activities have helped over 253,000 farmers and livestock owners resume production quickly. In Kenya the
 project will support microprojects developed by common interest groups (CIGs). Thus far, 671 microprojects covering nearly 13,000 farmers have
 been disbursed.

 COMPONENT 3. investments in national preparedness are underway and include investments in technology, raining, and strategic partnerships to
 improve the ability of countries monitor, forecast, and provide early warning of a desert locust threat. The ELRP countries are already part of the
 FAO/Desert Locust Information System (DLIS) and are investing in the survey/data information collection tools—i.e., eLocust3, RAMSES, and
 agrichemical inventory management—to fully engage in ongoing surveillance. Much of the training and technology can be applied to surveillance
 against other transboundary pests, such as fall armyworm, quelea bird, and other invasive pests. Activities are also underway in IGAD is
 establishing an information sharing and coordination platform for its member states and other stakeholders to coordinate planning, preparedness,
 and response activities to desert locust and other transboundary pest threats. The team IGAD has held close consultations with government
 representatives in Sudan, Ethiopia, Somalia, Uganda, and South Sudan. The Kenya and Djibouti consultations are being scheduled. It is also
 establishing a formal cooperative agreement with the Desert Locust Control Organization of East Africa (DLCO-EA).

 ELRP Phase 3 has officially launched in South Sudan where activities will focus on the safety of equipment and agrichemicals procured before the
 project started, in addition to additional training of communities and frontline staff. Livelihood protection and rehabilitation activities will also be
 supported in areas directly impacted by swarms and those indirectly impacted by the reduced food availability due to the locust upsurge in the area.
 There will also be investments in national preparedness.
