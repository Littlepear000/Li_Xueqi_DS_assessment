                         I.    BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING

                        A. Introduction

                        1.       This Project Paper seeks the approval of the World Bank Regional Integration Director for Africa and the Middle
                        East for the second Additional Financing (AF) in the amount of US$6.20 million equivalent to support the Harmonizing
                        and Improving Statistics in West Africa (HISWA) Project (P169265) in Ghana and Liberia . The proposed AF includes
                        allocations of US$3.00 million IDA Credits (US$2.90 million from the SML and US$0.10 million from the PBA allocation) for
                        the Republic of Ghana and US$3.20 million IDA Credit for the Republic of Liberia. The proposed AF is intended to address
                        the cost overruns related to the implementation of critical priority activities, particularly the Household Income and
                        Expenditures Survey (HIES) and the modernization of the Consumer Price Index (CPI) and economic statistics, Labor Force
                        Survey (LFS) in Liberia, Ghana Living Standards Survey (GLSS), as well as the expansion, rebasing and modernization of the
For Official Use Only




                        Consumer Price Index (Full Computer-Assisted Personal Interview “CAPI” migration), and improvements in economic
                        statistics (specifically, national account activities). The objective is to close significant data gaps in poverty, labor market
                        and economic statistics in the two countries and to enhance the production and use of high-quality data, thereby
                        supporting the achievement of the Project Development Objective (PDO).

                        2.       The parent project was approved by the World Bank Board of Executive Directors on March 26, 2020. The project
                        includes nine recipients with a total financing amount of US$379.00 million from the National Performance-Based
                        Allocations (PBA) International Development Association (IDA) and Regional IDA Windows. Seven project participating
                        countries, namely, Burkina Faso (IDA Credit 65730 - US$30.0 million and IDA Grant D5810 - US$30.00 million), Cabo Verde
                        (IDA Credit 65740 - US$15.00 million), Cote d’Ivoire (IDA Credit 65750 - US$54.00 million), Ghana (IDA Credit 65760 -
                        US$150.00 million), Liberia (IDA Credit 65770 - US$15.00 million and IDA Grant D5830 - US$15.00 million), Sierra Leone

                        3.        The parent project aims to address critical data gaps by harmonizing, producing, disseminating, and enhancing
                        the use of essential economic and social statistics. It seeks to improve the statistical capacity of participating countries,
                        ensuring that their National Statistical Systems (NSS) meet user needs and generate more comparable statistics, thereby
                        facilitating better decision-making. Furthermore, the project supports the modernization of statistical methods,
                        organizational structures, physical infrastructure, and Information and Communication Technology (ICT) within National
                        Statistics Offices (NSOs), as well as Ministries, Departments, and Agencies (MDAs) responsible for statistics. This
                        modernization will empower these entities to fulfill their mandates more effectively.

                        4.      The parent Project Development Objective is to strengthen the statistical systems of participating countries and
                        regional bodies in Africa to harmonize, produce, disseminate and enhance the use of core economic and social statistics.

                        5.       The first AF, totaling US$9.60 million equivalent, was approved on June 16, 2025, with allocations of US$6.00
                        million for Côte d’Ivoire and US$3.60 million for Sierra Leone. This brought the total project financing to US$388.60
                        million.

                        global supply chains, delaying procurement and bidding processes, and triggering sharp price increases for essential goods
                        and services. Items such as vehicles, computers, tablets, interviewer kits, and other data collection equipment became

                        implementation. Lockdowns and movement restrictions required major adjustments to fieldwork operations, leading to
                        increased costs for enumerator training, extended data collection periods, vehicle rentals, and additional staff
                        compensation. Collectively, these adjustments substantially raised the overall cost of survey operations.

                        18.       Governments across participating countries increasingly demand more frequent and timely data to monitor the
                        social and economic impacts of COVID-19 on households and businesses. In response, the project supported the rapid
                        design and implementation of multiple rounds of phone-based poverty surveys to address urgent data gaps. While these
                        surveys were more feasible during lockdowns and ensured continuity in data collection, they also introduced new cost
                        drivers. These included expenses related to call centers or mobile service providers, airtime and respondent incentives,
                        enumerator training for phone-based interviews, software customization, and remote data processing systems. In Côte
                        d’Ivoire and Sierra Leone, many rounds of the COVID-19 Impact Monitoring Survey (CIMS) were conducted, providing
                        critical real-time data to inform government response efforts.

                           IV. APPRAISAL SUMMARY

                          A. Economic Analysis

                        30.      The economic justification for the AF is the same as the parent project. Enhanced and harmonized statistical
                        data production are critical for policy planning and decision-making at national and local governments, benefiting
                        governments, the private sector, researchers, and civil society. Socio-economic data plays a crucial role that provides key
                        insights for informed decisions. Harmonized and comparable statistical data across the NSS leads to the production of
                        high-quality and reliable data. This strengthens governance, enhances accountability, and ultimately contributes to
                        improving the well-being of the entire population, both directly and indirectly.

                          B. Technical

                        31.     The Additional Financing activity is strategically relevant and timely for Ghana and Liberia. Harmonized social
                        and economic data are essential for effective planning and policy-formulation, particularly for efficient allocation of
                        resources and service delivery. The living standards household surveys are particularly critical for understanding the
                        evolving socio-economic landscape, especially poverty, informality and employment trends, income distribution, and
                        household welfare. The national account and CPI data are critical to better monitor the country's economy. Without a
                        robust data system and regular high-quality surveys to support data analysis and evidence-based decision-making, much
                        of the potential of data to improve development outcomes and address inequalities will go untapped.

                        41.      Like the parent project, the AF will help address some gender data gaps. By collecting data on sex, age, marital

                        43.     Grievance Redress: Communities and individuals who believe that they are adversely affected by a project
                        supported by the World Bank may submit complaints to existing project-level grievance mechanisms or the Bank’s
                        Grievance Redress Service (GRS). The GRS ensures that complaints received are promptly reviewed in order to address
                        project-related concerns. Project affected communities and individuals may submit their complaint to the Bank’s
                        independent Accountability Mechanism (AM). The AM houses the Inspection Panel, which determines whether harm
                        occurred, or could occur, as a result of Bank non-compliance with its policies and procedures, and the Dispute Resolution
                        Service, which provides communities and borrowers with the opportunity to address complaints through dispute
                        resolution. Complaints may be submitted to the AM at any time after concerns have been brought directly to the attention
                        of Bank Management and after Management has been given an opportunity to respond. For information on how to submit
                        complaints to the Bank’s Grievance Redress Service (GRS), visit http://www.worldbank.org/GRS. For information on how
                        to submit complaints to the Bank’s Accountability Mechanism, visit https://accountability.worldbank.org.
