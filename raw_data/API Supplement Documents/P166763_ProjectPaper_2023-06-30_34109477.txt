    I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Background

1.      The restructuring of the Gender-based Violence Prevention and Response Project (hereafter “PRVBG project”)
aims to introduce necessary changes to project implementation in light of the dissolution and liquidation of the original
Project Implementing Entity “Social Fund of the Democratic Republic of Congo (FSRDC)”. On April 27, 2023, the FSRDC
was dissolved by presidential Ordonance no 23/0481, thereby requiring a restructuring to: (a) adjust implementation and
disbursement arrangements; (b) ensure adequate fiduciary arrangements are in place; and, (c) extend the project closing
date. In addition, this restructuring will include cancellation of funds and reallocation of funds between disbursement
categories.

B. Project status

2.       The PRVBG Project was approved in 2018 with an aim to increase participation in gender-based violence (GBV)
prevention programs, increase the utilization of multi-sectoral response services by GBV survivors, and provide
immediate and effective response in the event of a crisis or emergency in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC).
The project implementation area covers four provinces in Eastern DRC – North Kivu, South Kivu, Maniema and Tanganyika
– and has the following components: (a) GBV prevention and integrated support for survivors at community level which
supports comprehensive GBV prevention interventions by promoting behavior change, strengthening referral
mechanisms and providing immediate support for survivors at community level; (b) GBV response to improve
responsiveness of front-line service providers through integrated Centers of Excellence (CoEs), mobile clinics and de-
centralized One Stop Centers, and by improving the health sector's capacity to respond to GBV; (c) support for policy
development, project management and monitoring and evaluation; and, (d) a Contingency Emergency Response
component.

3.        The project is making Satisfactory progress towards achieving the Project Development Objective (PDO). While
initially targeting 785,000 beneficiaries over the life of the project, the project has far exceeded this goal, reaching 7.4
million beneficiaries through awareness raising, GBV prevention and mental health activities at the community level. A
total of 60,767 survivors have received care through the two CoEs, community-based organizations and health centers
across the four provinces. Other project achievements include 60 percent of survivors who have received services
provided by the project's partners have received at least two response services; 99 percent of eligible reported GBV cases
received Post-Exposure Prophylaxis treatment within 72 hours of the incident. The mental health program included among
the project activities has yielded promising results, including reductions in post-traumatic stress disorder, anxiety, and
depression, as well as significant increases in self-esteem and local functioning among the people given this training. As
part of its livelihoods activities, the project supported the development of 1,228 Village Savings and Loan Associations
(VSLA) and organized over 100 VSLA exchanges to support social inclusion of Indigenous Peoples to take part in the
economic support services and livelihoods activities. The project includes partnerships with the Ministry of Public Health
to ensure coordination with the Health Sector, as well as with the Ministry of Gender, Family and Children and the Office
of the Presidential Representative for GBV to build capacity for policy coordination and GBV data collection and
management. Through these partnerships, 433 health personnel were trained to provide care and services to GBV
survivors, the national sexual and gender-based violence policy was disseminated through outreach with stakeholders in

C. Rationale for restructuring

5.       As mentioned above, on April 27, 2023, presidential Ordonnance 23/048 approved the dissolution and
liquidation of the FSRDC, the Project Implementing Entity, which requires the project to revise its implementation and
institutional arrangements. Through presidential Ordonnance 23/049 dated on April 28, 2023, a new FSRDC was created
with a new legal status, expanded objectives and larger mission. As a result of the dissolution of the Project Implementing
Entity, the Ministry of Finance (MFIN), through the Cellule de Suivi des Projets et Programmes (CSPP), will be responsible
for taking over the implementation of the Project, pending a comprehensive assessment satisfactory to the Association of
the new FSRDC.

6.       Created through a ministerial decree no. 069/CAB/MIN/FIANCES/2011, the Cellule de Suivi des Projets et
Programmes is the institution under the Ministry of Finance with oversight responsibility of all donor-funded programs,
including those funded by the World Bank. CSPP is actively involved in the negotiation process in all new World Bank-
funded projects and closely follows up on the project’s implementation, including fiduciary and disbursement matters.
Over the last two years, CSPP has increased its support for project implementation by systematically having
representatives in key meetings during project supervision missions. CSPP is actively involved in regularly following up
with project implementing units and line ministries and providing portfolio performance updates to the MFIN. This is also
a key focal point between the World Bank and the Government. The CSPP staff have an average of five years of relevant
experience in finance and are familiar with World Bank procedures. Based on its mandate, CSPP has the necessary capacity
to play the oversight role over the project implementing teams. The project implementing unit’s financial management
teams will include designated CSPP staff that will play the expenses and financial report validation role.

7.      This restructuring also includes an extension of the project closing date, cancellation and reallocation of funds
among the disbursement categories. In addition to delays caused by the dissolution of the FSRDC, project implementation
was also delayed due to disruptions caused by COVID-19 restrictions and the security situation in Eastern DRC. These
implementation delays have also impacted project disbursements. In order to ensure that proper project closure
procedures can be undertaken, and improve disbursements, this restructuring includes an extension of the project closing
date from June 30, 2023, to September 30, 2023, and a cancellation of US$10.5 million (7,875,138 in Special Drawing Rights).
The cancellation of funds will be reflected in a revised reallocation of funds in the project’s disbursement categories.

 II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

Revised implementation and institutional arrangements

8.    Project Implementation Unit (PIU): The Ministry of Finance (MFIN) will have overall responsibility of project
implementation, coordination, monitoring and evaluation, fiduciary aspects (including disbursements), and environmental

and social management. The CSPP anchored within MFIN will implement the project activities. The CSPP will be
responsible for all project-implementation management aspects as follows:

       Ministry of Finance. MFIN will be responsible for overall responsibility for project coordination, oversight and
        withdrawal of proceeds. Given the increased workload, the MFIN has agreed to second staff who will also be
        signatories to the project Designated Account (DA), and open subaccounts at banks at the provincial level. With
        the MFIN staff as signatories to bank accounts, it will facilitate flow of funds and maintain project oversight.

       Cellule de Suivi des Projets et Programmes. The CSPP will be in charge of overall project implementation,
        monitoring and environmental and social management and will comprise former FSRDC technical and fiduciary
        staff. This will help cope with the increased workload, ensure institutional memory and continuity and help with
        a smooth transition. Specifically, there would be two teams. First, the technical team (WB project financed
        consultants) structure of the dissolved FSRDC will be maintained at national and provincial levels. To this end, the
        antenna (i.e., former FSRDC regional office) coordinators reporting to the Chargé des Operations (PIU coordinator
        at the central level) at provincial level will be evaluated, and those meeting performance and delivery standards
        could continue with the PIU technical team as consultants. Former FSRDC technical staff hired for the
        implementation of project activities will be retained for the purposes of implementing the on-going project
        activities, to ensure smooth continuation of activities and keep the institutional memory of the project activities.
        The Chargé des Operations (CoP) will lead the technical team. Second, the fiduciary team will be a dedicated team
        to manage financial management and procurement. The financial management and procurement teams of the
        former FSRDC (financed under the WB project) with additional staff seconded by MFIN will be retained. The MFIN
        team through CSPP assigned to the project financial management teams will be nominated specifying the province
        of intervention. An alternate CSPP person will be also designated for back up purposes before July 15, 2023. In
        parallel, the CSPP will hire two consultants by end July 2023 to strengthen its technical and fiduciary team to allow
        an efficient monitoring of project implementation activities.

       Designated Account. A new DA will be opened to implement project activities under the new implementation
        arrangements. The same applies for the commercial bank accounts at the provincial level. The previous DAs will
        be closed and the available funds will be returned to the World Bank no later than July 15, 2023.

       Sub-accounts. Signatories under the sub-accounts (at the provincial level) will be composed of the antenna (i.e.,
        former FSRDC regional office) coordinator and the provincial accountant. Prior to each payment from the sub-
        account, the representative of the CSPP assigned to the province will provide a formal approval. This measure will
        also apply for the request of the sub-account replenishment from the DA.

       Project activities monitoring. The PIU will be responsible for project monitoring. Current project monitoring
        mechanisms in place should be maintain including the Grievance Redress Mechanism systems to manage project
        beneficiaries’ complaints as indicated in the Project Implementation Manual.


Extension of project closing date

9.      Project closing date. The project closing date will be extended by 3-months to allow for proper closing procedures
and final disbursements to go forward without interruptions. The new project closing date will be September 30, 2023.
