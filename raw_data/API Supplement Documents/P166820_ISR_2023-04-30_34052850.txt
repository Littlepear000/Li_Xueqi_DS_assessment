 Project Development Objectives

  Project Development Objective (from Project Appraisal Document)
  The Project development objectives of the Fourth Phase of the Central Asia Regional Links Program are to enhance the efficiency of cross-border
  trade for participants of the regional economy and to improve the resilience and safety of regional connectivity infrastructure in Sughd region and
  Gorno-Badakhshan Autonomous region.

  The project contributes to the Central Asia Regional Links Programs higher-level development objectives of increasing cross-border connectivity
  and enhancing integrated regional development to revitalize historically active economic exchanges in Central Asia and beyond along the Silk
  Route.

  Has the Project Development Objective been changed since Board Approval of the Project Objective?
  No

 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Project Additional Finance. The World Bank has considered the provision of Additional Finance for CARs-4 Project as a response to the request
  received from the Government of Republic of Tajikistan dated October 27, 2022, subject to availability of IDA20 resources. The proposed AF was
  not requested due to any potential negative affects to the ongoing project. It is a request to scale up the scope, and finance the construction of 32

  km Gulistan – Kulob road section design, which is completed under the ongoing project. Necessary safeguards and procurement documents have
  already been drafted. A detailed economic analysis was also carried out for the mentioned road section which was shared with the bank for
  review. During the last mission, the Bank team had detailed discussions with Ministry of Transport (MoT) and Customs Services (CS) on the scope
  of the additional finance which will mainly focus on the infrastructure and Customs components. Based on the initial estimates, US$70.00 million is
  required to finance only the additional scope which does not include IDA loss due to SDR depreciations (estimated at US$3.75 million). The target is
  to deliver the AF by early 2nd quarter of FY24.
  Disbursement. The latest major disbursement was made for the provision of advance payment under the civil works contract in December
  2022. The current project disbursement stands at US$13.81 million, which is 10.68% of total funds. The project has committed nearly 75% of total
  project funds, and disbursements are expected to pick up over the next year.
  Component 1: Improve regional connections in Sughd and GBAO regions:
  Sub-component 1.1 rehabilitation of roads in Sughd (CARS-4/RFB/W-PQ/02), The procurement process for this contract is completed. Following
  Bank’s clearance, the civil works contract was awarded to China Railway WUJU (Group) Corporation with a contact value of US$56.679.833,61 on
  November 25, 2022, which was 13.47% higher than the cost estimate. The reason for the price increase was due to the rise in the cost of
  construction materials. The mobilization advance has been processed and the contractor is mobilized on site, and civil works started on April 01,
  2023.
  Sub-component 1.2 construction works in the GBAO region (CARS-4/RFB/W-PQ/01), MoT/PIG completed the procurement process. The revised
  BER was prepared and sent to the Bank for review and clearance. The Bank issued no objection to the recommendation of the Evaluation
  Committee on March 03, 2023. The contract was awarded on March 27, 2023 with a contract value of US$ 31,283,742.39. The recommended bid
  has cost overrun of 14.23% above the engineer’s estimate.
  Sub-component 1.4 Feasibility studies for Khatlon region, and Sughd and GBAO regions: For this component two consulting works are ongoing: (i)
  final designs drawings, bidding documents and safeguard documentation for the Guliston-Kulob (Kathlon) section, awarded to TEMELSU; and the
  (ii) development of feasibility Studies for Sughd and GBAO regions awarded in January 31, 2022, to a second consultant company ALTINOK. Both
  contracts are progressing well.
  Component 2: Road asset management and road safety:
  Sub-Component 2(b) Improve Road asset preservation, Road Asset Management System. The development of a Road Asset Management System
  (RAMS) for the country is a joint effort between several donors. The Bank is closely coordinating this activity with the rest of the donors by holding
  regular coordination meetings. The World Bank, under the CARs-4 project, will support MoT in further developing the RAMS database and
  expanding its functionality. MoT-PIG hired a local Road Safety Specialist on September 01, 2022, to support the team on this component. PIG also
  prepared a ToR for hiring an International Technical Advisor. The main task of the adviser will be to ensure coordination between various partners
  supporting the development of the RAMS in the Ministry of Transport, preparation of terms of reference for the implementation of an effective road
  asset management system, as well as training of MoT staff on the implementation of RAMS.
  Component 2(c). Improve Road Safety. The project supports the Department of State Automobile Inspection (DSAI) to increase the use of 4-wheel
  vehicle safety belts, by carrying out a legal and regulatory review, a publicity campaign to promote the use of safety belts, and design of working
  procedures and capacity-building to raise compliance. It was agreed that MoT will further engage with DSAI and that an NGO would be recruited to
  carry out the capacity-building and promotional campaigns in this regard. It was agreed during the mission that MoT-PIG together with DSAI, will
  develop a ToR for hiring an advisor who will prepare terms of reference to support the DSAI in increasing the use of seat belts on vehicles.
  Sub-Component 2(d). Retrofit or construct safety barriers along selected road sections in severe mountainous terrains as a demonstration pilot.
  Based on field visits, the mission recognized that that there were additional needs that could be addressed under this sub-component. Instead of
  just designing and installing safety barriers, it was suggested to review the budget for this activity and to carry out a road safety pilot section
  developments at the identified location, which could serve as a future reference for forthcoming development partner-funded projects. This project
  may include additional signaling, road markings, speed calming, lighting, and other road user safety facilitation features/devices etc. A
  comprehensive follow-up was agreed during the next mission.
  Component 3. Facilitate cross-border movement of goods:
  Component 3.1& 3.2 Upgrading of the customs Information and Communication Technology (ICT) platform and strengthening of institutional
  capacity. UNCTAD has been hired for the “Provision of Technical Assistance for Migration to ASYCUDA World and the Implementation of Customs
  Reform”. UNCTAD has commenced the work on development and testing using the facilities and environment provided by the CS.
  Project Management. The Project Implementation Group (PIG) is steered by the second Deputy Minister of Transport, working as Project Director
  and the technical team is led by well qualified Project Coordinator. The PIG is fully functional and have completed recruitments.
