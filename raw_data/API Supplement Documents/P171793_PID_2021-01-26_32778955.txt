   B. Introduction and Context

   Country Context

  1.      Niger is a low-income, landlocked and fragile country in the Sahel region experiencing a three-fold crisis:
  security, humanitarian, and more recently health crisis related to the coronavirus (COVID-19) pandemic. The
  regional insecurity and violent extremist groups threaten stability, fuel pre-existing tensions and cause the
  displacement of population. Among the estimated 22 million population, growing at about 3.8 percent per year,
  about 280,000 persons are in high intensity conflict zones, 3.5 million are in medium intensity conflict zones, and
  6.2 million are in a zone at risk of conflict. The effects of the ongoing civil insecurity continue to affect negatively
  food security for poorer and displaced households. About 309 security incidents have been reported between
  January-October 2020, resulting in 968 fatalities according to the Armed Conflict Location and Event Data (ACLED)
  dashboard. The regions of Diffa, Tillabery, and Tahoua (see map in Annex 2) are the ones most affected by
  insecurity and hosted an estimated 223,048 displaced people in April 2020, compared to 184,634 people in April
  2019.

  2.      Against this background, the recent outbreak of coronavirus disease is likely to have severe economic and
  social consequences in Niger. On March 11, 2020, the World Health Organization (WHO) declared the virus
  outbreak a pandemic and Niger confirmed its first cases on March 19, 2020. The COVID-19 health crisis not only
  negatively impacts people’s lives (69 deaths and 1222 total infected persons as of Novembe r 3, 2020) but also
  affects their livelihoods due to the economic slowdown and lower government revenues to finance social
  programs for its vulnerable citizens. The global COVID-19 outbreak is expected to have a significant negative
  impact on Africa’s economy, and it is expected that growth in Sub-Saharan Africa is predicted to fall to -3.3

  economy are difficult to predict at this stage, but it is likely that impact will be significant and negative. Health
  care related spending could rise by as much as 2 percent of GDP and measures will be needed to cushion the

  (iii) business support (predominantly temporary tax relief).

  3.      There is a high disparity between women and men, with Niger ranking 154 out of 155 countries3 on the
  inequality index between sexes which measures three important aspects of human development—reproductive
  health, empowerment, and economic status. According to the 2017 Systematic Country Diagnostic (SCD) for
  Niger, 34 percent of Nigerien women are out of the labor force as opposed to 10 percent for men; those working
  are employed on average for fewer hours than men (28 versus 43) and receive lower earnings.4 Gender norms
  are still prevalent, which translates into men participating more than women in productive activities, while the
  latter are still in charge of household responsibilities. The four main barriers that women face to participate in
  productive activities are (a) lack of resources; (b) social norms; (c) inadequate human capital (low levels of
  education and skills); and (d) legal limitations for women to conduct business. According to a nationwide survey
  on the magnitude and risk factors of gender-based violence (GBV), funded by the United Nations Funds for the
  Population (UNFPA), three out of five women (60 percent) declared having been a survivor of at least one type
  of gender based violence (GBV) in their lifetime. The regions that have the highest percentage of women survivors
  of GBV are Maradi and Zinder where, respectively, 95 percent and 88 percent of women declared having been a
  survivor of at least one type of GBV in their lifetime.

  4.      Access to natural resources is limited due to climate change impacting significantly the Sahel region,
  high population increase and non-inclusive territorial development policies.5 Niger has a hot and semi-arid to
  arid climate and is one of the hottest and driest countries in the world. The country is already experiencing severe
  droughts and heat waves that result in chronic food insecurity.6 Climate change is expected to aggravate the
  situation with temperatures projected to increase in 1.6-2.9°C by 2050 and with higher rainfall variability leading
  to increased occurrence and severity of heavy rainfall events. With elevated risk of extreme droughts, heat waves
  and floods, Niger’s population is highly vulnerable to climate shocks.

  5.     Landlocked conditions and high costs of doing business obstruct Niger’s development prospects. Being
  landlocked and at least 1,000 km far from the nearest seaport, combined with low levels of human capital, are
  formidable obstacles to developing more extensively private sector economic activities. These disadvantages
  worsen with elevated costs of doing business. Niger’s ranking in the logistic performance index dropped from
  100th to 157th out of 160 countries between 2016-2018, below the average logistic performance index of Sub-
  Saharan Africa. Transport costs are unnecessarily high because roads are not maintained, roadblocks are
  common, and competition in the trucking sector is limited.

   Sectoral and Institutional Context

  6.     Road transport is by far the most dominant transport mode in Niger, and the National Road No 11 (RN
  No11) is the main transport axis connecting Zinder and Agadez. The country has a total road network of 20,268
  km, of which 10,649 km national roads, 2,075 km regional roads, and 6,952 km unclassified. Within the total
  network, only 4,655 km are paved roads. The Zinder - Agadez road segment is a 425-kilometer-long section of
  RN No11, one of Niger’s main transport axis, which connects the southern agricultural surplus regions to the
  northern deficit regions. RN No11 is a major trade route through which the northeastern region of Agadez
  receives agricultural goods coming from the Southern regions of Maradi and Zinder, and through which the
  southern regions receive livestock.

  7.       Decades-long lack of regular maintenance of the Zinder - Agadez section of the RN No11 negatively
  affected the connectivity of the northeastern Agadez region. The paved road section starting from Arlit and
  passing through Agadez, Zinder and down to the Nigerian border was originally constructed in the mid-1980’s as
  a 2-lane road with a 6-meter-wide paved driving surface and 1-meter-wide usable shoulders on each side.
  Political instability in the 1990s and a resulting lack of funds allocated for regular maintenance has led to
  substantive deterioration and significantly shortened the life span of this road section. The pavement failed
  structurally, which means that vehicles could only travel at slow speeds, travel times and transport costs
  increased substantially, and flash floods, sand dunes movement and encroachment sometimes interrupted
  traffic. Connectivity along the RN No11 is expected to deteriorate further without interventions to improve road
  and drainage infrastructure standards, and without introducing maintenance practices that consider climate
  change impacts. The relatively narrow road platform, absence of road safety equipment through urban areas and
  absence of both vertical and horizontal markings also negatively affect road safety. The deteriorated road
  conditions also hindered the government’s efforts to establish and maintain their presence in the region and
  control illegal trafficking of people and goods, arms smuggling and other criminal activity.

  8.      Donor programs helped upgrade a part of the Zinder - Agadez section of the RN No11, leaving only the
  Tanout - Tiguidit part in need of rehabilitation. In recent years, the Government of Niger (GoN) has been able
  to mobilize funding from various external and internal sources for the rehabilitation and upgrading of several
  sections of the RN No 11 corridor between Zinder and Agadez. The European Regional Development Fund (EDF)
  has provided funding for the rehabilitation of 200 km of road between Zinder and Agadez, namely for the sections
  Zinder-Soraz (40 km) and Soraz-Tanout (98 km) where the rehabilitation works are almost completed, and for
  Agadez-Tiguidit (62 km) where the rehabilitation is fully completed. The road section between Agadez and Arlit
  has also been upgraded with funding related to the uranium mines near Arlit. The road section between Arlit and
  the border with Algeria is currently being upgraded to a paved standard with funding from the Arab Fund for
  International and Social Development. As a result, the only missing link between the already completed sections
  is the one between Tanout and Tiguidit (226 km) which remains in a very deteriorated condition and urgently
  requires rehabilitation, upgrading and road safety improvements.

  9.     Private sector is active in the road construction sector. Since 1998, the Government of Niger (GoN) with
  support from the World Bank has implemented major reforms in the road sector which focused, among others,
  on the private sector participation. In response to the termination of the State’s role as executor of physical road
  construction and road maintenance works by force account, this reform has since given rise to numerous private
  sector contractors and consulting firms active in Niger’s road sector. The presence of qualified road contractors
  and engineering consultants in Niger has allowed the recent introduction of the first Output-and Performance-

  10.     Niger’s National Transport Strategy (2016-2025) guides all interventions in the road, railway and inland
  water transport sub-sectors. The general objectives of the strategy are (i) to optimize, without overinvesting,
  the development and the management of transport infrastructure in support of the country’s socio -economic
  development and transport sector planning, (ii) to boost the transport sector by improving the management
  capacity of both the public and the private sector, through institutional and regulatory reforms, training of staff
  and the use of new technologies, (iii) to facilitate transport and transit through major corridors, through the
  development of road and rail infrastructure, as well as by improving the supply chain and simplifying procedures,
  and (iv) to continue to strengthen the broader social objectives associated with the transport sector, such as road
  safety, adaptation to climate change, protection of the environment and public health, gender and citizen
  engagement.

  11.     The Ministry of Equipment (ME) manages the entire road network while the Ministry of Transport (MT)
  is responsible for land (road and railway), air, and inland water transport policies and regulations. The General
  Directorate of Major Works (Direction Générale des Grands Travaux, DGGT) within the ME manages the
  reconstruction and construction of the primary road network, while the Directorate of Road Transport (Direction
  des Transports Routiers, DTR) oversees the road transport services. The General Directorate of Rural Roads
  (Direction Générale des Routes Rurales, DGRR) oversees the construction and rehabilitation of the rural roads
  network.

  12.     The lead road safety agency (Agence Nigérienne de Sécurité Routière, ANISER) is not operational since
  its creation in 2014. The agency is confronting a lack of capacity for conducting its mission of promoting the
  national road safety agenda and of coordinating road safety actions among different actors because of lack of
  resources, qualified staff, and a national strategy for road safety. Furthermore, under-reporting of accident data
  makes road safety activities ineffective.

   D. Project Description

  15.     Component 1: Road reconstruction, maintenance and safety improvements along Zinder - Agadez
  section of National Road No11 (approximately 133.2 million USD, of which US$ 133 million IDA and US$ 0.2
  million counterpart funding). The component is designed to support the GoN strategy to improve connectivity
  within Niger by facilitating the safe movement of people and goods on one of the key lifeline road axis, namely
  the RN No11 connecting the southern part of the country with the northern regions.

  16.    Sub-component 1.1: Reconstruction and maintenance of Zinder - Agadez section (about US$128.2
  million, of which US$ 128 million IDA and US$ 0.2 million GoN funding). This sub-component will include as
  main activities (i) the rehabilitation of the 226 km road section between Tanout and Tiguidit; and (ii) the
  performance-based management and maintenance of the 425 km road section between Zinder and Agadez
  These activities are combined for execution by the same contractors under long-term (8 years) Output- and
  Performance-based Road Contracts.

  17.     Subcomponent 1.2: Road safety improvement program along the Zinder - Agadez road section (about
  US$5 million, 100% IDA). This subcomponent will build the capacity of ANISER and will improve the road safety
  conditions of the Zinder - Agadez section based on best international “safe system approach�?. The project will be
  financing holistic activities comprising of safe road infrastructure, improvement of post-crash response to save
  lives, dissuasive and awareness-raising measures, and improving accident data collection.

  18.     Component 2: Improved accessibility and community infrastructure along National Road No11 (about
  US$32 million, 100% IDA). The component will strengthen the climate and economic resilience of populations
  living along Zinder - Agadez section of the RN No11 by financing approximately 100 km of rural access roads,
  small community infrastructure along the area of influence of the project, and capacity building activities for
  strengthening the knowledge and skills of women. Activities to be financed will be selected in close consultation
  with the local communities and other projects under implementation in the region.

  19.   Component 3: Project management and implementation support (about US$10 million, 100% IDA). This
  component will finance overall project management and coordination of activities, activities designed to prevent
  and mitigate SEA/SH, and violence against children (VAC) risks linked to project civil works sites, as well as the
  assessment and management of human security risks (including human trafficking).

  20.    Subcomponent 3.1: Project management (about US$6 million, 100% IDA). Activities to be financed
  include operational costs of the Project Coordination Unit (PCU) staff, pilot smart monitoring approaches
  leveraging digital technologies to facilitate the implementation of project activities.

  21.     Subcomponent 3.2: Monitoring of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), sexual harassment (SH), and
  risks of human trafficking (about US$4 million, 100% IDA). This subcomponent will finance activities designed

  Summary of Assessment of Environmental and Social Risks and Impacts
  .

  23.     Environmental Risk Rating is substantial. This project will support the rehabilitation of a 226 km road
  segment from Tanout to Tiguidit and maintenance of the longer Zinder-Agadez Road Corridor (425 km) that
  includes this section. The road corridor linking Agadez to Zinder is a crucial lifeline for the delivery of services and
  the movement of people and goods. Over the years, however, the condition of the road has been negatively
  impacted by extreme temperatures, droughts, high winds and sandstorms. The project aims to make this road
  section more climate resilient for year-round travel which will provide numerous benefits at the local, regional
  and national levels. However, the planned civil works may generate some limited adverse environmental risks
  and impacts that will need to be managed in order to comply with the Environmental and Social Framework
  (ESF). These risks and potential negative impacts are related to: (i) Resource efficiency and pollution prevention
  and management (sourcing of raw materials for civil works, water and energy use, GHG emissions, dust, noise,
  potential contamination of water sources due to spills and run-off of petroleum products, infiltration into storage
  areas and improper disposal of fuels, solid and liquid waste management mainly due to management and
  disposal of construction and hazards waste; (ii) Occupational and community health and safety; (iii) Potential loss
  of vegetation and biodiversity during civil works/construction and preparation of sites, quarry areas for materials
  and higher demand of fuelwood by workers; and (iv) Potential impact of civil works and excavations on previously
  unknown cultural heritage.

  24.    Social Risk Rating is high. The social risk of the project is rated High. This section of the road is in a fairly
  remote part of the country and is part of a very complicated corridor that both contributes to improving
  connectivity between Nigeria and northern Niger but is also used for a significant amount of human, drug and
  arms trafficking. It is a major transportation route for long haul freight trucking that provides important goods
  and access to markets for the population along the road but an improved road may also contribute to an increase
  in SEA/SH, banditry, illegal smuggling and security threats to the local communities. In addition, the rehabilitation
  of the Tanout-Tiguidit road section is likely to generate some economic displacement (no physical resettlement
  is expected). As the road is in a difficult to access area of the country, the adequate management of these risks
  and their mitigation will be difficult and this may contribute to some social conflict and/or loss of support for the

      E. Implementation

      Institutional and Implementation Arrangements

      25.     The Ministry of Equipment will host the Project and be the implementing entity (IE). This will allow them
      to build on their extensive experience with the preparation and implementation of World Bank financed projects
      including the most recent, the Rural Mobility and Connectivity Project (PMRC; US$50 million).

      26.    Project Steering Committee (PSC). A PSC, to be established by decree, will be chaired by the Minister of
      Equipment or his representative and will include, among others, representatives of the Ministry of Planning,
      Ministry of Agriculture, Ministry of Transport, Ministry of Finance and Ministry in charge of Security. The PSC will
      ensure that throughout the life of the project, there is an adequate strategic direction, overall oversight, and
      coordination with other line ministries. The committee will also validate the yearly budgeted annual work plan
      of the project.

      27.    Project Technical Committee (PTC). A PTC, to be established by ministerial act, will be chaired by the
      general secretary of the MoE and will include, among others, designated technical experts representing the
      Directorate of major civil works, ANISER, the Directorate of road transport services, the General Directorate of
      Rural Engineering, the General Directorate of Agriculture and the Directorate of livestock development. The PTC
      will be responsible for the project technical oversight, the preparation of annual budgeted work plans and
      ensuring that the project meets its development objectives.

      28.     Project coordination Unit (PCU). The PCU, currently managing the implementation of the Niger Rural
      Mobility and Connectivity (PMRC), will coordinate the day-to-day project activities among stakeholders;
      fiduciary, social, and environmental safeguards aspects; and project monitoring and evaluation. A gender-based
      violence expert will be recruited to reinforce the capacity of the PMRC-PCU. The PMRC - PCU will also recruit an
      NGO specialized in GBV protection measures as part of the strategy to mitigate that specific risk in the context
      of this project.
