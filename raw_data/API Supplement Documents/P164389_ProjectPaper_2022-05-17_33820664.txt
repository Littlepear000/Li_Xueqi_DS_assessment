    I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING


A. Background and Project Status

      1.  The Water Security and Resilience for the Valley of Mexico Project (PROSEGHIR) (P164389) was approved by the
      World Bank Board of Executive Directors on February 27, 2020 with an International Bank for Reconstruction and
      Development (IBRD) loan of US$120 million (Loan No. 9049-MX). The Project was signed on February 28, 2022, after
      a retroactive extension of the signing deadline was approved on an exceptional basis on January 27, 2022, and has an
      expected closing date on December 31, 2025.

      2. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to improve the reliability of the Cutzamala System and strengthen
      the management of groundwater resources in the Valley of Mexico.1 Original activities financed under the Project
      are geared towards: (i) improving the energy efficiency and resilience of the Cutzamala System; (ii) strengthening the
      information systems for water management (including hydrometeorological and climate network, water quality, and
      groundwater monitoring); and (iii) supporting the implementation of infrastructure to improve the reliability of the
      Cutzamala System and groundwater recharge pilots. The Project objectives remain highly relevant to the priorities of
      Mexico as outlined in the 2019-2024 National Development Plan and in the World Bank Country Partnership
      Framework FY20-FY25 discussed by the Board of Executive Directors on February 27, 2020, namely Objective 6, to
      provide more inclusive and sustainable infrastructure services.

      3.  Progress towards achievement of PDO and implementation progress (IP) ratings were Unsatisfactory in the last
      Implementation Status and Results Report (December 2, 2021) due to the signing delays that hampered project
      implementation. However, given the strategic importance of this operation with the Valley of Mexico being the most
      water stressed region in the country on which Mexico City’s water supply depends, in December 2021 the Government
      of Mexico reconfirmed the alignment of the Project with the government’s priorities and its interest to move forward
      with implementation. In coordination with the World Bank, the Government has prepared a work plan to accelerate
      project execution, including a project restructuring to incorporate some changes to the project institutional
      arrangements, components and costs, followed by project effectiveness and disbursement. Project ratings will be
      reassessed based on these milestones.


B. Rationale for Restructuring

      4. The proposed restructuring strengthens the Project’s alignment with the Government’s priorities in the water
      sector, particularly the need to focus on the Cutzamala System’s immediate needs to ensure a reliable delivery of
      bulk water to the Valley of Mexico. The Project design provides for key structural measures for bulk water supply,
      dam rehabilitation and energy efficiency, along with the strengthening of information, monitoring and control
      systems. Since Board approval, the Cutzamala System has faced a critical drought in 2020-2021, as well as operation
      and maintenance issues, that have shifted the project executing agencies’ – the National Water Commission (Comisión

   met, namely: (i) the PDO remains achievable; (ii) the Bank and the Borrower agree on actions that will be undertaken

   of the Contrato de Mandato (agreement between the Borrower and the financial agent), including the respective legal
   opinions. The Contrato de Mandato is at an advanced stage of development and is expected to be completed shortly
   after project restructuring.

II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

   6. The proposed changes to the Project are as follows: (i) revise project components and costs, mainly to reallocate
   resources originally destined to carrying out infrastructure works for managed aquifer recharge initiatives in the Valley
   of Mexico (Sub-component 2.2) to strengthening the reliability of the Cutzamala System (Sub-component 1.1); (ii)
   update the Results Framework, particularly by eliminating indicators pertaining to the implementation of Sub-
   component 2.2 that is eliminated; (iii) revise the disbursement estimates and implementation schedule; (iv) adjust the
   implementation arrangements to reflect the change of financial agent from the Wellness Bank (Banco del Bienestar)
   to Nacional Financiera (NAFIN); and (iv) update the technical analysis to incorporate aspects related to the revised
   components.

A. Components and Costs

   7. The scope of components 1 and 2 has been modified to reflect CONAGUA’s revised priorities. The following
   changes were introduced:

   (a) Sub-component 1.2. Improving energy efficiency and reliability of the system’s infrastructure. Due to the above-
       mentioned need to embark on an intensive rehabilitation and maintenance program, this sub-component’s
       amount is increased to address the current state of the Cutzamala System’s pumping stations, including
       replacement of motors, valves, as well as other electromechanical components. While these activities were
       included in the original scope of the Project, their respective associated costs have increased, due to the additional
       two years of wear and tear and operation without proper maintenance. The scope of this sub-component will be
       increased to include the replacement of transformers at each of the six pumping stations, a critical component to
       ensure that the System can function in a reliable fashion. Replacing these electromechanical components will not

B. Results Framework

    11. The Results Framework has been modified to reflect the elimination of Sub-component 2.2 from the project
    description. The following changes were introduced:
    (a) PDO-level indicator “One managed aquifer recharge pilot operationalized (Number)” is eliminated;
    (b) Intermediate-level indicators, “Construction of advanced wastewater treatment facilities to meet effluent
        standards for aquifer recharge (Number)” and “Engineering analysis and designs completed for advanced
        wastewater treatment facilities (Yes/No)”, linked to the design and construction of the originally planned aquifer
        recharge pilots are eliminated.

C. Disbursement Estimates and Implementation Schedule

    12. Disbursement estimates. Disbursement projections have been revised and can be found below in section IV
    “Detailed Change(s)”.

    13. Mid-Term Review. Originally envisaged by end 2022, the Project’s Mid-Term Review is planned for the second
    semester of 2023 to assess the progress over the course of the first year of implementation and ensure the continued
    efficient and effective execution of the Project, including proactively working with the client to make up for
    implementation delays.

D. Institutional Arrangements

    14. Change of the Project’s financial agent. The Wellness Bank (Banco del Bienestar) is replaced by Nacional
    Financiera (NAFIN) as the Project’s financial agent given the latter longstanding experience with World Bank financed
    projects supervising fiduciary aspects, including financial management and procurement. This change would not
    impact the Project’s overall fiduciary risk rating, which remains Moderate.

    15. Financial Management (FM) arrangements will not change as a result of this restructuring. With the change of
    the financial agent, NAFIN will undertake this role and manage the loan disbursement process, as well as provide FM
    support to CONAGUA, which will remain responsible for the overall Project’s FM arrangements, including staffing,
    budgeting, accounting and financial reporting, internal control and external audit. NAFIN, in replacement of the
    Wellness Bank, will manage the loan disbursement process, and CONAGUA will remain responsible for preparing
    withdrawal applications and statements of expenditures, to be submitted to the World Bank through NAFIN.

E. Economic and Financial Analysis

    16. The economic and financial analysis of the Project remains unchanged as benefits from the managed aquifer
    recharge pilot under Subcomponent 2.2 were not quantified at project appraisal. Project benefits will still accrue to
    individuals and to society in terms of strengthened public sector capacity to manage water resources and improved
    dam safety. The Project will continue to generate environment benefits such as reduced air pollution and reduced
    greenhouse gas emissions due to the energy efficiency focus of Subcomponent 1.2. In addition, as subcomponents 1.1
    and 2.1 remain unchanged, the strengthening and expansion of existing information systems will continue to provide
    CONAGUA staff and other stakeholders with the necessary tools to make informed decisions and to efficiently manage
    ground- and surface water. It was also determined that the cost increase under Subcomponent 1.2 does not impact
    the cost-effectiveness analysis conducted during preparation as part of a stress test of the Cutzamala System - using
    the Decision Tree Framework.3 These activities are indeed non-regret investments that would still need to be carried
    out in the case of selecting other solutions to improve water system yield. As a result, the selection of investments
    resulting from the associated optimization exercise searching for cost-effective solutions and taking other key
    performance indicators into account such as drought response, robustness, resilience and reliability, remains valid.

F. Technical Analysis

    17. The Resiliency and Water Security Plan for the Sub-Basins of the Cutzamala System is based on a sound technical
    diagnostic carried out with the World Bank’s assistance including stakeholder involvement and technical inputs from
    various research institutions in Mexico. The diagnostic followed a multidisciplinary approach including an evaluation
    of the hydrology, water uses and water balances of the basin, as well as the challenges of the existing infrastructure,
    including eight reservoir dams and pumping/conveyance systems. This Plan included several infrastructure
    improvement measures that were thoroughly modeled and prioritized using the Decision Tree framework, allowing
    for the prioritization of investments and critical maintenance needs to be identified. In line with this methodology,
    and in light of the recent technical discussions between the World Bank, CONAGUA and OCAVM, the replacement of
    the transformers in six pumping stations of the Cutzamala System has emerged as a key component to initiate an
    intensive rehabilitation and maintenance program and to improve reliability and energy efficiency in the Cutzamala
    System. In addition, technical specifications have been prepared for the acquisition of electro-mechanical equipment
    and the rehabilitation works of a number of the Cutzamala System dams, including for the replacement of the above-
    mentioned transformers. It is expected that the bidding process for these activities will start shortly after project
    effectiveness.

G. Social and Environmental Analysis

    18. The Project’s safeguards category will remain B under OP 4.01 on Environmental Assessment. The restructuring
    will not change the triggered safeguard policies nor increase related risks. The Project’s Environmental and Social
    Management Framework (ESMF) remains valid to properly address the associated risks and impacts. The ESMF will be
    updated to include the revised project description.

    19. No involuntary resettlement, land taking, or economic displacement is expected due to implementation of the
    proposed expanded scope of activities under the Project. Electromechanical equipment replacement activities will
