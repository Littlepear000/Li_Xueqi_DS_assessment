1. OBJECTIVE


 1.1 Development Objective
   Original Development Objective (Approved as part of Approval package on 26-Mar-2020)
   Strengthen the statistical systems of participating countries and regional bodies in Africa to harmonize, produce,
   disseminate and enhance the use of core economic and social statistics.
   Has the Development Objective been changed since Board Approval of the Project Objective?

   No

  This Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) is based on face-to-face and virtual implementatin support missions
  in all participating countries and regional organizations.

  1. Burkina Faso: The project has met all development objectives and intermediate targets. Overall, the project
  successfully strengthened data collection, analysis, and dissemination across multiple sectors, significantly boosting the
  country’s statistical capacity and international ranking. The national statistical system’s performance has been recognized
  internationally as the national system ranked 24th globally and 2nd in Africa in the 2024 Open Data Inventory (ODIN),
  which measures the openness and coverage of official statistics. During the last six months, key achgievements met
  include :
      • Employment Survey: The 2024 employment survey was published, and data collection for early 2025 is complete.
          The next survey will use both phone and face-to-face methods, supported by an operational call center.
      • Living Standards & Business Census: Pilot and no standard unit surveys for EHCVM-3 are done. The General
          Business Census data has been collected and processed, with results expected by September 2025.
      • Macroeconomicss data: National accounts rebasing is finished, and a peer review is upcoming, though final
          synthesis and back-casting will continue beyond project close. Agricultural survey modules (arboriculture,
          fishing/aquaculture) have been processed and analyzed.
      • Price and economic indexes: Monthly price indices and Harmonized Index of Consumer Prices analytics are
          released regularly. Reports on trade, production, turnover, and inflation for early 2025 are available. Construction
          Cost Index has been processed and analyzed.
      • Education & Health statistics: Equipment for decentralized data collection in education has been distributed. A
          web mapping app for educational supply is live, and regional staff are trained. Health account data processing is
          completed, and hospital staff in several regions have been trained to enter monthly reports.
      • Environmental statistics: A methodology for non-timber forest products data has been developed, and a
          statistical dashboard is available.
      • Access to data: National data platforms are routinely updated and publicized through media and public events.
      • Institutional reform and human resources: Several key reports—including reviews of strategic and statistical
          master plans—have been finalized. Scholarships and software training for staff continue to strengthen capacity.

  2. Cabo Verde: The progress toward the achievement of the PDO and overall IP for Cabo Verde are maintained as
  Moderately Satisfactory. Four out of six PDO indicators achieved, eight our of eight intermediary indicators also
  achieved. However, the overall implementation of project activities in Cabo Verde remains limited. The disbursement
  rate stands at 78%, below the regional average of 85%, and the overall financial execution rate is 70%, leaving 30% of the
  total budget unexecuted with only six months remaining before the project’s scheduled closure. Key initiatives such as
  the cadastral registry in Santiago, the diaspora mapping—are progressing slowly, while the IDSR survey, the SIMPLEand
  the census of human resources in public administration are experiencing delays. Access to microdata from surveys
  remains a significant challenge. Although a large portion of survey microdata has been anonymized with HISWA project
  funding, it remains inaccessible. Despite the challenges, there has been encouraging progress in several areas:
      • The first round of integrated agricultural surveys has been completed, and the second round is underway.
      • Biannual IMC surveys are being conducted regularly, alongside annual surveys on nutrition, vulnerability, and
          food insecurity.
      • The enterprise census has been completed.
      • The land cadastral registry in São Vicente was finalized, and in March 2025, Mindelo was officially certified as a
          “Cadastral Registered Area.”

  4. Ghana: The project has performed well since its inception and is on track to achieve all its development objectives
  (PDO-Level) and intermediate indicator targets. Ghana has achieved six out of the seven PDO indicators. The project has
  been instrumental in helping push the boundaries of what is possible with data and has been a cornerstone for the role of
  statistics in shipping public policies and driving inclusive development in Ghana. Indeed, the project has helped improve
  the availability and quality of core statistics by strengthening coordination across Ministries, Departments, and Agencies.
  It has also helped modernize data systems. Furthermore, with more time disaggregated and access to geospatial data,
  there is more active use of data in decision making from central government to a district level. However, in 2025, the
  project faced some challenges. The project has seen delays in execution of the work plan. Consequently, the
  implementation rate of the 2025 AWP remains low at the end of June 2025. However, accelered implementation of the
  annual work plan is expected between now and Decemcer 2025.

  5. Liberia: The progress toward the achievement of the PDO for Liberia remains satisfactory. Five out of six PDO
  indicators have been completed. The last indicator (Statistical Capacity Index) is slightly below target (62.2, compared to
  the target of 65), but will be achieved once the HIES is completed. Project Implementation of Harmonization, Data
  Collection, Quality Improvement, Dissemination Components: Household-Based Surveys: Population and housing census
  is complete; household living standards survey data collection runs from April 2025 to March 2026. Data cleaning,
  analysis, and dissemination will follow, finishing nine months after data collection ends.
       • Economic Statistics: Most activities are finished, including the business establishment census, agricultural
           surveys, and the producer price index. GDP estimates now use the 2008 SNA with a recent base year. Integrating
           ICBT and crop survey data into national accounts remains.
       • Consumer Price Index (CPI): CPI data collection is ongoing, with urban coverage expanded and rural expansion
           pending. The transition to digital data collection has begun, and CPI is published monthly.
       • Administrative Data Improvement: Activities completed, resulting in a comprehensive statistical yearbook
           covering agriculture, education, health, and sanitation.

  6. Sierra Leone: The progress toward the achievement of the PDO and IP ratings for Sierra Leone remain Satisfactory.
  The project’s PDO and Implementation Progress ratings remain satisfactory. Five out of Six PDO indicators are fully
  completed. The primary PDO-level indicator—strengthening Sierra Leone’s statistical system—has been met, with the
  Statistical Capacity Index surpassing its target. Most indicators for The second PDO-level indicator —producing and
  disseminating harmonized statistics—have been achieved, though completion of the Sierra Leone Integrated Household
  Survey is still required to fully meet the target. The final indicator, focused on enhancing the use of statistics, has been
  fully accomplished.
  Significant progress has been made in implementing the following components of the Harmonization, Data Collection,
  Quality Improvement, Dissemination and Use of Core Social and Economic Statistics project:
       • Household-based surveys: Preparatory work, including technical documentation, equipment procurement and
           training, was completed in 2024. Data collection for the SLIHS using CAPI began in January 2025 and is ongoing,
           with 10 out of 24 cycles completed and the remainder scheduled. The non-CAPI survey runs monthly for 12
           months. Data cleaning, report writing and dissemination will continue into 2026.
       • Economic Statistics: Nearly all activities are complete. Major surveys and data sources have been finalised and
           published. The System of National Accounts 2008 was adopted in 2024, with GDP rebased to 2018. Ongoing work
           includes regular updates to the national accounts.
       • Consumer Price Index Modernization: Significant progress has been made in this area, with a CPI manual having
           been developed and CAPI having been partially adopted for data collection. Full national coverage has been
           achieved, but the implementation of CAPI is pending additional financing. Both CAPI and PAPI are currently in
           use.
       • Administrative Data Sources: All planned activities have been completed, including the publication of the Annual
           Statistical Digest, which covers key sectors; improvements to education evaluation; SDG data collection; and the
           establishment of research sites. Additional funding is needed for further training and data collection.
       • Data accessibility and dissemination: This activity has been completed. The redesign of Stats SA websites,
           creation of national databases and development of user-friendly applications have made statistical data widely
           accessible online.

  The rehabilitation and expansion of the physical infrastructure at Stats SL HQ Progress on this component is currently
  assessed as satisfactory. The modernization efforts at Stats SL HQ are ongoing, with completion of all the works expected
  by November 2025.

  8. African Union:
  Harmonized Methodologies across Countries: Overall the proejct implementation is saftisfactory to achieve the PDO
  and IP. Under HISWA, the AU has made significant progress in promoting harmonized statistical standards to achieve the
  Project Development Objective.

  With support from the HISWA project, STATAFRIC has made significant progress in implementing the Strategy for the
  Harmonization of Statistics in Africa (SHaSA2). Key activities include advocating for SHaSA2 adoption across the continent;
  collaborating with Regional Economic Communities (RECs) to promote the use of standardized statistical practices;
  organizing training sessions on the updated features of National Strategies for the Development of Statistics (NSDS), in
  partnership with key stakeholders; monitoring member countries’ compliance with agreed standards; and engaging
  development partners to mobilize both financial and technical support.
      • African Statistical Coordination Committee (ASCC): A major milestone was the annual meeting of the African
          Statistical Coordination Committee (ASCC), which reaffirmed the commitment of African Union Member States to
          strengthening institutional coordination. At the December 2024 meeting held in Banjul, several strategic
          resolutions were adopted, including: mapping ongoing statistical initiatives across the continent; supporting the
          development of gender-sensitive NSDSs; promoting the transition from the 2008 System of National Accounts
          (SNA) to 2025 SNA, which was adopted in March 2025; and accelerating efforts toward ratifying and
          implementing the African Charter on Statistics.
      • Strengthening Coordination: In May 2025, the project facilitated a workshop hosted by the Arab Maghreb Union
          (AMU) to assess the alignment of AMU member countries’ National Strategies for the Development of Statistics
          (NSDSs) with regional and continental frameworks. This workshop served as a strategic platform to enhance
          statistical capacity in the region through dialogue, peer learning, and cross-country collaboration. Looking ahead,
          a regional training workshop is planned to engage civil society and national statistical offices (NSOs) in promoting
          greater use of data in policymaking.
      • Use of Harmonized Standards and Guidelines: The HISWA project continues to support STATAFRIC in advancing
          the development of harmonized methodologies and guidelines for statistical production across the continent.
          Recruitment is currently underway to hire consultants for several key tasks: developing standardized
          methodologies for agricultural and industrial statistics; updating the STATAFRIC website; creating a compliance
          framework for the Code of Conduct on data security and confidentiality; preparing a guidance note for
          establishing the African Fund for the Development of Statistics (AFDS); and supporting the production of major
