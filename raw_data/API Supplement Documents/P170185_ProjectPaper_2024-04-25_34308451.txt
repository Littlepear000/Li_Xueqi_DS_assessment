 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

1.       This Restructuring Paper seeks a 12-months extension of the Closing Date of the Serbia Accelerating Innovation and
Growth Entrepreneurship Project (IBRD 9029-YF) from September 30, 2024, to September 30, 2025. The Bank received a
request for additional financing and the extension of the closing date from the Ministry of Finance on March 23, 2023, and from
the Ministry of Science, Technological Development, and Innovation (NITRA) on August 28, 2023. The rationale for extending
the Closing Date is the need to secure uninterrupted implementation of project activities, whilst the second Additional Financing
is being prepared. Although the Government requested a 21-month extension, it is estimated based on the current state of
preparation progress that the approval of the Additional Financing and its ratification will be completed within one year.
Therefore, 12-month extension is proposed for the current IBRD Loan (IBRD 9029-YF).

2.    The Project is financed by the World Bank loan – the original loan (IBRD 9029-YF) of EUR 43 million approved in
December 2019, and the EU IPA 2019 Grant (TF C1389) for Additional Financing (AF) of EUR 26.88 million approved in June 2023.

3.      The Project’s development objective (PDO) is to improve (i) the relevance and excellence of scientific research; and (ii)
innovative entrepreneurship and access to finance for enterprise growth, as a way of contributing to Serbia’s growth and
competitiveness. It has three components: 1) Research Sector Reforms; 2) Enterprise Acceleration; and 3) Project
Implementation, Monitoring, Capacity Building.

 A. Implementation Status

4.       The project performance ratings for the prospect of achieving the PDO and Implementation Progress have been
continuously rated Satisfactory since February 2023. The Science Fund (SF) progresses as planned and previously identified
delayes in implementaion are being resolved. The ongoing SF programs and activities are progressing well. The latest
programs, PRISMA (30 projects) and Proof of Concept (52 projects) started implementation. The Diaspora program call
closed at the end of March 2024, while the AI collaboration vouchers program is in preparation. The SF remains pivotal
institution in increasing the amount and competitive nature of research funding in Serbia. Furthermore, the SF has simplified
many of its procedures, streamlined overall governance, programs, and long-term planning, and focused on further capacity
building of the SF team, in particular ethics, environment and social aspects. The Bank is considering the option of hiring a
third environmental and social expert on a short-term basis at the request of the SF in order to support the ES screening of
PRISMA projects. RDI reforms are progressing very well. Eighteen (18) R&D Institutes (four from social sciences and
humanities, and the remaining 14 from natural sciences) are taking an active part in the transformation process. Three (3)
RDI Networking Forums were organized by the firm engaged by the Bank to deliver R&D capacity building training. Topics
covered included 1) Research Data Management and Open Access, 2) Sales, Branding, Business Development and Market,
and 3) Basics for Horizon Europe Project Preparation. These were well attended and followed by networking among RDIs
participating in the transformation process. RDIs continue to work with their national and international consultants and
show excellent progress in achieving set milestones, with TT Offices capacity observed as the main challenge. The Enterprise
Acceleration program (Katapult), implemented by the Innovation Fund (IF) is advancing very well. The level of private
investments raised by early-stage firms in the first two cohorts of the Katapult acceleration program is well above
expectations, currently at $24 million in total, leveraged by $7.7 million of public funds disbursed from SAIGE grants. The
third cohort (K24) of 23 selected companies (out of 114 applications), participated in Residency Week, organized jointly by
the Innovation Fund and the World Bank during the March 2024 mission.


B. Disbursement

5.      As of April 2024, disbursement of the IBRD loan amount stands at EUR 29.2 million or 67.46 percent of the original
loan (EUR 43 million), while the Additional Finance from EU IPA 19 Grant has disbursed 56.2 percent (EUR 15.2 million).

6.      The current SAIGE Closing Date for the IBRD part of the Project (IBRD 9029-YF) is September 30, 2024, which does
not allow sufficient time to initiate some of the remaining activities and complete them within this timeframe. Most notably,
the Science Fund and Innovation Fund face limitations in preparing and announcing calls for their respective programs,
primarily as they cannot launch a public call for programs with the current end date, since this timeline is insufficient for
the selection process instituted by the SAIGE project. Therefore, this restructuring is meant to allow for continued
implementation and full disbursement in line with the current PDO.

7.       There were initial delays to the start of the project that this restructuring will address by extending the closing
date. These delays were caused by a) later than anticipated effectiveness, b) the COVID-19 pandemic, which particularly
impacted the Science Fund Diaspora Facility, and c) Science Fund institutional capacity constraints, which have now been
largely addressed.

8.       At the request of the Ministry of Finance, the Bank is preparing the second Additional Financing (EUR 25 million)
for scaling up of the project activities. The Additional Financing is expected to be approved by the Board in late fall of 2024
and it will take some more time until the AF is effective. A gap between the current Parent Project, IBRD loan, closing date
and the second AF causes operational issues for NITRA (the line Ministry), and project beneficiaries SF and the IF. The PIU
is unable to hire new staff and is facing pressures in retaining the office space and the current staff as they are not able to

offer employment to key experts and staff beyond the current loan closing date. The proposed extension would thus ensure
smooth transition and continuation in all activities and processes until the AF is fully operational.

9.       Fiduciary arrangements for the Project are Satisfactory. The financial project audit for FY23 has been received
timely and was found acceptable. No accounting issues and internal controls deficiencies were reported by the auditors in
the management recommendation letters. There are currently no overdue project audit reports and interim unaudited
financial statements. All legal covenants have been complied with. The project management performance is rated
Satisfactory, with a PIU that adequately monitors the project activities and implementation progress.

10.      The overall environmental and social risk of the project remains the same with moderate risk rating and
moderately satisfactory E&S performance rating, in accordance with the WB Environmental and Social Framework (ESF).
The environmental and social performance remains moderately satisfactory. While a slight improvement was made after
the engagement of a new E&S specialist last year, the PIU E&S team encountered challenges in meeting all the requirements
set during the last reporting period and outlined in the ESCP. The reporting required is delayed and needs to be structurally
and qualitatively improved, and corrective actions taken. Additionally, the Bank advised of improvements to be made,
specifically the improvement of E&S screening quality, and further engagement of the E&S PIU team on the ESMP checklists.
The time-consuming communication between the Bank and PIU E&S teams is further impeded by the strict deadlines of the
SF for publication of the final lists for grant recipients. To counter additional burden on the grant recipients regarding the
E&S, the roles of the PIU E&S specialists will be reorganized. A number of steps have been agreed to improve the
performance and bring it to the satisfactory rating. These include:
    a) PIU to take full responsibility for production of quality E&S instruments;
    b) PIU to fully and correctly address comments provided by the WB;
    c) The ESMF, ESCP and SEP need to be revised to address the environmental and social risks associated with the
         project's restructuring, particularly with regard to the increased focus on subprojects and research activities related
         to artificial intelligence (AI) and Bio4, and based on the latest version of Project Paper.
    d) The E&S screening of all future subprojects as well as the preparation of ESMP/ESMP checklists and progress
         reporting, to be carried out in compliance with the quality standards and requirements set forth by the World Bank.
    e) The WB team will select the subprojects under implementation to visit during the monitoring mission

11.      This proposed restructuring does not include any new activities or any other changes except the extension of the
closing date, and there are no additional environmental and social impacts or implications and as such, there are no changes
to the environmental category assigned, nor is there any change in the due diligence.

 II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

12.     The only change proposed under this restructuring is a 12-month extension, to allow completion of all activities
financed by Parent project and full disbursement of the original IBRD loan (IBRD 9029-YF) amount.

13.      Furthermore, this extended implementation period would provide continuity for the Project teams (PIU and the
SF/IF),, while the Bank considers and processes the requested Additional Financing mentioned above.
