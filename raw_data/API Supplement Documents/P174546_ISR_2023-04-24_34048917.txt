 MPA Table
 Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA) Status

 MPA Table Body
 The Emergency Locust Response Program (ELRP) is an emergency regional integration project under the multi-phase programmatic approach
 (MPA). The program provides funding across 3 technical components: Component 1 (Surveillance and Control Measures) to address the immediate
 threat posed by the upsurge; Component 2 (Livelihoods Protection and Rehabilitation) to safeguard food security of affected households and restore
 farmers and livestock herder to productivity in the wake of an invasion; and Component 3 (Coordination and Early Warning Preparedness) to
 strengthen national systems and regional cooperation to address future threats.

 The ELRP-MPA program, with its Phase 1 first-moving countries, was approved by the World Bank's Board of Executive Directors on May 20, 2020,
 with an overall IDA financing envelope of US$500 million equivalent. Overall, ELRP MPA has committed US$375.7M equivalent to 5 countries and
 one regional organization. Across all 3 phases disbursement is 73.5% (US$276.2M). PHASE 1 has committed US$207 million equivalent in grants
 and credits to Djibouti, Ethiopia, Kenya. A minor restructuring of Phase 1 for the Ethiopia project was completed in December 2021. An additional
 finance (AF) of US$35 million equivalent to Kenya was approved in March 2022. An AF of US$60 million equivalent to Ethiopia was approved in
 June 2022. A level 2 restructuring of Djibouti to adjust some activities and reallocate funds across expenditure categories was completed in January
 2023. Phase 1 is currently 61.1% disbursed. PHASE 2 was approved on June 29, 2020, providing a US$40 million equivalent grant to Somalia. An
 AF US$75.0 million was approved in June 2021. The Phase is 93.7% disbursed, and it is undergoing a Level 2 restructuring to program saved funds

 RESULTS

 The desert locust upsurge ended by March 2022. ELRP’s has financed surveillance on over 10 million hectares of land, and this activity
 continues, especially in Ethiopia, which has known breeding areas. It also financed locust control (ground and aerial) on over 530,000 hectares
 across Djibouti, Ethiopia, and Kenya, but treatment has all but stopped as the upsurge did.

 Thus far, 285,491 households have benefitted from direct income support in Somalia, Djibouti, and South Sudan. This is equivalent to about
 1.65M beneficiaries using 6 people/HH for Somalia and a conservative estimate of 4 people/HH in Djibouti and South Sudan. In Somalia, which only
 includes a safety net, 86 percent of beneficiary households are self-reporting improved food consumption and 78 percent are self-reporting protection
 of their productive assets, and 72 percent of the cash transfer beneficiaries are women. To resume production, ELRP has reached over 167,000
 farmers and 113,000 pastoralists with inputs and emergency feed. In Kenya, the project has approved 30 county-level subprojects to provide
 emergency livestock feed, water, and vaccines and soil and land management investments. In addition, 3000 micro projects providing input support
 to common interest groups (CIG) of 15 to 20 farmers have been approved, of which nearly 2000 are already funded. Taking the conservative
 estimate of 15 farmers/CIG, 30,000 farmers are already benefitting from the funds.

 Investments in national preparedness are underway and include technology, training, and strategic partnerships to improve the ability of
 countries monitor, forecast, and provide early warning of a desert locust threat. Djibouti and Ethiopia are already part of the FAO/Desert Locust
 Information System (DLIS) and are investing in the survey/data information collection tools to fully engage in ongoing surveillance. Much of the
 training and technology can be applied to surveillance against other transboundary pests, such as fall armyworm, quelea bird, and other invasive
 pests. Kenya has completed its National Migratory Pests Management Plan and Ethiopia has completed an assessment of their DL response
 system, which the government has adopted. Also in Ethiopia, the Government collaborated with the Desert Locust Control Organization of Eastern
 Africa to field test Green Muscle biopesticide against nymphs and hopper bands in Afar region. The successful trial has convinced the Government
 agreeing to register it for ongoing use. This will have significant positive implications for human and environmental health moving forward. In Djibouti,
 Ethiopia, and South Sudan, the project is investing in safe pesticide storage facilities. The activities by countries under component 3 link to
 transboundary pest platform managed by IGAD.

 The Transboundary Pest Platform is up and running with a Steering Committee and two active working groups: capacity building and
 early warning systems (EWS). At the request of IGAD, the ELRP and FSRP teams are exploring collaboration on EWS to ensure the common
 data gathering needs follow agreed methodologies so teams can share information and warnings more readily. Formal memoranda of understanding
 with DLCO-EA and Centre for Agriculture and Bioscience International (CABI) to share information and collaborate on EWS and pest control
 technology. Additional partners include the UN Food & Agriculture Organization (FAO), International Centre of Insect Physiology and Ecology
 (ICIPE), and bilateral donors. IFAD has recently expressed interest in collaborating with Platform as well, and the Government of Madagascar has
 requested to participate in meetings and knowledge sharing where possible.

 The IGAD/ICPAC platform team has been working with the European Space Agency (ESA) on 2 fronts: (a) using remote sensing to assess damage
 from desert locust; and (b) for damage assessment and to predict the potential shift in breeding sites in face of climate change. Preliminary work has
 been completed and a second ESA “use case” is now starting to scale up the predictive capacity based on numerous data sets. This is innovative
 work that we envision to be scalable to apply to other pests and even plant disease, thus providing more advanced warning to governments.
