 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The Program became fully effective in January 2023 and maintains its overall implementation progress as Moderately Satisfactory. The
  Implementation Support Mission (ISM) held in September 2023 confirmed implementation progress for several Disbursement Linked Results
  (DLRs) but noted substantive delays in implementation of project activities among certain Implementing Agencies. Progress by Borrower is as
  follows:
          The Kenya component has made commendable progress in completing six DLRs due in the first year that are due for disbursement after
           verification. These include the release of the poverty and labor estimates from the 2021 Kenya Continuous Household Survey, the
           publication of the 2022 Kenya Demographic and Health Survey and the 2023 Gross County Product estimates. However, the Program
           continues to face a funding shortfall, that has affected the activities KNBS is able to undertake during this financial year.
          The Tanzania component has made progress in several results areas but overall Program implementation remains delayed. The workplan
           for the current financial year was updated to reflect the new timelines for completing activities due in the first two years of the Program and
           would need close monitoring to ensure timely achievement of results. Key items in the Program Action Plan relating to environmental and
           social safeguards, financial management, procurement and fraud and corruption controls are being closely monitored to ensure timely
           implementation and support to other Program activities.
          The Rwanda component is on track, with all results for Year 2 of the Program expected to be achieved on time. Several actions in the
           Program Action Plan (PAP) are delayed but these delays are not affecting implementation progress and progress towards the PDO. The
           NSO committed to making substantial progress on these actions by the next mission in February 2024.
          The EAC component has made progress on various milestones, supporting the regional harmonization of key statistical indicators and
           developing guidelines. However, there have been significant setbacks in the procurement processes due to delays in hiring core project
           consultants, further exacerbated by the departure of the project’s Procurement Specialist. The procurement delays pose a high risk to the
           project’s implementation.
          The AUC component is delayed. The AUC component became effective on November 4, 2022, but progress since has been slow. Delays
           are mainly due to the following: (i) STATAFRIC did not get clearance from AUC management on recruitment of PIU consultants due to the
           tension between AUC's own rules and regulations, which say that consultants can only be hired for a period of 6 months renewable only
           once, and the WB procurement regulations on hiring longer term consultants for PIUs. This has by now been resolved and the ToRs for
           PIU staff will shortly be advertised; (ii) STATAFRIC is understaffed despite managing several WB-financed projects.
