I. PROGRAM STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

          A. PROGRAM STATUS
Summary.

1. This Project Paper seeks the approval of a restructuring of the Senegal Agriculture and Livestock Competitiveness
   Program for Results (PCAE) (P164967, Cr.6616) at the request of the Government of Senegal (GoS) dated July 26, 2022,
   to accommodate US$20 million as co-financing from the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD).

2. The Program Development Objective (PrDO) is to enhance productivity and market access of priority commodity value
   chains and livestock, in the extended groundnut basin and agro-pastoral areas. The proposed IFAD co-financing is
   solely intended for two purposes: (i) to expand the scope of some facets of the ongoing Program, within the same
   three results areas and under the same objectives; and (ii) closing the emerging incentive gaps with the potential to
   undermine achievement of some Disbursement-Linked Indicators (DLIs). There will therefore be no change to the
   design of the Program, PrDO, Program Action Plan (PAP), or implementation arrangements. The targets (disbursement
   linked results) and amounts allocated under select DLIs will be topped up to reflect an expanded Program scope or
   augmented incentives for attainment of disbursement linked results agreed at appraisal through increased financing.
   The results framework will also be revised.

Implementation status.

3. The Program became effective on September 2, 2020. The Government had used its own resources to carry out the
   planned activities under the Program, even before the initial requested advance was granted in April 2021 and
   transferred by GoS to the Program’s account in December 2021. Implementation is on track and long pending issues
   such as the recruitment of the Independent Verification Agent (IVA) and the authorization of credits at the level of the
   Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Equipment (MAER) and the Ministry of Livestock and Animal Production (MEPA) have
   all been resolved. However, there is still need for allocating an adequate budget in line with the Program expenditure
   framework as it has been so far difficult for both ministries to receive from the Government the full necessary
   budgetary allocations to achieve the objectives of the Program. Steps are being taken to resolve this issue in a timely
   manner.

4. Significant progress has been achieved and IDA made a first disbursement further to the achievement of a first set of
   disbursement linked results (DLRs). The disbursement rate stands at 33 percent as of July 28, 2022. As per the April
   2022 implementation support mission, all key performance ratings are satisfactory, and the fiduciary risk is substantial.
   Steps are being taken to lower this risk such as strengthening the management system of the Program fund.

          B. RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING
5. In agreement with the World Bank and GoS, IFAD co-financing will enhance diversification and inclusiveness of the
   Program, in line with IFAD’s core mandate, and core commitments, namely: diversification of smallholder farming
   systems, climate change resilience, empowerment of women and youth, and enhancement of food and nutrition
   security. Further, scaling up the expected outcomes and outputs of PCAE is timely both in the context of the impact
   of the Ukraine crisis on food security, as well as the increasing threats of climate change on the livelihoods of the rural
   poor in Senegal.

6. Given the size and outreach of the Program, joining forces in supporting sustainable rural livelihoods, food security
   and nutrition in Senegal is a strategic investment for both IFAD and the World Bank (WB), especially now that the
   country is moving out of classical project implementation approach towards national programs, and has adopted a
   budget program approach since January 2020 for all sectoral ministries. In addition, participating in this PforR allows
   scaling-up IFAD’s experience in the region towards strengthening the resilience of smallholder farms by reinforcing
   diversification of their farming and value addition systems, not to mention improving their household food and
   nutrition security, as well as securing employment and revenues.

7. To mitigate risks related to the weak institutional capacity of the Program implementing entities technical assistance
   (TA) and institutional support were considered essential and built into the original Program design. In 2020, IFAD
   already committed US$ 3.5 million parallel financing under its ongoing Agriculture Development and Rural Enterprise
   Support Program, Phase II (PADAER-II), to support the Program’s Technical Coordination Teams (TCTs) and additional
   TA as required. This support was critical during the Program start-up. In May 2021, IFAD also approved an additional
   amount equivalent to US$ 3 million to be mobilized as parallel investment project lending, essentially to strengthen
   institutional support and capacity building activities and investments in MAER and MEPA.

8. A Level-2 restructuring will allow to integrate IFAD’s further contribution to the main Program, with minor changes
   related to the allocation of funding to the DLRs, the annual disbursement estimates, and the Results Framework. The
   baseline of the vaccination rate against Pest of Small Ruminants will also be corrected in light of new information.


 II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

               The following changes are proposed:

           A. SCOPE AND COSTING OF DLIS
     While IDA will continue disbursing against results achieved under all ten (10) DLIs, the IFAD co-financing will only be
     allocated to results achieved under four (4) DLIs (#2, #4, #7, and #10).

i.   Results Area 1 (Improved Crop and Livestock Productivity and Resilience): Under this Results Area, additional
     resources from the IFAD financing are allocated to DLI2 and DLI4 to reinforce expected results on diversification and
     productivity increase in crop and livestock systems.

         (a)     Under DLI2 (Cumulative quantity of certified seeds acquired for diversification crops), IFAD will provide $6
                 million as a further incentive for the GoS to reach the earlier agreed target of 53,000 tons that had been
                 envisaged with the IDA financing.

         (b)     For DLI4 -- Vaccination coverage rate for small ruminant plague (PPR) (percentage coverage), the amount
                 allocated to this DLI will increase by IFAD’s contribution of $7 million to give more incentives for
                 vaccination and fight against PPR along with other priority diseases. While the target of 80 percent of
                 vaccination coverage has been maintained, the percentage increase point for disbursement has increased
                 from 10 percent to 12.5 percent of vaccinated ruminants.

ii. Under Results Area 2 (Improved Business Environment and Market Integration), DLI7 - Number of producers with
    access to agriculture insurance for crops and livestock (per year) has been prioritized. For DLR#7.2, the target number
    for producers with access to insurance to be achieved prior to disbursement of the IDA funds, has increased from
    400,000 to 475,000 farmers and herders, and the total amount allocate increased by IFAD’s contribution in an amount
    of US$ 5 million.

iii. Under Results Area 3 (Improved Governance Sector Coordination and Program Management), DLI10 will be co-
     financed. IFAD support will be used to further digitalize and decentralize the MAER’s and MEPA’s monitoring and
     evaluation (M&E) system and promote policy dialogue and knowledge management, in particular the consolidation
     of inter-professions for diversification crops that were established under previous IFAD-funded projects. For the DLR
     on setting up a functional M&E, IFAD’s contribution of US$1 million will only add up to the IDA funds upon the
     achievement of the same targets. This addition of IFAD financing only increases the incentive to achieve the target.
     With respect to the restructuring of the diversification crop inter-professions of MAER and MEPA, only IFAD will be
     providing a financial incentive in an amount of US$1 million to this DLR.

          B. PROGRAM COSTS
10. The Program total cost is now US$230 million of which US$150 million from WB, US$20 million IFAD co-financing,
    and US$60 million Government contribution. In parallel, IFAD will provide US$3 million through PADAER II for
    institutional capacity building.

          C. RESULTS FRAMEWORK
11. To monitor the implementation of the Program as modified with the addition of IFAD financing, a few indicators have
    been added to the Results Framework: (i) yield of the diversification crops for both maize and millet; (ii) number of
    young farmers (age between 15-40) reached with agricultural assets or services; (iii) number of young farmers
    adopting improved agriculture technologies; (iv) vaccination coverage rates for African horse sickness and for
    Pasteurellosis of small ruminants ; and (iv) restructuring of interprofessions organization finalized. In addition, the
    quantitative target for DLI7 has been increased to absorb the additional funding provided by IFAD, and the baseline
    percentage rate of PPR vaccination coverage has been revised, based on actual figures - the initial baseline which was
    estimated at 30 percent has been corrected to 17.4 percent. One PrDO indicator (increase in dairy farms productivity)
    was removed due to attribution issues and complexity of measurement.

          D. SUPERVISION ARRANGEMENTS
12. The World Bank will be appointed as cooperating institution within the meaning of Article III of the General Conditions
    of Agricultural Development Financing dated 29 April 2009, as lastly amended in December 2020, under a letter of
    appointment (the “LoA”). Under the LoA terms, the WB will be formally responsible for supervising the PCAE,
    administering the loan and authorizing payments. The presence of the World Bank Office and IFAD Regional Hub in
    Dakar will facilitate close monitoring of progress and provision of implementation support.

          E. ECONOMIC AND FINANCIAL ANALYSIS
13. The appraisal Economic and Financial Analysis was updated to consider the IFAD co-financing of US$20 million, as well
    as some additional beneficiaries. The NPV of the Program would increase from US$336 million to US$373.5 million.
    The Economic Rate of Return (ERR) is expected to remain stable at 24.2 percent as the same type of activities are
    financed. Sensitivity analysis shows that the baseline results remain robust under most scenarios.
