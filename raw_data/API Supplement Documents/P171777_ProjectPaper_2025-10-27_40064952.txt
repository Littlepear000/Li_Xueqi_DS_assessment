 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING


Project Status

The GEDES Project has delivered notable progress across its core components, though uneven implementation and financing
constraints continue to slow momentum. On the statistics front, Djibouti published the results of its third Population and
Housing Census in June 2025, along with thematic analyses on women, disability, refugees, households, and localities. The third
National Strategy for the Development of Statistics (2025–2029) was launched in April, setting a demand-driven agenda for data
sharing and ICT-enabled integration. At the same time, the fifth household budget survey (EDAM5-IS) was completed, capturing
migrant and refugee households for the first time. Yet delays persist in macroeconomic modeling outputs, with no policy notes
or papers published by mid-2025.

One of the most significant implementation bottlenecks has been the construction of the INSTAD building. While bidding
documents have been finalized, progress has been repeatedly delayed due to limited government funding committed under the
terms of the Initial Financing Agreement (IDA Credit No. 6672-DJ, as amended on June 8, 2020) and the Additional Financing
Agreement between the Government and the Association dated April 5, 2024 (IDA Credit No. 7521-DJ). Following design
revisions, a financing gap of USD 2.9 million emerged, slowing implementation considerably despite the overall project cost
being reduced to USD 6.9 million.

Rationale for Extension: Extending the project closure date from December 31, 2025, to December 30, 2026, is therefore
warranted to secure these gains and complete pending activities. The extension will allow time to finalize the INSTAD building
procurement, operationalize macroeconomic models, publish overdue policy briefs, and embed the debt strategy within fiscal
planning. It will also provide space for continued training, donor coordination reforms, and impact evaluation rollouts.

On April 2, 2025, the authorities officially requested an extension of the project to allow sufficient time to complete the
construction of the INSTAD building. This commitment was reaffirmed in a follow-up correspondence dated July 7, 2025, in
which the Government pledged to mobilize USD 2 million through the CY26 and CY27 budget bills to help finance the building
and to finalize the enterprise census currently underway. Without such an extension, achievements in transparency, statistical
capacity, and fiscal reforms risk being only partially realized, limiting the project’s long-term development impact.


 II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

Based on the above, the proposed restructuring will extend the project closing date from December 31, 2025, to December
30, 2027. In parallel, the Financing Agreement will be amended to introduce a disbursement condition for the building
component, ensuring that the government’s commitment, outlined in its July 7, 2025, letter, to allocate USD 2 million through
the CY26 and CY27 budget laws is effectively secured.

In line with the World Bank’s letter of August 26, 2025, the Recipient was required either to deposit USD 2 million in a
designated account or to include equivalent allocations in the CY26 and CY27 budgets for the construction of the INSTAD
building. The authorities have chosen the latter, committing USD 1 million in CY26 and USD 1 million in CY27.

The Financing Agreement will be amended to reflect this arrangement. Before any IDA disbursement under the building
component, the Recipient must provide evidence that the initial USD 1 million in counterpart funding has been spent. Proof of
expenditure will consist of a debit notice issued by the Directorate General of the Treasury for USD 1 million in favor of the
building activities. This measure safeguards project resources, secures counterpart financing, and supports the timely
completion of the INSTAD building.
