 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The COVID-19 pandemic has put on ice several complex and large-scale projects that were considered during preparation. Nevertheless, TDB
  approved two sub-projects under the project and infrastructure finance facility (component 1) in infrastructure value chains.
  The off-grid SME facility (component 2) was formally launched in March 2021 by TDB, and TDB is ready to receive applications.
  The COMESA Secretariat is expected to finalise the recruitment of its project implementation team and launch consultancies to develop quality
  standards and a common tariff system for solar systems.

 Overall Comments PDO

  The two first movers projects have positive impacts that are not reflected in the indicators and that are cross-border.

  The Project in the cold chain infrastructure in Kenya will reduce the carbon footprint of food waste, by a factor of 10 compared to the emissions
  it is expected to generate (GHG emissions of 5,155 tCO2e p.a.). 94% of post-harvest losses in East Africa are due to inefficient cold chain
  infrastructure. If food losses and waste were to be concentrated as one country, such a country would be the world’s GHG third-largest emitter,
  only surpassed by China and the United States. In addition the project will support the vaccine rollout as 20% of the cold chain infrastructure is
  expected to be used to store vaccines. For land-locked East African Countries, the development of a large-scale cold chain infrastructure will
  assist a logistics network that operates from Mombasa port, through Kenya and into Uganda, Rwanda and Burundi. Thus, reducing their
  reliance on expensive and polluting air transport and promote international trade through imports and exports further afield.

  The project in the housing value chain in Rwanda will reduce the cost of import of housing material by 38% and is expected to help Rwanda
  save up to ~US$40 million annually in foreign exchange whilst generating ~US$4 million annually in export revenues. There will be a positive
  cross border impact with DRC as the project is expected to generate cheaper exports to DRC. The Project site is ~160 kilometers from Goma
  in eastern DRC, which is landlocked and relies on imports of ceramic tiles. ~10% of the project' products (up to 300,000m2) are forecast to be
  exported to DRC thereby, enhancing trade between the two countries. DRC is a landlocked country relying on imported ceramics which are
  more expensive.
