B. Introduction and Context

Country Context

   1. The conflict in Yemen has devastated the country’s economy, institutions, and population. Years of war have left
      Yemenis with few coping mechanisms, collapsing living standards, and extreme poverty now affecting nearly
      three-quarters of the population. The economy has fragmented into two zones—Houthi-controlled areas with
      most of the population and IRG-controlled areas holding oil and gas—deepening disparities and undermining core
      state functions like public finance, statistics, and civil service management. Attacks on ports, extractive industries,
      and Red Sea shipping routes have further eroded revenue, while fiscal collapse has forced the IRG into debt
      distress, relying heavily on grants to function. Moving forward, strengthening national statistics, rebuilding public
      financial management, improving customs and tax administration, and ensuring transparent payment of public
      workers’ salaries are critical for sustaining essential services, rebuilding trust in government, and laying the
      groundwork for eventual peace and economic recovery

Sectoral and Institutional Context

   2. The protracted conflict has deeply weakened Yemen’s statistical system and public finance management,
      undermining evidence-based policymaking and economic recovery. The Central Statistics Organization (CSO) has
      been split between Sana’a and Aden since 2019, leaving CSO-Aden with limited resources and capacity. Despite
      launching a roadmap in 2023 that included reactivating statistical units, initiating a household budget survey, and
      reviving the Statistical Training Center, CSO-Aden struggles to produce core national accounts. Reliable data
      collection has not occurred since 2014, forcing the IMF and World Bank to provide partial estimates. Developing
      a new system of national accounts (SNA) is essential, as it would strengthen macroeconomic planning, resource
      allocation, and transparency while boosting donor confidence and enabling effective recovery strategies.
   3. At the same time, Yemen’s public financial management (PFM) and human resource management (HRM) systems
      remain fragmented and weak. The IRG continues to rely on pre-conflict budget frameworks, with extra-budgetary
      transactions and poor cash management fueling arrears and debt. Payroll systems are incomplete, relying on pre-
      conflict data as a foundation, and despite some progress in rebuilding the Unified Payroll System (UPS), gaps
      remain in accuracy and coverage. Payment delays and arrears have further strained public trust, while salary
      payments remain a central issue in the UN-led peace negotiations. Strengthening PFM and HRM through a
      pragmatic roadmap, development of a fit-for-purpose FMIS/HRMIS, wage bill management reforms, and better

 Relationship to CPF
   4. The project aligns with the World Bank Group’s Yemen Country Engagement Note (2022–2023), the Strategy for
        Fragility, Conflict, and Violence (2020–2025), IDA21’s Strategic Directions, and the IRG’s own development plans
        with the AMF and IMF, aiming to maintain engagement during conflict, safeguard critical institutions, and prepare
        for recovery. It focuses on strengthening IRG capacity for core functions—particularly official statistics, evidence-
        based policymaking, and public financial management including payroll—while modernizing foundational systems
        to promote fiscal stability, improve service delivery, and foster conditions for private sector growth and
        investment.

 D. Concept Description

     5. The project seeks to modernize Yemen’s core state functions to strengthen public institutions, improve service
        delivery, and support economic recovery. It will focus on building foundations for effective public resource
        management through the development of the system of national accounts, stronger public financial
        management (PFM), human resource and payroll systems, and improved revenue mobilization. Using a
        modular, fit-for-context approach, the project will enhance institutional capacity, core processes, IT systems,
        and infrastructure, while allowing flexibility to adapt if a political settlement occurs. It emphasizes innovative,
        locally adapted solutions to “leapfrog�? gaps and create scalable systems. The main components are: (i)
        strengthening Yemen’s statistical system and national accounts, (ii) reinforcing PFM and HRM/payroll, and (iii)
        project management and coordination, with the potential addition of customs reform during project
        implementation, informed by ongoing analytical work:


Component 1: Strengthening Yemen’s Statistical System and System of National Accounts (US$8.5 million)

 Summary of Screening of Environmental and Social Risks and Impacts



   6. The overall environmental and social risks and impacts of this project are rated as moderate, based on the nature
      of the activities and interventions planned under each component. Key potential social risks include exclusion and
      elite capture, which could restrict access to training, systems, and services for female staff; risks of sexual
      exploitation, abuse, and harassment (SEA/SH) during system installation and training for beneficiary entities such
      as CSO, MoF, and MoFI, particularly involving interactions between firm personnel and female staff or users;
      concerns regarding data privacy and trust due to possible misuse or breaches of personal data in HR/payment
      registry linkages and statistical surveys; labor and working condition risks, such as misconduct and the lack of
      effective worker grievance mechanisms; and security and access constraints typical of fragile, conflict, and
      violence (FCV) contexts like Yemen, which may pose risks to firms and beneficiaries and limit supervision and
      implementation. To address these risks, the project will implement an inclusive Stakeholder Engagement Plan
      (SEP) featuring targeted outreach, transparent eligibility and verification processes, dual reviews, audit trails,
      independent spot checks, and a dedicated grievance track with time-bound appeals. An SEA/SH Action Plan will
      be adopted and enforced, including mandatory Codes of Conduct, survivor-centered and confidential grievance
      mechanisms, trained focal points, service referral mapping, and vetting and training for security personnel and
      frontline providers. Data protection will be ensured through a formal policy and privacy-by-design measures such
      as encryption, secure hosting, access controls, role-based permissions, and data minimization, alongside clear
      data-sharing protocols, consent procedures, regular audits, and incident response drills. Labor Management
      Procedures (LMPs) will be required, including Codes of Conduct, induction and periodic training, confidential
      worker grievance mechanisms, and regular reporting on grievances and resolutions. A Security Management Plan
