 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Overall progress toward achieving the Project Development Objective (PDO) and the overall implementation progress were assessed as
  Moderately Satisfactory. This is mainly due to delays caused by the continued restrictions associated with the COVID-19 pandemic.
  Component 1: PFM IT Systems Roll-Out, has progressed well. Implementation of the IFMIS technical assessment recommendations is on track.
  There was a setback in the implementation of Component 2: Accounting and Financial Reporting, due to a delay in hiring a consulting firm for expert
  advice on the implementation of the International Public Sector Accounting Standards (IPSAS). Despite this, significant progress was made in:(i)
  finalizing the consolidation module in IFMIS; (ii) training of preparers of financial statements; (iii) consolidation of district hospitals in districts
  financial statements; (iv) sensitization of the Office of Auditor General’s team on new accounting policies implemented during the fiscal year 2020-
  21; (v) preparation of inventory recording requirements by the IPSAS team; and (vi) assets identification.

  Under Component 3: Performance-Based Budgeting (PBB) and Medium-Term Budgeting, the National Budget Department has successfully
  undertaken a full rollout of PBB in the FY2021/22 budget. The published budget has an annex on PBB. A citizen’s guide to the budget for
  FY2021/22 has been published and, each year improvements are made to the content.
  Component 4: Professionalization of PFM Staff - PFM Capacity Development, is the most affected by the COVID-19 related restrictions. While
  targeted results have largely been achieved in terms of the number of public finance staff with a level of professional qualification and the number of
  public finance officials who received training or are in training under the project, the achievement of future results is at risk. The mission team noted
  the downward trajectory in the number of qualifications achieved in recent years. A number of factors were reported to contribute to the poor
  performance including: (a) due to COVID 19, in-person learning was suspended and with restrictions in travel, both students and lecturers could not
  access facilities to collect learning material; (b) tuition service providers were not fully prepared for the rapid shift to online learning; (c) slow uptake
  and embracing of online learning by students (e.g. rapid increase in the numbers of students with zero attendance); and (e) challenges in internet
  connectivity to facilitate teaching and learning. It was also noted that there may be insufficient incentives (e.g. financial gain, career progression) for
  public finance officials to want to pursue these qualifications.
