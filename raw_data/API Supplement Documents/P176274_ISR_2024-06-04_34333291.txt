4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
       1. The implementation of the i-Compete Project is deemed as moderately satisfactory.
       2. Initial delays in the Project's commencement have impacted the results framework, as none of the high-impact
          technical activities have been initiated yet.
       3. The Project Implementation Unit (PIU) is fully staffed for the main positions, and the preparation of activities is
          advancing smoothly. Following extensive technical efforts, agreements with nine partner agencies for activity
          implementation have been approved by the World Bank. The signing of these agreements by the PIU and the
          partners took place on May 17, 2024 and is expected to expedite disbursements. These agreements describe the
          partners' contributions to the Project's goals and the resources and services required to fulfill the associated
          deliverables throughout the Project's lifespan, in accordance with World Bank procedures.
       4. The framework agreements that have received no objection are with the following institutions: (i) The Joint
          Consultation Framework for Business Improvement (CMCAA); (ii) The National Investment Commission (CNI); (iii)
          The Ministry of Small and Medium Enterprises and Private Sector Promotion (MPMEPSP) ; (iv) The
          Interprofessional Group of the Central African Republic (GICA); (v) The National Union of Central African
          Employers (UNPC); (vi) The Centre for Assistance to Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises and Handicrafts
          (CAPMEA); (vii) The Chamber of Commerce, Industry, Mines and Handicrafts of the Central African Republic
          (CCIMA); (viii) The Global Platform of Women Entrepreneurs (PLAMFE); (ix) The Collective of Small and Medium-
          Sized Enterprises of the Central African Republic (CPMEC). The rest of the partner agencies are still working to
          transmit the proposed framework agreements to the PMU: (x) The Single Window for Business Formalities
          (GUFE) benefits from technical assistance funded by the European Union which informs the activities that will be
          contained in its framework agreement with the PMU. (xi) The Central African Agency for Vocational Training and
          Employment (ACFPE) is awaiting the World Bank's fiduciary review because the PMU plans to make financial
          advances to the ACFPE, rather than making all purchases of goods and services on its behalf, as is the case for all
          other institutions that have proposed a framework agreement.
       5. The hiring process for a firm to manage the Guarantee Fund is in progress.
       6. Rehabilitation work for the building that will accommodate the PIU is on track. The contract with the construction
          firm was executed on March 4, and work commenced on March 22, with an anticipated completion timeline of
          four months.
       7. The PIU itself still needs to complete some of its technical hiring process and be more diligent in updating the
          processes it needs to comply with (STEP, PTBA, FM, E&S).
       8. The technical assistance provided through the EU SB4A fund is proving itself useful (i) to strengthen the oversight
          of the Guarantee Fund inception process, (ii) to design a tailored approach to reform the One-Stop Shop for
          business registration, (iii) to identify how to better serve forcibly displaced populations and their host
          communities, and (iv) to identify means to enhance the competitiveness of key sectors such as mining,
          agriculture and forestry. The partnership and coordination with the EU during the delivery of this TA has been
          reinforced, including along cooperating on the support for the elaboration of the National Development Plan.
       9. Next steps include
