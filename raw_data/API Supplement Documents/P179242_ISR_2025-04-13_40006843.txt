4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  The project was approved on May 16, 2023. Financing Agreements were signed by the World Bank with the Republic of
  Maldives on June 18, 2023, and with the Indian Ocean Commission on June 22, 2023. Implementation activities are being
  initiated by the two implementing agencies - the Indian Ocean Commission for regional activities and the Ministry of
  Fisheries and Ocean Resources for activities in the Maldives.
  The Project’s objectives and design remain highly relevant and aligned with the strategic direction of the new
  government of the Maldives for the fisheries sector, which includes modernizing fisheries sector management, promoting
  processing to reduce raw material exports, decarbonizing the sector through the adoption of green practices, investing in
  critical infrastructure, and leveraging private sector financing, among other objectives. The World Bank and the
  implementing agencies are currently discussing a proposal to make some adjustments to the design and targets to
  further align the project with these priorities.
  Main progress includes:
  • Staff have been mobilized to carry out project management, fiduciary management, environmental and social risk
  management, and M&E for the Maldives part of the project, as well as several technical roles.
  • At the regional level, the Indian Ocean Commission signed a contract with the United Nations Food and Agriculture
  Organization in July 2024to provide technical support and the first Steering Committee meeting was organized in
  December 2024 to approve the annual budget for the regional activities.
  • In terms of fisheries management in the Maldives, progress has been made in (i) digitizing historical catch reports,
  conducting stakeholder consultations and redrafting Fisheries Management Plans; (ii) creating a digital registry of
  fishermen and conducting awareness activities for the Fisheries Information System; (iii) refurbishing some quarantine
  and laboratory facilities; and (iv) ongoing capacity building and preparatory works for the initiation of a degree program
  and preparations for a mariculture training program. Delay have been experienced in the mobilization of consultants to
  develop the various curricula for the training programs and construction investments on the premises of the Maldives
  Marine Research Institute.
  • In terms of efforts to decarbonize the fisheries sector in the Maldives, progress has been made in installing echo
  sounders and tracking devices on anchored Fish Aggregating Devices aiming to enhance the efficiency and sustainability
  of fishing practices by providing better data and monitoring capabilities, ultimately contributing to improved fisheries
  management and reduced environmental impact. In addition, a consultant for the energy audit was mobilized to assess
  the fish processing sector and fishing vessels to develop bankable business plans under matching grants. This will help
  improve energy efficiency and reduce energy consumption, leading to cost savings and environmental benefits. The
  commencement of the matching grants program has been delayed because of government request to make some
  changes to the agreed terms.
