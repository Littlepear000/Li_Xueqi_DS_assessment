                          B. Introduction and Context

                          Country Context

                          1.      Peru has demonstrated strong macroeconomic resilience over the past two decades, with an average annual GDP
                          growth rate of 6.1 percent between 2002 and 2013, although growth has moderated to around 3 percent per year since
_For Official Use Only_




                          2014. Prudent fiscal policies, inflation-targeting monetary strategies, and an open investment climate have helped maintain
                          macroeconomic stability, with public debt at approximately 34 percent of GDP in 2023 and international reserves providing
                          a buffer against external shocks. The mining sector, particularly copper and gold, has been a major source of foreign
                          exchange, fiscal revenues, and GDP growth, contributing to Peru's favorable sovereign credit ratings and investor
                          confidence. However, regulatory inefficiencies and permitting delays have slowed private investment in the mining sector,
                          highlighting the need for reforms to attract private capital and unlock the country's growth potential. The global energy
                          transition presents a significant opportunity for Peru, with its vast copper reserves positioning it as a key supplier for
                          renewable energy and electric mobility, potentially driving long-term economic growth and national development.

                          Sectoral and Institutional Context

                          2.      Peru's mining sector is a cornerstone of its economy, generating significant employment and export revenues,
                          particularly from copper. Despite global uncertainties, Peru has a strong pipeline of mining investments, with 66 projects
                          valued at US$63.4 billion as of 2025. However, regulatory inefficiencies and complex permitting systems have hindered
                          competitiveness and stalled many projects. Improving geoscientific data and modernizing permitting processes are crucial
                          for attracting exploration investments and junior mining companies. Sustainability standards and social engagement are
                          gaining traction, but challenges remain with socio-environmental disputes and environmental liabilities. Addressing
                          informal mining and implementing comprehensive reforms can position Peru as a global leader in sustainable mining, driving
                          long-term growth and stability.

                          Relationship to CPF

                          3.     The World Bank’s Country Partnership Framework (CPF) FY23-FY27 for Peru identifies mining as a strategic sector to
                          mobilize sustainable private capital and promote the sustainable use of natural capital. The CPF emphasizes enhancing the
                          competitiveness and sustainability of the mining sector through regulatory reform, institutional capacity building, and
                          environmental remediation. The World Bank’s Country Economic Memorandum (CEM) for Peru highlights the importance
                          of leveraging the global energy transition to unlock the country’s mining potential, particularly its copper reserves, by
                          advancing key structural reforms. Effective implementation of these reforms could significantly boost Peru's GDP growth
                          and help the country achieve high-income status by 2042.

                          C. Proposed Development Objective(s)

                          4.       To improve the sustainability and competitiveness of Peru’s mining sector.


                          Key Results (From PCN)

                          5.       The proposed key result indicators are:

                               •   Increase in geoscientific data coverage (from 649,667 km² as the baseline to an additional 715,000 km²).
                               •   Increase in investment in mining exploration (25%) and developing the “Mineral System Approach1�? for 75 percent
                                   of the country (Private Capital Enabling, PCE).
                               •   Reduction in the average time required for mining permit approvals (percentage decrease in processing time,
                                   optimization of mining sector administrative processes).
                               •   Reduction in delays for prior consultation processes (percentage decrease in the average duration of structured
                                   community consultation).
                               •   Reduction in environmental contamination levels in remediated mining areas (percentage decrease in heavy metal

                          D. Concept Description

                          6.     This project aims to enhance the competitiveness and sustainability of Peru’s mining sector by de-risking investments
                          across the mineral development cycle. It includes three technical components and a project management component.

                          7.     Component A: Strengthening geological knowledge and innovation. This component aims to enhance Peru’s
                          geoscientific knowledge through improved geological mapping, prospectivity analysis, and foundational research. By
                          increasing the quality, resolution, and accessibility of precompetitive geodata, adopting modern analytical techniques, and
                          strengthening institutional research capacity, the component will support more informed land use planning, resource
                          governance, and strategic decision-making. These upstream activities will reinforce Peru’s ability to manage its mineral
                          resources sustainably and transparently through data collection for improved generation and management of information.
                          This project component is not associated with any preliminary stage of the authorization/financing of exploration or
                          extraction activities. Proposed subcomponents include the following: A1) Establishment of the national drill core library;
                          A2) Expansion of geophysical, geochemical, remote sensing precompetitive geological data; and Subcomponent A3)
                          Strengthening geochronology research capabilities.

                          8.     Component B: Regulatory and digital modernization. This component will improve the regulatory efficiency,
                          transparency, and social governance of Peru’s mining sector by modernizing administrative procedures, streamlining
                          permitting systems, and ensuring systematic, early, and continuous community engagement. Proposed subcomponents


                          1 The “mineral systems approach�? is an innovation because it shifts mineral exploration from a narrow, deposit-by-deposit method to a predictive,
                          system-based strategy. Traditional exploration often relies on surface clues and past discoveries, which can miss deeper or hidden resources. In
                          contrast, the mineral systems approach integrates advanced geoscience, big data, and modeling tools to understand the larger geological processes
                          that create mineral deposits over millions of years. This approach is innovative because it allows countries like Peru to uncover new exploration
                          frontiers, optimize resource mapping, and guide smarter, lower-risk investments. It also supports more strategic and sustainable land-use decisions,
                          helping align exploration with environmental and social priorities.

                          include the following: B1) Optimization of mining sector administrative processes; B2) Development of the integrated and
                          transparent mining permitting system (VUD-MINEM); and B3) Technical support to formal artisanal and small-scale mining
                          (ASM).

                          9.     Component C: Sustainable environmental remediation. This component will finance the remediation of two mining
                          environmental liabilities (PAMs) and promote sustainable land use in areas contaminated by legacy mining operations. The
                          implementation of these activities aims to mitigate environmental and public health risks, improve soil and water quality,
                          and create enabling conditions for restoring sustainable livelihoods in affected communities. Proposed subcomponents
                          include the following: C1) Ecosystem restoration in the Hualgayoc river micro-basin; and C2) Restoration of ecosystem
                          services in the Carhuancho micro-basin.

                          10.    Component D: Project management. The Project Implementation Unit (PIU) will be housed within the Ministry of
                          Energy and Mines (MINEM), which will lead and directly implement Components A and B. MINEM will ensure effective
                          coordination, financial management, monitoring and evaluation, and regulatory oversight to facilitate successful project
                          execution across all components. As the executing agency, MINEM will oversee and support INGEMMET and AMSAC in their
                          respective technical leadership roles.
