 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Introduction

1.     This restructuring paper seeks the approval of the Regional Vice President on a proposed Level II restructuring of
the Malawi COVID-19 Emergency Response and Health Systems Preparedness Project (the project).

2.      The parent project, financed by an International Development Association (IDA) grant in the amount of US$7.0
million was prepared under the Multiphase Programmatic Approach (MPA) Fast Track COVID-19 Facility (FTCF). It was
approved by the Board on April 14, 2020 and became effective on April 30, 2020. The project supports the Government
of Malawi (GoM) to prevent, detect and respond to the threat posed by COVID-19 and strengthen national systems for
public health preparedness. The parent project had three components, with the first component further split into two
subcomponents. Component 1 (originally allocated US$5.30 million) supports prevention of the spread of COVID-19
through surveillance and containment strategies. It supports enhancement of disease detection capacities through
provision of technical expertise, laboratory equipment and systems to ensure prompt case finding and contact tracing,
consistent with WHO guidelines; and strengthening of case management capabilities. Subcomponent 1.1 supports case
detection, confirmation, contact tracing, recording and reporting, while subcomponent 1.2 provides assistance to the
public health care system to provide optimal medical care, maintain essential community services and to minimize risks
for patients and health personnel, including training health facilities staff and front-line workers on risk mitigation
measures and providing them with the appropriate protective equipment and hygiene materials. Component 2 (originally
allocated US$0.95 million) supports strengthening the capacity of the public health system for preparedness and response
to the COVID-19 pandemic and to future pandemics and other threats to health security. Lastly, Component 3 (originally
allocated US$0.75 million) covers implementation management and monitoring and evaluation (M&E).

3.      The project is in its third year of implementation and has received two additional financings (AFs). The first AF
or AF1 in the amount of US$30 million IDA grant was approved on June 18, 2021 and became effective on August 23, 2021.
The primary objective of the AF1 was to provide upfront financing to help the GoM purchase and deploy COVID-19
vaccines that meet the World Bank’s vaccine approval criteria (VAC) and strengthen relevant health systems that are
necessary for a successful deployment, and to further strengthen preparedness and response activities under the parent
project. Accordingly, US$26 million out of the US$30 million under AF1 (86.7 per cent) was allocated to a new
subcomponent 1.3 for COVID-19 vaccine procurement (US$21.2 million) and deployment (US$4.8 million).

4.      Additional US$49.9 million from IDA (a credit in the amount of US$25.4 million equivalent, and a grant in the
amount of US$24.5 equivalent) and US$10.0 million grant from the Global Financing Facility for Women, Children and
Adolescents (GFF) were added to the project through the second AF or AF2, which was approved on June 21, 2022 and
became effective on September 13, 2022. The purpose of AF2 was to further scale-up affordable and equitable access to
COVID-19 vaccines and sustain support to ensure effective vaccine deployment through vaccination system strengthening,
and to further strengthen preparedness and response activities under the parent project. A new subcomponent 1.4
financed by GFF was added to the project to mitigate the disruption of essential health services due to the COVID-19
pandemic. Component 4 (Contingent Emergency Response) was also added to allow for rapid reallocation of project
proceeds in the event of a natural or man-made disaster or crisis that has caused or is likely to imminently cause a major
adverse economic and/or social impact during the life of the project. The two AFs bring the total financing for the project
to US$96.9 million (US$86.9 million IDA and US$10 million GFF).

5.      The project’s progress towards achievement of the PDO and overall implementation progress have been rated
Satisfactory and Moderately Satisfactory respectively in the last Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) of
September 20, 2023. As of February 8, 2024, most of the activities under the parent project were completed with 100
percent of the US$7 million financing disbursed. Of the US$30 million AF1 funds, US$13.5 million (45 percent) has been
disbursed. For AF2, US$20.0 million of the US$59.90 million (33 percent) has been disbursed. Overall, US$41.7 million out
of US$96.9 (43 percent) has been disbursed. The rating for Environmental and Social Safeguards, Financial Management
and Procurement are Moderately Satisfactory, Satisfactory and Moderately Satisfactory, respectively. The overall risk
rating has remained Substantial throughout the life of the project.

6.       The project has supported health system strengthening for pandemic preparedness and response, purchase and
roll-out of the COVID-19 vaccination and continuity of essential health services. Malawi’s capacity to prevent, diagnose,
and treat COVID-19 has been strengthened through the procurement of critical medical and diagnostic supplies and
equipment, as well as the training of health workers on infection prevention and control, COVID-19 testing, case
management, and integrated disease surveillance. Rapid response and contact tracing capacity has been strengthened
through the training of rapid response teams and health surveillance assistants (HSAs), as well as the procurement of
vehicles. The project has co-financed procurement and deployment of 1.86 million doses of COVID-19 vaccines and
associated supplies such as syringes, which have already been delivered in the country. To ensure continuity of essential
health services, the project procured essential medicines and supplies. It also supported 2022/23 cholera outbreak
response through triggering of the CERC in the amount of US$12.2 million.

7.       The key challenges facing the project thus far are disruptions due to multiple health emergencies, including the
COVID-19 pandemic, and natural disasters. The implementation was affected by the health sector’s response to the
cholera outbreak, which escalated between October 2022 and February 2023, measles and polio outbreaks for which
immunization campaigns were launched, and the Tropical Storm Ana (February 2022) and Cyclone Freddy (March 2023)
that hit Malawi and their negative impact on the health service infrastructure and human resource capacity as the health
sector dealt with multiple crises. There is also stalled progress on (i) various critical procurements, including medical
oxygen plant, medical equipment, and supplies, and (ii) slow implementation progress of civil works. Delays are due to
global disruption of the supply chain partly due to COVID-19 pandemic, combined with the need to have required
environmental and social safeguards measures in place. To address some of these issues, an independent Project
Implementation Unit (PIU) for the project has been established (transitioned from the Southern Africa Tuberculosis and
Health Systems Support Project – P155568) and strengthened with new positions, including a full-time coordinator.

 C. Rationale for Restructuring

11.      Through a letter dated November 25, 2022, the Government of Malawi requested activation of the CERC under
the project in the amount of US$12.2 million equivalent to support the GoM’s efforts in responding to the cholera
outbreak and to address the chronic shortage of essential drugs and supplies. The CERC is funding the Emergency Action
Plan (EAP) for cholera response, which includes a subset of key interventions across seven pillars of the National Cholera
Response Plan, including commodities for emergency response (i.e., essential medicines). This level II restructuring is
proposed to: (i) amend the PDO to incorporate CERC-related objectives on epidemic preparedness and response; (ii) add
CERC-funded activities on cholera response; (iii) revise selected indicators and target values in the Results Framework, (iv)
reallocate costs (funds) across the components and disbursement categories to accommodate the CERC activation; and
(v) revise the disbursement projection accordingly. This restructuring will also reallocate across disbursement categories
uncommitted balances from the COVID-19 vaccines procurement and deployment budget. The restructuring was formally
requested by the Government of Malawi in a letter dated December 21, 2023.

12.     Impact of restructuring on project risks is minimal. The macroeconomic risk remains high in view of the
constrained fiscal environment and the negative impact of shortage of foreign exchange. The risks associated with the
Technical Design, Institutional Capacity for Implementation and Procurement are likely to reduce given the experiences
and lessons learned from shared implementation arrangements with the Southern Africa Tuberculosis and Health Systems
Support Project (SATBHSSP; P155658), while the proposed restructuring will add to the PDO statement to incorporate the
CERC. The project will continue to address the risk that the objective is not achieved by its sustained support to maintaining
essential health services and related health systems strengthening. As such, the overall rating for the risks is not expected
to change.

   and (iii) development of manuals, guidelines, policies and roadmaps for public health preparedness and response.

e) Subcomponent 1.3: Vaccine procurement and deployment (Current Financing US$44.00 million IDA; Proposed
   Reallocation of -US$21.7 million IDA). Of the US$44 million allocation to this subcomponent, US$28.7 is for
   procurement of the COVID-19 vaccines and associated supplies (i.e., syringes) and US$15.3 million is for the
   deployment related activities, including demand creation, strengthening vaccine waste management systems, and
   upgrading of digital health systems for vaccine management. Of the US10.5 million uncommitted funds from the
   vaccine procurement allocation, US$9.5 million will be reallocated to other components and categories while the
   remaining US$1 million will finance deployment activities under the same subcomponent and category, i.e.,
   support integration of the COVID-19 vaccines into the country’s routine immunization program through
   strengthening vaccine waste management facilities and development of a nationwide digital heath system for
   vaccine management.

f)   Subcomponent 1.4: Monitoring and Maintaining Essential Health (Current Financing US$0 million IDA; US$10
     million GFF; Proposed Financing of + US$3.85 million IDA). This subcomponent finances mitigation and resilient
     strategies against disruption of essential health services due to the impact of COVID-19 and other public health
     emergencies. The subcomponent is currently financed by GFF only. Through this restructuring, US$3.85 million
     co-financing from IDA will be reallocated to this subcomponent to support continuity and monitoring of essential
     frontline health services during health emergencies. The additional resources will among other things finance the
     (i) deployment of essential medicine and supplies that are being procured by this project to health facilities across
     the country, (ii) service level agreements (SLAs) between the MoH and Christian Hospitals Association of Malawi
     (CHAM) for the CHAM to continue providing primary health care to the population in hard-to-reach areas who
     would otherwise not be able to access routine and emergency health services.

g) Component 3: Implementation Management and Monitoring and Evaluation (Current Financing US$4.45 million
   IDA; Proposed Reallocation of + US$0.2 million IDA). The proposed additional resources will cover deficit due to
   foreign currency exchanges losses and increased administrative costs such as external audits and staff costs.
