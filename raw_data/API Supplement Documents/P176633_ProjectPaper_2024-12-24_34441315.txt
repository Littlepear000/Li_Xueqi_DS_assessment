    I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

Project background and status

1. This Project Paper seeks the approval to process an extension of the closing date of TF0C1586 (Off grid solar delivery
   for rural clinics – RETF – ESMAP grant) by 12 months for the Mali Electricity System Reinforcement and Access
   Expansion Project (YELEN SIRA– P176633).
2. The Mali Electricity System Reinforcement and Access Expansion Project (Yelen Sira) ("Project”) was approved by the
   International Development Association (Association or IDA) Board on June 23, 2023. The Project has three sources of
   financing and three corresponding legal agreements: (i) Financing Agreement between the Republic of Mali (Recipient)
   and the Association - Credit No. 7376-ML, Credit No. 7377-ML (IDA Financing Agreement); (ii) Financing Agreement
   between the Recipient and the Association acting as an Accredited Entity of the Green Climate Fund - Loan No.
   TF0C1876-ML, Grant No. TF0C1681-ML (GCF Financing Agreement); and (iii) Grant Agreement between the Recipient
   and the Association acting as an administrator of Energy Sector Management Assistance Program Multi-Donor Trust
   Fund - (Grant No. TF0C1586-ML) (ESMAP Grant Agreement), each dated June 30, 2023.
3. The Project includes four components as follows.

      Component 1: Reinforcement of grid infrastructure and access expansion, US$144 million (IDA US$112.5 million;
      GCF US$31.5 million).

1. This component will finance critical grid infrastructure and new connections through the upgrading and expansion of
   transmission and distribution networks in Bamako and its perimeter, upgrading and expanding of resilient distribution
   networks and new connections outside of Bamako in several priority secondary cities, to reduce system losses,
   improve grid efficiency, increase reliability of electricity supply, increase access, and absorb additional generation
   capacity, including generation from solar parks to be supported by the project. It also includes the provision of
   transaction advisory services to mobilize IPPs, through competitive bidding, for the construction of solar PV power
   plants, and construction of the public grid infrastructure required to connect the future solar PV plants to the existing
   grid.

      Component 2: Green mini-grid and off-grid electrification of selected localities in rural areas, US$49.5 million (IDA
      US$37.5 million; GCF Credit US$11.0 million; ESMAP US$1.0 million).

4. The proposed activities will contribute to reducing disparity of access to electricity services between urban and rural
   areas and providing cleaner and more affordable electricity for socioeconomic development in rural areas. The
   proposed component aims to deploy technologies1 and new business models that would reduce fossil fuel use, save
   costs, tap into local renewable energy resources, and ensure more reliable service provision.

      Component 3: Institutional Capacity Building and Implementation support, US$7.5 million (IDA US$7 million; GCF
      Grant US$0.5 million).



1As it relates to activities described under Component 2 involving water pumping, it should be noted that no boreholes will tap into international
waterways or aquifers. In general, any activities that use or risk polluting international waterways will not be financed under the Project.

5. The component will finance activities to create a robust institutional arrangement to implement the proposed
   activities and to support the energy sector’s overall development. It would strengthen the capacity of the ministry in
   sector planning, resource mobilization, and overall oversight of the project’s implementing agencies: EDM-SA for
   Component 1 and AMADER for Component 2.

    Component 4: Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC).

6. A CERC with zero allocation may be used to contribute to an emergency response through the timely implementation
   of activities in response to an eligible national emergency. The CERC mechanism will be further defined in a CERC
   Operational Manual which will be part of the PIM, which will include triggers and conditions for the use of funds. This
   manual will clearly outline the triggers, eligible expenditures, and procedures for tapping into the CERC.

7. The Project is effective since February 29, 2024, after two effectiveness deadline extensions at the request of the
   Recipient, from October 28, 2023, to December 29, 2023, then to February 29, 2024.

    Status
8. Even though the project is effective since February 29, 2024, the implementation is still delayed due mainly to the
   government desire to revise the initial design. On February 27, 2024, the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MoF)
   notified the World Bank of the Government of Mali's (GoM) intention to revise the financing agreements to exclude
   the private sector from the construction and operation of the mini grids planned under the rural electrification
   development component (component 2.1). In addition, through various exchanges, the MEF indicated that they are
   no longer interested in building solar photovoltaic (PV) plants with private developers (independent power producers).
   Despite several formal follow-up letters from the Bank, the GoM has not yet confirmed the specific changes to be
   made to the project.
9. However, the PIUs are moving forward with the recruitment process, completing all the steps before signing contracts.
   Once the MEF has given a formal indication of the amendments to the project design, contracts could be concluded.
   The recruitment process for all the profiles is expected to be completed by December 31, 2024.
10. EDM-PIU is recruiting four (4) additional staff to reinforce fiduciary and safeguard functions, in accordance with the
    project implementation support plan: a Senior Accountant, a Financial Management Specialist, a Procurement
    Specialist, a Junior Environmental Specialist, and a Junior Social safeguard Specialist. All the ToRs have been reviewed
    and accepted by the World Bank and the selection process is underway. However, since the project implementation
    is delayed the PIU is moving slowly even though they agreed to move forward until they have agreed contracts to be
    signed as soon as the MEF gives the expected responses. The recruitment process for all the profiles is expected to be
    completed by December 31, 2024.
11. For AMADER-PIU, five (5) additional staff members would need to be recruited: a Procurement Specialist, a Senior
    Accountant, an Environmental Specialist, a Social Safeguard Specialist, and a GBV Specialist. All ToRs have been
    reviewed and accepted by the World Bank team and the selection process is underway. Similarly to EDM-PIU,
    AMADER-PIU is moving slowly with the recruitment process given the delays mentioned above and since they have
    no dedicated resources. The recruitment process is expected to be completed by December 31, 2024.
12. The recruitment processes of an External Audit firm for both PIUs (in charge of the external audit of both Project
    Implementing Units, process managed by EDM-PIU) for 2024 – 2026, and an Internal Auditor for both PIUs (process

13. Regarding project components, EDM-PIU prepared ToRs for the recruitment of the owner’s engineer that will assist
    the PIU oversee the works planned on the transmission network (subcomponent 1.1). ToRs are accepted by the World
    Bank team on September 2024, after a series of comments addressed by the PIU. The formal approval will be provided
    through the World Bank Procurement system. The PIU submission is expected by end of November 2024. Finally, the
    World Bank team reviewed and accepted the ToRs for a Transaction Advisor that will assist the GoM in the selection
    of an Independent Power Producer to develop least-cost Solar PV plants, as part of subcomponent 1.2. However,
    because of the GoM willingness to revise the project design and remove this activity, formal approval is not provided
    yet.
    Rationale for restructuring
14. As the project implementation is delayed because of the government willingness to revise the scoop of the project
    without any confirmation yet and considering the current grant (TF0C1586) closing date (December 31, 2024), the
    team is requesting an extension of 12 months to allow time to implement the grant activities that support the project
    once its implementation begins.

15. Pending the Bank's formal receipt of MoF's amendment request, the status quo for implementation remains,
    especially as MoF has not completed its financial management requirements (sending the list of personnel designated
    for withdrawal requests and their specimen signatures to the World Bank).

16. However, the project remains of strategic importance to the Government of Mali. The project contributes to improving
    people’s lives and supporting economic development, including post-COVID-19 recovery. Its PDO continues to be
    highly relevant and achievable and is in line with the country’s strategic energy-sector objectives to achieve universal
    access by 2030.

17. In this context, the Government of Mali has requested an extension of the closing date of ESMAP grant for 12 months
    to allow the completion of works in a letter dated December 12, 2024. The Project Implementation Unit (PIU) has
    prepared an action plan, discussed, and agreed with the World Bank, reconfirming timelines, and approaches to
    complete all trust fund activities within the proposed closing date. The proposed extension will not result in additional
    costs to the project. The proposed extension does not involve any change to the project design, results framework, or
    implementation arrangements.

18. Should the Bank receive the MoF formal letter for amendment of the project design, the team will be able to proceed
    with a new restructuring and start implementation.
