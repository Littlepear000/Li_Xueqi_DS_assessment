4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  The Project became effective in December 2020. Despite challenges over the last few years, the Government of Papua
  New Guinea remains committed to achieving the Project Development Objectives and the National Department of Health
  (NDOH) sees the Project as essential towards achieving the national health plan. The Restructuring of the Project was
  completed on December 12, 2023 to implement changes required because of these delays and to streamline
  implementation, particularly for the verification of results.

  Results (as of June 30, 2024). Progress towards achievement of the Project Development Objective (PDO) – to contribute
  to increasing the utilization of quality essential health services in Project supported provinces of [PNG] – remain as
  moderately satisfactory. The rate of disbursement continues to increase and is anticipated to expedite. Disbursement
  currently is US$ 4.95 million as of December 4, 2024, 17% of project total. By end of the Financial Year 2025, it is
  expected that an additional US$ 5.15 million will be disbursed when the performance funds for disbursement linked
  indicators (DLIs) are released for year 3 (Component 2) and two procurement contracts to be completed under
  Component 1 (increase to 33% of project total). In the 4 project provinces (ENB, and three expanded provinces – Simbu,
  Central and East Sepik), 96% of eligible health facilities (151 of 157) were assessed using the digital Integrated Supervision
  Checklist to measure the Minimum Service Quality of which 60% (91) health facilities scored above 60% (minimum
  threshold). The quality scorecard results is anticipated to improve despite the progressively harder DLI targets for Year 4
  and further progress achievement towards the PDO on improving essential service delivery quality. Additionally, there
  are some positive improvements observed for two of the three PDO indicators to track improving access to essential
  services using the Competitive Disbursement Linked Indicators (C-DLIs) to incentivize improvements in number of
  services being provided for antenatal care 4 and pentavalent 3rd dose (21 of 22 of PHAs MOU on hand participating in
  the competition).

  In addition, 2 key priority activities are being processed to support capacity development of health facilities and
  Provincial Health Authorities, equivalent to almost 47% of the project envelop (US$14.2 million) and is expected to
  expedite progress towards the Project Development Objective. This includes planned health facility audits in East Sepik,
  Central and Chimbu and investments to support the equipment delivery to all 156 health facilities (community health
  posts (level two), health centers (level 3) and district hospitals (level four) in the four project provinces and rehabilitation
  of up to 16 health facilities brought up to national health service standards. Upskilling health workers for maternal and
  child health services and community roll out of village health assistants to improve demand of health services in the 4
  project provinces. Given the positive progress, the Project remains to be Moderately Satisfactory.

  The next technical mission is scheduled for May of next year.
