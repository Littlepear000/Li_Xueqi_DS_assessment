 4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
Component 1:

   •    Progress has been made on finalizing the bidding documents for the construction of the INSTAD building. However,
        implementation has experienced delays due to a financing gap of USD 2.9 million, following the redesign of the
        building features. The revised total cost is now estimated at USD 6.9 million, down from the original estimate of USD
        11 million.

   •    The final results of the Population and Housing Census (RGPH-3) were published in June 2025. Provisional results of
        thematic analyses including the State and population structure (Volume 1), Socioeconomic situation of women
        (Volume 12), People with disabilities (Volume 13), Refugees and asylum seekers (Volume 14), Characteristics of
        households, housing and living environment (Volume 15), Directory of localities and resident populations (Volume
        20) were released between April and July 2025.


   •    The final report of the third National Strategy for the Development of Statistics 2025–2029 (SNDS 3) was published in
        April 2025. This strategy aims to address challenges identified during previous SNDS cycles, including issues
        concerning funding statistical activities and promoting a culture of statistics. SNDS 3 is structured around a demand-
        driven approach that prioritizes user needs and incorporates ICT to support data sharing and centralization.


   •    Between January and February 2025, INSTAD conducted the fifth household budget survey (Enquete Djiboutienne
        Aupres des Menages pour les Indicateurs Sociaux, EDAM5-IS), which included a boosted sample of migrant and
        refugee households. Data treatment and preparation have been finalized. A report on the poverty profile is currently
        being developed.


   •    In 2025, for the first time in Djibouti, INSTAD established a quarterly labor force survey (Enquête Djiboutienne sur les
        Statistiques du Travail, EDST) to systematically collect and disseminate key labor market indicators on a quarterly
        base. A report including the analysis of Djibouti's labor market for the first quarter of 2025 has recently been
        completed. The official EDST results are expected to be released in the first quarter of 2026.


   •    The implementation of the Economic Census (Recensement General des Entreprise a Djibouti, REGED) is underway. In
        June 2025, INSTAD completed the pilot enumeration phase. Approximately 50 enumerators are expected to be
        deployed for the data collection stage between March and April 2026.

Revamping macroeconomic modeling: In partnership with AFRISTAT, Djibouti is stepping up efforts to strengthen
macroeconomic forecasting and policy planning. The Directorate of Economy and Planning has advanced work on the quasi-
accounting and computable general equilibrium (CGE) models, while also producing economic briefs and annual reports.
However, progress remains limited on several key deliverables, including the Triennial Public Investment Program. As of end-
July 2025, no macro-simulation-based policy notes have been published, and the latest macroeconomic papers have yet to be
made available online.

Rolling out sectoral Medium-Term Expenditure Frameworks (MTEFs): In April 2025, Djibouti relaunched sectoral MTEFs with
a hands-on workshop led by the Budget Directorate and expert consultants. Using real fiscal data, six pilot ministries co-
developed a draft Excel-based model, laying groundwork for reform and practical adaptation.

Laying the Groundwork for Fiscal Dialogue: From May 1–5, 2025, Djibouti participated in the METAC Steering Committee
meeting in Egypt, an important step in preparing for its Second National Fiscal Conference. The event reaffirmed Djibouti’s
commitment to regional cooperation and showcased progress in public financial management, taxation, and statistical reform.

Rebooting aid coordination mechanisms: To address longstanding fragmentation in aid coordination, a consultant was hired
in February 2025 to review and redesign Djibouti’s framework for development cooperation. The assessment, grounded in the
principles of the Paris Declaration and the Accra Agenda, involved interviews with government ministries and development
partners.

Impact evaluation tools for development projects: In the first half of 2025, the Directorate of External Financing advanced its
commitment to evidence-based policymaking by hiring a consultant to develop impact assessment tools for development
projects. A draft evaluation guide and methodology have been submitted for review, laying the groundwork for enhanced
accountability and performance oversight.

Upgrading debt management systems: Debt transparency and operational efficiency received a boost in April 2025, as debt
management staff underwent training on producing statistical bulletins using the SYGADE platform, in collaboration with
UNCTAD. A follow-up training course is scheduled for September 2025, focusing on integrating SYGADE with the CORIOLIS
platform, an important step toward more streamlined data sharing and reporting.

Development of DSA and Medium-Term Debt Strategy (MTDS): In February 2025, the World Bank delivered training on
developing a customized DSA model, leading to the formation of a multi-institutional task force. However, progress has been
slowed by staffing constraints. To sustain momentum, a follow-up World Bank mission is planned before December 2025 to
support the formulation of a Medium-Term Debt Strategy, key to embedding fiscal planning within a more structured
macroeconomic framework.
