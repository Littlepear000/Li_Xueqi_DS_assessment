                        MPA Status
                        The Food Systems Resilience Program for Eastern and Southern Africa (MPA, P178566) was approved by the Board on
                        June 21, 2022, with an overall financing envelope of US$2.3 billion and four recipients under Phase 1: Ethiopia (US$600
                        million equivalent), Madagascar (US$158.1 million equivalent), IGAD (US$25 million equivalent) and CCARDESA (US$5
                        million). Disbursement in Ethiopia has reached US$175.66 million and Madagascar has disbursed US$77.07 million. On
                        June 6, 2024, Madagascar triggered US$50 million CERC to respond to a request from the Government and also
                        received US$70 million Additional Financing, including US$50 million from CRW ERF and US$20 million from GAFSP.
                        Phase 2 for Tanzania (PforR, US$300 million equivalent) and a phase 3 for Comoros, Malawi, Somalia, Kenya, and the
                        Africa Union Commission (IPF, US$603 million) were approved by the Board on May 31, 2023. Malawi also received a
                        grant in the amount of US$15 million. At the same time (May 31, 2023), the Board approved an increase of the overall
                        envelope of the MPA to US$2.75 billion. Additional Financing to Tanzania of US$8.3 million was approved on
                        September 30, 2024. On February 20, 2025, Malawi received Additional Financing of US$61.0 million from the Food
                        Systems 2030 and AGCOM 2.0 Multi-Donor Trust Funds.

                        MPA Expected Results

                        Original MPA Program expected Results and Indicators
                        (Previously approved on 23-Jun-2025 as part of Additional Financing in P177816)
                        Increased resilience and preparedness for food insecurity. Program level indicators are: (i) reduction of food insecure
                        people in program targeted areas; (ii) farmers adopting resilience-enhancing technologies and practices; (iii) land area
                        under sustainable land management practices; (iv) increased volume of agricultural production sold on domestic and
                        regional markets and (v) policy products related to agriculture, natural resources management, and food systems

                        I.     BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING

                        A. Purpose and Project Context

                        1.       This project paper seeks the approval of the Director for Regional Integration to provide an additional financing
                        (AF) for two country-level projects currently being implemented under Phase 3 of the Food Systems Resilience Program
                        for Eastern and Southern Africa (FSRP Phase 3, P177816). The AF includes grants of US$50 million from the Crisis
                        Response Window Plus and US$14.6 million in national International Development Association (IDA) resources to the
                        Malawi Food Systems Resilience Project (Malawi FSRP, also known as AGCOM 2.0); and of US$30 million in national IDA
                        resources to the Somalia Food Systems Resilience Project (Somalia FSRP). The central purpose of the AF is to restore, in
                        full, the resources both projects reallocated to emergency operations undertaken upon activation of their Contingency
                        Emergency Response Component (CERC). A total of US$50 million was reallocated to the CERC by the Malawi FSRP, and
                        US$28.28 million was reallocated by the Somalia FSRP. The AF will thereby allow the projects’ original components to
                        recover the resources originally budgeted to them. In addition, it will provide the two projects, specifically the Malawi
                        FSRP, additional resources beyond those originally budgeted. For reasons detailed below, the Malawi FSRP’s non-CERC
                        components will gain US$34.6 million, and the Somalia FSRP’s US$1.72 million. The AF will increase the Malawi FSRP’s
                        overall budget by 20 percent, bringing it to US$390.6 million, and it will increase the Somalia FSRP’s budget by 20 percent,
                        bringing it to US$180 million, CERC included. The paper provides the context and rationale for the proposed AF and

                        describes the modifications needed to effectively utilize the additional resources.

                        2.       The parent Food Systems Resilience Program for Eastern and Southern Africa (P178566) (the MPA, or the
                        Program) was approved by the World Bank Board of Directors on June 21, 2022, with an overall envelope of US$2.3

                        for Coordination of Agricultural Research and Development for Southern Africa (CCARDESA) (US$5 million). The program
                        development objective (PDO) of the MPA is to increase the resilience of food systems and preparedness for food insecurity
                        in Project areas.

                        available IDA financing raising the total International Development Association (IDA) envelope to US$2.75 billion. Phase
                        2 (P179818) provides US$300 million in national and regional IDA financing to Tanzania. Phase 3 (P177816) provides
                        US$603 million in national and regional IDA financing to Kenya (US$150 million), the Union of Comoros (US$40 million),

                        ERF) and US$20 million from the GAFSP. In September 2024, the Tanzania FSRP received an AF of US$8.3 million.

                        B. Context

                        5.     Both Malawi and Somalia have experienced significant deteriorations in food security since the approval of
                        FSRP Phase 3.

                             •   In Malawi, the food security crisis has worsened since 2023 due to a succession of climate and market shocks. The
                                 destructive passage of Tropical Cyclone Freddy in early 2023, the 44 percent devaluation of the Kwacha against
                                 the dollar in November 2023, and fuel and electricity price inflation triggered steep increases in the prices of maize
                                 and other staple foods. 2 As a result, the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) projected that 3 million
                                 people, or about 15 percent of Malawi’s population of 20 million, would face crisis-level food insecurity (IPC Phase
                                 3) in 2023. Conditions continued to deteriorate and between October 2023 and March 2024, the number of people
                                 facing IPC Phase 3 or worse was projected to rise to 4.4 million people (22 percent of the population). As the El-
                                 Niño phenomenon persisted, droughts devastated an estimated 44 percent of the national crop area in 2024;3
                                 and as of March 2024, year-on-year food price inflation reached nearly 40 percent. Between October 2024 and
                                 March 2025, 5.7 million people, or around 28 percent of the population, were expected to face crisis-level or
                                 worse food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+). Of those, some 416,000 people were expected to face acute, emergency-
                                 level food insecurity (IPC Phase 4).
                             •   In Somalia, extreme rainfall associated with El-Niño during the 2023 dyer season (the second rainy season,
For Official Use Only




                                 October–December) caused widespread damage. The flood destroyed irrigation infrastructure, crops, and
                                 pastures, and resulted in large-scale livestock deaths, leading to estimated damages and losses of US$57 million.4
                                 Approximately 68,000 households in southern and central Somalia were directly affected. The resulting
                                 disruptions to household incomes and food supply were expected to push nearly 305,000 additional people into
                                 food stress or crisis-level food insecurity (IPC2+), including nearly 122,000 people into acute levels of food
                                 insecurity (IPC 3).

                        6.       Both the Malawi FSRP and Somalia FSRP have activated their CERC to address emerging crisis situations.

                             •   Malawi has activated its CERC twice: first in November 2023, reallocating US$20 million in project funds, and again
                                 in November 2024, reallocating an additional US$30 million. Upon the second activation, the CERC under the
                                 Regional Climate Resilience Program for Eastern and Southern Africa 2 Project (RCRP 2, P181308) was also
                                 triggered allocating an additional US$5 million. These two CERCs were jointly implemented as a single emergency
                                 operation under the Malawi FSRP. The first operation concluded in August 2024 and the second is expected to
                                 continue through May 2025.
                             •   Somalia activated its CERC on February 29, 2024, reallocating US$28.28 million of project funds. The emergency
                                 response includes a combination of immediate relief and longer-term recovery interventions, expected to
                                 continue as the situation requires.

                        budgeted resources away from the two projects planned, non-CERC components. This AF seeks in large part to address
                        the resulting resource shortfall faced by the projects’ originally planned activities.

                              II. DESCRIPTION OF ADDITIONAL FINANCING

                        A. Sources and Allocation of AF

                        14.     The primary purpose of this AF is to retroactively finance the emergency operations undertaken by both projects
                        which were initially funded by diverting funds from planned project activities. While both projects used their CERC to
                        respond to crises, they differ slightly in how these operations were financed. All three CERC operations were financed—
                        at least initially—by reallocating resources from existing project components.

                              •   The Malawi FSRP’s first CERC operation (US$20 million) was initially financed by reallocating funds from other
                                  project components. It was later retroactively financed through the first AF, allowing the originally allocated
                                  components to recover their funds and proceed as planned. Once approved, the current, (second) AF will assume
                                  that retroactive financing role thereby freeing up US$20 million from the first AF to expand the project’s outreach
                                  as outlined below.

                              •   In the Somalia FSRP, majority of the current AF will retroactively finance the CERC operation (US$28.28 million).
                                  The remaining US$1.72 million will be used to strengthen the government’s institutional capacity to coordinate
                                  and implement emergency food security responses.

                        15.       Two sources of financing will be mobilized for this AF.

                                  Plus (CRW+); and a US$14.6 million equivalent grant from the country’s performance -based allocation (PBA)

                        US$0.6 million increase in Subcomponent 1.1 resources will allow the soil health pilot activity - which aims to inform a

                        increase by 1.5 percent. In addition, the project expects to support 25 percent more water users’ associations and farmer
                        or community organizations involved in water and landscape management.

                             IV. APPRAISAL SUMMARY

                        A. Technical, Economic, and Financial Analysis

                        36.      The economic and financial analysis (EFA) was updated to reflect the addition of funds to the projects’ non -
                        CERC activities. Due to their humanitarian nature, CERC activities are not reflected in the EFA. As with the initial analysis,
                        a cash flow model was used to compare “with project�? and “without project�? scenarios applying a 6 percent discount rate.
                        Economic returns are estimated based on project improvements in farm productivity, household incomes, and crop yields.
                        The analysis also incorporated the project’s expected impacts on greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions using the FAO’s EX-ACT
                        tool with results valued according to the social cost of carbon.

                        37.      The second AF is expected to significantly enhance the Malawi FSRP’s overall performance. Comparing the
                        project performance after the first AF to that after the second AF, the economic net present value (ENPV) is expected to
                        rise from US$322 million to US$534.2 million. Similarly, the economic internal rate of return (EIRR) is expected to increase
For Official Use Only




                        from 18 percent to 19.9 percent. These improvements are largely attributed to expanded irrigation coverage, greater
                        adoption of climate-smart agriculture (CSA) practices, wider access to digital services, and enhanced market integration.

                        38.     Besides the increase in outreach, modeling assumptions were largely unchanged. Benefit streams were derived
                        from improved agricultural performance and market integration across supported value chains. These include incremental
                        returns from increased yields, reduced post-harvest losses, and higher farm-gate prices for crops and livestock products.
                        Representative farm models were applied to irrigated and rainfed systems and scaled using adoption targets from the
                        updated results framework. Additional benefits - such as enhanced land tenure security and access to digital services—
                        were incorporated indirectly through their effect on uptake and returns. As previously, the analysis reflects a situation
                        where low productivity, limited irrigation, and poor market connectivity continue to constrain sectoral growth. Financial
                        costs were converted to economic values using standard conversion factors, excluding taxes, subsidies, and other
                        transfers. Inputs such as labor and land were priced at opportunity cost, and all capital investments were valued over their
                        economic lifespan.

                        39.      Sensitivity analyses indicate that the project will remain economically viable under a range of adverse scenarios.
                        For example, a 20 percent reduction in benefits would result in a lower yet still acceptable EIRR (above 6%). However,
                        significant cost overruns (+50%), or implementation delays (of 2 or more years) would be expected to undermine project
                        viability, bringing the EIRR below the 6 percent threshold (corresponding to the discount rate). When carbon mitigation
                        benefits are valued in accordance with the World Bank carbon pricing guidelines (see below), the ENPV increases further
                        to 598.3 under the low carbon price scenario and to US$667.7 million under the high carbon price scenario. Corresponding
                        EIRRs increase to 27.2 and 32.4 percent respectively.

                        40.     The AF is also expected to increase the Malawi FSRP’s contributions to climate change mitigation. The GHG
                        emissions assessment, conducted using the FAO’s EX-ACT tool, estimates a cumulative net reduction of approximately
                        from 2.76 million tons of carbon dioxide equivalent (tCO₂e) to 2.54 million tons (after the first AF). These reductions are
                        primarily driven by sustainable land and water management, CSA adoption, and improved agronomic practices.

                        41.     The Somalia FSRP’s ENPV and EIRR remain unchanged, and the project is expected to remain economically
