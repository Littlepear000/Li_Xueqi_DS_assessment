 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  This is an updated ISR based on Implementation Support Mission conducted May 16-27, 2022 and project progress are summarized below:
       1. A second external shock is affecting the private sector. Just as the country was coming out of the COVID-19 shock, recent macro-
            pressures including a sharp Kip devaluation; a spike in inflation; recent fuel shortages; and strict land border controls by China under the
            zero COVID-19 policy are imposing mounting operational uncertainty and cashflow pressures on businesses. This amplifies the project’s
            importance. Accelerating regulatory reforms and policy changes on the ground in business and trade regulations is even more important to
            reduce external constraints on firms and improve firm’s productivity and resilience to shocks.
       2. The project remains on track to achieve its development objectives. Good progress continues to be achieved in simplifying
            procedures to start a business. The matching grants under the Second Business Assistance Facility is facing headwinds amid COVID-19
            and the recent macro-pressures but remain effective in assisting firm competitiveness. Close attention to the Operating licenses agenda,

              the broader business climate reforms and the implementation of the joint risk management framework is required to secure real gains for
              businesses in Lao PDR.
      3.      Pillar A (Improving the Business Environment). The average time to obtain the Enterprise Registration Certificate nationwide is 1.94
              days (as of May 2022) down from 2.23 days six months ago. The Draft revised Enterprise Law reflects well the streamlined enterprise
              registration process. On operating licenses (OL), progress are picking up. The MOIC OL task force made good progress on several OL
              simplification. The inter-ministerial task force prepared the list of OL to be streamlined.
      4.      Pillar B (Facilitating Trade and Connecting to Markets). Implementation of the TFA Challenge Facility (CF) Proposals needs to be
              accelerated. Progress was made in supporting the National Trade Facilitation Committee (NTFC) in leading the dialogue on trade
              facilitation issues and reform agenda. Border regulatory agencies are implementing recommendations from the 2020 TRS for regulatory
              and procedural reforms under import and export permits, licenses and certification. Both Department of Import and Export and Food and
              Drug Department have reviewed and streamlined procedures for issuing import licenses resulting in significant reductions in processing
              times. The Joint Risk Management Framework was signed on December 13, 2021 to promote inter-agency border risk management and
              joint border inspection. Standard Operating Procedures on risk-based border inspection are being developed by Food and Drug
              Department, Department of Livestock and Fisheries and Department of Agriculture.
      5.      Under pillar C (Improving Firm-level Competitiveness) BAF-2 continues to help private enterprises access business development
              services including product designs, marketing, software development and training to improve management and innovation capacity. By
              June 30, 2022, 287 matching grants had been approved to firms in Lao PDR (48 since January 2022 alone) providing USD1.55 million
              excluding taxes in support to Lao businesses. Among those 171 approved matching grants were to women-led businesses.
      6.      Pillar D (Supporting Better and More Inclusive Policies). The new safeguards instruments added with the AF and applicable to all
              project activities continue to be implemented. The dialogue between the government and the private sector on trade and private sector
              development issues has been conducted though technical discussion with LBF secretariat and alignment of Government priorities and
              donors support in this area through the Trade Private Sector Working Group.
