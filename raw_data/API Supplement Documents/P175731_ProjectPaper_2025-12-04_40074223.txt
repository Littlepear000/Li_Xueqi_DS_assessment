    I.        BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING

     Background

     1.     The SADC Regional Statistics Project, approved on June 8, 2023, seeks to modernize data ecosystems of
     participating countries by addressing gaps in data production, harmonization, and use. The PDO is to strengthen the
     institutional capacity of SADC and Participating Countries to produce, disseminate, and use quality statistics while
     increasing regional harmonization and collaboration. The project includes seven recipients: Comoros (US$1.5 million),
     Madagascar (US$45 million), Malawi (US$15 million), Mozambique (US$60 million), São Tomé and Príncipe (US$3
     million), Zambia (US$30 million), and the SADC Secretariat (US$10 million), for a total amount of US$164.5 million. The
     closing date is October 31, 2028.

         2.   Project description. The project comprises the following components:

              • Component 1: Regional Component: Improve harmonization, quality, and dissemination of core social and
                economic statistics. The regional component supports the implementation of continental and regional
                agreements on statistics – specifically, the SADC’s Regional Strategy for Development of Statistics (RSDS) 2020-
                2030 -by improving the capacity of the SADC Secretariat to help Member States improve harmonization,
                quality, and dissemination of core social and economic statistics.
              • Component 2: Country Component: Close gaps in data production, statistical capacity, and data use at
                country level. The country component finances key data collection and production (such as National Accounts,
                Consumer Price Index (CPI), Household and Expenditure Surveys, and Agriculture and Livestock Censuses), as
                well as statistical capacity building and data use activities.
              • Component 3: Project management ensures the implementation of the project in each participating entity.
              • Component 4: Contingency Emergency Response Component (CERC) for Malawi and Madagascar only.

     3.    The IDA Grant E1860 for Comoros in the amount of SDR 1.2 million (US$1.5 million equivalent) became effective
     on August 29, 2023. Parent project activities in Comoros include: (a) Economic data and National Accounts; (b)
     Household Survey; and (c) CPI data collection and improvement. The project supports the production of new national
     accounts for the period 2019-2025, consistent with the 2008 United Nations System of National Accounts (SNA) and
     rebased to 2019. In addition, the project provides technical assistance to update the CPI and finance the extension of
     the CPI coverage of collection points to include all three autonomous islands1. The project also finances the upgrade of
     the ICT equipment and operational materials to ensure efficient workflows for data collection, processing, analysis,
     dissemination and archiving. The project also supports skills development for staff of the national statistics institute of
     Comoros, Institut National de la Statistique et des Etudes Economiques et Démographiques (INSEED), through training
     and capacity building initiatives and builds statistical capacity in the country by providing scholarships for students in
     renowned African statistical programs.

     4.    Status of Implementation in Comoros. The project in Comoros has been progressing well and completed most of
     the planned activities. The disbursement rate is 100 percent, with limited unutilized resources for the remainder of the
     project duration. The Client has completed work on Economic Data and National Accounts Rebasing and is preparing to
     present the updated figures to authorities ahead of a national dissemination. The Household Survey was finalized in
     December 2024, with data cleaning completed and publication of key statistics expected in December 2025. The client
     is preparing to update the CPI reference year from 2011 to 2024 and has extended the geographical coverage of CPI to

  III. KEY RISKS

26. The overall project risk remains as Moderate. The proposed AF is not expected to cause any major change in the
current risk ratings. Several activities proposed in the AF are continuation from the first phase of the project with no
major changes. For the additional new activities, INSEED and the PIU have already significant experiences since their
core activities consist of the same process: data collection, data analysis and capacity building.

27. Fiduciary Risk for the project remains Substantial, mainly due to low financial management (FM) and
procurement capacity in several participating countries. For Comoros, the procurement risk rating is substantial due to

funds flow as the project is highly decentralized. Ongoing monitoring and mitigation of any potential risks will be made
through regular reporting on the progress and implementation of fiduciary activities, the World Bank implementation
support, and further capacity building.

   IV. APPRAISAL SUMMARY

A. Economic Analysis

28. Cost-benefit analysis of statistical capacity building projects: Though the cost-benefit analysis of statistical
capacity building projects is not easily quantifiable, it is well established that official statistics are a public good necessary
for effective policy design, implementation, and monitoring.

29. Improved data availability, open access, and capacity to use data will facilitate evidence-based decision-making
for achieving development goals. The population census provides crucial information on the size, location, and socio-
economic characteristics of the population at the smallest geographic level throughout the country. In addition, the


                                           Official Use Only                                                              Page 7
         The World Bank
         Comoros Additional Financing for SADC Regional Statistics Project (P175731)


economic census will provide new information on the size and structure of the economy. This information will provide
critical insights for public policy makers and the private sector to identify and improve targeting of public and private
investments. Quality census data is also necessary to improve the quality and reliability of the entire national statistics
system, thereby enabling better accountability, governance, and monitoring and evaluation. The supported work on
economic data will enhance the government’s ability to monitor and evaluate the extent to which policies and
investments are translating into growth. Evidence-based policymaking, combined with the ability to track results can
lead to better development outcomes for the population.

B. Technical

30. The proposed technical design builds on the successful implementation of the first phase and reflects the
specific needs of Comoros’s national statistical system . It prioritizes high-impact data production activities—such as
the Population and Economic Censuses—while ensuring continuity in core statistical domains like national accounts and
price statistics. The design is appropriate to Comoros context, balancing ambition with implementation capacity through
careful sequencing and targeted capacity building. It also conforms to international standards, including the United
Nations System of National Accounts (2008 SNA), the United Nations Statistics Division (UNSD) guidelines for gender-
sensitive indicators, and regional harmonization frameworks under SADC. The inclusion of diaspora data collection and
thematic agricultural surveys further reflects global best practices in statistical modernization and ensures relevance to
national development priorities.

C. Financial Management

31. The Financial Management (FM) arrangements for the AF were assessed as satisfactory and with the adoption
of proposed risk mitigating measures, the residual FM risk is assessed as substantial. An FM assessment of the current
PIU implementing the parent project concluded that its existing FM arrangements will provide reasonable assurance
that funds provided by the World Bank will be used for the intended purposes. The most recent FM supervision of the
parent project, conducted in May 2025, concluded that the FM arrangements in place are generally sound, the
performance was assessed as satisfactory, with a Substantial residual FM risk. As a result, no changes are expected to
the agreed arrangements under the parent project within the PIU.

32. The project complies with its Interim Financial Report (IFR) covenants. IFRs are submitted timely. The first audit
report of the parent project was received on time. The auditor issued an unmodified opinion, meaning the financial

the internal control system, primarily: (i) compliance with procedures for documenting expenditures and implementing
controls; and (ii) compliance with procurement procedures and the associated documentation. The PIU, working closely
with the World Bank, has implemented the recommendations of the auditor.

33. The Project Operational Manual (POM) will be updated by the PIU to extend the existing FM arrangements to
the AF. This will include: the preparation and submission to the World Bank, within 45 days after the quarter end, of
quarterly unaudited interim financial reports; the preparation of annual financial statements subject to audit by an
independent external auditor, acceptable to the World Bank, and the submission of these to the World Bank along with
a management letter, no later than six months after the end of each fiscal year-end.

H. Climate Screening

42. Comoros faces severe climate challenges, and the project design incorporates climate risks in its
implementation plans. Comoros is experiencing rising temperatures, shifting rainfall patterns, and frequent cyclones,
droughts, and floods, threatening water security. The country has already warmed by 0.9℃, intensifying drought risks,
while sea levels—rising to 6mm annually since 1993—continue to endanger coastal infrastructure, particularly in Grand
Moroni. The project considered risks of floods and extreme heat in climate and disaster hazard areas in planning the
timing of data collection activities, and contingency plans exist to adjust plans in the case of the occurrence of a disaster
risk.

I. Paris Alignment

43. The proposed AF aligns with the Paris Agreement on both mitigation and adaptation. The AF is consistent with
Comoros’ climate commitments as outlined in its Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC) submitted to the United
Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). The NDC emphasizes the importance of robust statistical


                                         Official Use Only                                                          Page 10
         The World Bank
         Comoros Additional Financing for SADC Regional Statistics Project (P175731)


systems for climate-informed decision-making, particularly in areas such as disaster risk management, agriculture, and
urban planning.

44. Mitigation. The project’s activities are considered “universally aligned” with the Paris Agreement’s mitigation
goals. The production and dissemination of statistical data, capacity building, and institutional strengthening are
inherently low-carbon activities and do not pose a risk of increasing greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions or creating
persistent barriers to a low-emissions development pathway. The project does not involve major civil works, energy-
intensive processes, or activities that could result in significant GHG emissions. Equipment procurement (such as IT
hardware and office supplies) is limited in scale and scope, and does not materially affect Comoros’ emissions profile.
The project is therefore assessed as having negligible mitigation risk.

45. Adaptation. The population census, business census, and digitized cartography for the population census will
provide critical information for disaster risk planning and management by enabling analysis of the number and
characteristics of households and businesses in areas at risk or that have experienced a climate-related disaster.
Agricultural surveys can provide insights into how the sector is affected by and adapting to climate change and
opportunities for further adaptation and mitigation measures in the sector. Improved economic data, including rebased
national accounts and national CPI data, enable more reliable analysis of the economic impact of climate-related shocks.

J. Data Privacy.

46. To ensure data privacy, INSEED will ensure that the collection, use, and processing (including transfers to third
parties) of any Personal Data collected under the Project is done in accordance with the applicable national regulations

manner.

ENVIRONMENTAL & SOCIAL

Overview
Operation Location
 Operation location and salient physical characteristics relevant to the ESS Assessment (geographic, environmental,
 social) (if known)
 The scope of the additional financing is expected to cover all three islands: Grande Comore (Ngazidja); Mohéli (Mwali);
 and Anjouan (Nzwani).
 Detailed operation location(s) and salient physical characteristics relevant to the ESS Assessment (geographic,
 environmental, social)
 The proposed Project is a regional integration project which intends to strengthen the regional statistical system
 through supporting the harmonization, production, dissemination, and use of quality controlled social and economic
 statistics for evidence-based policy making. The project invests primarily in technical aspects to strengthen the
 institutional capacity of SADC and participating countries to produce, disseminate and use quality statistics while
 increasing regional harmonization and collaboration. The Union of Comoros is a small island developing state (SIDS) off
 the coast of East Africa, with a population of about 850,000. The country faces persistent development challenges due
 to its small size, geographic isolation, vulnerability to climate shocks, and high dependence on imports, remittances,
 and external aid. The national poverty rate increased from 35.2% in 2018 to 38.3% in 2022, with rural areas and youth
 particularly affected. Poverty is higher and more severe in rural areas, where 24.6% of the population is severely food
 insecure, compared to 17.9% in urban areas. The AF will scale up by continuing ongoing activities (national account and
 prices statistics) and adding new activities (population census, economic census, thematic agricultural surveys, and
 refurbishments of offices) aligned with the project objectives, and revise the results indicators and targets under the
 project’s country component in Comoros (Component 2). INSEED is planning to sequence AF activities by prioritizing
 the largest initiatives to begin as early as January 2026. These include the Population Census, the Business Census, and
 the renovation of the INSEED office building. These activities are national in scope, often crossing regions with
 heightened vulnerability due to poverty, limited state presence, and periods of insecurity.
 Borrower’s Institutional Capacity
 In Comoros, the implementing agency for the Project is the Ministry of Finance, Budget and Banking Sector through
 the INSEED (Institut National de la Statistique et des Études Économiques et Démographe) which has established a PIU.
 The project will continue to be implemented under the existing institutional arrangements of the parent project. An
 environmental and social specialist is operational to support managing the project E&S risks and impacts. The Specialist
 has benefited from training sessions on E&S risk management and has demonstrated satisfactory capacity during the
 first years of the project's implementation. Relevant capacity building on ESF will be continuously conducted to
 enhance the capacity of the Staff at the PIU, for the contractors and the whole Stakeholder.

Summary of Assessment of Environmental and Social Risks and Impacts
 The environmental and social risk rating has been classified as Moderate and the environmental and social
 performance of the parent project satisfactory for Comoros. Key adverse environmental and social risks and impacts
 relate to ensuring that (i) minor civil works and installation of ICT equipment are managed adequately considering
 mitigation hierarchy, particularly in relation to construction waste generation and occupational health and safety
 issues; (ii) the replacement, transportation and disposal of old ITC equipment (e-waste) as well new acquired are
 undertaken properly; (iii) TA activities including face-to-face trainings, workshops and statistical survey follows
 prevention measure against COVID-19; (iv) the Terms of Reference for TA type-2 activities (review or development of
 new policies, plans & strategies) have been reviewed and approved by WB to ensure consistency with the ESF; (v) any
 statistical guidelines and frameworks established under the project include considerations of digital data protection
 and digital security; (vi) there is appropriate stakeholder engagement at the regional and national level with all
 stakeholders, including civil society and marginalized groups, in a manner that is understandable and transparent and
 explains the benefits and impacts of the activities; (vii) labor conditions, especially with regards to HR restructuring
 activities, salary re-adjustments and skills needs assessments, as well as occupational health and safety (minor cuts
 and injuries, road safety, etc. during work); (viii) ensuring that inclusion and data protection covers vulnerable and
 marginalized groups; and (iv) possible risks of sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment (SEA/SH) at the
