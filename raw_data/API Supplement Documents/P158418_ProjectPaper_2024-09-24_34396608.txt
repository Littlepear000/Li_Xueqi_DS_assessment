I.    BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING


 A. Introduction and Context

 Country Context

 1.  Türkiye’s development achievements over the past two decades have been remarkable. Real gross domestic
    product (GDP) growth averaged 5.4 percent between 2002 and 2022, resulting in income per capita (in real terms)
    that was more than double over the same period. Moreover, growth was accompanied by rapid poverty reduction,
    with the poverty rate (US$6.85 2017 PPP poverty line) halving from above 20 percent in 2007 to less than 10
    percent in 2022. As in other countries, the COVID-19 pandemic had a negative impact on growth in 2020, but the
    country was one of the few that did not register a GDP contraction that year, instead it’s GDP grew by 1.9 percent.
    This performance was due, to a large extent, to the government’s economic policy response to said pandemic,
    which focused on loosening monetary policy and rapid credit expansion. Moreover, supported by domestic and
    external demand, Türkiye achieved double-digit GDP growth in 2021 (11.4 percent) and maintained significant
    momentum in 2022 (5.5 percent) and 2023 (5.1 percent).
 2. However, the policy framework that ensured a strong economic performance during and after the COVID-19
    pandemic also heightened macroeconomic risks. As a result of the monetary policy, the country experienced high
    inflation (with annual inflation at 52.0 percent in August 2024 after having peaked at 85.5 percent in October
    2022), currency depreciation (82 percent against the US$ between January 2020 and September 2024), corporate
    and banking sector vulnerabilities, and declines in reserve buffers.
 3. Following the May 2023 elections, the Government has taken steps towards normalizing the economy in a
    gradual way in order to manage risks associated with the adjustment process. This included monetary policy
    tightening, of interest rates increasing from 8.5 percent in May 2023 to 50 percent in March 2024, the unwinding
    of distortive financial regulations, and fiscal revenue measures to curtail the fiscal deficit. Markets are reacting
    positively with 5-year Credit Default Swaps (CDSs) declining from above 500 basis points (bps) in May 2023 to
    around 250bps in September 2024, all three of the major rating agencies upgraded the credit rating (most recently
    Fitch to BB- on September 6, 2024). The authorities are also contemplating on how to complement these actions
    with structural reforms that may help with growth prospects going forward. These efforts will need to be sustained
    and supported in the coming months.


Sectoral and Institutional Context

 4. With over 60,000 km2 of irrigated land, a sophisticated State Hydraulic Works Agency, and traditionally high
    emphasis on extending service delivery, Türkiye is one of the top irrigation and drainage countries in the world.
    Türkiye’s agriculture is heavily dependent on irrigation, which triples productivity compared with rain-fed
    agriculture and provides resilience of steady agricultural production and food security. Climate change patterns

       are expected to significantly impact yields, especially through increased water scarcity, threatening agricultural
       productivity, rural incomes and employment, and food security.
    5. Türkiye is a water-stressed country, with climate change, urbanization, and increasing demand from economic
       activities posing significant risks to its water security. Over two-thirds of the country’s 25 river basins are facing
       water scarcity, yet demand is steadily increasing. In 2018, total water withdrawals reached 61.1 billion m3, double
       compared with 28.1 billion m3 in 1990 and is estimated to be about 112 billion m3 in 2024. About 77 percent of
       annual freshwater withdrawals in Türkiye is used for irrigation and 23 percent for domestic and industrial
       purposes. An estimated 95 percent of exploitable groundwater resources are already being used for irrigation,
       domestic, and industrial purposes, with irrigation consuming about two-thirds of the total amount.
    6. Climate change is one of the most critical risks to Türkiye’s development. Frequency and Severity of Climate
       change and disaster-related vulnerabilities and extreme events have increased and are exacerbating poverty in
       Türkiye. The water sector is the primary medium through which the negative impacts of climate change are felt.
       The sector is also a key entry point for interventions to ensure effective adaptation and build resilience to climate
       change. The negative impacts of climate induced water scarcity have been particularly felt in basins where
       populations and economic activity are concentrated, including those hosting large cities and economic hubs such
       as İstanbul, Ankara, İzmir, and Antalya, as well as important agricultural areas, such as the Konya plains. According
       to a 2016 Climate Change Assessment, water demand may exceed Türkiye’s exploitable water level as early as the
       2030s in a worst-case scenario. Irrigated agriculture would be the hardest hit, which would in turn affect the
       country’s economy. According to Türkiye’s Climate Change Development Report (CCDR), 1 a 10 percent reduction
       in water supply could cost Türkiye 6 percent of GDP, about US$50.0 billion per year.
    7. Projected increase in population and decreasing agriculture area poses risk to food supply in the country.
       Irrigation opens possibilities to grow a whole range of crops, boosting wheat and barley as well as corn, sugar,
       cotton, citrus, grapes, and various horticulture crops. Yet Türkiye’s irrigation sector is still characterized by
       widespread low-performing irrigation systems and obsolete infrastructure based on open channel and traditional
       systems in many parts of the country that contribute to substantial losses and unsustainable use of water
       resources. Moreover, many farmers are tapping into limited groundwater supplies contributing to over-
       exploitation of the resource. Thus, the Government has launched a 10-year Water Efficiency Action Plan (2023 to
       2033), which includes modernizing 1.3 million hectares of irrigated area with an estimated cost of US$6.5 billion.
       This plan is supported in part by the World Bank through the ongoing Türkiye Irrigation Modernization Project
       (TIMP)(P158418) and Water Circularity and Efficiency Improvement Project (WCEIP)(P174915).

    B. Project Status and Background

    8. This Project Paper seeks the approval of the Board of Executive Directors to provide an Additional Financing
       (AF) loan in the amount of EUR 87.7 million (US$95.0 million equivalent) to the Republic of Türkiye for the
       Türkiye Irrigation Modernization Project, to cover the cost overrun within the existing project scope. The
       original Türkiye Irrigation Modernization Project was financed through an International Bank for Reconstruction

    9. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to improve irrigation service delivery, through rehabilitation and
        modernization of distribution infrastructure in selected schemes, and through strengthening of capacity of
        Water User Associations (WUAs). The parent Irrigation Modernization project (TIMP) is aimed to deliver several
        key outcomes, including increasing the net irrigation area under operation, raising irrigation efficiency, expanding
        the use of water-saving modern irrigation systems, decreasing the use of groundwater as well as ensuring more
        equitable engagement among female farmers in WUAs. The total irrigation service area planned to be improved
        under the project is 50,764 hectares, of which 15,280 hectares are completed. The length of modernized irrigation
        network already reached 694.54 km (i.e., more than one third of the total 1,521 km envisaged for the project
        financing). The project also aims to provide pressurized and more reliable irrigation supply to farmers, allowing
        cultivation of high-value crops, reducing water losses, and increasing irrigation efficiency. It is expected to benefit
        about 17,312 water users, with a focus on increasing the incomes of farmers, contributing to general economic
        growth in the project areas, strengthening the enabling environment and sector capacity for managing water
        resources, and improving service delivery efficiency and resilience through promotion of innovative technical and
        management solutions. The TIMP delivered on addressing climate change-exacerbated water scarcity and
        lowering Greenhouse Gas (GHG) emissions via a comprehensive initiative aimed at improving irrigation efficiency,
        promoting water savings, and enhancing the livelihoods of farmers in Türkiye.
    10. The parent project includes three Components: Component (1) ‘Irrigation systems modernization’ to modernize
        Devlet Su İşleri 2 (DSI)’s irrigation systems in selected schemes; Component (2) ‘Innovation and institutional
        support’, to support DSI on designing and piloting a National Program for WUA Capacity-Building, and
        implementation of a sustainable energy and groundwater management pilot; and Component (3) ‘Project
        management’. As appraised, the project components and activities were aligned with the Government of
        Türkiye’s (GoT's) national development priorities, as outlined in its 10th Development Plan, including a
        “Transformational Program” (2014-2018) for improving the efficiency of water use in agriculture, and continues
        to be aligned with the country’s current 12th National Development Plan for 2024-2028 3 and the General
        Directorate of State Hydraulic Works development strategy.
    11. Progress towards PDO and Overall Implementation Progress. The progress on the PDO achievement is
        Moderately Satisfactory. Disbursement stands at US$113.2 million (45 percent of the total project funds,
        including 44.2 percent of the IBRD loan disbursed, while disbursement of GEF grant reached 95 percent). Farmers
        in the project-supported areas are reporting significant benefits due to improvements in irrigation delivery
        services including shielding them against the risks of uncertain rainfall and unreliable irrigation schedule,

    reduction in crop and livestock production costs, increases in cropping intensity, increases in crop yield, increased
    total production, and diversification towards high value crops. The project has made good progress on reaching
    female farmers: 1 in 5 water users who were provided with improved irrigation services were female, with the
    project reaching 250 female water users out of the target of 519. Overall, the emerging results show that the
    project contributed to sustaining the livelihoods of farmers, who hitherto were affected by the scarcity of
    irrigation water, which was exacerbated by climate change. The component-by-component implementation
    progress is presented below.
12. Component 1. Irrigation Systems Modernization (IBRD, US$249 million): The loan and grant funds are largely
    earmarked for the works at five irrigation schemes- namely, Atabey, Alasehir, Eregli-İvriz, Uzunlu irrigation
    schemes - financed from the loan, and Bolvadin irrigation scheme financed under the GEF grant. The overall
    works progress rate under the ongoing civil work contracts is 48 percent and contributed to total disbursement
    of US$104 million of the project funds. The construction works are progressing well in the Eregli-Ivriz and Uzunlu
    irrigation schemes. At Eregli-Ivriz irrigation scheme, the contract has been completed, and the work is in the
    provisional acceptance phase- one year earlier than the official contract end date of May 11, 2025. At Uzunlu
    irrigation scheme, 80 percent the works is completed, and the contract is expected to be completed in December
    2024. However, the work implementation progress for Alasehir and Atabey irrigation schemes, which constitute
    47 percent of the total work contracts of the project has been unsatisfactory due to poor performance of the
    contractor that won the contracts for Atabey and Alasehir; and these contracts were terminated on August 8,
    2023, and May 3, 2023, respectively and rebid. The current disbursement level is only 9.4 percent and 1.2 percent
    of the total signed contract values for Atabey and Alasehir, respectively. Furthermore, the inability of the initial
    contractor to start the works on time exposed the project to significant cost overrun due to inflation and currency
    devaluation.
13. Component 2: Innovation and Institutional Support (GEF, US$2 million): Implementation is progressing well.
    Irrigation Facilities Spatial Information System (SUTEM) is developed and is under temporary acceptance,
    warranty, and maintenance phase. The necessary hardware for the implementation of the SUTEM software has
    been already procured and distributed to WUAs. The SUTEM software testing and verification phase is expected
    to be completed on December 24, 2024. The SUTEM software training is completed with the execution of a three
    day long five training sessions from third week of March 2024 to first week of April 2024. A total of 803 personnel

    mechanisms to engage women. As a result, the project conducted 10 WUA consultations and is starting to track
    female consultation participants reported increased awareness of WUA activities. While this initial percentage is
    9.8 percent out of a target 90 percent, the project expects to conduct a robust follow-up survey to assess
    awareness at the end of project completion. Additionally, the project will make a more concerted effort to
    increase women’s awareness of the activities by ensuring the consultations are implemented in all WUAs,
    including the female engineers from WUAs in outreach/follow-up to female farmers, and centering the
    consultations around benefits for female farmers (i.e., explaining the new gender mainstreaming program and
    how female farmers will benefit).
55. Climate Change. There is no change in project design, components, and activities. Thus, the carbon balance
