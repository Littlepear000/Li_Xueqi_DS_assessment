I. PROGRAM STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING


A. Program Status
1.      The Punjab Resource Improvement and Digital Effectiveness (PRIDE) Program for Results (PforR) was approved
by the World Bank’s Board of Executive Directors on October 22, 2020 and became effective on December 17, 2020. The
Program has an overall allocation of US$304 million and aims at increasing own source revenue, and improving reliability
of resource allocation and access to digital services for people and firms in the province of Punjab, by focusing on the
folowing three key result areas:
       Result Area 1: Strengthened budget formulation and fiscal risk management;
       Result Area 2: Increased use of digital technology for delivery of selected public services; and
       Result Area 3: Improved collection of own source revenue (OSR).

2.      The overall progress towards achievement of the Program Development Objective (PDO) is rated Satisfactory and
Implementation Progress (IP) is rated Moderately Satisfactory1. Program implementation remains on track and
disbursements, currently at 42 percent, continue to improve.
3.      Program restructuring is carried out in the light of the findings of Mid-Term Review and aims at improving the
program performance, allow for successful implementation of key pending activities, give an opportunity to the Client to
achieve the targeted DLRs, improve disbursements, achieve the overall PDO, and to factor in some of the effects of activities
which are beyond the control of the Program.
Part 1: The Program ($274 million)
Result Area 1: Strengthened budget formulation and fiscal risk management
4.     To improve fiscal risk management, the Government of Punjab (GoPb) has published a Fiscal Risk Statement (FRS)
on the website of the Finance Department (FD) (https://finance.punjab.gov.pk/system/files/FRS22-23.pdf), following
Cabinet approval of the FD fiscal policy on disclosure of fiscal restraint. Accordingly, the FRS was presented to the Cabinet
and the provincial assembly as part of the set of budget documents. The Statement discusses key critical risks, such as
pension expenditure, commodity debt, disaster risks and buffers for liquidity management, and is a major step towards
improved management of fiscal risks.
5.      The Government has drafted a Public Financial Management (PFM) Law that has been approved by the Cabinet.
This law is now notified after approval by the Cabinet.
6.      The Pension Reform Plan was prepared and approved by Cabinet in December 2022. Upon review, the World Bank
found the plan to be far-reaching and the reforms, if implemented, should be sufficient to make a fiscal impact and satisfy
the objectives of DLI 1 (moderate and reduce the annual pension cost of the GoPb).
7.     A Disaster Risk Financing Strategy (DRFS) has been prepared and is under consideration by FD and the Punjab
Board of Revenue (BoR). The draft of the Strategy is yet to be finalized.
8.     Enhancing the transparency of the Commodity Debt Stock is an important intervention covered by the Program.
Progress on the reform has been slow and requires better collaboration between FD and the Food Department.
Result Area 2: Increased use of digital technology for delivery of selected public services

9.       The Program has achieved significant results under this results area. As of June 2022, 4,491,655 citizens, of which
12 percent are female, have access to digital services. As per the recent data shared by GoPb, they have surpassed the end
target of 1,800,000 digital users for the related PDO-level indicator, which is currently being reviewed by a third party
verification agency.
10.      There is a need to further expand the scope considering the potential achievement of the final target for this
indicator and the Citizen Perception Survey completed by the GoPb. Although the number of services offered digitally has
increased from nine to 20, each service is being provided through stand-alone platforms, such as e-auction, Baldia online
and Police Station. These include patient registration, death certificate, domicile certificate, police verification certificate,
violence against women, consumer protection, etc. The Government is now focusing on developing a single platform to
avoid fragmented landscape of systems. Key components of this government-wide uniform architecture include Digital
Identification (ID), interoperability and digital payment. The Government has prepared initial design of these components
which is being coordinated with federal agencies, such as the National Database and Registration Authority (NADRA) and
State Bank of Pakistan, among others. The restructuring proposal includes enhancing the scope of DLI 4 by adding a new
DLR to support the establishment of a single digital platform.
11.     After a slow start, the activities to implement the e-government procurement (e-GP) system are picking up.
Regulations to facilitate the e-Procurement process are notified. However, these regulations would now require a cover of
an enabling clause in the Punjab Public Procurement Rules 2014 to satisfy the requirements of the Disbursement Linked
Results. Standard Bidding Documents (SBDs) are prepared, and extensive consultations with the Government and private
sector practitioners are being held. The SBDs are approved by the Punjab Public Procurement Regulatory Authority (P-PPRA)
Board. P-PPRA is also working closely with the Federal PPRA (F-PPRA) in order to adopt the e-GP System which is being
developed by the F-PPRA. Adoption of a customized e-GP system for supporting the provincial Procurement Rules and
Regulations is in progress. This is an excellent example of close collaboration between the two PPRAs and their
commitments to work closely, as stipulated by the National Procurement Strategy. F-PPRA has arranged a few training
sessions exclusively for P-PPRA on few modules of the e-GP system which are developed by the Federal PPRA. Both PPRAs
have discussed and agreed on a draft Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) which will be by signed the Federal and Punjab
PPRAs for sharing of services once the P-PPRA approves the draft MoU. This will pave the way for piloting the e-GP System
in one of Punjab’s departments, fulfilling the DLI 3 (operational budget expenditure processed through the e-procurement
system) requirements.
12.     Accounting and Reporting System for Local Governments is rolled out to 19 Local Government Authorities. The
GoPb is undergoing a third-party review of the electronic system. Based on the results of the review, some changes are
proposed in the timelines and targets of the associated DLI, to be processed through this restructuring (for more details,
please refer to the DLI Matrix below). An important action of this area was updating the Provincial Finance Commission
Award, which could not be achieved due to absence of elected local government in Punjab.
Result Area 3: Improved collection of OSR
13.      This result area is on track. Over the past two years, Punjab’s revenue mobilization effort has improved. Punjab’s
own-source tax collection increased from Rs. 188bn in FY20 to Rs. 277bn in FY22. An assessment of impact of COVID-19 on
tax collection has been finalized, approved and published on FD’s website. Punjab has also designed a three-year domestic
revenue mobilization (DRM) strategy, which is approved by the Cabinet for consistent implementation of envisaged reforms.
The strategy targets about 150 percent growth in tax collection (from Rs. 234 bn in FY21 to Rs. 586 bn in FY25), supported
by selected policy and administration measures.
14.     The database integration in tax administration is also on track, but to achieve future targets of the related DLI,
increased efforts are needed. The data integration plan has been approved by the Punjab Cabinet’s Committee on Finance.

The approved plan will help guide consistent implementation of targets under DLI 7. The Punjab Revenue Authority (PRA)
has created data linkages with three third parties, and is working on data linkages with additional third parties.
15.     The expansion of sales tax on services (STS) base and filing compliance is on track. During the past two years, the
STS base has expanded by 34 percent (from 82,285 in June 2020 to 109,950 registered taxpayers in June 2022), and filing
compliance has improved (from an average of 23,947 in FY20 to 66,199 in FY22). It is expected that PRA will achieve all
future DLI targets.
16.      However, the progress on indicators related to simplification and automation of key business processes for tax
administration, and enhanced capacity for generating revenue from real estate are moving slow, mainly due to initial
delay in approval of PC1 to activate the technical assistance (TA) funds for hiring consultants. The Terms of Reference (TORs)
to hire consultant to prepare business process reengineering plan have recently been approved. This study will also
complement the functional reviews of tax authorities, planned under the TA component of the Program.
Part 2: The Project ($30 million)
17.     IPF component of the Program is being implemented as per the approved annual work plan. Two Designated
Accounts are in operation. The first is operated by the Public Financial Management Reform Unit (PFMRU) and the second
is operated by Project Support Unit (PSU). Appropriate staffing for both units are hired, and the fiduciary systems are in
place. Consultants are being hired for technical assistance under the Program.
B. Rationale for Restructuring
18.    To improve the Program performance, allow for successful implementation of key pending activities, give an
opportunity to the Client to achieve the targeted DLRs, improve disbursements, achieve the overall PDO, and to factor in
some of the effects of activities which are beyond the control of the Program, few changes are introduced to the Results
Framework indicators and DLI Matrix, including allocations to DLRs, as described below.
