 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  This is an updated ISR based on Implementation Support Mission conducted November 13- December 6, 2023 and project progress are
  summarized below:
       1. Pillar A (Improving the Business Environment). Good progress was achieved (i) the design of the electronic Business Registration
            System (eBRS) is underway since end of June 2023. Agreements were reached with the Special Economic Zones (SEZ) Promotion and
            Management Office to ensure integration of SEZ businesses in the registry and with the Tax Authority to use the Tax Identification Number
            (TIN) as the unique ID for businesses as part of the new system; (ii) Four additional operating licenses were streamlined for agriculture
            and forestry businesses, job placement service businesses, environment compliance certificate, and auditing and accounting services
            businesses, bringing the total to 14, resulting in reducing procedural steps, time to obtain the license or increase in license validity.
       2. Pillar B (Facilitating Trade and Connecting to Markets). Significant progress has been made on several important Pillar B activities
            including (i) completion of the electronic trade statistics system (e-Stat); (ii) moderate progress in implementation of the Joint Risk

              Management Framework, including signing of the Joint Inspection SOP by the Minister of Finance; (iii) uploading into the ASYCUDA
              system of initial risk profiles by IAs; (iv) significant progress on the development of IT systems (NSWA+ and Dashboard and Alert system);
              and (v) a review of user fees by the LCD has resulted in a decision to rescind a new NSWA+ fee. Other key achievements include (vi) the
              first meeting of the newly constituted National Trade and Transport Facilitation Committee Meeting has been held in June 2023, Chaired
              by the Deputy Prime Minister; (vii) The 2022 Time Release Study (TRS) were completed and approved, and a final report was shared with
              stakeholders at the end of September 2023.
      3.      Pillar C (Improving Firm-level Competitiveness). Private-sector activity continues to be impacted by the exchange rate and inflation
              shocks although the later as moderated since the summer in 2023. Demand for BAF-II services remains strong. The BAF2 has excellent
              progress in extending grants, having reached 504 grant approvals above their internal target. These approvals total $2.460m, still well
              short of the total fund available of $3.150m. The total number of grants disbursed reached 401. The proportion of grants going to women-
              led firms (64 percent against 40 percent target), and to firms adversely impacted by the pandemic (202 grants against 100 target) are both
              well above the targets set. The Interim Impact Assessment results in 2023 showed that BAF2 supported firms had sales growth of 41%,
              compared to sales growth in comparable non-supported firms of only 14% during the same period.
      4.      Pillar D (Supporting Better and More Inclusive Policies). The integrated project level Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) is in
              place, but NIU needs to communicate effectively on the purpose of the tool as well as on different channels to report grievances, while
              recognizing that cultural issue restraining potential complainants might also be at play. Good progress made in the dialogue between the
              government and the private sector on trade and private sector development issues. Following the 14th Lao Business Forum (LBF), held
              on March 25, 2023, out of the 20 issues raised within LBF14 last year, 4 have now been fully resolved, and a further 13 have made what
              LBF describes as “good progress,” meaning that LBF is confident of an eventual agreement on the solution to be implemented. Only 3 are
              considered to be issues that “cannot be resolved.” The formal structure established, with a small high-level Steering Committee meeting
              quarterly, under the chairmanship of the Deputy Prime Minister is operational. The LBF Secretariat at the LNCCI consists of three
              permanent staff. Leadership and close collaboration between LNCCI and MOIC have led to further increase in the resolution rate for
              issues.
