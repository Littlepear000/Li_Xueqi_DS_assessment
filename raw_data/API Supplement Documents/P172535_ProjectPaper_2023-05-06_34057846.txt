I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING


A. Project Status

1. The International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) loan in the amount of US$100 million (Ln. 9246-
   CO) for the Resilient and Inclusive Housing Project (P172535), accompanied by (a) a concessional financing portion
   in the amount of US$21.7 million from the Global Concessional Financing Facility (GCFF), and (b) a grant in the amount
   of US$15 million from the IBRD Fund for Innovative Global Public Goods Solutions (GPG), was approved by the Board
   of Executive Directors on May 27, 2021 and declared effective on October 5, 2021. The closing date of the loan is July
   24, 2026. The Project Development Objective (PDO) of the original operation was to improve the quality of housing
   and public space for vulnerable households, including migrants from Venezuela, in selected municipalities. The
   project comprises three components: Improve Homes and Neighborhoods for Low-Income Households (Component
   1); Institutional Strengthening To Improve Homes and Neighborhoods (Component 2); and Project Management
   (Component 3).

2. The PDO remains relevant to the restructured operation, and the main PDO indicators remain achievable. The overall
   aim of the project is to improve the quality of housing and public space for vulnerable households, including migrants
   from Venezuela, in line with national targets in the housing sector. In March 2022, under subcomponent 1.1, the
   project disbursed US$5.7 million (4.2 percent of total funds) to finance home improvements for beneficiary
   households.

3. The status of activities under the three components is as follows:

    Component 1: Improve Homes and Neighborhoods for Low-Income Households. This component finances home
    improvement subsides for residents, and rental subsides for migrants from Venezuela, in selected municipalities
    (subcomponent 1.1); and finances targeted infrastructure investments in selected municipalities most affected by
    the migration from Venezuela (subcomponent 1.2).

    Under subcomponent 1.1, home improvement subsidies are provided for eligible households under the Casa Digna,
    Vida Digna (CDVD) program, and rental subsidies for migrants from Venezuela under Semillero de Propietarios, SDP
    program). The Ministry of Housing, City and Territory (Ministerio de Vivienda, Ciudad y Territorio, MVCT) will progress
    towards delivering approximately 3,080 home improvement interventions in eligible municipalities (1,200 in urban
    areas and 1,880 in peri-urban areas). The National Housing Fund (Fonvivienda) has made arrangements with the
    municipalities of Cucuta, Bucaramanga, Cartagena, Bogotá, Villavicencio, Sincelejo, Itagüí, Cali, Floridablanca, Villa
    del Rosario, Pereira, Riohacha, Maicao and Bello for the implementation ot these activities. In addition, the MVCT
    will be piloting one scheme in Bogota to allocate at least 200 rental subsidies and is advancing in conversations with
    the cities of Bucaramanga, Cali, and Medellín for 3 additional pilots, which would allow the allocation of additional
    rental subsidies. Under subcomponent 1.2, the MVCT has selected four neighborhood upgrading interventions in

    Arauca, Sincelejo, Cartagena, and Soledad, and 10 public space projects (parks) to which the WB has already given its
    No Objection. The MVCT is advancing in the hiring of the Implementing Agent and is expected to start its operational
    activities by April 2023.

    Component 2: Institutional Strengthening to Improve Homes and Neighborhoods. This component finances
    technical assistance to build the capacity of the MVCT and its main counterparts, including municipalities affected by
    the migration from Venezuela. The project will make progress on the hiring and implementation of technical
    assistance activities, including those relevant to the Government of Colombia’s new technical guidelines for (i)
    sustainable and alternative materials for home improvement, and (ii) the Habitat Participatory Network. Likewise,
    the technical directions of the MVCT are exploring new assistance needs, where the definition of economic impacts
    derived from housing improvement and community participation has been raised.

    Component 3: Project Management. This component finances the creation and staffing of a Project Implementation
    Unit (PIU) within the MVCT, as well as eligible activities such as the engagement of consulting services; capacity
    building and technical assistance to carry out procurement, safeguards, financial management and fiduciary services
    requirements; outreach campaigns to disseminate project activities; project auditing; a baseline survey of
    beneficiaries; monitoring and evaluation; and citizen engagement. The MVCT has completed the hiring of PIU staff.

4. The project has no outstanding audit reports and no unresolved audit observations. The ratings for Financial
   Management, Procurement, Monitoring and Evaluation, Project Management and Environmental and Social
   Standards are satisfactory or moderately satisfactory.

B. Rationale for Restructuring

5. The Government requested a minor restructuring of the project on August 19, 2022 in order to adjust the scope of
   the rental program and increase the flexibility of the grant from the Global Public Goods Fund. The aim of this
   restructuring is to allow the project to (a) improve the operational efficiency of the rental subsidy by expanding
   available options to families to find adequate housing units on the market; (b) ensure compliance with resilience
   standards through a third-party verification system to assess housing quality conditions at scale; and (c) provide more
   flexibility to implement the project’s funds, given the Government’s internal processing requirements and timeline
   for approving the budget allocation of trust fund resources.



II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES


6. Based on the Government’s request and its technical discussions with the Bank team, the proposed changes to the
   project are as follows: (a) modification of the amounts offered as rental subsidies to migrants from Venezuela; (b)
   adjustment to the mechanism to assign rental subsidies to migrants from Venezuela; (c) revision of the Results
   Framework; and (d) adjustment of the Legal Covenants. The proposed changes were discussed and agreed to during
        The World Bank
        Colombia: Resilient and Inclusive Housing Project (P172535)


   the supervision and implementation missions carried out in March 2022 and September 2022, confirmed during the
   implementation mission carried out in January 2023, and were reflected in the corresponding Aide Mémoires. Details
   of these changes are described below.

7. Rental Subsides. Through the restructuring, the amount offered as rental subsidies to migrants from Venezuela may
   be equivalent to up to 45 percent of a monthly minimum wage (MW) for a 12-month period. Under the original
   project, rental subsidies are transferred to a specific lessor after (a) signing a Contrato de Mandato between the
   property owner and the Real Estate Agent; (b) the lessor and the lessee sign a rental agreement; and (c) the rental
   subsidy is allocated to the selected beneficiary households through a Resolution of Allocation from Fonvivienda. The
   restructuring makes it possible for the lessor and lessee to sign a rental agreement directly, with no real estate agent
   or Contrato de Mandato involved. As before, Fonvivienda would allocate the rental subsidy to the beneficiary
   household through a Resolution of Allocation from Fonvivienda. The MVCT and Fonvivienda will verify that the
   housing units are suitable for rent, according to criteria set out in the Project Operations Manual.

8. Changes to the Results Framework: The increase in the amount the rental subsidy granted to migrants from
   Venezuela impacts the end target value of the PDO indicator “Number of rental subsidies granted to migrant
   households from Venezuela.”

10. Disbursement condition. The original Financing Agreement requires that each disbursement must be made on a pari-
    passu basis and comply with the set ratio of 82.2 : 17.8 percent between the IBRD and GCFF (Global Concessional
    Financing Facility) portions of the financing. The original agreement also provides that no withdrawal from the Grant
    Account can be made unless it is contemporaneously made from the Financing Account (IBRD plus GCFF funds), which
    together cover 89 percent of the eligible expenditures. The restructuring will allow the MVCT to request
    disbursements from the GPG grant independently from the IBRD and GCFF portion of the financing, but maintains
    the ratio originally established between the IBRD and GCFF portions. Consequently, the Withdrawal Table in the
    Financing Agreement of IBRD Loan 9246-CO and GCFF TF B6168-CO will be updated to reflect that the Bank may
    finance up to 100% of expenditures, but excluding expenditures covered by the Grant. Likewise, the Withdrawal
    Table in the Grant Agreement of GPG TF B6381-CO will be revised to indicate that the Bank may finance up to 100%
    of expenditures, but excluding expenditures covered by the Loan and Concessional Portion of the Financing.

11. Other changes. Clarifications will be introduced in two definitions in the Appendix of the Loan Agreement
    (“Cooperation Agreement”) which refers to the administrative arrangement entered between Fonvivienda and each
    Territorial Entity. This arrangement differs from a Convenio Interadministrativo and from the rental subsidies. Also in
    the revised Appendix, the term “Lessee” will be defined.
