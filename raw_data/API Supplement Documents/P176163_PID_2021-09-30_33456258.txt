  B. Introduction and Context

  Country Context

 1.      Albania became an upper-middle-income country in 2008 and its GDP since then grew on average by 3.2 percent
 per year. Poverty fell by 2.3 percent in 2014- 2017. The projected economic contraction of about 3.3 percent in 2020 (vs.
 2.2 percent growth in 2019) reflects Albania’s dependence on tourism sector and remittances. Albania’s fiscal deficit of
 6.7 percent to GDP and public and publicly guaranteed debt of 77.4 percent to GDP both remain high (2020). While, a
 recovery is expected in 2021 with construction and tourism leading growth, beyond this recovery, private investments
 remain constrained by the challenging doing business environment. The European Commission decided to open accession
 negotiations with Albania in March 24, 2020. EU accession is a strong incentive for Albania to boost its reform agenda
 including on better stewardship of natural resources and environmental protection and addressing climate challenge.
 However, budget allocations have been further reduced due the ongoing crisis thus jeopardizing the transposition of
 relevant EU acquis, including on environmental issues.
 2.       The Adriatic-Ionian coast of Albania contains a rich natural and cultural capital with relatively high but still
 untapped economic potential. Albania’s Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) represents 40 percent of the country’s territory.
 It includes several Technological and Economic Development Areas (TEDAs) also referred to as free zones or industrial
 zones. Fisheries and aquaculture, and tourism are key contributors to the national economy. Albania’s highly productive
 yet fragile coastal and marine ecosystems are under increasing pressure from urbanization, development, pollution, and
 climate change. If left unattended these pressures could impact their economic potential and deter the Blue Economy (BE)
 development of Albania. The BE is defined as the sustainable use of marine resources for economic growth, improved
 livelihoods, and jobs while preserving the health of marine ecosystems.

  Sectoral and Institutional Context

 3.      Albania’s General National Spatial Plan 2015–2030 singles out the coast as the most important zone of the
 national territory because of its position, natural values, biodiversity significance, and cultural and historical heritage.
 The coastal districts contribute around 37% of the national GDP with growth centered around large cities such as Fier with
 contribution of 12.4 percent to GDP, followed by Durres 9.9 percent and Vlora 5.9 percent. Competing demands from
 multiple users in the coastal and marine space have resulted in sub-optimal development or missed economic
 opportunities, while imposing multiple stresses on finite coastal systems and resources.
 4.     The Government of Albania is taking actions for tackling coastal and marine pollution, aspiring to progressively
 become the first European plastic-free country. The new 2020-2035 National Strategy for integrated waste management
 that was approved in May 2020, aimed at incorporating circular economy principles in the national waste management
 system. However, the effectiveness of measures for preventing waste generation is limited. The roles and functions of
 enforcement and policing institutions remain unclear.

 5.       Albania’s coastal waters foster a diverse aquatic species of economic value undermined by depletion of the fish
 stocks. The overall economic contribution of the fish and seafood sector is more than double the value of its primary
 production with US$111 million, representing 0.9 percent of GDP in 2017. Albania’s impressive seafood export
 performance is a result of the country’s dynamic fish processing/trading industry. Illegal and unregulated fishing, and
 inefficient management of the resource have led to progressive depletion of the fish stocks. Up to 80 percent of Albania’s
 stocks are now overexploited. The current management measures are not properly enforced. Albeit some recent
 improvements, the Albanian fisheries administration remains understaffed and lacking adequate capacity in policy and
 legislative development. Fish resources are also threatened by increasing pollution of marine and coastal waters,
 especially in lagoons. The Fisheries Strategy highlights a series of priorities for every key aspect of the sector, while the
 Law on Fisheries establishes institutional arrangements for sector governance.
 6.      Albania’s pristine nature and rich cultural traditions contribute enormously to the value of coastal and marine
 tourism. In 2019, Travel & Tourism recorded a direct contribution accounting for about 20.5% of GDP, and represented
 243,900 jobs, 21.3% of total employment, mostly in microenterprises and small firms. Albania’s Tourism Development
 Strategy (2019–2023) acknowledges that for many years sun-sea-sand type of tourism has been the main product
 accounting for the largest share of tourism in the country. It highlights the need to diversify its product offerings by looking
 for new opportunities to brand Albania’s tourism.
 7.       The Albanian coast is highly vulnerable to climate change impacts. Temperatures are projected to continue to
 increase across South Eastern Europe and Albania’s summers are expected to experience the greatest degrees of warming,
 with an increase of 2.4℃ to 3.1℃ during June to August. Increased temperatures and extreme heat also pose great health
 risks for the population. Projected temperature changes indicate that more hot days and heat waves will occur over the
 coastal areas, which also increases risks of drought and greater fire risks. Intense rainfall is expected as well as impacts
 from an increase of flooding along the coastline due to expected sea level rise of 48 to 60 cm by end of the century.
 Changes to precipitation patterns and intensity can also lead to more frequent flooding in certain areas and to more
 droughts, landslides, or erosion along embankments. There are clear signs of coastal erosion along the coast including
 erosion of riverbeds, and modification of turbidity and sediment load particularly in the past 10 years. Some coastal
 lagoons are expected to become more sea-like as sea-level rise results in a breach of the sand and gravel bars that separate
 them from the sea.
 8.       As an EU accession candidate Albania will have to comply with the EU climate and sustainability action plans
 and regulations and align domestic legislation with the EU acquis. In addition to overall targets to reduce GHG emissions
 and achieve carbon neutrality, the European Green Deal (EGD) includes regulations to protect maritime areas and utilize
 their resiliency and resources to complement other efforts to improve sustainability. These include (i) Circular Economy
 and Preventing Waste, to reduce the impacts of human activities on the sea; (ii) Biodiversity and Investing in Nature, both
 as a prerequisite for economic activities like fisheries and tourism and to preserve economic opportunities related to
 biodiversity conservation and restoration; (iii) Coastal Resilience, based on natural and nature-based solutions; (iv)
 Responsible food system by managing fisheries resources more sustainably and by choosing alternative sources of food
 and feed in aquaculture. The project will support efforts to address these topics by promoting circular economy solution,
 by strengthening and expanding marine and coastal ecosystem contributing to coastal resilience and by improving
 fisheries management and promoting green innovation in the aquaculture sector.

  Relationship to CPF

 9.      The objectives of the proposed project are aligned with Albania’s 2015-2019 Country Partnership Framework
 (CPF). Sustainable tourism development being an essential component of the proposed project, it is in line with the second
 focus area of the 2015-2019 CPF (‘Creating conditions for accelerated private sector growth’), objective 2b: ‘Support
 sustainable tourism development’. By strengthening public institutions involved in all aspects of the BE, the project will

 also contribute to the CPF’s third focus area (‘Strengthening public sector management and service delivery’), objecti ve
 3a: ‘Support increased efficiency of public service delivery’. The project is also aligned with the two cross -cutting themes
 of the CPF: gender and EU accession.

 10.     The project is also aligned with the 2019 update of the Systematic Country Diagnostic (SCD). The second strategic
 objective of the SCD update (‘Making local governments more effective’) identifies the mismanagement of natural
 resources, in part due to absent policies or implementation gaps, and the impacts of pollution, including untreated solid
 waste, as key threats undermining the potential of strategic sectors to grow in value and importance in the future. It
 recommends expanding fishing and tourism to contribute to sustainable and spatially inclusive growth and adopting a BE
 development plan to enhance the sustainability of coastal and marine activity and address environmental challenges. The
 project will therefore directly implement the vision developed in the SCD.

  C. Proposed Development Objective(s)


 11.      To strengthen the capacity for sustainable management of marine and coastal resources at the national level and
 in selected area(s).

  Key Results (From PCN)

 12.      The proposed PDO-level results indicators are:
 (i)     National BE Development Framework developed (score). This indicator could include the transposition of the EU
 Marine Strategy Framework Directive, Marine Spatial Planning for the [southern and northern] parts of the Albanian
 coastline, and an operational Cross-sectoral coordination mechanism supporting this process.
 (ii)    Area of coastal and marine ecosystems brought under sustainable management (Ha). This indicator will focus on
 selected marine and coastal ecosystems which could include marine protected areas, wetlands, and coastal forests.
 Improved management will be measured from the baseline.
 (iii)   People benefitting from BE investments in the project areas (# disaggregated by gender).


  D. Concept Description

 13.     The proposed project is designed to facilitate the transition to a BE development model in Albania.
 Implementing a BE approach is a long-term process that has started in Albania and the World Bank report on ‘Realizing
 the Blue Economy Potential in Albania’ (2020) provided important inputs to this process. The proposed project seeks to
 demonstrate the feasibility and benefits of adopting a BE approach in targeted sectors at a scale smaller than the national,
 while also supporting the enabling environment for BE reforms at the country level.
 14.     The area currently under consideration for project activities is Vlora which has the potential for transitioning from
 grey to green-blue development. The outcome will be coastal and marine assets sustainably managed which will
 contribute to mitigation and adaptation efforts. Inclusive, resilient, and environmentally sustainable approaches for
 management of coastal and marine assets will create more opportunities for financial, economic, environmental, and
 social benefits. Local economic benefits will be facilitated through integration and facilitation of green investments in the
 area. The model will promote economic growth, improved livelihoods, and jobs while preserving the health of coastal and
 marine ecosystems. At national level the project will support the implementation of a BE development framework,

 facilitate knowledge transfer, stakeholder engagement, and institutional capacity and coordination needed for scaling up
 the BE model to other priority areas.
 15.     The project will strengthen policies and institutional capacities for removing binding constraints to the
 development and scaling-up of the BE at the national level. It will support a national BE institutional and governance
 framework, national plastic pollution management policies and practices, environment for low impact coastal and marine
 tourism and sustainable fisheries and aquaculture sectors.
 16.      Component 1: Enabling environment for the Blue Economy (US$2 million and additional grant resources). This
 component will strengthen policies and institutional capacities for the implementation of the national BE framework. This
 will include a set of multi-sectoral, participatory, transparent, and iterative processes covering information collection,
 planning, decision making, management and monitoring of implementation. To better coordinate the investment planning
 and implementation, the project would support an investment screening process based on costs and benefits, will
 facilitate knowledge transfer and decision making through the establishment of a multi-sectoral BE dashboard, and a
 multi-partner coordination platform. Based on a careful review of sectoral interactions, impact of climate change and the
 valuation of marine natural capital, a country-level Marine Spatial Planning process would support decision making. This
 component will also support activities for further development of the national policies for prevention of marine plastic
 pollution, including a monitoring system and data base to inform policies and improve implementation, introduction of
 market-based instruments as precursors for transitioning to a circular economy, and national-level awareness raising.
 Other activities under this component will include technical assistance and advisory services to identify sustainable
 tourism development models from an economic, social, and environmental perspective, including incentive frameworks
 to support BE cluster development that will also help to diversify the tourism sector and reduce known negative impacts.
 17.     Component 2: Blue Economy development in selected area(s) (IBRD financing of US$73 million including IBRD
 loan of US$63 million and guarantee of US$10 million). This component will support the development and
 implementation of a BE model at the local level through public investments to support the integrated management of key
 BE assets. Currently, Vlora is being considered given its potential to transition from grey to green-blue development
 through support for green infrastructure, protecting coastlines from erosion risks and flooding, preserving biodiversity and
 protection of land and seascapes, controlling and reducing pollution, all of which will be benefitting tourism and the
 broader local coastal economy. The component will support activities for switching to a circular economy and reducing
 pollution, including through actions to reduce plastics and microplastics pollution. The proposed activities will therefore
 encompass key pillars of a BE development framework in an integrated manner. The component will support activities
 that will enable the adoption of a Blue Economy action program at the local level with elements of governance and
 investment framework like marine spatial plan for the demarcated marine area, and establishment of a coordination
 platform between sectors, partners, and stakeholder groups for key new and on-going investments in the coastal and
 marine space. Given the climate vulnerabilities of the coastal economy, placing an emphasis on resilience will be central
 to the development of the BE action program and investments.
 18.      Component 3: Project implementation (US$5 million in IBRD loan). This component will finance the Ministry of
 Tourism and Environment (MTE) and its Project Management Team (PMT) responsible for project management and, the
 incremental costs associated with project management – e.g., hiring of project support staff, procurement of consultants,
 training, equipment; financial management and reporting; and, monitoring and evaluation, knowledge management as
 well as activities necessary to ensure compliance with the environmental and social framework and studies and surveys
 as needed during implementation. The establishment and maintenance of the grievance redress mechanism will also be
 financed under this component. The project will be implemented through a two-tier structure: a PMT which will be under
 MTE and part of it will be a local coordination unit in the project selected region.
