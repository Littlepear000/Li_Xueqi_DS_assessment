 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

1.     This Project Paper seeks approval of the Country Director to process a no-cost extension of the closing date of the
Bangladesh COVID-19 Emergency Response and Pandemic Preparedness Project (P173757) by one year cumulative from
December 31, 2023, to December 31, 2024.
2.      This restructuring responds to a request made by the Government of Bangladesh (GoB) through a letter dated
March 19, 2023, from the Economic Relations Division of the Ministry of Finance. The proposed no-cost extension was
discussed during the mid-term review (MTR) of the project held in June 2022 as documented in the aide-mémoire.

3.      Project background: The Bangladesh COVID-19 Emergency Response and Pandemic Preparedness Project
(P173757), in an amount of XDR 72.9 million (US$100 million equivalent) IDA credit (IDA 6605-BD) was approved in April
2020 under the COVID-19 Strategic Preparedness and Response Program (SPRP) using the Multiphase Programmatic
Approach (MPA). In March 2021, an additional financing (AF) of XDR 347.1 million (US$500 million equivalent) IDA credit
(IDA 6846-BD) was approved by the Board to enable affordable and equitable access to COVID-19 vaccines and help ensure
effective vaccine deployment in Bangladesh through vaccination system strengthening, and to further strengthen
preparedness and response activities under the parent project. Along with processing the AF, the project was restructured
to incorporate US$100 million soft loan as co-financing from the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB). The financing
percentage of the original IDA credit (IDA 6605-BD) and the AIIB loan (COFN-C1850) is set at 50:50. The AIIB soft loan is
financing all project components as IDA except sub-component 1.5 (for COVID-19 vaccination related activities).

4.     Project implementation status: The project has been instrumental in Bangladesh containing the COVID-19
epidemic by helping vaccinate people and strengthening health systems for better preparedness of future pandemics.
More specifically, the project supported the following:
     a. Vaccinated 46.5% of the total population with 68 million doses of vaccines and 110 million pieces of auto-disable
        syringes procured, shipped, and deployed.
     b. Liquid medical oxygen (LMO) systems established at 30 public hospitals that had previously not had centralized
        LMO systems. This marks a key milestone in strengthening the health system, as access to oxygen supplies is
        critical for the treatment of COVID-19, as well as other respiratory diseases.
     c. Additional technical capacity ensured through hiring of around 1,200 staff (doctors, laboratory consultants,
        medical technologists, data operators) who have been placed at public health facilities dealing with COVID-19
        cases.
     d. Critical equipment and supplies ensured during the peak of COVID-19 outbreak: 220 beds for intensive care units
        (ICU); 300 ventilators supplied to various hospitals across the country; strengthening capacity of a field hospital in
        Dhaka; COVID-19 testing kits and reagents in large quantities; and large quantities of personal protective
        equipment, masks, body bags, gloves, goggles, coveralls, and aprons were supplied for the protection of frontline
        workers.

5.      Achievement of the project development objective (PDO) is rated satisfactory and implementation progress as
moderately satisfactory. The PDO is to support the government “to prevent, detect and respond to the threat posed by
COVID-19 and strengthen national systems for public health preparedness”. Two out of the four PDO indicators have
overachieved the end-of-project target, one is fully achieved, and the remaining one (fourth) indicator is in progress. At
present, 85.8% of total IDA allocation and 10% of AIIB funds have been disbursed. Besides, the project is rated satisfactory
with respect to its performance in environmental and social safeguards, project management, and monitoring and
evaluation, while the ratings for financial management and procurement are moderately satisfactory. There are no
pending audit reports.

 II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

6.       The project closing date requires a no-cost extension of one year (cumulative) for the following reasons:
         (i)     To complete ongoing activities that have been delayed as:
                 a. contractors are not able to process letters of credit due to pressure on the foreign currency reserves;
                    and
                 b. delays in shipment due to the ongoing war (many of the equipment are heavy and cannot be airlifted).
                    The civil works have been completed but the ICUs and modern microbiology laboratories, for example,
                    cannot be made functional as the equipment have not been delivered yet.
         (ii)    To use unspent funds for new activities including construction of medical gas production plants and
                 setting-up additional capacity of intensive care, testing, and isolation.

7.      Ongoing activities are at various stages of implementation and require additional time to complete, financed by
IDA and AIIB, which include: setting-up 27 modern microbiology laboratories; establishing vaccine testing laboratory;
procurement of coldchain equipment for storing vaccines; setting-up isolation units and ICU at 30 public hospitals; and
bio-safety laboratory for testing.

8.       With the unspent funds (IDA and AIIB), additional capacity for ICU, testing, and isolation as well as three medical
gas production plants will be constructed. The medical gas production plants will be an asset for Bangladesh as it will
reduce the country’s reliance on importing critical medical gases (like oxygen). During the peak of the COVID-19 epidemic,
the country faced challenges in sourcing oxygen from abroad as globally the demand had surged and supply was limited.
With the construction and establishment of these medical gas plants, Bangladesh will be self-sufficient to meet its regular
as well emergency needs for medical gases.

9.      AIIB is also extending the closing date of their loan up to December 31, 2024.

EXPECTED MPA PROGRAM RESULTS

Current Expected MPA Results and their Indicators for the MPA Program
Progress towards the achievement of the PDO would be measured by outcome indicators. Individual country-specific
projects (or phases) under the MPA Program will identify relevant indicators, including among others:
•       Country has activated their public health Emergency Operations Centre or a coordination mechanism for
COVID-19;
•       Number of designated laboratories with COVID-19 diagnostic equipment, test kits, and reagents;
•       Number of acute healthcare facilities with isolation capacity;
•       Number of suspected cases of COVID-19 reported and investigated per approved protocol;
•       Number of diagnosed cases treated per approved protocol;
•       Personal and community non-pharmaceutical interventions adopted by the country (e.g., installation of
handwashing facilities, provision of supplies and behavior change campaigns, continuity of water and sanitation service
provision in public facilities and households, schools closures, telework and remote meetings, reduce/cancel mass
gatherings);
•       Policies, regulations, guidelines, or other relevant government strategic documents incorporating a multi-
sectoral health approach developed/or revised and adopted;
•       Multi-sectoral operational mechanism for coordinated response to outbreaks by human, animal and wildlife
sectors in place;
