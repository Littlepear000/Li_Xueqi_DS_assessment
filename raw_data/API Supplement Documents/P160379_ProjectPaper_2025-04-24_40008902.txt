I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

  1. Project Background: The Innovation in Solar Power and Hybrid Technologies Project (ISHTP) was approved by the World
     Bank’s (the Bank) Board on March 29, 2019. The Project was originally financed by an IBRD Loan (US$150 million), Clean
     Technology Fund (CTF) Loan (US$28 million), and CTF Grant (US$21.81 million), whose Agreements were signed on
     December 14, 2022, and became effective on March 7, 2023. On February 14, 2023, the financing amounts were revised
     downwards to US$67 million for the IBRD Loan, US$13 million for the CTF Loan, and US$9 million for the CTF Grant. The
     Borrower/Recipient and the implementing agency for ISHTP is the Solar Energy Corporation of India Limited (SECI),
     which is a central sector public utility that plays a strategic role in India’s renewable energy initiatives.

  2. Project Development Objective (PDO): The PDOs are to demonstrate the operational and economic feasibility of utility-
     scale innovative renewable energy (RE) technologies and battery energy storage solutions (BESS), and to strengthen
     institutional capacity to facilitate scale-up of such technologies on a commercial basis in India.

  3. Previous restructuring: The Project has been restructured thrice in response to changing implementation conditions.
       • The first restructuring was conducted on March 11, 2022, to change the PDO Indicators, Intermediate Result
           Indicators and Disbursement Estimates, because of unforeseen delays in loan signing and effectiveness. In
           addition, the legal covenants were updated to reflect the change in the first subproject (solar-wind hybrid with
           BESS subproject at Ramagiri, Andhra Pradesh), after the new state government in the off-taking state declined
           to buy power from this subproject.
       • The second restructuring was conducted on February 14, 2023, to effect a partial cancelation of US$110.81
           million, hence reducing the project size from US$199.81 million to US$89 million. This was done since SECI had
           decided to limit the financing to the two subprojects instead of three, since the first subproject at Ramagiri,
           Andhra Pradesh, could not be taken forward.
       • The third restructuring was conducted on December 20, 2024, to extend the Project Closing Date by 12 months,
           from December 31, 2024, to December 31, 2025, to allow for additional time to complete the design, installation
           and commissioning of the second subproject, 100 MW Floating Solar PV Subproject in Getalsud, Jharkhand.
       • The current proposed restructuring is the fourth restructuring of the project, as described below.


  4. Current status of the project: The Project supports two subprojects and strengthens the institutional capacity of SECI:
       a. 100 Megawatt (MW) Solar Photovoltaic (PV) with a 40 MW/120 MWh Battery Subproject at Rajnandgaon,
           Chhattisgarh: This subproject was commissioned on February 1, 2024. It is the largest battery project
           commissioned in India to date and was formally inaugurated by the Honorable Prime Minister of India on
           February 24, 2024. It is also the first solar-with-battery project commissioned for the World Bank globally.
       b. 100 MW Floating Solar PV Subproject in Getalsud, Jharkhand: The contract for this subproject was awarded in
           May 2024. As per the contract, 18 months have been provided to the contractor to design, install, and
           commission the subproject. The project has recently been extended to December 31, 2025, to ensure sufficient
           time for the completion of these works.

  5. Project Ratings: PDO and the implementation progress (IP) are Moderately Satisfactory given the progress made on
     both the subprojects, despite initial delays. There are no overdue audit reports for the project.

  6. Rationale for Restructuring: This restructuring proposes to update the Results Framework. This is due to (i) the
     downsizing of the Project, both in terms of funding (from US$199.81 million to US$89 million) and the number of
     subprojects (from three to two in the second restructuring), and (ii) the extension of the closing date (in the third
     restructuring).


II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

  7. Change in Results Framework: The Results Framework of the Project needs to be updated to (i) align it with the reduced
     scope of the project which now includes two subprojects instead of three as part of the previous restructuring, and (ii)
     reflect the extension of the project closing date by 12 months from December 31, 2024, to December 31, 2025. Further,
     in line with the institutional mandate of periodic reporting on the World Bank’s corporate scorecard, the indicators
     ‘Generation Capacity of Renewable Energy (other than hydropower) constructed (MW)’ and ‘Renewable Energy
     Generated (megawatt-hour (MWh))’ will now also be reported as Corporate Results Indicator (CRI) - Renewable Energy
     Capacity Enabled. The proposed changes to the Results Framework are detailed in Table 1. This in reference to
     Department of Economic Affairs’ Letter dated November 13, 2024, that requested for an extension of the Project closing
     date (done through the third restructuring) and revision of the key performance indicators (results framework).
