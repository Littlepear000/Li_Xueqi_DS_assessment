 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Project Context
  The Burkina-Faso Higher Education Support Project (US$70 million) was approved by the World Bank’s Board of Executive Directors on July 10,
  2018, and became effective on December 19, 2018. The Project is expected to close on September 30, 2023. A mid-term review (MTR) of the
  Project was conducted from 20 -25 June 2022. The mid-term review saw the participation of all project stakeholders - ministry, universities,
  coordination unit, higher education unions, World Bank team, minister of higher education, etc. - The findings and recommendations are
  documented below. Overall, this operation constituted the first support from the World Bank to the Higher Education subsector in Burkina Faso. The
  Project sought to address constraints with regard to access, quality, and relevance of tertiary education in Burkina Faso.

  Relevance of Objectives, Design, and Implementation

  This ISR evaluates the Project’s performance at the mid-term stage across the three harmonized criteria of (i) relevance of objectives, design, and
  implementation; (ii) efficacy; and (iii) efficiency. The Project’s performance is assessed in relation to each of these three criteria. The evidence was
  collected during the MTR mission that took place in June 2022

  Relevance of Objectives. The PDO of the Project is “strengthen higher education institutions to increase access and deliver quality education in
  priority subject areas.” The project will support over 200,000 students in selected universities and over 1,000 teachers and administrators and will
  strengthen the capacity of tertiary institutions. The Project’s objective remains highly relevant as it responds to key sector challenges identified at
  appraisal. The objectives are also strongly aligned with the Government’s key strategic documents, including, among others, the Education and
  Training Sector Plan (ETSP) covering the period 2017-30 and also the country National Plan for Economic and Social Development II (NPESD
  2021 – 2025) in its Axis 3: consolidate the development of human capital and national solidarity and in particular its Strategic Objective (SO) 3.2:
  "increase the offer and improve the quality of education , higher education and training in line with the needs of the economy"..

  Relevance of Design. The design of the Project is aligned with the priorities outlined in Burkina Faso’s ETSP (2017-30). Project preparation was
  characterized by a high level of participation of various stakeholders, including key development partners and representatives from all the
  Universities. This collaborative approach fostered harmonization and alignment of activities in support of the design of the Project. Lessons learned
  from other Bank-supported operations, especially the 2iE and the Regional ACE Project, were incorporated into the design of the Project. Some of
  these lessons included the importance of using the results-based financing modality. The design also benefitted from some studies undertaken
  during project preparation which identified specific needs of the sector and guided the development and choice of interventions to address the
  identified challenges. The activities selected were appropriate and tailored to achieve the operation’s envisaged outputs and outcomes. Together,
  the choice of key project-supported activities—the establishment of the VUBF, the connectivity of Universities, the Competitive Funds/PBCs to
  foster better governance of universities, the support for teacher training and research activities, and capacity-building support— was logical as these
  could be expected to have a positive impact on the quality and relevance of tertiary education in Burkina Faso. Based on the above, the relevance
  of design is rated borderline Substantial.

  Relevance of Implementation. The relevance of project implementation is rated Modest. This can be attributed to several issues, early in
  implementation and persisted through the first years of the project. First, some project activities were not well defined from the start. For example,
  the establishment of the BF-VU should have required the development of a clear strategic plan and business plan before launching it. This could
  explain the current limited enrollment capacity of the VUBF, resulting in higher unit costs as the BF-VU enrolls only about 500 students. Second,
  there are significant delays in the launch of major activities, especially the recruitment of doctoral students, connectivity of the selected universities
  and the construction of the ODS.

  Efficacy (Achievement of Project Development Objectives)
  The following provides an overview of outputs and outcomes achieved under the Project over the first four years of the Project’s life.

  Component 1. Enhancing Access to Higher Education: Establishment of the Virtual University of Burkina Faso. Most activities under this
  Component are underway and can be classified into two categories based on their progress:
        The first category comprises activities that are on track and show good results. An integrated virtual platform (CampusFaso) has been
         established and is fully functional. The platform supports improved information and data management and carries out many administrative
         functions (student enrolment, filling administrative forms, archiving of pedagogical materials, etc..). The Project also supports an innovative
         program (One student / One computer) which provided one computer to each of the 14,122 students via grants as of May 2022. In
         addition, several digital equipment and resources, including more than 4,000 e-books, were acquired for selected University libraries.
        The second group encompasses activities that are delayed and exhibit poor results. The VUBF was established in 2018, and to-date, 10
         training courses are being offered. The Project supported the provision of equipment and broadband connection to enable the delivery of
         virtual courses. However, the VUBF is not addressing constraints related to limited access to higher education as originally envisioned. In
         fact, since its creation, it has enrolled about 500 students, which is below the expected enrollment number of approximately 8,000
         students. Given the current choices made by the Government in terms of the training offers, the target will likely not be reached by the end
         of the Project. Furthermore, there were delays in the construction of the three ODS. Given the limited enrollment capacity of the VUBF, the
         construction of these ODS no longer seem viable and relevant. Finally, the provision of connectivity to the selected Universities is severely
         lagging behind because of lack of clarity in the Agreement signed between the Ministry and ANPTIC, as well as the delays in the
         acquisition of the Wi-Fi equipment, whose procurement process has been ongoing for 10 months. An official meeting was organized
         during the MTR mission, and the Ministry of Digital Transition has committed to provide 4 Gigabits of bandwidth for the Universities as well
         as finalize the connectivity on 17 (out of 86) sites for the Universities by the end of September 2022. At the same time, the MHERI
         committed to complete the acquisition of the Wi-Fi-equipment.

  Component 2. Strengthen higher education institutions and support innovations. Component 2 supported the award of funds on a
  competitive basis to selected Universities. Along the course of Project implementation, the process was transformed from a competitive basis to a
  PBC modality. A total of ten PBCs (amounting to US$22 million) were approved on March 19, 2021 and are being implemented. The
  implementation of the performance-based contracts is relatively strong and is expected to be completed in 2024 (which is after the current project
  closes). Further, this component supports research activities by providing scholarships to doctoral students. Currently, 300 doctoral students benefit
  from this intervention. Finally, nine internal quality assurance units (above the target of 7) are in place, the development of the national quality
  assurance strategy and the establishment of the national higher education qualifications framework have been finalized.

  Component 3. Project coordination, management, and implementation activities. Under this Component, support was provided for
  coordination and management of project activities, including those related to M&E, procurement, and FM, as well as monitoring of activities at the
  decentralized level. The PCU is composed of qualified staff who provided the necessary support to carry out these activities. The PCU has been
  proactive in building its capacity and actively engaging consultants to support project activities and evaluations as needed. For example, the Project
  will support the organization of the National Conference on Higher Education and Research in Burkina to contribute to the strengthening of Higher
  Education.
  Based on the currently available evidence, efficacy as evaluated at the MTR stage is rated Modest.

  Efficiency
  The cost-benefit analysis of the Burkina Faso Higher Education Support Project undertaken at Project Appraisal confirmed the cost-effectiveness of
  the Project. The calculations were premised on the following: (i) the VUBF proves to be successful; (ii) the strategic plans of all Universities focus on
  quality improvement reforms; and (iii) the higher education system governance and management is reformed. Moreover, the analysis indicated that
  acquiring a university education in Burkina Faso could potentially increase a student’s income by at least 50 percent compared to countries in Sub-
  Saharan Africa. Recent analytical work by the Bank (PER 2021) has reconfirmed the positive association between educational attainment and
  earnings in Burkina Faso and, as such, the economic justification for investment in tertiary education remains strong. While financial audit reports
  over the life of the Project have indicated that project resources have generally been used judiciously, there are some challenges given the high unit
  cost of realized activities. For example, the VUBF benefits only 500 students, resulting in a high unit cost per student. If the ODS are constructed,
  these unit costs are most likely going to increase and threaten the cost-effectiveness of the Project. Based on the above, efficiency under the
  Project is rated Modest.

  The project's sustainability is likely as key project-supported activities will continue to be supported by the Ministry of Higher Education, Research
  and Innovation and public universities following the project’s closing. Additionally, the project’s support for important sector reforms, including
  establishing a national quality assurance framework, will be critical in maintaining project gains and further strengthening the sector. Key activities
  such as the connectivity of universities aimed to ensure the sustainability of access to education by reducing the costs of teachers and
  infrastructures. The Government confirmed during the MTR mission that it would fund some of the continued activities with its own financing from
  the annual budget (for example, the VUBF). Finally, promoting youth employment is one of the current administration’s priorities.

  MTR Recommendations
  The Moderately Satisfactory rating for PDO achievement and IP performance remain unchanged based on the progress and evidence obtained
  across the three components. Based on the analysis conducted, the MTR recommends the restructuring of the project to help address the above-
  mentioned issues. These recommendations are as follows:

            The restructuring of the project to reallocate the remaining funds under Component 1 dedicated to the VUBF and the construction of the
             three ODSs to invest in other activities that are systemic in nature (Component 2), such as research activities via the provision of
             scholarships for doctoral students and the equipment of research laboratories, the overall set of activities under the PBCs to improve
             quality of programs and governance.

            The introduction of the digitalization of the Universities to allow both virtual courses to be given in addition to face-to-face courses would
             address access constraints such as limited infrastructure, an insufficient number of teachers, and overlapping school years.

            The necessary adjustments to project description to clarify the following activities: (i) the program one student one computer which was
             part of the support to the creation of the UVBF as a separate activity; (ii) rehabilitation of the central library of the university Joseph Ki
             Zerbo of Ouagadougou which was not clearly described as an activity but included in the improvement of the quality of HEIs; (iii) merging
             sub-components 2.1 and 2.2 and signing performance contract between HEIs and the Ministry of Higher Education as described in the
             PAD.

            The extension of the project closing date by at least one year (i.e., September 30, 2024) will allow the Universities to finalize the execution
             of all the activities planned under the performance-based contracts.
