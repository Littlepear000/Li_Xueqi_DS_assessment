 Project Development Objectives

  Project Development Objective (from Project Appraisal Document)
  The Project Development Objectives are to: (i) increase electricity supply to the Eastern part of Guinea; (ii) enable electricity trade between Guinea
  and Mali; and (iii) increase Guinea’s electricity export capability towards other West African Power Pool countries.

  Has the Project Development Objective been changed since Board Approval of the Project Objective?
  No

 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  As of June 15, 2022, cumulative disbursement rate stands at 20.03%, with a total of US$15.81 million disbursed, and a total of US$63.11 million
  undisbursed. The FY disbursement ratio is 5%. The disbursement indicators have been negatively impacted by the political context in the region
  that led the Bank to suspend disbursements, first in Guinea for 6 months from September 5, 2021 to February 28, 2022, and in Mali from March 15,
  2022.
  Component 1: Power transmission infrastructure (US$71.8 million)
  For the Siguiri -border Mali/Guinea transmission line (54 Km), the contract was signed in January 2021 and an advance payment of US$1.7 million
  was made in April 2021. For the substations in Siguiri, Kankan, and Kerouane (Guinea), the contracts were signed in January and March 2021
  allowing the payment of a total amount of US$5.8 million as advances in April 2021. The payment for the Kankan substation was delayed because
  the contractor failed to submit all the required documentation. The process was then stopped after triggering of the OP 7.30. When disbursements

  resumed, the project was able to collect complete documentation from the contractor and proceeded the payment on of US$1,87 million on June 16,

  As for the extension of the post of Sanankoroba (Mali), the Bank gave no objection for direct contracting with Tata, the contractor already in charge
  of the construction of the main substation under the financing of the Exim Bank of India, as part of the 225 kV Sikasso-Bougouni-Sanankoroba-
  Bamako project. The contract has been concluded but cannot be signed until the disbursements suspension of the Mali portfolio is lifted. A contract
  financing gap of about US$3 million has been identified and the team agreed with the client to co-finance the contract with an ongoing national IDA
  operation (P166796). The contract for the supply of O&M equipment of EDM is being executed. Most of the equipment has been manufactured.
  Component 2: Implementation support and capacity building (US$12.2 million):
  In addition to the procurement specialist recruited in March 2019 and the monitoring/evaluation specialist recruited in March 2020, the PIU in
  Guinea is to be strengthened with an environmental specialist and social development specialist. The two experts have been selected and have
  received their contract proposals. They are expected to joint the project and start working in July 2022.
  Regarding the study to connect the mining companies to the national grid, the recruitment process of the consultancy firm is quite advanced. 14
  firms have tendered. The selection is ongoing and the PIU is finalizing the technical evaluation report.
  Safeguard activities
  The Bank approved the RAP. Both the Government of Guinea and the Government of Mali have opened dedicated bank accounts and deposited
  funds for the implementation of the resettlement action plans before the construction works. On the Guinean side where major expropriation
  activities are expected, GNF92,4 billion have been secured. However, the process of compensation was significantly delayed due to the political
  change that occurred in September 2021. Indeed, new authorities had blocked the project bank account (financing under the national
  budget) leading to the suspension of activities of the the National Compensation Commission which could resume working. In March 2022 the
  Commission resumed its activities and performed field missions to meet the PAPs and validate the amounts of compensation. Meanwhile, the NGO
  CADES was recruited to assist PAPs filing their compensation requests. The NGO has launched the communication and awareness component
  and actively participated to help the PAPs obtain the National Identity Cards (CDNs) which will is required for the payment of compensation. The
  compensation process for the components financed by the Bank have been completed on May 31. That will allow contractors to start works on the
  project sites on July 12, 2022.
