 Project Development Objectives

  Project Development Objective (from Project Appraisal Document)
  The Project Development Objectives are to: (i) increase electricity supply to the Eastern part of Guinea; (ii) enable electricity trade between Guinea
  and Mali; and (iii) increase Guinea’s electricity export capability towards other West African Power Pool countries.

  Has the Project Development Objective been changed since Board Approval of the Project Objective?
  No

 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  As of November 30, 2021, the overall disbursement rate of the project stands at 16 percent. The procurement for the construction of the main
  infrastructures was completed in the Guinea side. However, after the COVID-19 has affected the project implementation, the triggering of the OP
  7.30, due to the military coup in Guinea, could further undermine the implementation and disbursement progress of the project.
  Component 1: Power transmission infrastructure (US$71.8 million)
  For the Siguiri -border Mali/Guinea transmission line (54 Km), the contract was signed in January 2021. For the substations in Siguiri, Kankan, and
  Kerouane (Guinea), the contracts were signed in January and March 2021. As for the extension of the post of Sanankoroba (Mali), the PIU has
  launched the procurement process of awarding the contract to Tata, the contractor already in charge of the construction of the main substation
  under the financing of the Exim Bank of India, as part of the 225 kV Sikasso-Bougouni-Sanankoroba-Bamako project. TATA had submitted a first

  is expected to submit a revised offer. The procurement for the supply of O&M equipment of EDM has already been awarded.
  Component 2: Implementation support and capacity building (US$12.2 million):
  In addition to the procurement specialist recruited in March 2019 and the monitoring/evaluation specialist recruited in March 2020, the PIU in
  Guinea is to be strengthened with an environmental specialist and social development specialist. Initially, it was planned to recruit one expert
  combining the two specialties (environment and social). However, to comply with the new safeguard rules of the Bank, the Project needs to
  relaunch the process and recruit two experts. The recruitment of the environmentalist was closed to finalize but the selected candidate declined
  just after the military coup happened in Guinea. In the meantime, EDG’s and EDM’s safeguards specialists, with the support of the Bank's
  safeguard specialists, will continue to handle safeguards aspects of the project.
  The selection process for the study to connect the mining companies to the national grid in Guinea and improve the financial situation of EDG is still
  ongoing. The ToR are still expected to be revised and finalized by the PIU and Ministry of Energy taking into account the Bank's observations and
  the study on the demand of the mining companies that was performed with the support of Power Africa. The project is also supporting technical
  audit of the performance contract between EDM and GoM to ensure sustained monitoring of the EDM’s performance improvement. Training and
  capacity building activities for staff of EDG and the Ministry of energy have been put on hold due to the triggering of the OP 7.30.
  Safeguard activities
  Both the Government of Guinea and the Government of Mali have opened dedicated bank accounts and deposited funds for the implementation of
  the resettlement action plans before the construction works. On the Guinean side where major expropriation activities are expected, GNF72 billion
  were deposited in the Central Bank of Guinea. A request for remaining amount of GNF42 billion was formally submitted to the Ministry of Economy
  and Finance and introduced in the budget for 2021. The Bank approved the RAP after it was updating taking into account the Bank's observations.
  In the Guinea side, the National Compensation Commission was established and resumed its conciliation activities on the ground. The NGO
  CADES was recruited to make payments to PAPs. The NGO has launched the communication and awareness component and actively
  participated to help the PAPs obtain the National Identity Cards (CDNs) which will be required for the payment of compensation. One the side of
  Mali, a central budget line for the execution of RAPs of public projects will be used to implement the project’s RAP.
