I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Introduction

  1. This Paper seeks the approval of the Country Director on a Level II restructuring of the Yemen Desert Locust
     Response Project (YDLRP). The proposed change concerns the revision of Project Development Objective (PDO)
     Indicators of the Project. The proposed change will replace two PDO indicators that measure restoration and
     treatment of pasture/rangeland and agriculture land, with a single indicator that measures surveyed and restored
     area of locust-affected land. This change will allow for a more accurate reporting of the project results, which
     despite the reported absence of locust activity, has made significant progress in preparedness and control
     measures.

B. Implementation status and progress towards achievement of the PDO

  2. The Project was approved on June 17, 2020, and was declared effective on July 14, 2020 having been under
     implementation for 2.5 years. The PDO is to control the desert locust outbreak, support livelihoods in locust-
     affected areas and strengthen Yemen’s preparedness for future locust infestations. Over its implementation
     period, the project has shown satisfactory and moderately satisfactory performance with tangible results on the
     preparedness and control objectives of the PDO, despite the ongoing violent conflict in Yemen and the global
     COVID-19 pandemic. The project was prepared, approved, and its activities were launched during the COVID-19
     pandemic, resulting in delays in the first year of implementation. Notwithstanding these delays, the project has
     demonstrated substantial results in the area of strengthening Yemen’s preparedness for future locust infestations.
     Notable progress has been made under project indicators related to preparedness and control. The COVID-19
     pandemic, the associated lockdowns and travel restrictions severely impacted the implementing entity’s ability to
     implement the project as per the initially envisaged schedule. Since the relaxation of COVID-19 restrictions
     worldwide, the project implementation progress has improved.

  3. Both locust control and preparedness aspects of the project have been well advanced. The livelihood restoration
     activities of the project are currently underway. Bank missions conducted in March and December 2022, as well
     as regular meetings with the project implementing entity – the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the
     implementing partner – Social Fund for Development (SFD) – confirmed that the PDO remains achievable. The
     delays in the livelihood activity were due to long period of finalization of the operational partnership agreement
     between FAO and SFD, which was partly the result of FAO HQ level delays due to remote and home-based work
     requirements as part of the COVID-19 precautionary measures. The reported absence of major locust activity in
     Yemen resulted in lesser pressure on the project teams to push for urgent implementation of the livelihoods-
     focused activities. Combined with in-country restrictions with travel and long periods required for clearances with
     the authorities, the livelihood component activities had not started until around November 2022. Nonetheless,
     the project increased its emphasis on control and preparedness activities, achieving significant results on the
     ground. Based on these conclusions, the project design overall continues to be relevant allowing for continued
     strong emphasis on control and preparedness.

  4. To-date, the project has supported 120 survey teams to conduct large scale surveillance campaigns. The teams
     have utilized digital Elocust3 devices, the el3M app installed on mobile phones, and survey forms. The surveillance
     areas included 17 governorates in the inner areas (locust summer breeding) (Marib, Al-Jawf, Shabwah,
     Hadramaut, and Al-Maharah) as well as the coastal areas (locust winter breeding) in the Southern (Aden, Lahj,

      Abyan, Shabwah coasts) and Red Sea (Hajjah, and Al Hodeidah). To-date a total of 2,159,684 (PDO target is 400,000

  6. In addition, FAO prepared a community health and safety plan and GBV action plan as part of the Environmnental
     and Social Management Framework, shared with the World Bank for review and clearance, to minimize
     community exposure to hazardous chemicals and diseases and the exposure of women to potential sexual
     harassment.

  7. There are no outstanding financial management audits. However, there were delays with submission of unaudited
     Interim Financial Reports (IFR) by FAO. These delays were attributed to difficulties with access to the
     ClientConnection system. All technical issues were resolved during the World Bank and FAO Financial
     Management teams’ meeting in early March 2023. The project is in compliance with its legal covenants.

C. Rationale for Restructuring

  8. The restructuring proposes to replace two PDO level indicators that are no longer considered relevant, given that
     no widespread and damaging locust infestation has been reported during the life of the project and hence there is
     no need to restore productivity on pasture/rangeland and agricultural land. Instead, the restructuring proposes to
     introduce a composite PDO indicator that would measure the extent of preparedness and control measures carried
     out under the project.

  9. The delays experienced in project implementation due to the COVID-19 pandemic have affected the overall progress
     of the project. Effectivelly, the project could have helped restore pasture/rangeland and/or agriculture land affected
     by desert locust prior to the start of the project. However, during the lifetime of the project no major desert locust
     infestation was reported, and the project implementing entity oriented its efforts on preparedness and capacity
     building.


II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

  10. During the project’s mid-term review, conducted through a number of face-to-face and virtual meetings over the
      period of May-December 2022, it was identified that the two PDO level indicators were no longer applicable and
      cannot be effectivelly measured given that no major widespread and damaging locust infestation has occurred
      during project implementation. The areas affected by desert locust have been very small (422 ha) and have been
      treated and restored to productivity. However, the overall control and surveillance activities have been carried
      out on a large scale - already covering an area that substantially exceeds the project targets. Hence the scope of

       the problem and its scale have changed, and focusing PDO level indicators on a small area of control and treatment
       under the current scope would not do justice to the scale of project activities and would not demonstrate the
       comprehensive preparedness and survellance work carried out.

  11. Under the restructuring, the following two PDO indicators “Share of affected pasture/rangeland restored to
      productivity (percent)” and “Share of affected agricultural land restored to productivity (percent)” are replaced
      with the current intermediate results indicator "Area of locust-affected land surveyed (Hectare)", which is
      reformulated as: “Area of locust-affected land surveyed and restored (Hectare)” to become the new PDO
      indicator.

      of the project. The end of project target for the new PDO indicator has also been revised to reflect the current
      achievements.
