 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Overview. The five-year Burkina-Faso Higher Education Support Project, approved by the World Bank Board of Executive Directors on July 10,
  2018, became effective on December 19, 2018, and it is expected to close on September 30, 2023. The Project’s development objectives are to:
  strengthen higher education institutions to increase access and deliver quality education in priority subject areas.

  Progress by Component: The Project has three main components. Progress by components is summarized below.

  Component 1. Enhancing Access to Higher Education: Establishment of the Virtual University of Burkina Faso

  The November 22 – 30 supervision mission noted that the implementation of Component 1 of the Project is underway, and the following significant
  results have been achieved.

  Virtual University of Burkina Faso.

  The UV-BF has begun its operational phase of implementing its activities for the opening of its training courses at the start of the 2020-2021 school
  year. To date, ten training courses, namely bachelor's degrees in mathematics, energy physics and engineering, life and earth sciences, geomatics
  and "pure developer" software engineering (computer science),as well as the masters in digital health, education and training sciences,
  cybersecurity and data mining and artificial intelligence and a certificate in pedagogy, are now available to students .
  In terms of activities, we can note:
            The effective launch of ten training offers, including six at Bachelor level and four at Master level, for a total of nearly 500 students.
            Installation of e-learning platforms for the benefit of all public universities (https://edu.gov.bf).
            Setting up of a digital library with more than 20 million scientific articles, etc.
  The delay in finalizing the business plan and the strategic plan, as well as that of the construction of open digital spaces (ODS), negatively impacts
  the functionality of UV-BF. Assurances were given by the Government that efforts will be made to ensure the rapid resizing of the ODS to allow for
  the start of the construction activities and the finalization of all the related studies.

  Establishment of the Campus-Faso platform

  The Project made progress in setting up the Campus-Faso platform with the development of different modules for registration, orientation, one
  student / one computer program. The mission underscored that this platform aims to be integrative, which is not yet the case and noted that some
  HERIs continue to work on independent platforms.
  It can be noted that a number of modules have been successfully integrated into the integrated platform and which have enabled, in particular: (i)
  the continuous integration of training offering, with 529 streams in public and private universities on Campus Faso; (ii) the management of
  orientations, registrations and re-registrations of public universities; (iii) the module for the integral management of student catering and
  accommodation; (iv) the module for receiving national and foreign scholarship offers; and (v) the modules for receiving applications for licenses,
  masters and doctoral students with 189 types of applications open on November 20, 2021.
  The major difficulties are, among other things, the insufficient number of developer engineers due to two departures, the need for a vehicle to
  ensure better monitoring at the level of the various structures, and the need for capacity building for a ramp-up of the developers.

  Building open digital spaces.

  As a reminder, the Project finances the construction of three open digital spaces (ODS) to facilitate the deployment of teaching for the benefit of
  virtual university students. To date, the implementation of this activity has come up against the sizing proposed by the PCU, which places the cost
  of the ODS at around XOF two billion (US$4 million), far from the original XOF 850 million (US$1 486 299,08) of the Senegalese model. The mission
  agreed with the guidance of the Minister that the model and the cost of these ODS would be resized in order to allow the start of construction work
  before the end of the current academic year and have the work finalized by September 2023.

  Connectivity infrastructure and availability of bandwidth.

  Following discussions with the various actors involved in this process, the mission recommended that the firm produce a technical note which will
  indicate the various legal implications on the options in terms of acquiring bandwidth (national or international call for tenders, direct agreement or
  open call, etc.) and to transmit by March 31, 2022, the final report which will include: (i) the description of the projections envisaged with an
  economic analysis relating to long-term sustainability; (ii) the legal part with didactic conclusions; and (iii) the next steps including the
  implementation schedule with the service provider.

  Program One student / One computer.

  Out of a potential fifty-seven thousand three hundred and seventy-three (57,373) students, the program registered twenty-three thousand six
  hundred and eleven (23,611) expressions of interest on the program management platform.

  As of November 15, 2021, a total of 10,974 computers have already been delivered, 13,005 grants have been awarded to students who have paid
  their quota after requesting it. As part of the quotas paid by students, the program was able to mobilize nine hundred and eleven million five
  hundred and XOF one thousand eight hundred (911,501,800).

  Support for the implementation of digital libraries.
  Regarding the support for the implementation of digital libraries, the mission reviewed a number of activities planned and to be carried out by the
  Permanent Secretariat for National Commitments and Emergency Programs. These are: (i) continued digitization of documents already identified at
  the Central University Library / UJKZ; (ii) putting digitized documents online and training stakeholders in the use of the platform and (iii) contacting
  the Cairn Info representative for the terms of subscription to specialized books.
  The mission recommends that the 2,952 books that will be ordered each have an electronic version and that this be included as a clause in the
  service provider's contract and that an organizational and functional audit of the HERIS libraries be included in the framework of the Project’s 2021
  budgeted annual work plan. These measures are needed to ensure the proper functionality of these libraries.

  Component 2. Strengthen higher education institutions and support innovations.

  The implementation of the activities programmed under component 2 continues and progress is notable both with regard to the implementation of
  the competitive funds mechanism (sub-component 2.1), based on performance and merit, as well as in relation to the objective of strengthening the
  teaching staff with the recruitment of the first 100 doctoral students. To date, all activities are being implemented and it is expected that for the next
  academic year (2022/2023) progress will be made in terms of improving teaching, construction or redevelopment of laboratories and libraries, and
  encouragement of innovation within Campus Faso. The World Bank task force continues to work closely with the government to find creative ways
  to ensure improved implementation in the months to come.

  Sub-component 2.1. Competitive Funds. A total of ten performance-based contracts (PBC), worth a total amount of approximately 11 billion CFA
  francs (around $US 22 million), were approved on March 19, 2021 and are being implemented.

  The mission stressed that the implementation of the strategic plans supported by the performance contracts (PC) must, as far as possible, provide
  lasting solutions to the major difficulties that higher education institutions have been facing for several years, particularly the high numbers and the
  discrepancy. academic years, etc. In addition, to have a strategic character and impact, plans must also aim to transform training programs through
  innovations in content and pedagogy and strengthen research capacities by consolidating scientific teams to reach a critical mass in cutting-edge
  fields directly related to national development priorities.
  The action plans developed should highlight the large-scale adoption of distance education in the various HERI. This adoption, which reflects the
  realities of the present times that the COVID-19 has highlighted on a planetary scale, will be made possible by the investments in progress or
  planned at the level of each HERI in the connectivity infrastructures, the equipment of the classrooms and amphitheaters. The dialogue on doctoral
  training should therefore fall within this framework so that the link between the grant award programs and the activities of the laboratories in which
  the doctoral students will carry out their training and conduct their research is clearly established.
  Finally, the mission has taken note of the overall PC envelope and invites the PCU to reassess these contracts to avoid any overrun.


  Sub-component 2.2 - Developing the Faculty in Higher Education Institutions (HEI). This sub-component aims to finance efforts to increase
  the teaching force in higher education at the local level through the doctoral schools of the bodies located within the public universities and the HERI
  which provide doctoral training focused on research and are authorized to award doctorates in specific subjects.

  A first call for applications was launched and 100 doctoral students were identified to benefit from the support provided under the Project (against
  the end-target of 300). The difficulties encountered in the implementation thus far are linked to the insufficient number of applications in certain
  fields such as Arabic, German, etc. but also the low availability of supervisors in specific areas. To cope with the shortage of supervisors, the
  mission strongly recommended the approach of co-supervision of doctoral students. As the project is about two years from its end, the mission
  suggested, in view of the candidates registered on the waiting list for the recruitment process, but also the various calls for applications to recruit
  doctoral students being launched in several universities, to federate initiatives (meaning that doctoral students could also be recruited from the lists
  of other current doctoral schools) so that the 300 doctoral students expected from the project are recruited now and can benefit from two to three
  years of scholarship. The results of this call will therefore be published followed by an additional list and possibly another call for applications in
  collaboration with interested universities.

   Sub-component 2.3 - Strengthen Quality Assurance in HEI. The implementation of quality assurance in Burkina Faso with regard to the
  development of the national quality assurance strategy, the establishment of the national qualification framework for higher education, and the
  establishment of internal quality assurance units (IQAU) continues. In view of all the difficulties that punctuated the process, the mission provided
  indications and orientations so that not only the national strategy is quickly validated but that the Government makes the choice of the structure best
  suited to its national framework.
  The mission expressed its satisfaction with the results obtained and lavished encouragement for the future.

  Sub-component 2.4 - Strengthen HEIs Management Capacity. The development of the organizational and functional diagnostic document for the
  HERI has experienced significant challenges and delays in implementation. The major difficulty in developing the document lies in the survey of
  teachers, whose participation so far has been very low. The mission was concerned about the progress of this activity and suggested greater
  involvement of university officials in order to trigger the necessary click at the level of teachers to allow the finalization of the survey. The new
  schedule for the submission of the diagnostic report as well as the update on pending activities at the level of this sub-component are expected on
  January 31st, 2022.
  .

   Component 3. Project coordination, management and implementation activities. The higher education program which oversees the Higher
  Education Support Project held its second review committee session of the year 2021 on December 23rd, 2021, roughly around the examination
  and adoption of the summary report of the first ordinary session of 2021; the presentation of the project's draft 2022 ABWP and the 2022 draft
  procurement plan. The review committee adopted the proposed 2022 ABWP and project procurement plan.

  The overall physical achievement rate of this component is estimated at 59.14% out of a total number of 61 activities registered in the 2021 ABWP.
  This overall rate depends on the large number of activities (nine 9) which have not yet been launched.
