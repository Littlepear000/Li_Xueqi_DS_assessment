I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Project Overview and Implementation Status

 1.    The Central Asia Regional Links (CARs) program is a series of projects (SOP) in multiple countries in Central Asia.
 The CARs SOP is the result of a collaborative effort initiated by client governments. The program-level objective of the
 SOP is to increase cross-border connectivity and enhance integrated regional development to revitalize historically
 active economic exchanges in Central Asia and beyond along the Silk Route.

 2.    The Third Phase of the Central Asia Regional Links Program (CARs‐3, P159220) was approved on October 5,
 2018, in the amount of US$55 million (US$27.5 million grant and US$27.5 million credit). The Project became effective
 on January 30, 2020.

 3.    The CARs‐3 Project finances activities related to removing physical bottlenecks with neighboring countries and
 addressing sector-specific constraints to create market opportunities for the development of regional trade
 (agriculture) and tourism in Issyk‐Kul Oblast, Kyrgyz Republic. The Project is implemented by a single implementing
 agency, the Ministry of Transport and Roads (MoTR), with the technical support of the Department of Tourism within
 the Ministry of Culture, Information and Tourism (MoCIT).

 4.     The Project Development Objective (PDO) of the CARs‐3 Project, to increase regional connectivity and support
 sustainable tourism development in Issyk‐Kul Oblast, is rated Moderately Unsatisfactory, and will not be achieved in
 full by the current closing date of November 30, 2024. Overall Implementation Progress is rated Moderately
 Satisfactory due to satisfactory progress in implementation of project activities including progress on civil works and
 institutional components and increase in the disbursement ratio to 34% (up from 14% in June 2023) or US$18.7 million.
 Of undisbursed balance of US$36.3 million, around 80% of funds is committed.

B. Implementation Status

 5.     The project encountered substantial challenges and delays in its initial years of implementation. Firstly, the
 Project suffered from an initial 15-month setback due to lengthy internal government procedures for the effectiveness.
 Secondly, delays in all procurement procedures and overall progress were caused due to worldwide pandemic COVID-
 19 in 2020 for about 4 months (from end of March to end of summer 2020). Thirdly, this was due to insufficient readiness
 of the design documentation for the civil works on reconstruction of 50 km sections of the Tyup-Kegen road under the
 Component 1. The conduction of all set of topographical and geological field surveys had been required for the design
 documentation development. Due to unfavorable weather conditions caused by heavy snowfall in project area the study
 completion was delayed for 4 months. Respectively, the issuance of final bidding documents was delayed from July to
 October 2021.

 6.    Implementation has picked up with the commencement of road civil works under Component 1 in May 2023.
 The following is the summary of Project progress by components:

       (a)   Component 1. Regional Connections, Associated Facilities and Equipment in Issyk‐Kul Oblast: the first
             construction season's overall progress was slightly slower than anticipated (Table 1). The Contractor China
             Road and Bridge Corporation (CRBC) managed to achieve some progress on earthworks on both lots and
             completed a section of 1 km with a binder course at Santash village. While the Contractor was fully
             mobilized, inadequate equipment and access to earth materials slowed the pace of works. The current
             rating of the component is Moderately Satisfactory (MS).

                   by the National Statistics Committee.
 7.    The Project is fully compliant with environmental and social safeguards. The overall Safeguards rating is
 Moderately Satisfactory. Adequate fiduciary controls are in place (including required audits), and regular,
 comprehensive, and timely financial reports are provided. All audit and financial management reports are being
 receiving on time and found acceptable to the Bank. Both Financial Management and Procurement ratings have been
 upgraded to Satisfactory.

 8.    Financial Management. The Project audit report for FY2022 was submitted by the due date and was acceptable
 to the Bank. The auditor issued unmodified audit opinion, and no issues were raised in the management letter. Currently
 there are no overdue auditor reports.

C. Rationale for restructuring
 9.     On March 29, 2024, the Ministry of Finance formally requested an extension of the closing date until October 31,
 2026. The 23-month extension of the closing date will allow to complete the remaining activities and contracts which
 are critical for the full achievement of the PDO. Additionally, the restructuring will include changes to the project results

 framework matrix to allow for alignment with corporate requirements (WBG Scorecard retrofitting) and adjustment of
 baseline and target values based on the recently conducted survey.

 10. The requested closing date extension will be complemented by a course of tailored actions included in the detailed
 Action Plan, which was prepared by the WB IPIG and agreed with the MoTC to ensure timely completion of the remaining
 project activities by the extended closing date.


II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

A. Description of proposed changes
 11.   Proposed changes to the Project are the following:

       (a)    Extension of Project closing date by 23 months to October 31, 2026. This is driven by the additional time
              required to ensure completion of the remaining activities and to achieve the PDO, particularly road civil
              works under Component 1. The duration of the construction season in the Project area is short due to
              severe weather conditions during the fall and winter months.
       (b)    Adjustments to the Results Framework are required to synchronize with the current implementation
              progress and adequately monitor the implementation and results of activities under Components 1, 2 and
              3. Adjusted results framework will allow better tracking of activities.
                   i. Changes to PDO indicators: the number of PDO indicators will increase from three to four and
                      some baseline and end target values will be adjusted:
             •    Increase the baseline and end target value for PDO Indicator 1 (number of vehicles passing through
                  Karkyra border crossing point). Baseline for this indicator will change from 7,000 vehicles to 8,131
                  vehicles, which is the total number of vehicles that crossed the Karkyra checkpoint in both directions
                  (from the Kyrgyz Republic to Kazakhstan, and from Kazakhstan to the Kyrgyz Republic) in 2017.
                  Additionally, the end target will be amended from 8,500 vehicles per year to 21,000 vehicles crossing
                  the Karkyra checkpoint per year. The end target value was calculated using the average annual
                  incremental increase of vehicles crossing border (about 1,688) from 2013 to 2019 up to COVID-19 year
                  multiplying to 8 years (from 2017 to 2025 full years) plus data from base year 2017.
              •   Add new PDO Indicator – Reduction in time required for travel by motor vehicle from km 39 to 76 of
                  Tyup-Kegen road and from km 0 to km 13 of Karkyra-Turuk-Sary-Zhaz road section). The estimated
                  baseline for this component is 94 minutes and covers travel time through both road sections. The
                  average time required to travel through the entire 50 km of fully constructed road with an average
                  permitted speed limit of 60 km/h is about 50 minutes. Following such estimations, the end target for
                  this indicator would be 50 minutes.
             •    Adjust the baseline and end target value for PDO Indicator 3 – Change of community satisfaction with
                  sustainable tourism -- in order to evaluate the project outcome and the actual results of Component 3
                  of the project, it was suggested to change PDO level indicator to “Assessment of the local population
                  satisfaction level with the sustainable development of tourism in the location areas of the Tourist
                  Service Centers (TSC) and of the construction of the Tyup-Kegen road”. Relevantly, it was suggested to
                  indicate the baseline as 62% according to the survey among the local population of the Issyk-Kul oblast
                  which was conducted in 2022 by THR consulting company as part of the work on the development of
                  a strategy for sustainable tourism development, the development of the Strategic Environmental and
