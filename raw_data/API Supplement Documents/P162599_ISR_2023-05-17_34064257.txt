 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The project was approved by the World Bank Board on July 1, 2019, and became effective on December 3, 2019. The implementation of the project
  has encountered significant delays in part due to the Covid-19 crisis.
  On Component 1:

  I. On Rural Connectivity: The Request for Bids (RFB) for the extension of digital coverage in the targeted areas was successfully closed on June
  16th, 2022. The contract was awarded to two mobile operators (notification of award was provided to the operators on March 2023). All parties
  involved signed the contracts on April 20,2023. The procurement process is expected to be finalized by end of May 2023 at the latest. The
  construction works of the rural digital connectivity sites should start by end of FY and be finalized within 12 months at the latest, before the current
  closing date (July 30, 2024).
  II. On Connecting decentralized State agencies and administrations:
  A - Decentralized administrations (Tender #1): On-site works are scheduled to begin in June 2023, awaiting finalization of environmental and social
  instruments (Management plan and Impact Notice) as well as processing of advance payment to the supplier.
  B - Decentralized state agencies (Tender #2.1): On-site works are scheduled to begin in June 2023 awaiting finalization of environmental and social
  instruments (Management plan and Impact Notice).
  C - IoT Labs (Tender #2.2): The competitive bidding process was unsuccessful. The technical specifications are being revised and the tender will be
  relaunched in June 2023.

  On Component 2:
  I. On Sub-Component 2.1 (“Digital financial inclusion”):The "grant manual for the merchants' payment devices” was finalized by the PIU and
  adopted on March 7, 2023. The procedure for the lifting of the related disbursement condition (Category 3 as per the Financing Agreement) was
  finalized as of March 22, 2023. After reaching a consensus among all stakeholders in March 2023, the tender on the microfinance platform is now
  planned to be launched by June 2023 and awarded by September 2023.
  II. On Sub-Component 2.2 (“Strengthening the data ecosystem”) and Sub-component 2.3 (“Mobilization of the digital ecosystem”) supporting digital
  solutions for rural development, two platforms have been developed so far: (A) the digital platform for agricultural e-Advisory services to foster
  access to information for producers; and (B) the digital platform for the management of agricultural materials and equipment for the benefit of
  producers within the value chains targeted by the project. These platforms are currently being piloted with a group of 200 producers/service users.
  The business models were also developed and will be piloted once the first pilots on the technical design of the platforms are completed. The full
  scale-up of the platforms is planned for June 2023 (after the end of the pilot phase). The demo versions of a third platform on farm management
  and monitoring is available as of February 2023 and has been pre-validated. Residual improvements to the platform have been addressed. The
  platform has been validated on March 31, 2023.There is a need to closely follow up the PIU to ensure a pilot of the third platform is launched by
  May 2023 with at least 200 users and full deployment, including testing of the approved business model, by July 2023.

                                        As of January 31, 2023, 6,222 farmers (of which 33% women) were reached with non-financial digital
                                        services out of the initial target to reach 500,000 farmers (of which 50% women) by project closing. The
                                        initial target had to be set during the last restructuring without knowing the results of the national census
                                        (Recensement National de l’Agriculture RNA) that started in 2019, and whose results were eventually
                                        published in late November 2022. After analyzing the data, the PIU and Task Team concluded that the
                                        total number of producers for all value chains combined was 785,987 (including 220,918 women) in the
   Comments:
                                        project area (called Agricultural Development Poles – or Pôles de Développement Agricole PDA – number
                                        1, 2, 3 and 4). After restricting the perimeter to the producers active in the value chains targeted by the
                                        Project (rice, maize, shea, vegetable crops), the figures are even lower with 400,000 producers. After
                                        reviewing the situation, the PIU suggested revising the target to 150,000, but the Task Team considers
                                        that with additional efforts the project can reach 200,000 farmers, including 100,000 women.
