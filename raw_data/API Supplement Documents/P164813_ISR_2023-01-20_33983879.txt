 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  This is an updated ISR based on Implementation Support Mission conducted November 14-30, 2022 and project progress are summarized below:
       1. Pillar A (Improving the Business Environment). Following progress was achieved (i) Procedures to start a business have been further
            simplified, moving the requirement of social security application to ex-post enterprise registration. A survey conducted in 2019 showed
            around 40 percent of companies reported getting an Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) in less than 10 days. Administrative data
            from the Department of Enterprise Registration Management (DERM) reported an average of 1.79 days for the issuance of the ERC in
            2022. (ii) The inter-ministerial task force prepared a list of 31 priority operating licenses (OLs) to be streamlined. Seven out of these have
            already been streamlined in the following sectors: tourism (OLs for accommodation, restaurant, tour operator), precious metals (OLs for
            import-export and trading of jewelry), mining, land services. The business operating licensing portal is under review and will be updated
            and published in the first quarter of 2023.

      2.      Pillar B (Facilitating Trade and Connecting to Markets). Following progress was achieved (i) The National Trade Facilitation
              Committee (NTFC) is the main forum to resolve trade facilitation issues raised by the private sector at the central and provincial levels. Out
              of the fifty-five issues raised at the last NTFC in January 2021, twenty-eight have been satisfactorily resolved. The NTFC will be
              restructured and its mandate expanded to incorporate transport facilitation. (ii) Data collection for the 2022 Time Release Study (TRS) was
              completed end of October 2022. Data entry and analysis should be completed first quarter of 2023. (iii) Progress was made in
              implementing recommendations on regulatory and procedural reforms under import and export permits, licenses and certification in
              DIMEX and FDD. (iv) Under WTO-TFA CF proposals work has picked up following lifting of Laos Covid-19 related-travel restrictions. Food
              and Drug Department (FDD) and Department of Livestock and Fisheries (DOLF) completed their tasks under the CF proposals; work is
              now progressing on the development of risk profiles and SOPs.
      3.      Pillar C (Improving Firm-level Competitiveness). Despite very challenging macro-economic conditions, business demand for BAF-2
              matching grants remains strong. BAF-2 continues to help private enterprises access business development services including product
              designs, marketing, software development and training to improve management and innovation capacity. Since the last ISR, BAF-2 team
              completed several outreach events in northern, central and southern regions. By November 25, 2022, 367 grants had been approved to
              firms both in urban and remote areas for a total support of USD1.88 million (excluding taxes). Among those approved matching grants, 63
              percent were to women-led businesses and 21 percent of them to firms located outside of Vientiane Capital, spanning across 12
              provinces. By the same date, 261 matching grants had been fully disbursed, equivalent to US$1.32 million. Since the start of the scheme
              356 firms have received free advisory service on how to achieve growth.
      4.      Pillar D (Supporting Better and More Inclusive Policies). The new safeguards instruments added with the Additional Financing and
              applicable to all project activities are being implemented. The gender work continues to progress and strengthen. A new NIU Gender
              Advisor is on board. The integrated project level Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) based on agreed structure and procedure is
              operational from January 01, 2023. A communications specialist is on board and the communications firm tasked with project
              communications efforts both in Vientiane as well as at provincial level will be hired by the first quarter of 2023. The dialogue between the
              government and the private sector on trade and private sector development issues has made some good progress in identifying issues
              and technical discussions led by Lao Business Forum (LBF) secretariat. The result in terms of issues resolved remains limited. Senior
              Management of MOIC and the LNCCI have met to discuss options to improve the resolution mechanism and rate. The LBF 14 should take
              place in early 2023. Private sector issues were discussed in alignment with Government priorities and donors support at the 19th Trade
              Private Sector Working Group in November 2022
