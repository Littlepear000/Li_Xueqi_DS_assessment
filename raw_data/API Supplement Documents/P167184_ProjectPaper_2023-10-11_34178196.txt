    I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Request for Restructuring

      1. This restructuring paper seeks the approval of the World Bank’s Country Director for Papua New Guinea and
         Pacific Islands for a Level Two restructuring of the Papua New Guinea Improving Access to and Value from Health
         Services in PNG: Financing the Frontlines (IMPACT Health, P167184). The proposed restructuring is needed to
         address project implementation challenges and limited progress towards the project development objective (PDO).
         The restructuring comprises the following proposed changes: (a) reallocation of SDR 3.104 million or US$ 4.275
         million1 equivalent in undisbursed Disbursement Linked Indicator (DLI) financing from Component 2 to Component
         1 and updating the disbursement projections; (b) revise Component 1 and Component 2 to streamline project
         interventions and simplify implementation; (c) revise the DLIs and results framework indicators and targets to
         reflect the changes made to components and the implementation schedule; (d) revise the date of the mid-term
         review (MTR) from December 2023 to June 2024; and (e) increase the designated account ceiling from PGK 200,000
         to PGK 800,000. The World Bank received a formal restructuring request from the Government of PNG (GoPNG)
         Treasury on June 23, 2023.

B. Project Status

    1. Progress towards achievement of the PDO2 and implementation progress are rated Moderately Unsatisfactory.
       The Government of Papua New Guinea (GoPNG), the National Department of Health (NDOH) and the Provincial
       Health Authorities (PHAs) remain committed to the achievement of the PDO and view the project as essential to
       implement parts of the recently approved National Health Plan and National Health Service Standards. However,
       implementation is significantly delayed. Limited financing has been committed under Component 1 and there are
       delays in the flow of funds against verified results under Component 2. The Project was made effective on December
       16, 2020, and the closing date is June 30, 2026. Total disbursements remain low at US$1.00 million or 3.45%3 of the
       original financing amount (US$30.0 million equivalent). Due to exchange rate fluctuations, the current value of the
       remaining funds is approximately US$28.94 million equivalent.

    2. Some achievements have been gained in technical areas of project implementation including the accomplishment
       of several DLI targets for Year 1 and Year 2 for the NDOH and PHAs, the trialing of a digitized integrated supervision
       checklist and the development of a PHA Dashboard. Good progress has been made since the last implementation
       support mission in May 2023 on three key targets which are required for an upgrade of the project ratings: (i) the
       Independent Verification Agent (IVA) for Year 1 and Year 2 is appointed (on June 8, 2023) and the verification
       process for Year 1 DLIs is currently underway; (ii) a Project restructuring on agreed changes (i.e., summarized by
       this restructuring paper) and cancellation of the memorandum of understanding (MOU) with Enga province
       (completed); and (iii) the Special Procurement Committee (SPC) has been sworn in and established on June 20, 2023
       to enable NDoH to conduct larger value procurements.

         could cover these activities in multiple provinces. This will capitalize on existing NDOH initiatives and alleviate
         the challenge of multiple small procurements.

         activities which remain: (i) increasing the transportation capacity to conduct supportive supervision and
         outreach; and (ii) strengthening the Public Financial Management (PFM) capacity of the Project PHAs. A recent
         Public Financial Management assessment that was conducted in East New Britain will be used to inform the
         approach towards strengthening PFM with a focus on on-the-job training, skills development, and production
         of job aids. To support this approach, a partnership between the NDOH and the Department of Finance’s
         Provincial Capacity Building and Enhancement Programme (PCaB) has been discussed, with the Project paying
         for the incremental costs to support the PHA PFM training activities.

C. Rational for Restructuring

    11. IMPACT Health remains relevant, viewed by NDOH as a flagship health project and provides the highly needed
        support to essential health services in PNG as the country continues to face poor health outcomes and lagging key
        health indicators. Maternal and child mortality rates remain high for the country’s economic development status
        and immunization rates low, a tracer indicator for basic population health. Diphteria, Tetanus and Pertusis vaccine
        for children under two for example is only at 31%6 . The condition of health facilities remains poor with 30% to 60%
        of health facilities from level 3 and level 4 facilities in PNG requiring significant refurbishments according to the
        National Health Plan 2021-2030. The introduction of the DLIs as performance-based funding for the health sector
        is viewed by local stakeholders as a catalyst for improving and incentivizing improved service delivery results, where
        fund flow for routine health expenditures is seen as a critical bottleneck. PHAs participating in the project continue
        to have strong ownership and engagement in the project. Since the inception of the project at the end of December
        2020, implementation disruptions stem from COVID-19 travel restrictions and the overall strain on the health sector
        during the pandemic; the PNG elections in 2022 which impeded the ability of the provinces to operate and achieve
        their DLIs; as well as ongoing violence in one Project-province. In addition, the health project faced lengthy
        government procurement procedures which has impeded progress, but this is expected to improve with the
        establishment of the SPC. It was agreed with NDOH that a restructuring to address the one-year delay in the
        achievement of the DLIs, account for the cancellation of the MOU with one of the Early Adopter Provinces and
        streamline the Project where appropriate. This decision was discussed and endorsed by the special Project Steering
        Committee, requested by the NDOH to the Department of Treasury and communicated by the Department of
        Treasury to the World Bank on June 23, 2023.
