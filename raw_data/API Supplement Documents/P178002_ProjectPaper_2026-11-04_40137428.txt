I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING
A. Project description
1. Project background. LAOSTAT-II is an IDA-financed Investment Project Financing operation supporting the
Government of Lao PDR to strengthen the national statistical system. The project supports production and dissemination of good-quality statistics; enhanced use of statistics through data integration, dissemination, ICT infrastructure, and user engagement; and institutional capacity and project management. The PDO is closely aligned with the Country Partnership Framework FY22-26 in terms of strengthening governance and institution; it also directly supports the M&E framework of the National Socio-Economic Development Plan 2021–2025. The project was approved on 19 December 2022, became effective on 12 May 2023, and is scheduled to close on 15
February 2028.
2. Project components. The project has four components: i) improving availability and quality of statistics; ii)
enhancing use of statistics; iii) strengthening institutional capacity and project management; and iv) Contingent
Emergency Response (CERC). Each of the three main components consists of several sub-components, as displayed in Table 1 below. To date, the CERC component has not been activated.

3. Implementation arrangements. The project has been managed by the Lao Statistics Bureau (LSB) under the
oversight of a Project Steering Committee, consisting of representatives from involved ministries. The Project
Implementation and Management Team (PIMT) is fully staffed with the Head of LSB serving as the Project
Director and a Deputy Director General of LSB serving as the Project Manager. The project initially involved 17
Project Implementation Units (PIUs) across eight ministries, two research institutes, the Bank of Laos, and the
National University of Laos. After the government restructuring in 2025, the number of PIUs decreased to 13, as illustrated in Figure 1 below.

B. Project implementation status
5. Operational performance:
a. Disbursement: As of the most recent ISM (18-21 May 2026), total disbursement reached 54%. Actual project spending, while still low as a percentage of the approved Annual Workplan and Budget, has gradually improved over time from 6% in 2023, to 40% and 41% in 2024 and 2025, respectively, and 44%

b. Procurement: 55 packages have been cleared by the Bank in STEP, of which 52 packages have signed or completed contracts with a total value of US$5.79 million. Due to delays in some key packages (e.g.

Procurement performance rating has been downgraded to Moderately Satisfactory at the last ISM in May
2026.
c. Financial Management: The project maintains compliance with core FM requirements including timely submission of Interim Financial Reports and Audited Financial Statements of acceptable quality. Satisfactory. Agreed FM actions from the MTR were successfully implemented.

Satisfactory” to “Satisfactory” at the last ISM. The risk level remains at “Low”. No new GRM case was reported at the last ISM. Additionally, the PIMT recently appointed new ESF focal points at the province level to help coordinate E&S tasks and reporting.

6. Implementation of project activities.
a. Achievements: Since effectiveness in May 2023, the project has achieved important milestones on GDP statistics (Component 1.1) and Social Statistics (Component 1.2). In particular, since 2024 the project has produced and regularly published GDP by expenditure (current and structure) and, for the first time in
Lao PDR, quarterly GDP (current, constant, structure, growth rate), raising the number of published economic aggregates to 13. The project has also produced key social data and statistics, namely the Laos
Expenditure and Consumption Survey 2024/25, the Gender Factbook based on the Labor Force Survey
2022, and the Population and Housing Census (with fieldwork completed on 28 Dec 2025 and additional funding provided by the project in response to request from LSB). Export-Import Price Index and a new
Consumer Price Index basket have been constructed to serve the upcoming National Account rebasing exercise. The first Annual Labor Force Survey commenced fieldwork in late August 2026, with on-going monthly data collection. Various capacity building activities, including data literacy workshops, training, and funding for key personnel to attend international conferences on statistics, have been conducted.
The project has also provided ICT equipment and infrastructure to LSB, National University of Laos, and selected PIUs to improve their statistical and operational capacity.
b. Progress toward the PDO indicators has been mixed, with slow progress and critical delays in Component 1.1
(Economic Statistics) and Component 2 (Enhancing Use of Statistics) now threatening overall project success.
There has been limited progress on these components since the MTR in November 2025, and consequently, no progress on PDO 1 and PDO 3. The project is on track to achieve PDO 2, as shown in Table 2.
i. The sub-optimal performance on PDO 1 is due to delays in activities that feed into the National Account rebasing, limited technical capacity, weak coordination among and within PIUs, and lengthy approval processes for procurement of consulting services. While 13 economic aggregates earmarked for publication under PDO 1 have been produced, they are still based on the existing GDP base year of
2012 and thus do not reflect the recent economic landscape and are not compliant with the 2008
System of National Account guidelines. To achieve PDO 1, the rebasing of National Accounts to the new base year of 2025 must be completed and the rebased economic aggregates published by
February 2027.

temporal. They include:
a. On-going challenges:
i. Low absorptive capacity in PIUs relative to the project’s scale and complexity.
ii. Weak coordination mechanism across PIUs and within LSB iii. Cumbersome approval processes and a lack of empowerment of technical staff in PIUs iv. Difficulties in collecting administrative data from line ministries to construct macroeconomic statistics due to weak data sharing mechanisms and weak coordination.

iii. Since LSB is mapped to MoF, new approval processes at MoF for project activities and procurement packages require extra preparation time and have caused significant delays in key procurement packages.
iv. The government restructuring in 2025 also caused significant disruptions and loss of time to the project. Uncertainties in duty assignment created confusion and unwillingness to take actions in both
LSB and line ministries. Project staff were only endorsed by MoF on 14 September 2025 and staffing of PIUs in several line ministries was only confirmed at the end of 2025. This challenge has now been resolved.

8. The proposed Level 2 restructuring follows findings from the MTR and the 5th ISM as well as technical discussions
between LSB/MoF and the World Bank task team. A formal request letter for the Restructuring, signed by the
Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Finance, was received by the World Bank on 23 June 2026. This will be the first restructuring of the project.
9. The restructuring would involve (i) removal of some project activities under Components 1 and 2 to strengthen
efficiency and reduce implementation bottlenecks, while maintaining the PDO and core outputs, (ii) revise some result indicators to better and more realistically monitor progress, and (iii) reallocate some budget from
Component 2 to Components 1 and 3 to optimize resources for evolving technical needs. The proposed removal of some project activities requires a corresponding amendment to the Financing Agreement. The project remains focused on (i) production of key economic and social statistics, including national accounts, labor statistics, and price statistics; (ii) data integration; and (iii) data dissemination. The proposed restructuring would address three key challenges that are impeding implementation progress, as follows.
10. Limited capacity and human resources in PIUs are overstretched to handle the current complexity and scope of
the project. The executing agencies’ low technical capacity, weak coordination, and limited resources remain key obstacles to project implementation. Currently, both PIMT and the key PIU within LSB – the Department of
Economic and Social Statistics – are stretched thin to implement Components 1 and 2, with the activities under their responsibilities accounting for the major share of the project’s budget and planned procurement packages.
The National Account rebasing exercise alone requires various activities under sub-components 1.1.1, 1.1.2 and
1.1.3 to be implemented in parallel, with extensive data sharing and coordination among LSB and the PIUs in line ministries and the Bank of Laos. While the Task Team provides regular technical assistance and helps bridge the coordination across PIUs, maintaining the current activity load on them is unsustainable and counterproductive.
With only 18 months left until closing date, the project would be more impactful to focus its resources on key building blocks of the statistical system, namely the production of key economic statistics, including the rebased
National Accounts. By dropping non-essential activities, the PIMT and PIUs can better focus their efforts and resources on the more important tasks that feed directly into the result indicators.
11. Evolving needs of the government call for adjustments on budget allocations.
a. Component 1 requires more budget to meet evolving needs of the government in monitoring poverty and social development.
b. As stated in the Project Appraisal Document, sub-component 1.1.4 supports the Enterprise Census and
Survey. At Appraisal, it was expected that the Enterprise Census would be conducted in 2025 with government funding, and the Enterprise Survey would start in 2026 with the project’s funding, using the
2025 census as a sampling frame. However, due to conflicting schedules with the 2025 PHC, the Economic census was postponed. The AES was implemented in 2026 as planned, but with an incomplete sampling frame. The Economic Census is now needed to re-weight the Enterprise Survey sample for the purpose of rebasing National Accounts. Given the government’s on-going efforts to improve domestic resource mobilization, MoF has also expressed renewed interest in the Enterprise Census to strengthen the country’s tax base. The project is well positioned to finance a part of this census.
The World Bank

Official Use Only c. As stated in the Project Appraisal Document, sub-component 1.2.2 supports the design and implementation of the LECS. At the project’s Appraisal, it was expected that only one round of LECS, i.e.
LECS 2024/25, would be implemented within the life of the project. However, the government has recently set poverty reduction as a formal National Agenda for the period of 2026-30. To monitor poverty progress and assess outcomes of this agenda and the National Socio-economic Development Plan (NSEDP)
2026-30. MoF now expects the next LECS to be pushed forward to 2028 instead of 2029, for which design of survey instruments, purchase of enumeration kits and training of enumerators need to be completed in 2027. Given the fundamental role of LECS in monitoring poverty and informing the assessment of the
NSED and the Bank’s strong engagement on poverty reduction in Laos, there is solid value for the Bank to continue supporting this survey, and the project is well positioned to do so.
d. Component 3.2 needs slightly more budget to finance increased costs of project management. As noted in the Aide Memoire of the 5th ISM, budget estimates for many procurement packages, particularly the costs of individual consultants were unrealistic due to reactive (instead of proactive) communication between PIMT and PIUs on procurement planning and the finalization of technical specifications/TORs.
Due to inflation-based wage adjustments and high-than-expected cost of individual consultants, the current allocation is expected to run short by the end of project.
e. The project’s current budget allocation across sub-components does not support the above needs. In addition to the emerging needs that were not expected at Appraisal, another factor has contributed to the need for budget reallocation. Responding to a request from MoF in September 2025, the project provided additional financial resources (approximately US$1.04 million) to the PHC 2025 (sub-component
1.2.3), funding the training of trainers, training of enumerators and part of the data collection fieldwork.
This was on top of the initially planned budget for this sub-component. While this extra support did not affect the budget allocation at that time, it meant that as new needs emerge under Component 1, adjustments to allocation across subcomponents are needed.
12. Some existing endline targets were hard to measure or too ambitious. Table 6 presents the rationale for each
adjustment to the result indicators. Most notably:
a. PDO 3 measures changes in number of downloads number of downloads relative to the baseline period of
June-November 2023, i.e. 6 months after the project effectiveness. However, monthly downloads fluctuate, partly depending on the release of new publications and datasets. As noted in the Aide Memoire of the MTR, the definition of PDO 3 does not effectively indicate progress on data dissemination, and it should be reviewed and revised.
b. The ALFS was delayed for more than two years due to slow start of project implementation during the first year of the project and the PHC in 2025 fully occupying LSB’s Social Statistics division. By the end of the project, only 5 quarters of labor market data can be completed. This means that the original endline target of IR 2 is no longer realistic.
Both intermediate and endline target of IRI 10 are well above the total number of LSB staff members which is only 101.
The indicator’s definition also makes it difficult to capture the fact that one staff member could receive multiple training as part of the project’s capacity building.

II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES
13. The following adjustments to the project are proposed:
a. Drop non-critical activities to optimize project resources to support the building blocks of the Laos
Statistical System and accelerate progress toward full achievement of the Project Development
Objectives. These include sub-components 1.1.5, 2.1.2, 2.1.3, 2.1.4, and 2.2.4 (see Table 4). The
Financing Agreement will be amended to reflect the removal of these sub-components from the
Project’s scope.
b. Reallocate budget from Component 2 to Component 1.2 and Component 3.2, to better support evolving needs of the government and reflect the removal of sub-components above. See Table 5 for more details.
c. Adjust the result framework as follows:
i. Revise PDO 3 to reduce seasonal fluctuation in the data and better monitor progress in data dissemination and access.
ii. Revise four intermediate results indicators (IRI 1, IRI 2, IRI 9, and IRI 10) to better track progress and set more realistic endline targets in line with current implementation progress and data availability. Table 6 describes the proposed revisions and their rationale in detail.
14. Expected impacts of the restructuring on implementation performance and result framework:
a. The restructuring does not change the existing PDO, financing envelope, implementation arrangements, fiduciary arrangements, procurement arrangements, E&S scope and risk classification, and closing date.
b. The activities to be dropped have not commenced; no disbursement or expenditure has been incurred on them. They are not operationally related to any of the remaining activities. Their expected outputs, if they are to be retained, do not contribute to the PDO indicators and IRIs. Thus, dropping them at this stage would not result in unfinished activities nor affect the remaining activities or the result framework.
The task team will ensure that the dropped activities are reflected consistently in STEP, updated Annual
Workplan and Budget, future ISRs, and the project’s record.
c. The proposed budget reallocation would allow sufficient funding for key data collection activities that have become more policy relevant, namely the Economic Census, the 8th LECS, as well as increased project management costs due to inflation.
d. The revision of the result framework will more realistically and more accurately monitor progress, given the current implementation status and data availability.
