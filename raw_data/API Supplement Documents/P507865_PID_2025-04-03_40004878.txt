    B. Introduction and Context

    Country Context

1.      The education sector in India has undergone significant transformations over the years. This transformation has
been marked by several major initiatives and reforms aimed at improving access, equity, and learning outcomes. The
National Education Policy, 2020, has been a cornerstone in this transformation, introducing a new pedagogical structure,
emphasizing foundational literacy and numeracy, and promoting holistic development of students. The focus on teacher
professional development, digital learning, and school to work transition has further contributed to elevating the quality
of education, ensuring that students are better prepared for the demands of the 21st century.

    Sectoral and Institutional Context
2.       With 31.2 million population, Assam accounted for 2.2 percent of Indian population in 2011 census . Economic
growth in the state has been positive but continues to rely heavily on agriculture and allied services. The state has one of
the highest proportions of rural population in India. Despite significant decline in the poverty head count ratio, the state
still has one of the highest levels of multi-dimensional poverty in India. The state performs poorly on NITI Aayog’s
Sustainable Development Goals index, especially on hunger, quality education, gender equality, and industry, innovation
& infrastructure. Climate-related vulnerabilities and extreme climate events pose additional challenges to the
development.
3.       School enrollment rates in the state have improved in the last decade, but learning levels remain low. Findings
from representative surveys measuring foundational skills tests and curricula-based tests consistently show that students
in Assam are not achieving minimum learning levels, and this has not improved over time. Several factors explain the low
learning levels, including student’s low school preparedness, limited availability of qualified teachers, and school
fragmentation, among others. The curriculum taught in Anganwadi Centers (AWCs)1 and pre-schools requires
strengthening to enhance focus on cognitive, language and social skills necessary for school readiness. The majority of
Anganwadi Workers require capacity building to deliver Early Childhood Care and Education effectively. As per Unified
District Information System for Education, 2023-24, the percentage of trained teachers in the state for primary and

4.       Assam has higher than country average dropout rates at all levels and significantly higher dropouts at secondary

5.      Assam’s adolescent population, comprising close to 21 percent of the state population, faces several challenges
including poor health outcomes and high unemployment. Social factors, high drop-out from schools, early marriage, and
lack of adequate progression pathways lead to high level of NEET (not in education, employment, or training) among
adolescents.
 Relationship to CPF
6.       The proposed project aligns well with the objectives of the India Country Partnership Framework (CPF) FY18-22
(Report No. 126667-IN, July 25, 2018), which was extended to FY25. The CPF emphasizes building state capabilities,
developing human capital, addressing gender issues, and leveraging India's federal structure by collaborating with state
governments. The project is relevant to two focus areas of the CPF: (a) increasing investment in children's and adolescents’
development, and (b) improving the quality of education in schools. The project will support the CPF's focus on enhancing
public service quality and efficiency and promoting innovative practices in states to foster human capital development by
aligning education systems to prepare youth for future jobs, reduce poverty, and advance inclusive prosperity.

7.      Paris Alignment: The project is consistent with India’s Nationally Determined Contributions (2021-2030)
commitments under the Paris Agreement. By supporting enhancements in education quality, and children and adolescent
well-being, the project contributes to India’s goals of sustainable development and climate adaptation by building human
capital and resilience to future shocks. The project inventions will help foster long-term capacity building, enabling
communities to adopt climate-friendly practices, and thereby furthering India’s climate resilience objectives.

 C. Proposed Development Objective(s)
 To improve student learning outcomes, secondary school completion, and adolescent well-being

 D. Concept Description
8.       The project aims to provide a continuum of support through different stages of human capital development by
improving Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE) and school readiness, equipping school-age children with
foundational skills, improving access to and quality of secondary education, and preparing youth for quality jobs and
livelihood opportunities. The project adopts a multi-sectoral approach to expand and strengthen the delivery of services
required to improve education and adolescent outcomes through four components.

9.       Component 1: Improving Foundational Learning Outcomes: This component is aimed at improving school
readiness and reducing the disparities in learning outcomes in the state by focusing on the delivery and quality of ECCE
services and improving foundational literacy in primary grades. The project will support (i) integrated curriculum for
children at AWCs and pre-primary, (ii) training of AWWs and Helpers in delivery of ECCE services; and (iii) upgradation of
a sub-set of AWCs to increase access to improved ECCE services. AWC infrastructure upgradation will prioritize those in
underserved and rural areas with special focus on centers co-located within primary schools. The project will also support
the design of a robust measurement tool with accompanying resources to assess children’s skills to succeed in school to
strengthen monitoring and reporting. To improve literacy at primary level, the project will support activities that promote
effective reading practices available to students, improvements in the curriculum and reading tools, and efficient
instructional procedures. To assist teachers in effective teaching, the project will support the development of structured
lesson plans providing clear curricula and pedagogical guidance for literacy instruction. The project will build the capacity
of the SCERT and the District Institute of Education and Training for on-the-job and pre-service teacher training.
10.     Component 2: Strengthening School System Capabilities: This component is aimed towards improving students’
completion rates at secondary level by development of a state-wide strategy for school network optimization, upgradation
of schools to secondary and senior secondary, and a data driven school management system. The project will support the
planning and implementation of a state-wide strategy for school network optimization through infrastructure upgradation
of schools. The planning for school network optimization will draw on detailed geographical mapping of schools based on
location, school size, availability of physical and human resources, and their distance from neighboring schools (public and
private), among others. Geographically difficult and tribal areas with lower secondary school density will be prioritized for
upgradation. Disaster risk reduction measures will be integrated into the planning, design, and construction of school
buildings. For improving school management, the project will support an integrated Education Management Information
System (EMIS) to enable data informed policy decisions.
11.      Component 3: Improving wellbeing, education and economic pathways for adolescents: This component will
deliver a set of services to enable transitions from school to work and family formation for adolescents who are in school
as well as to those who are out-of-school. The project will support curriculum enhancement and delivery of life skills
education covering three major themes - resilience and soft skills, physical and reproductive health and nutrition, and
career and aspirations. To improve the delivery of the curriculum for in-school adolescents, teacher capacity will be
augmented through specialized training and interactive tools. For the out-of-school adolescents, life skills curriculum will
be customized and delivered through Anganwadi and the community-based mechanism. The project will develop a
platform based on personality and capability assessment to help parents and students choose pathways aligned with their
aptitude and aspirations. To facilitate the completion of secondary education for out-of-school adolescents (below 14
years), the project will support appropriate remedial/bridge education for mainstreaming them into elementary schools.
12.     Component 4: Institutional Capacity Building and Project Management: This component will focus on
strengthening the institutional capacity of the state for improved coordination, governance and sustainability of
interventions through capacity building of the implementing agencies. At the DWCD, the project will support setting up of
a State Resource Centre for Women, Children and Adolescents that will lead the overall agenda of strengthening the last
mile service delivery, monitoring and supervision, design and rollout of need based research and evaluation studies,
among others. The project will also support an integrated management information system for DWCD programs along
with the relevant standards and protocols for data security and privacy. This component will also support project
implementation through (i) staffing of the PMU, (ii) monitoring and evaluation, (iii) communication and information
dissemination, (iv) stakeholder engagements, including beneficiary surveys and (v) other incrementing operational
expenditures.
13.     Gender: The project will address gender gaps in educational and economic pathways through (i) safe and inclusive
learning environments that can help address infrastructural barriers and risks of school related gender-based violence, (ii)

empowerment pathways, especially in STEM fields. Outcomes will be measured through improved secondary completion
rates that lead to better academic and jobs pathways.
14.     Citizen Engagement: The project includes beneficiary-centric and citizen engagement actions, including
stakeholder consultations during preparation and implementation, outreach and capacity building activities for
community institutions and teachers, enhanced transparency and accountability through improved data management
system, provision for collecting beneficiary feedback from students, teachers, parents and communities, as well as
grievance redressal mechanisms.
15.      Climate co-benefits: The project will significantly contribute to climate co-benefits by enhancing the resilience of
Assam's education infrastructure and promoting sustainable practices. It will strengthen the institutional and technical
capacity of the state to mainstream responsiveness of the education sector to growing climate and environmental
shocks/disasters. Any infrastructure/facilities enhancement under the project will embed green, climate and disaster
resilient approaches in the planning and construction process. Additionally, the project will sensitize and build capacity of
teachers, administrators and communities on adopting energy efficiency measures, emergency preparedness and usage
of technology for education continuity in cases of unplanned school closures owing to climate shocks.
16.     Environmental and Social Risks: Civil works and supply of goods under the project, which if not appropriately
managed, can potentially create adverse environmental impacts. Ensuring fair labor practices, non-discrimination, and
Occupational Health and Safety standards in project activities is essential. Improved capacity for proper planning/design,
and adoption of good standards/practices for construction and maintenance of school facilities and ensuring student
safety and community health and safety aspects during construction activities will be included. The project will address
several social issues, including the need for community consultation in selecting schools for upgradation, especially for
Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribes and those with language barriers. A sexual exploitation and abuse/sexual
harassment sensitive grievance redress mechanism will be put in place for the project. A Stakeholder Engagement Plan
will be prepared. Assessment on relevant environmental and social standards will be conducted, considering partial
application of Borrower Systems and any improvements needed (if any). These will be reflected in the Environmental and
Social Commitment Plan.
