    I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

    A. Project Status

      1. This paper seeks approval from the Regional Vice-President for a Level II restructuring of the National Social
      Safety Net Program Scale-Up (NASSP-SU, P176935, Credit No. 7019-NG), an SDR 565.3 million (equivalent toUS$800
      million at approval) Investment Project Financing (IPF). The proposed restructuring includes three key changes: (i)
      an extension of the project closing date by six months from December 31, 2025, to June 30, 2026; (ii) the
      discontinuation of Component 2 (Extended regular cash transfer [ER-CT] for the poor and vulnerable) as its original
      design is no longer relevant and the government has opted to reallocate resources to Component 1 (Economic shock
      responsive cash transfer [ESR-CT]); (iii) a reallocation of resources to adequately finance essential activities under
      Component 3 (Delivery system strengthening and project management), including the newly introduced activity under
      Component 3 (a) to integrate National Identification Number (NIN) or Bank Verification Number (BVN) with the Social
      Registry. This restructuring represents the second amendment and extension of the project’s closing date. As the
      proposed closing date extends the original by two years (24 months), Regional Vice-President approval is required.
      The request for restructuring was formally received from the Ministry of Finance in a letter dated October 14, 2025.
      The request follows extensive discussions during the July 2025 implementation and supervision mission and
      subsequent consultations with the Project Implementing Units (PIUs), the Federal Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs
      and Poverty Reduction, the Social Investment Program (SIP) Panel Secretariat, and representatives of the Federal
      Ministry of Finance. The proposed restructuring accommodates the government’s request and updates project
      activities and the results framework accordingly.

      2. NASSP-SU was approved on December 16, 2021, and became effective on January 30, 2023. The Project
      Development Objective (PDO) is to expand coverage of shock responsive safety net support among the poor and
      vulnerable and strengthen the national safety net delivery system. The project has three components: (i) ESR-CT that
      provides temporary cash transfers to provide relief against economic shocks to eligible beneficiaries in urban and rural
      areas, identified in the Social Registry1; (ii) ER-CT to continue supporting poor and vulnerable households identified
      under the National Social Safety Net Project (NASSP, P151488) with a regular cash transfer support for up to two years;
      (iii) Support to Project Management and Social Protection system strengthening activities.

      3. Overall progress towards the PDO is rated Moderately Satisfactory. The project has made significant progress in
      the recent months despite initial delays and challenges. The project became effective in October 2023 after delays in
      National Assembly’s approval of the external borrowing plan. As of October 2025, social safety net programs have
      reached approximately 42 million beneficiaries, with 8.1 million poor and vulnerable households receiving shock
      responsive cash transfers under Component 1 ESR-CT; 4.9 million of these households have female primary recipients.
      The payments delivery mechanism is fully digital, employing straight-through-processing to directly deliver the
      transfers into beneficiary accounts or wallets. Recently, the Social Registry is being linked with the NIN to further
      strengthen beneficiary verification and identification, and to enhance credibility and integrity of the delivery systems.

      4. The project implementation progress is rated Moderately Satisfactory. The PIUs at federal and state levels are
      in place. Implementation was paused from January to March 2024 during the government’s review of the National
      Social Investment Programs, including the cash transfer program supported under NASSP SU. The multi-ministerial
      Presidential Panel on SIP Panel, currently also the steering committee of the project, recommended to resume the

      procurement performance is also rated MU. The Procurement Post Review (PPR) in April 2025 revealed several non-

      Exchanges in Procurement - STEP) and recommended to attend World Bank-supported procurement trainings at the
      Ghana Institute of Management and Public Administration. The World Bank is currently conducting a FM review and
      PPR which reveals substantial improvement from April 2025, and the team expects an improvement in rating in the
      next ISR mission. An action plan has been put into place to expedite improvements in performance, including
      retirement of outstanding advances and completion of documentation of expenditures and procurement processes
      by mid-January. A detailed action plan is provided as an annex. The World Bank has continued to provide embedded
      support to meet the targets and to strengthen FM and procurement practices. There are no overdue audit reports
      and there will be no change in the audit requirements.

      6. The Environmental and Social (E&S) performance is Moderately Satisfactory, and the overall rating remains
      Substantial. The project prepared an Environmental and Social Management Plan (ESMP) which was disclosed on
      World Bank external website in January 2024 and commenced reporting on its implementation in March 2024.
      However, due to emerging occupational safety concerns, the ESMP was revised and redisclosed by the Borrower on
      4th November 2025 and in the World Bank website on 6th November 2025, and is being implemented nationwide.2,3
      Three incidents were recorded in the Environmental and Social Incident Response Toolkit (ESIRT), with root cause
      analyses completed, and corrective action plans under implementation. Significant progress has been made in
      implementing these corrective action plans and the ESIRT cases are expected to be resolved by December 30, 2025.
      The project’s Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) is functional, with the NASSP-SU Call Centre upgraded and
      operational. During the May-June 2025 reporting period, 2,473 grievances were received, with approximately 28
      percent pending further review and resolution. All security issues are being managed in accordance with the Security
      Risk Assessment and Management Plan. Continued implementation of corrective actions and strengthened
      monitoring are expected to support improvement in E&S performance ratings.

    B. Rationale for Restructuring

      7. Nigeria has faced persistent double-digit inflation since 2016, surging to multi-decade highs in 2024 (31.7
      percent after 24.7 percent in 2023), eroding real incomes and contributing to rising poverty. By 2023, an estimated
      139 million Nigerians were poor, up from 80 million in 2018/2019. While mid-2023 macro-fiscal reforms—fuel subsidy
      removal and exchange rate unification—were necessary, they exacerbated the cost of living for most Nigerians and
      pushing millions more into poverty.

      8. The government announced support programs in October 2023 to cushion the impact on the poor and
      vulnerable population. The largest of such support programs is the temporary cash transfer program to poor and
      vulnerable households. Under this program, the government plans to reach 15 million households with Nigerian Naira
      (N) 75,000 (US$51.93 equivalent) per household provided over three tranches of N 25,000 (US$17.31 equivalent) each
      month.4 The World Bank supported the broader reform agenda and the safety net support for the poor and vulnerable
      through the Reforms for Economic Stabilization to Enable Transformation (RESET) Development Policy Financing
      (P501661), including prior actions and targets tied to the rollout of the cash transfer program. A restructuring in June
      2024 aligned the project to exactly match the parameters of the government’s support program. The government is
      using this project to finance the rollout of the cash transfer program and to strengthen the safety net delivery system
      to serve as a foundation for future safety nets program. Given the program’s high-profile nature and finite resources,
      the government prioritized Component 1 (ESR-CT) and paused the implementation of regular cash transfers under
      Component 2 (R-CT). The beneficiaries and payments under Component 2 have been converted to Component 1. This
      decision reflects a tradeoff between coverage and benefit size. The government prefers to reach more people with
      limited support rather than offer larger benefits to fewer households.

      9. The government is strongly committed to meeting its target and covering the financing gap beyond IDA
      resources. Even after using all the resources allocated to Component 2 towards ESR-CT, the IDA resources will not be
      sufficient to reach 15 million households. However, the government remains committed to covering the financing gap.
      The government can draw on necessary resources up to N 300 billion (US$208 million equivalent) from the service-
      wide-vote line item in the 2025 budget towards this program when IDA resources run out. This allocation will be
      sufficient to cover the gap at current exchange rates.

      10. The project underwent key changes during implementation, most notably the integration of biometric
      identification, which needed the delivery systems and field processes to be adapted. The March 2024 introduction
      of biometric identification as a requirement for beneficiaries necessitated that the project adapt to the newer model
      of implementation than originally envisioned. First, NINs and BVNs already in the Social Registry were verified and
      validated for beneficiaries for payments. But only a small share of households had reported NINs or BVNs, and many
      among them could not be credibly verified, which led to a sluggish pace of cash transfer rollout. To address the issue
      of low coverage, NASSCO, in partnership with National Identity Management Commission (NIMC), launched a NIN
      Drive to collect and verify NINs from households in the Social Registry, accelerating rollout in recent months.
      Nonetheless, many households in the Social Registry still lack an adult with a verified and validated NIN. Adults in
      these households will have to be enrolled to obtain a NIN and their NINs be integrated into the Social Registry for
      these households to be eligible for transfers. A partnership with NIMC-licensed private sector agencies operating as
      front-end-partners for NIN enrollment has been initiated, with field deployment preparations underway. Extending
      the project closing date and reallocating resources to finance the NIN Drive will provide sufficient time and funding
      necessary to meet the government’s target and continually improve and strengthen the safety nets delivery system.

    II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

      11. Extension of the closing date. The NASSP-SU closing date will be extended from December 31, 2025, to June 30,
      2026. This extension is necessary for the shock-responsive cash transfers to reach 15 million beneficiary households.
      It also allows for institutionalized improvements in safety nets delivery systems for other current and future social
      programs. This is the second extension of closing date from the project’s original closing date of June 30, 2024. With
      this extension the project implementation period will be four years and six months. The proposed closing date
      extension meets the requirements of the World Bank IPF Directive regarding extension of closing dates, specifically:
      (i) the PDO remains achievable; (ii) the performance of the Borrower remains satisfactory; (iii) the World Bank and the
      Borrower agree on actions that will be undertaken by the Borrower to complete project critical activities; and (iv)
      there are no outstanding audits and/or interim financial reports. An action plan of critical activities has been provided
      as an annex to this Restructuring Package.

      12. Removal of Component 2. Component 2 will be dropped from the project to focus on the delivery of shock-
      responsive cash transfers. Component 2 sought to provide continued support to the beneficiaries of NASSP Project
      (P151488) so that they would receive at least two years of regular cash transfer support between NASSP and this
      project (NASSP-SU). However, due to delays in the project launch and the decision to pause Component 2 after only
      one round of payment of N10,000 per household covering two months of support, this component has lost its
      relevance as designed.5 More importantly, it does not reflect current government priorities to cover more households
      with the shock responsive cash transfer program. Since all of the beneficiaries under Component 2 also meet the
      eligibility criteria for Component 1, they will be converted to beneficiaries under Component 1 and paid additional N
      15,000 to bring them up to the same benefit level for the first tranche as Component 1 beneficiaries. They will
      additionally receive two tranches of N 25,000 each to complete the three tranches of payment under Component 1.
      Since both components are under expenditure Category (1) pertaining to cash transfers, no reallocation of resources
      will be required to implement these changes.

      13. The NIN Drive is now a key project activity under Component 3. Biometric identification became critical since
      the government’s decision in March 2024 to provide transfers under this program only to biometrically verified
      beneficiaries, alongside the Central Bank of Nigeria’s mandate to link all bank accounts and wallets to either a NIN or
      a BVN. The program has partnered with NIMC and relevant private sector stakeholders and service providers to
      integrate NINs with the Social Registry. Since April 2025, the government has launched the NIN Drive to: (i) collect
      data on existing NINs of household members through a limited data update of the Social Registry financed through
      output-based disbursement (OBD); and (ii) enroll households without at least one adult with a verified NIN to obtain
      one, in partnership with NIMC and its private sector front-end-partners and integrate biometric identification into the
      Social Registry. Additional means of integrating NIN into the Social Registry, such as linking available phone numbers
      or other information in the Social Registry, to expedite rollout will also be explored through engagements with
      relevant public and private sector stakeholders and service providers. These changes are critical to meet program
      objectives and will be reflected in the revision to Part 3.1 of the Financing Agreement.

      14. The project activities will be reoriented towards post-transfer targeted notifications, outreach, and
      communications. With NIN or BVN as a credible biometric identifier, beneficiaries can be directly included into the
      National Beneficiary Registry (NBR) and paid without a separate field-based enrolment and registration exercise. As a
      result, project activities will pivot to targeted notifications, beneficiary outreach, and communications. activities.
      Resource reallocation will reflect this shift.

                          increased to SDR 14,721,470 (US$20 million equivalent) to finance the limited data update, or potential
                          expansion, of the Social Registry to integrate biometric identification. This reallocation reflects an increase of
                          SDR 6,100,645 (US$8,288,092 equivalent) to this category.
                      (b) Allocation of Category (4)—goods, works, consulting and non-consulting services, and operating costs of
                          NASSCO—will be increased to SDR 14,721470 (US$20 million equivalent) to finance NIN Drive costs not
                          covered by OBD—such as engagement with other public and private stakeholders and service providers to
                          integrate biometric identification into the Social Registry—and to meet NASSCO’s increased operating costs

                          equivalent) to this category.
                      (c) Category (3)—OBD financing of enrollment and registration of eligible beneficiaries—will be reduced to SDR
                          2,208,220 (US$3 million equivalent) to reflect the lower priority of field-based enrollment into the NBR that

                          this category.
                      (d) Category (5)—goods, works, consulting and non-consulting services, and operating costs of NCTO—will be
                          increased to SDR 16,396,631 (US$22.276 million equivalent) to finance post-transfer targeted notification,
