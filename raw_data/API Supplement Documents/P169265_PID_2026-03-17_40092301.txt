    B. Introduction and Context

    Country Context


    1.       Ghana has achieved significant economic and social progress over the past three decades, attaining
    middle-income status in 2011. However, in 2022, a combination of policy challenges and external shocks contributed
    to a severe macroeconomic crisis, resulting in the closure of international capital markets, sharp depreciation of the
    cedi, elevated inflation, and a contraction in private sector credit amid increased domestic borrowing. Economic growth
    slowed to 3.8 percent in 2022 and further to 3.1 percent in 2023, while public debt rose to 78.8 percent of GDP1.

    2.       In response to the macroeconomic crisis, Ghana launched an IMF-supported recovery program, including a
    comprehensive debt restructuring, which has contributed to improved macroeconomic conditions. While the
    situation remains fragile, Ghana’s macroeconomic performance has strengthened notably over the past two years. In
    2024, economic growth proved more resilient than anticipated, with GDP growth exceeding expectations at 5.6 percent.
    Inflation declined sharply from a peak of 53.7 percent in December 2022 to 22.9 percent by end-2024. The external
    sector also improved, with the current account recording a surplus of 2.0 percent of GDP in 2024, supported by higher
    gold and crude oil exports and strong remittance inflows. However, fiscal performance deteriorated in 2024 due to the
    accumulation of arrears and unbudgeted expenditures.

 3.      Growth momentum continued into 2025, supported by robust gold exports and the continued
 implementation of tight fiscal and monetary policies. GDP growth is estimated at 5.7 percent in 2025, driven primarily
 by a strong expansion in the services sector (8.3 percent), particularly in information and communications, transport,
 and education. Agricultural output rebounded, with growth estimated at 7.4 percent, reflecting gains in crop
 production, cocoa recovery, and livestock. In contrast, industrial growth moderated to 2.4 percent, reflecting weaker
 performance in the oil and gas subsectors.

 4.     Inflation, which remained above 20 percent in 2024, declined sharply over the course of 2025, supported by
 tight monetary policy, improved confidence, and easing supply-side pressures. By mid-2025, inflation had fallen to
 below 14 percent, before continuing its downward trajectory to 5.4 percent by end-2025 and further to 3.3 percent by
 February 2026.

 5.       Lower inflation and improved economic growth in 2025 contributed to a reversal of the previously rising
 poverty trend. At the lower-middle-income (LMI) poverty line (US$4.2 per day, 2021 Purchasing Power Parity), the
 poverty rate declined from 58.1 percent in 2024 to 57.3 percent in 2025. However, the absolute number of people
 living in poverty may have continued to increase modestly, reflecting population growth. Looking ahead, the poverty
 rate is projected to continue declining over the medium term, although the number of people living in poverty is
 expected to rise slightly, driven by population growth and slower income gains in agriculture and services.

 Sectoral and Institutional Context

6.       Despite being more advanced than many of its regional peers, Ghana’s statistical system continues to face
institutional and financial challenges that affect its performance and long-term sustainability. The Ghana Statistical Service
(GSS) has made notable progress in modernizing data production, including the rollout of nationwide Computer-Assisted
Personal Interview (CAPI)–based price monitoring and regular statistical reporting. However, key constraints persist, notably
limited fiscal space, uneven funding, and hiring restrictions, which continue to constrain staffing, retention, and the ability to
offer competitive incentives. In addition, coordination across ministries, departments, and agencies remains a challenge,
particularly with respect to the integration of administrative and survey data.

7.       Strategic frameworks continue to guide the development of the statistical system in Ghana, including the recent
adoption of a third-generation National Strategy for the Development of Statistics (NSDS). The governing bodies of the
Ghana Statistical Service (GSS) have endorsed this updated NSDS, which aims to strengthen statistical governance, expand
institutional capacity, and enhance data interoperability in line with international standards. This renewed strategic
commitment is expected to support evidence-based policymaking and contribute to progress toward national and global
development objectives.

 D. Project Description

8.       The parent project was approved by the World Bank Board of Executive Directors on March 26, 2020. The project
includes nine recipients with a total financing amount of US$379.00 million from the National Performance-Based Allocations
(PBA) International Development Association (IDA) and Regional IDA Windows. Seven project participating countries, namely,
Burkina Faso (IDA Credit 65730 - US$30.0 million and IDA Grant D5810 - US$30.00 million), Cabo Verde (IDA Credit 65740 -
US$15.00 million), Cote d’Ivoire (IDA Credit 65750 - US$54.00 million), Ghana (IDA Credit 65760 - US$150.00 million), Liberia
(IDA Credit 65770 - US$15.00 million and IDA Grant D5830 - US$15.00 million), Sierra Leone (IDA Grant D5840 - US$30.00
million) and Togo (IDA Credit 65780 - US$15.00 million and IDA Grant D5850 - US$15.00 million), as well as two regional
organizations, namely, the African Union (AU) (IDA Grant D5880 - US$2.50 million) and the Economic Community of West
African States (ECOWAS) (IDA Grant D5820 - US$7.50 million).

9.      The parent project aims to address critical data gaps by harmonizing, producing, disseminating, and enhancing the
use of essential economic and social statistics. It seeks to improve the statistical capacity of participating countries, ensuring
that their National Statistical Systems (NSS) meet user needs and generate more comparable statistics, thereby facilitating
better decision-making. Furthermore, the project supports the modernization of statistical methods, organizational
structures, physical infrastructure, and Information and Communication Technology (ICT) within National Statistics Offices
(NSOs), as well as Ministries, Departments, and Agencies (MDAs) responsible for statistics. This modernization will empower
these entities to fulfill their mandates more effectively.

10.     The parent Project Development Objective is to strengthen the statistical systems of participating countries and
regional bodies in Africa to harmonize, produce, disseminate and enhance the use of core economic and social statistics.

11.      Two Additional Financings totaling US$15.8 million equivalent were approved in June 2025 . The first, amounting
to US$9.6 million equivalent, was approved on June 16, 2025, with allocations of US$6.0 million for Côte d’Ivoire and US$3.6
million for Sierra Leone. The second, totaling US$6.2 million equivalent, was approved on June 30, 2025, with allocations of
US$3.0 million for Ghana and US$3.2 million for Liberia. These approvals increased the total project financing to US$394.8
million equivalent.

12.     The parent project, the first and second AFs, and the proposed third Additional Financing are structured around
three components:

    •   Component 1: Regional Component: Harmonization, Data Collection, Quality Improvement, Dissemination and Use
        of Core Social and Economic Statistics (US$364.03 million equivalent—Ghana US$154.43 million equivalent). This
        component funds activities aiming at supporting harmonized methodologies, producing core economic and social
        statistics, improving administrative statistics, providing training on methodological advancements, building human
        capacity, and enhancing data quality and timeliness.
    •   Component 2: Country-Specific Component – Improving or Modernizing Physical and Statistical Infrastructure
        (US$30.29 million equivalent—Ghana US$13.59 million equivalent). This component focuses on addressing the
        unique constraints and priorities of each participating NSO. It encompasses targeted interventions across physical,
        technological, and statistical infrastructure, as well as human capacity development and legal or regulatory

          implement the activities outlined under Component 1.
      •   Component 3: Project Management and Monitoring and Evaluation (US$19.48 million equivalent—Ghana US$3.98
          million equivalent). This component aims to support project management and monitor project outcomes in each
          participating country and regional organization, ensuring the effective and efficient execution of project activities.

          safeguards, and ensuring compliance with fiduciary policies. Specifically, this component encompasses project staff
          costs, project coordination expenses (such as those for the steering committee), operating costs, and other necessary
          expenditures.

13.      The proposed AF includes allocations of US$19.00 million IDA Credits from the Performance Based Allocation
(PBA). The proposed AF is intended to address the cost overruns related to the implementation of critical priority activities,
particularly the Ghana Living Standards Survey round eight (GLSS8), the Multiple Indicators Cluster Survey (MICS), the
Quarterly Labor Force Survey (QLFS) as well as the expansion and rebasing of the Consumer Price Index, the rebasing of
National Accounts, improvement of administrative data and modernization of the physical and IT infrastructure. The objective
is to close significant data gaps in poverty, labor market and economic statistics and to enhance the production and use of
high-quality data, as well as data security and statistical coordination, thereby supporting the achievement of the Project
Development Objective (PDO).

14.       The proposed AF encompasses the following changes.
          • Increase in total project cost, from US$394.80 million equivalent to US$413.80 million equivalent.
          • Increase of the financing amount for Ghana from US$153.00 million equivalent to US$172.00 million equivalent.
          • Increase project costs between components and subcomponents for Ghana.


 E. Implementation

  15.       The parent project commenced on July 16, 2020, with the Ghana Financing Agreement becoming effective on
  that date. As of now, the overall project disbursement rate stands at 93 percent. Both progress toward achieving the
  Project Development Objective (PDO) and overall implementation is rated satisfactory. Disbursement rates for Ghana
  95.45 percent. The project has made substantial progress in strengthening NSS by addressing critical data gaps and
  supporting the production of key statistical outputs—including population, business, and agricultural censuses, as well
  as a range of core household and socio-economic surveys. It has also enhanced the harmonization, dissemination, and
  utilization of high-quality data to inform, monitor, and evaluate public policies.

 Institutional and Implementation Arrangements

16.     The implementation arrangements under the Additional Financing will remain the same as those for the parent
project. As with the parent project, GSS will be the implementing agency and the Coordination and Program Management
Directorate (CPMD) within GSS will coordinate all project activities of the AF, including financial and procurement
management. No changes to the existing arrangements are anticipated.

A. Summary of Key Safeguard Issues


1. Describe any safeguard issues and impacts associated with the Restructured project. Identify and describe any
  potential large scale, significant and/or irreversible impacts.
  The parent project’s environmental and social assessment remains Category B, reflecting low to moderate risks. This
  classification also applies to the proposed AF in Ghana. The environmental and social performance rating for Ghana
  is Moderately Satisfactory. The safeguard issues and impacts asssociated with the proposed AF span low to moderate
  risks. These risks can be mitigated through good practices and appropriate mitigation measures. Ghana Statistical
  Service (GSS) has made some progress in addressing road safety and occupational health and safety (OHS). The
  project implementation in Ghana recorded a few road traffic and occupational safety incidents in connection with
  field activities. These incidents, which included fatalities, informed the strengthening of occupational health and
  safety and road safety protocols of GSS. Further, preventive measures have been implemented. The project will
  continue to apply enhanced safety measures and monitoring in accordance with the Bank’s relevant EHS Guidelines.

  The project implementation in Ghana recorded a few road traffic and occupational safety incidents in connection with
  field activities. These incidents, which included fatalities, informed the strengthening of occupational health and
  safety and road safety protocols of GSS. Further, preventive measures have been implemented. The project will
  continue to apply enhanced safety measures and monitoring in accordance with the Bank’s relevant EHS Guidelines.

  Overall, the GSS has integrated safeguards into both the planning and execution of project activities. It has shown
  commitment to managing environmental and social risks, while ensuring compliance with relevant World Bank
  safeguard policies and national regulations. Due to the frequent nature of field activities and reported road safety
  traffic incidents, there is a need to further strengthen road safety and occupational health and safety (OHS) risk
  management, including through enhanced training, supervision, and institutional capacity enhancement. The Task
  Team shall also monitor the implementation of corrective action plans and close open ESIRTS. The parent project has
  integrated gender and social inclusion issues in its activities. The GSS should keep enforcing its sexual harassment
  policy under AF2, specially making sure women aren't required to work during unsafe hours that put their travel at
  risk.


2. Describe any potential indirect and/or long-term impacts due to anticipated future activities in the project area.
  The activities under the proposed Additional Financing are not expected to have any potential indirect or long-term
  impacts resulting from future activities in the project area. The AF activities represent a continuation of the parent
  project activities in Ghana.

  prompt incident reporting, investigation, corrective action plan implementation, ESIRT closure and the adoption of
  preventive measures. The project will also maintain ongoing sensitization efforts on Sexual Exploitation and
