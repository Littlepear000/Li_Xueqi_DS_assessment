I.    PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING
 A. Introduction

1. This Restructuring Paper seeks the approval of the Regional Vice President (RVP) for a Level II Restructuring of the
   Dominican Republic Resilient Agriculture and Integrated Water Resources Management Project (P163260), Loan
   No. 89120-DO. The proposed restructuring responds to request of the Government of Dominican Republic to
   restructure the Project and will entail the following changes: (a) revision of the implementation arrangements; (b)
   extension of the closing date; (c) revision of targets in the Results Framework; (d) revision of the disbursement
   estimates and disbursement arrangements; (e) revision of the financial management (FM) and procurement
   arrangements; (f) update of the implementation schedule; (g) update of the project risks; (h) update of the
   environmental and social analysis; and (i) revision of legal covenants. As a result of restructuring, there will be no
   changes in PDO, project components, or in the loan amount.

 B.    Project Background and Status

2. The International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) loan of US$80 million for the Project was
   approved by the World Bank Group Board of Executive Directors on December 13, 2018, with a project closing date
   of June 4, 2024, and signed by the Dominican Republic (the “Borrower”) on January 28, 2020. The PDO is to
   contribute to improved sustainable landscape management and access to water supply and sanitation services in
   targeted river basins. The PDO is to be achieved through: (a) demand-driven packages of investments and technical
   assistance for family farmers to support sustainable land management practices such as conservation agriculture,
   agroforestry, and integrated crop-livestock systems; (b) rehabilitation works for hydraulic assets, including dams and
   irrigation schemes; (c) expansion of sanitation and water supply access; and (d) activities that would strengthen the
   capacity of participating institutions to support the sustainable management of natural resources. The PDO continues
   to be relevant and is achievable despite the COVID-19 pandemics environment. The Project has four components: (i)
   Sustainable Productive Management of Agroecosystems; (ii) Enhancement of Resilience and Management of
   Hydraulic Infrastructures; (iii) Reducing Contamination through Improvement of Water Supply and Sanitation Services
   in Prioritized Areas; and (iv) Capacity Building for Natural Resources Management and Governance, and Project
   Management.

 3. The signing of the Loan Agreement was delayed because the loan was not included in the Borrower’s budget for
    the 2019 calendar year, which only occurred at the end of 2019. When the Government was preparing to submit
    the package to Congress for approval, the global COVID-19 pandemic was declared, and the upcoming legislative
    sessions prioritized the COVID-19 response. Consequently, the Loan Agreement was terminated on July 26, 2020 in
    the absence of Congress approval for the Project.

4. A new administration took office in August 2020 and expressed renewed interest in the Project, given that its
   objectives and interventions were aligned with the Government’s priorities in the agriculture and water sectors. In
   a letter dated February 24, 2021, the Borrower requested the reinstatement of the Loan Agreement on an exceptional
   basis and the retroactive extension of the effectiveness deadline. In its letter, and through subsequent discussions,
   the Borrower confirmed that: (i) Project objectives and interventions are well-aligned with the policies, priorities, and
   the post-COVID-19 recovery strategy of the current administration; (ii) the Project is included in the 2021 National
   Budget Law; and (iii) it is committed to submitting the Loan Agreement to Congress for approval as soon as it is
   reinstated and amended.

5. Since the Project remains highly relevant and an effective means to support the implementation of the
   Government’s key development agendas in the agriculture and water sectors, including its post-COVID-19 recovery
   strategy, a process to reinstate the Loan Agreement and retroactively extend the effectiveness deadline until
   September 30, 2021, was carried out in parallel to this restructuring. On April 26, 2021, the Regional Vice President
   approved the Government’s request for the reinstatement of the Loan Agreement.

6. Project performance is currently Moderately Unsatisfactory as indicated in the latest Implementation Status and
   Results Report (ISR), filed in February 2021. The Progress towards achievement of PDO and Overall Implementation
   Progress, fiduciary, project management and monitoring and evaluation performance were all rated Moderately
   Unsatisfactory to reflect the prolonged delays in declaring the effectiveness of the Loan Agreement. Compliance with
   safeguards policies was rated satisfactory and compliance with the legal covenants were mostly not yet due as a result
   of the delays in effectiveness.

C. Rationale for Restructuring

7. The proposed restructuring responds to the Borrower’s letter dated April 8, 2021, requesting the following: (i)
   revision of the Project’s implementation arrangements to align them with the new Administration’s governance
   structure in the water sector put in place through Decree No. 498-20, dated September 2020, which no longer made
   the Ministry of Economy, Planning, and Development (Ministerio de Economía, Planificación y Desarrollo, MEPyD)1
   the best alternative for the PCIU; and (ii) an extension to the Project’s closing date to compensate for the elapsed time
   since the Board approval. This extension will allow the completion of the planned project activities and achievement
   of the PDO.

8. The Borrower has reiterated that the water situation in the country is a key priority and that there is a high-level
   commitment to address the challenges in the sector which make the country less resilient to climate-related risks.
   The Project is consistent with the key principles of a proposed National Water Pact (under formulation), including the
   objective of protecting and sustaining water resources and delivering efficient and reliable water supply, sanitation,
   and irrigation services. The Project is also aligned with the priority area identified in the 2018 Systematic Country
   Diagnostic, “Improving the management of natural resources,” which includes issues to water supply and sanitation,
   water resource management, and agricultural practices.

9. The implementation arrangements agreed at the time of Board approval called for MEPyD to be the project
   implementing entity (PIE) responsible for the overall project coordination and to host a Project Coordination and
   Implementation Unit (PCIU) in charge of directly implementing Components 1, 3 and 4; and for the National
   Institute of Hydraulic Resources (Instituto Nacional de Recursos Hídricos, INDRHI) to be responsible for the
   implementation of Component 2 through a dedicated Project Implementation Unit. Under the original
   implementation arrangements, the Ministry of Agriculture (Ministerio de Agricultura, MA), the Ministry of
   Environment and Natural Resources (Ministerio de Medio Ambiente y Recursos Naturales, MARENA), and the National
   Institute of Water Supply and Sewerage (Instituto Nacional de Aguas Potables y Alcantarillados, INAPA) are
   responsible for providing technical support for the implementation of individual components of the Project. A Project
   Steering Committee (PSC) composed of the Ministers of MEPyD, MA, and MARENA and the Directors of INDRHI and
   INAPA, is responsible for providing the overall policy guidance and oversight. The National Water Board (Mesa del
   Agua), a body created before Board approval which has an advisory role to the PSC, is responsible for ensuring that

10. The Borrower’s request to update the original project implementation arrangements (as described above) is based
    on the rationale that the primary implementation responsibility of sectoral projects should rest with the specialized
    government agencies dealing with the sectoral issues and not MEPyD. While MEPyD will still play an important role
    in promoting multi-sectoral coordination, the responsibilities for the day-to-day implementation of all project
    components will be assigned to INDRHI, which is the main government agency dealing with water management at the
    national, basin, and sub-basin levels, and is responsible for the management of irrigation infrastructure and dams
    throughout the country. In addition, updates to the implementation arrangements were also required due to changes
    with the coordinating body for the water sector. As mentioned earlier, the governance structure in the water sector
    changed and the National Water Board that was hosted within MEPyD was replaced by the Water Sector Board
    (Gabinete de Agua), which reports directly to the Office of the President. Under the 2021 National Budget Law, only
    MEPYD and INDRHI can be a PIE, therefore, unless the National Budget Law is amended, no other sectoral agency can
    be assigned for day-to-day project implementation responsibilities.

11. With the current governance structure, INDRHI is better suited as the PIE. INDRHI has an extensive infrastructure
    and institutional network in water management, and as such is uniquely positioned to promote integrated water
    resources management at basin-, sub-basin, and field levels because of its close functional and working relationships
    with the MA (on irrigated agriculture), MARENA (on natural resources management) and INAPA (on water supply and
    sanitation). As detailed in Section II, INDRHI has the adequate capacity to manage the Project and host the PCIU.


II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

    12. The proposed changes included in this restructuring are as follows:

        (a) Implementing Agency

    13. The new PIE of the Project will be INDRHI. The World Bank team assessed the capacity of INDRHI to implement the
        Project and found that it has significant experience in implementing investment programs and projects financed by
        the Government and multilateral financial institutions including two World Bank-financed projects,2 which provides
        a solid basis for it to implement the current Project. Notwithstanding, to guarantee a successful implementation, the
        World Bank team will work with INDRHI to further strengthen its capacity especially in areas of procurement, financial
        management, and safeguards. Loan funds will be transferred by the Ministry of Finance to INDRHI under a Subsidiary
        Agreement to be executed between the Ministry of Finance and INDRHI.

    14. As indicated above, INDRHI is the government agency responsible for managing all major hydraulic infrastructures
        including irrigation systems, canals, dams, and is also responsible for providing technical and institutional support
        to farmers and water users’ associations. INDRHI operates all irrigation systems, dominating an accumulated surface

    area of 300,000 hectares, with the participation of water users’ associations whose membership is nearly 90,000
    farmers. Currently, INDRHI has around 4,100 technical and administrative staff and is managing a portfolio of
    investment programs and projects of around US$95 million. Its 2021 annual operational and administrative budget
    is around US$10 million.
15. A new PCIU will be established within INDRHI to manage the Project’s day-to-day activities. It will be responsible
    for all aspects of project management including overall coordination, technical, financial management, procurement,
    environmental and social safeguards, and monitoring and evaluation. The PCIU will have two regional offices (one in
    each river basin, Yaque del Norte and Ozama-Isabela) to monitor and supervise the project activities on the ground
    and ensure coordination with branches of the ministries and INAPA at the provincial levels, as well as with the other
    local stakeholders.

    (b) Institutional Arrangements

16. Inter-institutional Agreements will be signed between INDRHI and MA, INAPA, and MARENA to facilitate
    implementation. The MA, INAPA, and MARENA will be involved in the implementation of the project activities as
    originally designed by providing technical inputs and field-level coordination. Under Component 1, MA will provide
    support to activities related to productive and resilient agricultural landscapes, and MARENA will support activities
    related to the management of natural resources in upper river basins. INAPA will provide support to activities under
    Component 3 related to the improvement of water supply and sanitation services. The technical support to be
    provided by these participating entities include inter alia: (i) the definition of the technical scope of Calls for
    Proposals; (ii) the preparation of terms of references for consulting services and technical specifications for the
    procurement of goods and works; (iii) the carrying out of the technical oversight of the implementation of activities
    and results; (iv) the inter-institutional coordination with other relevant governmental entities; and (v) the
    establishment and maintenance, throughout project implementation, of a technical team to coordinate the
    execution of project activities with INDRHI. The institutional responsibilities for each component/subcomponent are
    detailed in Annex 1 (Project Implementation Arrangements).

17. The National Water Board will be replaced by the Water Sector Board. The National Water Board that was housed
    in MEPyD has been replaced by the Water Sector Board (Gabinete de Agua), which reports directly to the Office of
    the President. The Water Sector Board is a new national-level body represented by various institutions and ministries
    and was created by Presidential Decree 498-20 in September 2020. Its main responsibility is to coordinate and
    facilitate decision-making for the Government’s policy on the water sector. The office of the Director of the Water
    Sector Board is physically located at INDRHI.

18. Composition of the PSC. As contemplated in the original design, the PSC will be established to provide strategic
    oversight and policy directions, inter-institutional coordination, and to review and resolve major implementation
    issues. The PSC will be co-chaired by the Minister of MEPyD and the Director of the Water Sector Board and composed
    of the Ministers of MA and MARENA along with the Directors of INDRHI and INAPA.

    (c) Financial Management (FM)

19. The World Bank team carried out a financial management assessment and found that INDRHI has the FM capacity
    to manage government-funded regular programs, has previous experience in implementing projects financed by
    multilateral institutions including the World Bank, and is currently executing an operation financed by the Central
    American Bank for Economic Integration (CABEI). Nevertheless, due to the complexity of this multi-sectoral project
    which INDRHI has not previously managed, the World Bank team also concluded that the overall FM risk remains
    High. To successfully manage this Project, INDRHI will need to strengthen the PCIU’s financial management
    implementation capacity and implement an action plan to mitigate the specific FM-related risks. The action plan is
    designed to adequately implement the Project, properly record all transactions and balances, support the
    preparation of regular and reliable project financial statements, and safeguard the Project’s assets. The key measures
    reflected in the action plan include: (i) assign qualified FM staff with World Bank project experience to the PCIU; (ii)
    update INDRHI’s financial and administrative manual; (iii) tailor chart of accounts and financial reports by project
    needs; and (iv) finalize the financial management section of the Project Operational Manual (POM). Further details
    on FM are provided in Annex 1 (Project Implementation Arrangements).

    (d) Procurement

20. The World Bank team also assessed the procurement capacity of INDRHI and found that the procurement unit
    within INDRHI has a team of experienced procurement specialists managing various projects funded both by the
    Government and donor organizations. However, as mentioned above, INDRHI has not managed large complex multi-
    sectoral projects, and INDRHI’s current staff still needs to fully familiarize with the World Bank’s procedures and
    regulations approved in July 2016 and updated in November 2020; therefore, the World Bank team also concluded
    that the procurement risk remains substantial.

21. To ensure that the PCIU has adequate capacity to manage all procurement aspects for the Project, the capacity
    strengthening measures that will be implemented include, inter alia: (i) the PCIU will have an experienced
    procurement specialist with adequate knowledge of the World Bank’s procurement procedures and regulations
    that were approved in July 2016 and updated in November 2020; and (ii) the POM will include a section on
    procurement that will detail procurement procedures, responsibilities and roles of the various departments at
    INDRHI and their interactions with the PCIU, as well as the roles of INAPA, MA, and MARENA in procurement
    processes. Also, the World Bank team will provide training to INDRHI on FM and procurement matters before
    effectiveness. During implementation, fiduciary training will also be provided to INAPA and MA to enhance their
    implementation capacities in supporting the procurement aspects of the Project.

    (e) Extension of Project Closing Date

22. The current closing date of the project will be extended by 28 months from June 4, 2024, to October 2, 2026. Since
    a considerable amount of time has elapsed since Board approval without implementation progress, the extension
    will allow the Borrower to complete all planned project activities and achieve the PDO targets. The Project
    implementation and disbursement schedule have been updated to reflect the new closing date.

    (f) Results Framework
23. The end date of all indicators is revised to reflect the new project closing date of October 2, 2026.

    (g) Disbursements

24. The disbursement estimates during the life of the Project have been revised to account for the new project closing
    date and implementation progress. In addition, a variable ceiling based on a six-month forecast approved by the
    World Bank will be used for advances and deposited to a segregated Designated Account (DA), denominated in US

      (h) Legal Covenants and Effectiveness Conditions
25. The legal covenants have been revised and new ones added to reflect the new implementation arrangements.3
    The effectiveness conditions of the Loan Agreement will also be updated to reflect INDRHI as the PIE, as per the new
    implementation arrangements. The updated effectiveness conditions consist of the following: (a) that INDRHI PCIU
    has been established and staffed as provided in Section I.A.1 of Schedule 2 to the Loan Agreement, and in a manner
    satisfactory to the Bank; (b) that INDRHI has adopted the Operational Manual in a manner satisfactory to the Bank;
    and (c) that the Subsidiary Agreement has been duly signed by the by the parties thereto.

      (i) Environmental and Social Safeguards

26. The project activities and environmental assessment category will remain the same and no new safeguard policies
    are triggered by this restructuring. However, the environmental and social (E&S) analysis prepared during appraisal
    was updated in the Integrated Safeguards Data Sheet (ISDS) redisclosed on April 30, 2021 to reflect the new PIE and
    include COVID-19 transmission prevention considerations to be adopted during project implementation.

27. INDRHI’s E&S safeguards capacity has been assessed by the World Bank team, which noted that INDRHI satisfactorily
    managed safeguards under previous World Bank-financed projects. Under the Project, the PCIU will include one
    environmental and one social specialist, based in Santo Domingo, and one socio-environmental specialist in each of
    the two regional offices in the Yaque del Norte and Ozama-Isabela river basins. Although most of the works are simple
    rehabilitation works without any major impact, there are specific risks associated with dam safety. INDRHI will be
    responsible for complying with all safeguard obligations. Regarding dam safety risks identified during preparation,
    the PCIU will include a dedicated international expert during implementation and strengthen INDRHI’s current Dam
    Safety Panel of Experts with a sediment management specialist. The Project’s safeguards instruments, which include
    an Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMF), two Environmental and Social Management Plans
    (ESMPs), and a Resettlement Policy Framework (RPF) were initially prepared, consulted, and disclosed in February
    2018. These instruments have been updated and were re-disclosed in-country on April 28, 2021 to reflect the new
    implementation arrangements.4 It was agreed that further updates of the safeguards instruments including the E&S
    baseline, E&S risks, impacts, and mitigation measures, and the Grievance Redress System (GRM), among other
    changes will be done within 60 days after the project effectiveness date. The overall environmental and social risk
    remains “Substantial” and the restructuring will not result in any E&S impacts. Further details are provided in Annex
    1 (Project Implementation Arrangements).

   river basin) to monitor and supervise the activities on the ground and ensure coordination with participating entities
   at the provincial level, as well as with the other local stakeholders. Figure 1 below depicts the overall project

6. INDRHI has previous and current experiences in implementing projects financed with external funds, including the
   World Bank-financed Emergency Recovery and Disaster Management Project (P109932).5 It is currently
   implementing a project with CABEI. To assess INDRHI’s capacity to undertake the role of the PCIU for overall project
   implementation, the World Bank team carried out an institutional assessment, identified residual fiduciary and
   safeguard capacity risks, and proposed mitigation measures to be implemented. These measures are described
   below.

7. The fiduciary risk remains “high” due to the complexity of managing a large multi-sectoral project. The FM
   mitigation measures that will be implemented include: (i) the PCIU will assign at least 3 professionals: Financial
   Specialist, Disbursement/Accountant, and Accountant for financial monitoring of project contracts and subprojects
   throughout the life of the project, (ii) the financial section of the Project Operational Manual (POM) shall be agreed
   with the World Bank and will include, inter alia, procedures for project accounting, recording, and reporting, including
   chart of accounts, planning and monitoring, flow of funds, payments process, and internal reviews/approvals,
   management of the Designated Account, disbursements/justification of expenditures with the World Bank, internal
   control reviews and external audit, financing and reimbursement of retroactive expenditures, formats for interim
   financial reports (IFRs), Sistema Integrado de Gestión Financiera (SIGEF)/Unidades Ejecutoras de Proyectos de
   Financiamiento Externo (UEPEX) forms,6 etc.; and (iii) INDRHI shall present evidence to the World Bank that it has
   progressed on implementing recommendations from the government’s Camara de Cuentas and updates to its
   financial and administrative manual so it is in accordance with Law No. 10-07 and the government’s Basic Internal
   Control Standards (Las Normas Básicas de Control Interno; NOBACI). In addition, the World Bank’s FM team will
   conduct dedicated training for the PCIU’s assigned FM staff, and its supervision plan will include virtual/partial on-
   site supervision, field visits, review of the semiannual IFRs, annual financial audit reports, and performing periodic
   spot checks of Statements of Expenditure (SOEs) and any other areas of concern.

8. The risk mitigation measures of project procurement that will be implemented include, inter alia: (i) the PCIU will
   have an experienced procurement specialist with relevant knowledge of the World Bank’s procurement procedures
   and regulations that were approved in July 2016 updated in November 2020 and (ii) the POM shall include a section
   on procurement that will detail procurement procedures and the responsibilities and roles of the different
   departments at INDRHI and their interactions with the PCIU, as well as the roles of the INAPA, MA, and MARENA in
   procurement processes. The World Bank team will provide training to the procurement specialists from INDRHI’s
   Compliance and Operation Unit (Dirección Operativa de Ejecución y Cumplimiento), technical staff at INDRHI, and
   members of the Bidding Committee from INDRHI (Comité de Compras y Contrataciones) to ensure compliance with
   the World Bank’s Procurement Regulations. Supervision missions will include reviews of the procurement processes
   and plans along with field visits.

9. The environment and social risks remain “substantial” primarily because of the complexity of managing a large
   multi-sectoral project, including dam safety issues, and local capacity. To strengthen the environmental and social
   safeguards capacity of the Project, the PCIU will: (i) include one dedicated environmental specialist and one social
   specialist, who will both be based in Santo Domingo, and one socio-environmental specialist in each of the two
