 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Implementation Status
  Overall, at mid-term, good progress is noted in the implementation of activities as well as in achievement of the targets for most of the PDO
  indicators with:
       1. groundnut yields currently standing at 1.38 ton/ha against the target of 1.1 ton/ha expected by this time into implementation and a final
            target of 1.5 ton/ha;
       2. a maize yield of 2.64 tons/hectare against a target of 2.7 ton/ha expected by this time into implementation and a final target of 3 ton/ha;
       3. a millet yield of 1.07 tons/hectare against a target of 1.2 ton/ha and a final target of 1.5 ton/ha;
       4. an increase in groundnut exports with 319,786 tons exported in 2021 (139 percent achieved) out of a target of 230,000 tons and 105,409
            tons exported as of 23 April 2023 against a target of 255,000 tons (41 percent achievement), and a final target of 350,000 tons. Lower
            than targeted export in 2022/2023, reflect a combination of overall reductions in production as well as effects of the quota imposed on
            groundnut exports;
       5. number of farmers reached with agricultural assets or services estimated at 150,875 compared to an annual target of 125,000 this time
            into implementation (120 percent achievement) and a final target of 300,000. A total of 81,560 women have so far been reached with
            agricultural assets or services (54 percent of beneficiaries) compared to a target of 37,500 women expected to be reached this time into
            implementation and an end of program target of 90,000 women
  With regard to the Disbursement-Linked Indicators (DLIs), the annual targets for 2021 or/and 2022 for seven out of ten DLIs of the Program have
  been achieved. In particular, significant progress has been made in: the acquisition of certified seeds for groundnuts (DLI 1) and for diversification
  crops (DLI 2); the targeting of smallholder producers and climate-smart technologies for agricultural input subsidies (DLI 3); vaccination coverage
  against the small ruminants plague - PPR (DLI 4); access to agricultural insurance for producers and livestock farmers (DLI 7); the
  operationalization of the warehouse receipt system (in the extended Groundnut Basin Zone (DLI 8);; and support to milk collection centers to make
  them operational (DLI 9).
  However, DLIs related to institutional reforms i.e., the restructuring of the CNIA (DLI 6) and the establishment of an operational M&E system to
  measure the performance of agriculture and livestock programs (DLI 10) are yet to be achieved
  Key decisions
  The mid-term review confirmed the relevance of the PDO to the new Senegal food sovereignty strategy currently being finalized. The three result
  areas remain relevant as well as the PDO indicators and their targets. The only agreed changes relate to select intermediate results indicators and
  DLIs whose targets have either been underestimated (seeds of diversification) or proven difficult to measure (vaccination rate) or whose content
  deserves adjustment to better consider the new strategic orientations in terms of government priorities. A second restructuring of the Program will
  be undertaken to consider changes made through the result framework.
