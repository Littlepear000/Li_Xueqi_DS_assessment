 Implementation Status and Key Decisions


  Issues

  Project implementation and disbursements for the PIC3 project are still low due to various challenges, leading to a downgrading of the
  project performance to Moderately Satisfactory. Disbursement from the World Bank (WB) to the project designated account amounts to about

  USD 5 million (equivalent to around 4 percent of the total budget). At project level, financial commitments amount to USD 11.6 million (equivalent to
  7.7 percent of the total budget) and disbursements amounts to USD 2.6 million (equivalent to 1.7 percent of total budget). The delays in project
  implementation are principally due to: (i) the long delay in processing the “régularisation budgétaire” (a national procedure within the Ministry of
  Economy and Finance which is a prerequisite for the clearance of funds withdrawal applications and therefore for payments of project contractors);
  (ii) delays to approve the Annual Work Plan and Budget; and (iii) delay in implementing activities that received clearance from the WB.

  The 2023 Annual Work Plan and Budget (PTBA) needs to be revised, and activities need to be prioritized. The initial PTBA submitted and
  reviewed by the WB team in December 2022 amounted to USD60 million. At the time the WB recommended the PIU to: (i) revise the budget
  downward to make it more realistic; (ii) organize working sessions with relevant Ministries and institutions to clarify certain proposed lines of
  activities; and (iii) begin activities that have received the WB’s clearance. However, following two meetings of the Project Steering Committee (PSC)
  in January and February 2023, the budget for the PTBA was revised upward, with the addition of several new activity lines that significantly
  increased the PTBA budget to USD83.8 million. For reference, the PIC projects have historically been able to disburse between 25-35 million USD
  per calendar year. It was agreed that the WB team will share recommendations for the prioritization of activities. The WB team also emphasized to
  the PIU and Government that the preparation of the next PTBA must consider: (i) previous experience and performance with regards to
  implementation of PTBA; (ii) prioritization of activities; (iii) timeframe; (iv) and procurement rules and procedures.

  Steering Committee. Conversations held during the supervision mission of March 2023 led to the agreement that the current institutional
  arrangement (including the composition of the Steering Committee) will remain unchanged and will continue to comply with the project institutional
  decree arrangement issued in 2022 (décret institutionnel n°2022-474 portant composition, fonctionnement et attributions des organes du Projet de
  Transformation Economique pour une croissance inclusive (PIC3)). It was agreed that representatives from other ministries, including the Secrétaire
  d’Etat en charge des nouvelles villes et de l’habitat, can be invited to be part of discussions in the PSC and in the Project Technical Committee as
  needed, to address and coordinate on specific subjects concerning their ministries and the project.

  Progress by components

  Component 1. Attracting and Retaining Private Investment and Removing Key Constraints to Support Economic Recovery from the
  COVID-19 crisis

  Changes in leadership at EDBM has led to delays in selection and implementation of activities related to improving business climate in
  the country. Changes at EDBM’s board and executive team have triggered a rethinking on the strategic position and roles of EDBM. A new
  institutional strategic framework (2023-2026) for the development of EDBM is under preparation, which will guide selection and implementation of
  activities to be supported by PIC3. In the meantime, PIC3 will support immediate and already identified needs such as support for the establishment
  of new EDBM regional offices in the new regions of intervention for the PIC3 (east coast), the hiring of legal consultants to support EDBM in various
  ongoing reviews of legislation related to business environment, and the provision of technical assistance to strengthen the Public Private Dialogue
  department.

  Component 2. Removing Constraints to Private Investment and Enhancing Local Economic Competitiveness to Support Recovery in
  Target Regions and Target Sectors

  Tourism

  The Ministry of Tourism (MINTOUR), the National Agency for the Promotion of Tourism (ONTM), and the Tourism Confederation of
  Madagascar (CTM) have demonstrated stronger coordination. This improvement in coordination was demonstrated particularly during the
  preparation of the PTBA 2023. The three entities worked together to discuss and prioritize activities to be included in the PTBA for the development
  of the tourism sector. The three entities still need to work and agree on a clearer and shared strategy for the diversification of products and target
  markets. The implementation of a consultancy to help ONTM prepare a tourism marketing strategy for the 2024-2028 period will help inform this
  strategy. Support to ONTM for 2023 will prioritize: (i) activities focused on international promotion of the destination; (ii) continuation of previously
  supported activities based on evidence of impact; (iii) activities focused on traditional or new markets for which data is available. The project will
  also support investment attraction by supporting, among other activities, the organization of the International Travel Market trade fair in Madagascar
  in June 2023, which will help the country with both destination promotion and investment attraction.



  The PIU has hired an additional tourism technical assistant to strengthen the central team, and has hired a tourism technical assistant responsible
  for coordination of activities linked to the Eastern circuit, based in Tamatave. The detailed study on the new circuits to be supported has been

  Agribusiness

  Work in the new poles has started. PIC technical teams in the new poles are in place and field activities have been launched, in particular the
  scaling up of PIC2.2 activities in key sectors in the new regions. PIC3 will also scale up support to the organic agriculture sector through the
  implementation of 2 pilots for the development of regions with strong potential for growth of the sector. The project will support the Madagascar
  Union for Organic Agriculture (SYMABIO) to, among other activities, participate in national and international fairs to promote exports and
  investments. The project will also expand its activities to support the development of new markets in Canada through its cooperation with the
  Madagascar-Canada Chamber of Commerce and Cooperation.

  Key project-supported value chains are facing challenges due to local context and the change in the European market regulations. Given
  the current issues on the governance of the vanilla sector in the country, the project's support to the vanilla sector is currently limited to upstream
  activities (dissemination of good agricultural practices, traceability at the producer level). The recent decisions of the European Union (EU) to
  change some regulations in the agribusiness sector (limitation of maximum level of nicotine residues in imported spices, imported deforestation
  regulation) pose important challenges to some of the value chains (such as vanilla, cocoa) supported by the project. The project is supporting
  engagements with the EU to help inform and clarify the Malagasy counterparts about the new regulations. It was agreed that the stakeholders,
  under the leadership of the Ministry of Agriculture (MINAE), will elaborate an action plan to ensure compliance with these new regulations.

  Other activities

  The rehabilitation of RNS 30 in Nosy Be began in January 2023. This rehabilitation will complete the ring road around Nosy Be, which has
  significantly improved the safety and quality of the transportation, circulation, and access to tourism sites around the island. As agreed, only the
  portions not requiring economic compensation have been started. This was done to accelerate the start of the works. However, the acceleration of
  the processing of these compensations at the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) will be important so that the rehabilitation can be completed
  by end of 2023 as currently planned.

  First infrastructure activities in the new poles have started. Project management is being procured for the works related to the rehabilitation
  and improvements to the Manangareza river port in Tamatave, as well as for the improvement to the Manambato and Ambila Lemaintso tourist
  trails.

  PIC3 will finance water supply works in island of Sainte Marie. PIC2.2 financed the Preliminary Project Summary (APS) study of the water
  supply improvements for the island of Sainte-Marie, which estimated the work to be done at USD2 million. Due to budget constraints, the works
  phase was not started under PIC2.2. Therefore, the detailed studies (APD), the preparation of the works (DAO), and the related works will be
  financed under PIC3.

  Component 3. Supporting SMEs and Entrepreneurship Recovery and Growth in Target Sectors and Target Regions

  The launch of activities for this component has been delayed. The delay primarily relates to the need for the PIU to revise manuals for the key
  instruments to incorporate lessons learned from PIC2.2 and to strengthen the PIU technical teams for each one of these instruments: start-up, SME
  and value chain matching grant support. Once this is done, the project will be ready to launch new call for proposals for these instruments,
  especially for the new poles. Nevertheless, the project is advancing well with the MIARY digital (the incubation of digital start-ups program), which
  has been prioritized. The recruitment of incubators for the first cohort for the MIARY digital is in the process of being finalized, and the launch of the
  program should happen in June 2023.
