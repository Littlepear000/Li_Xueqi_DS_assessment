 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Modest progress has been made under the project. The parent project and additional financing were declared effective on April 16, 2020 and
  June 21, 2020 respectively. The Project Implementation Manual (PIM) was developed and found to be satisfactory in form and substance to the
  Bank. All five Year 0 Performance Based Conditions (PBCs) have been achieved, verified and confirmed by the World Bank. These include under
  PBC 1.0: (a) Lesson observation tools harmonized by National Inspectorate Board (NIB) and approved by the Ministry of Education (MOE); and (b)
  National INSET Framework developed by National Teaching Council (NTC) and Ghana Education Service (GES) and approved by MOE; under
  PBC 2.0: (a) Agreed targeting criteria applied to list of public schools to identify targeted schools; and (b) Guidelines for learning grants developed
  by GES and approved by MOE; PBC 3.0: Accountability for Learning framework developed by participating agencies and approved by MOE; PBC

  Progress by Components

      1.     Key implementation progress under Component 1: Strengthen teaching and learning through support and resources for
             teachers. The In-service Training (INSET) Framework has been developed by GES in collaboration with the National Teaching Council
             (NTC), Special Education Division (SpED) and GES Guidance and Counselling. The framework is being disseminated at regional, district
             and cluster levels with the reopening of schools. GES is developing digital tools and web-based resources for effective delivery of INSET.
             These include e-learning modules include 350 recorded videos, GES mobile application with teaching tips and resources, peer-to-peer
             interactions and mobile reminders to attend trainings. Materials for targeted instruction (TI) have been reviewed, and a core INSET
             Implementation team has been set up to first focus on the 10,000 beneficiary basic schools under the GALOP before expanding to the
             non-GALOP schools (~11,000 schools). The GES INSET team will examine available platforms for teacher training (National Teaching
             Council-NTC, Ghana Library Authority-GhLA) and decide how INSET materials can be shared through these platforms. The INSET team
             is coordinating with different departments to ensure the proposed INSET will incorporate all the appropriate contents (inclusive education,
             Early Childhood Development (ECD), COVID-19 response, gender responsive teaching practices and remedial teaching and learning). A
             training plan/timetable has been developed and shared with the Bank team.

      2.     Key implementation progress under Component 2: Strengthen school support, management and resourcing The GES Finance
             Team conducted training for all beneficiary districts and GALOP beneficiary schools on the utilization of Learning Grants at the school
             level from November 4 to 21, 2020. The training includes how to conduct self-study, School Performance Improvement Plans (SPIPs),
             performance contracts for achieving planned GALOP activities, learning outcomes and accountability among others. An estimated 1,104
             participants (at the district and regional level) attended the training. Training of Trainers in a cluster form to adhere with social distancing
             procedure has also been completed. The World Bank recommended that an evaluation of the training programs be conducted to
             determine the success of the training, as well as any lessons learnt. The procurement process for the social investors and service
             providers under the EOF program has been initiated with early marketing engagement. Given the timelines for contract development, the
             MOE notified all bidders of a delay in the finalization of procurement. Social Investors and service providers are expected to be contracted
             by early-2022. The MOE has set up the EOF Management Unit which will report to the Project Oversight Committee (POC) through the
             GALOP Secretariat. Further, a communications officer from the MOE has been seconded to the Education Outcomes Fund (EOF)
             Management Unit and will be supported by external TA (through EOF), Reform Secretariat and the GALOP communications officer.

      3.     Key implementation progress under Component 3: Strengthen accountability systems for learning. The GES and MOE are
             developing an Accountability for Learning Framework dashboard with support from Big Win Philanthropy (a foundation which partners with
             national leaders to improve young people’s lives and gain demographic dividends for long term growth). The process is in three broad
             phases. First, the GES and MOE developed an accountability framework. Second, the GES and MOE is developing a dashboard which
             utilizes existing data sources for decision making. Finally, the dashboard will make use of existing data sources (Education Management
             Information System-EMIS and Mobile School Report Card-mSRC) which will be integrated in a single application. During the first phase of
             the work, MOE and GES developed an accountability framework that provides an overview of basic education accountability systems
             including levels of monitoring and reporting, roles and responsibilities of key stakeholders, key accountability indicators and purposes of
             assessments. While the framework, which will be the basis of the accountability dashboard, has been fully developed, MOE and GES are
             conducting stakeholder validations to enhance buy-in before disseminating more widely. As a follow-up to this work, MOE and GES are
             working on operationalizing the accountability framework to develop an accountability dashboard that will make use of existing data
             sources.

      4.     Key implementation progress under Component 4: Technical assistance for capacity building and institutional learning. The GALOP
             M&E manual has been developed. M&E Training for the District Directors, District M&E officers, Heads of Supervision, School
             Improvement Support Officers (SISOs), District Planning Officers has been completed between November 12, 2020 and December 5,
             2020. The areas for impact evaluation under GALOP include: (a) impact of learning grants; and (b) effectiveness of innovative delivery of
             INSET. For the INSET impact evaluation, a draft design has been completed with the assistance of DeliverED. For the impact of learning
             grants, MOE is working on the design of the evaluation.

      5.      Key implementation progress under Component 5. Substantial progress has been made under the COVID-19 Accelerated Funding
              under the GALOP AF. In response to the COVID-19 pandemic, basic education schools were closed since March 16, 2020 (with the
              exception of partial school reopening in June 2020 for about 1.5 million children in JHS2, JH3, and SHS2 and SHS3). Schools have fully
              reopened since January 18, 2021. The Ministry, through GhLA, Center for National Distance Learning and Open Schooling (CENDLOS)
              and GES has successfully developed a total of 1,641 TV and radio lessons for KG1 to SHS3 and rolled out a number of distance learning
              modules to students across the country. These lessons include 1,201 lessons for Ghana Learning TV and GhLA online enhanced video
              library (of which 975 video lessons have been uploaded to online GhLA). Printed student activity books were also developed and
              distributed to accompany Ghana Learning Radio lessons. GES and non-state actors also continued face-to-face community-based
              Complementary Basic Education classes observing COVID-19 protocols in districts with limited access to digital learning options, to
              support over 30,000 out-of-school primary-age children (with UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office -FCDO funding). The
              GES procured the Edmodo LMS which was adapted to host materials aligned to Ghana’s curriculum and launched during the National
              Education Week on November 5, 2020 (www.edmodo.com.gh). The Edmodo LMS provides a platform for online-learning and on-going
              communication and teacher between students, parents, and teachers. The platform will be integrated with all distance learning modules
              and will be available on laptops, tablets, and smart phones. The recruitment of an Independent Verification Agency (IVA) has been
              completed and verification is underway.
