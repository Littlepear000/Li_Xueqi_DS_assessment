 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  There has been progress since the last ISM in implementing project activities and advancing key procurement packages. The revised management
  structure that was introduced as part of the restructuring in June 2022, which consists of four PIUs under the CPMU (Coordinating Project
  Management Unit) responsible for coordination across Ministries, has led to improvement in day-to-day management. The new project leadership
  team appointed to accelerate project implementation under the direction of the Minister of Home Affairs reflect improved government ownership and
  commitment to the project.
  The disbursement rate increased from US$ 18,170,000 (6.06%) to US$ 28,170,000 (11.0%) and large procurements under Component 1
  amounting to a total of US$ 42,000,000 (16.8% of the total loan amount) were completed. However, the delays that occurred during the past three
  years contributed to a misalignment between the PDO, project-financed activities and results indicators. The current scope and sequence of project
  activities threaten the likelihood of achievement of the PDO. Project activities remain scattered across the PIUs and the latter still do not have a

  It is agreed that second project restructuring is required to enable the project to fulfill the PDO through more streamlined activities and further

 Overall Comments PDO

  The project is rejuvenating the planning and execution of project activities since the last restructuring in June 2022. No changes are reported in
  the results framework. That said, it is important to note that the delays in procurement and implementation of project activities over the past
  three years have contributed to a misalignment between the PDO, project-financed activities, and results indicators. The results framework will
  be revisited as part of the next project restructuring, the timeline and scope for which will be determined during the upcoming MTR.
