I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

   A. PROJECT STATUS


Introduction: The Lowlands Livelihood Resilience Project Phase I (LLRP-I) has been carried out over a six-year
period (November 2019–September 2025) in 100 woredas across seven regions of Ethiopia, reaching 2.5 million
pastoral and agro-pastoral individuals. The project aims to strengthen the livelihood resilience of these
communities by improving their absorptive, adaptive, and transformative capacities through integrated rangeland
management, diversification of livelihoods, enhanced basic service delivery, and effective project management.
The initial financing totaled US$451 million, comprising US$350 million from IDA, US$90 million from IFAD
co-financing, and US$11 million in cash and in-kind/labor contributions from the beneficiary communities.

Cancellation and Restructurings: The project has undergone two restructurings, first in May 2020 and again in
July 2023. The initial restructuring in May 2020 concerned the cancellation of US$125 million in IDA resources.
At that time, the Government of Ethiopia requested the Bank to cancel US$125 million from the IDA allocation
to address the macroeconomic effects of COVID-19. The project was subsequently restructured in May 2020 to
reflect this cancellation, adjusting the revised IDA financing to US$225 million. A second restructuring occurred
in July 2023, primarily due to changes in institutional arrangements. Following Government reforms in 2018,
overall project oversight shifted from the Ministry of Peace (MoP) to the Ministry of Irrigation and Lowlands
(MILLs). This restructuring also included updates to the results framework and a reduction of the originally
planned US$11 million community contribution to US$7.6 million, reflecting the impact of COVID-19 on
community contributions. Therefore, the revised project financing is now US$322.6 million, consisting of
US$225 million from IDA, US$90 million in IFAD co-financing, and US$7.6 million in cash and in-kind
contributions from beneficiary communities.

Implementation Status: Despite facing numerous challenges over the past six years, including conflicts, recurrent
drought, inflation, and COVID-19, the project has demonstrated impressive implementation progress and remains
on track to fully achieve its result targets and development objectives. A summary of the component-wise
implementation progress is provided below:

Component 1: Integrated Rangeland Development and Management (IRDM): This component received
nearly half of the project’s budget and focused on sustainable rangeland management, strategic investments, and
conflict management. Over 414,000 hectares of land were brought under sustainable management, surpassing the

end-of-project target. Twenty-eight Rangeland Management and Investment Plans were developed and
operationalized, guiding interventions in all project regions. The project completed feasibility studies and detailed
designs for 200 subprojects, including water resources, irrigation, rural roads, bridges, and public economic
infrastructures. As a result, 218 public economic infrastructure became fully operational, providing improved
access to economic and social services for 2.39 million people, almost double the original target. The IRDM
component also established and trained rangeland management committees, implemented extensive soil and water
conservation measures, and supported community-driven conflict management and resource access initiatives.
The project’s approach to participatory planning and capacity building has been recognized as a model for local
ownership and sustainability.

Component 2: Livelihood Improvement and Diversification (LID): The LID component promoted diversified
and climate-resilient livelihoods through two subcomponents: enhancing production systems and expanding
market access. Over 106,000 beneficiaries (89% of target) engaged in diversified income-generating activities,
with women and youth participation exceeding targets. The project established 2,347 Common Interest Groups
(CIGs), of which 2,334 received grants, supporting more than 33,000 members (56% women, 57% youth). A total
of 499 Rural Savings and Credit Cooperatives (RuSaCCOs) were established and supported (99.8 percent of the
target), mobilizing ETB 170.8 million in capital and disbursing ETB 269.7 million in loans to 40,815 members,
with a strong repayment rate. The project also facilitated 22 market linkages and productive partnerships,
generating ETB 23 million in profits for CIGs and RuSaCCOs. Technology adoption, capacity building, and
extension services reached over 65,000 community members, while demonstration activities in poultry,
apiculture, fishery, and horticulture improved food security and household incomes. The LID component’s results
framework indicators show strong progress, with 802,892 farmers reached with agricultural assets or services
(89% of target) and 61,839 beneficiaries accessing rural financial services (124% of target).

Component 3: Improving Basic Services and Capacity Building (IBSCB): This component focused on
expanding access to education, health, water, animal health, and rural infrastructure through the Community
Investment Fund (CIF) and institutional capacity building. A total of 822 CIF subprojects (99% of plan) were
completed and functional, benefiting 1.47 million people. These included 350 schools, 189 water supply projects,
107 health posts, 34 animal health posts, 108 access roads, and 11 small-scale irrigation schemes. The sectoral
distribution prioritized education (43%), water (23%), health (13%), animal health (4%), and roads (13%).
Community contributions in cash and kind reached over ETB 345 million, demonstrating strong local ownership.

Capacity building reached 14,871 committee members and 22,719 participants in quarterly learning events. The
project also documented indigenous knowledge, best practices, and success stories, and supported 17 research
studies to inform policy and practice.

Component 4: Project Management, Monitoring, and Evaluation: LLRP-I established robust management
and oversight structures at federal, regional, woreda, and kebele levels. The project’s monitoring and evaluation
system included baseline, mid-term, and endline surveys, a web-based management information system, and
regular reporting. Internal audit and compliance coverage reached 92% of planned auditees, with improvements
in risk management and financial controls. Environmental and social safeguards were mainstreamed, with
grievance redress mechanisms established at all levels and over 9,000 stakeholders trained in safeguards. The
Project has launched a digital grievance redressals system using a dedicated hotline for mobile phone-based text
and voice messages. The project’s financial management and procurement systems have been functioning well
assuring the use of the project funds for the intended purposes with all required audits and financial reports being
submitted on time.

Progress on Results Framework and PDO Achievement: The updated results framework demonstrates that
LLRP-I has exceeded or is on track to meet most of its key indicators. Land area under sustainable management
reached 414,010 ha (104% of target). People with improved access to economic and social services reached 2.48
million (198% of target), with 47% women and 27% youth. Farmers reached with agricultural assets or services
totaled 802,892 (89% of target). The number of strategic economic infrastructure investments operational reached
218 (95% of target). Livelihood diversification, financial inclusion, and technology adoption indicators all show
strong progress, with women and youth participation consistently surpassing targets. The project’s approach to
gender and social inclusion, community-driven planning, and capacity building has contributed to its success.

With the Project set to close on October 10, 2025, it has achieved notable progress in rangeland management,
livelihood diversification, service delivery, and institutional development. Its participatory approach encouraged
local ownership, gender equity, and sustainability. Despite security challenges, recurrent drought and the COVID-
19 pandemic over the past six years, the project continued to deliver and exceeded most targets, laying the
groundwork for LLRP-II and future resilience programs in Ethiopia’s lowlands. Nearly all objectives and
indicators have been met or surpassed, with only a few pending final verifications from the independent end-of-
project evaluation. Therefore, the project is ready for smooth closure and in the right track to achieve its
development objective.

                  B. RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

The Project has been under implementation under the Ministry of Irrigation and Lowlands (MILLs) since 2019
and is scheduled to close on October 10, 2025. As of September 23, 2025, the Project has utilized 98.95 percent
of its resources, which includes 100 percent of the IFAD co-financing (C1710-001: SDR 65,330,000) and 98
percent of the IDA allocations (IDA 64120-001: SDR 151,934,563.33 and IDA D4600-001 5,200,306.27). This
leaves an undisbursed balance of SDR 1,677,961.40, which cannot be utilized before project closure on October
10, 2025. Consequently, the Government of Ethiopia formally requested the Bank, on September 19, 2025, to
cancel this remaining amount.

Therefore, this restructuring is initiated to cancel the undisbursed balance as requested and update the allocations
accordingly.




  II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

The purpose of the restructuring is to cancel undisbursed funds from IDA allocation in the amount of SDR
1,677,961.40 that cannot be utilized before project closure. Therefore, the only change will be revised allocation
of the project funds under IDA 64120-001. The original allocation under IDA 64120-001 will be revised to SDR
151,934,563.33 from SDR 153,612,524.73 .


This restructuring does not entail any changes to the project design, results framework, development objectives,
or implementation arrangements. It also does not affect the original project closing date.
