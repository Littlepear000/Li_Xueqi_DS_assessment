I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING
A. PROJECT BACKGROUND AND STATUS

   1. This Restructuring Paper seeks a level II restructuring of the Supporting Egypt’s Universal Health Insurance
       System (UHIS) Project (P172426) to extend the Project closing date by twenty-three (23) months from October
       31, 2024, to September 30, 2026. The International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) approved
       the Project for a total financing of US$400 million (EG Loan Number 9132) on June 16, 2020. The Project became
       effective on August 29, 2021, and is scheduled to close on October 31, 2024. The development objectives of the
       Project are to (i) increase the coverage of Egypt’s UHIS in Phase I Governorates, (ii) strengthen UHIS-related
       governance and institutions, and (iii) provide temporary financial protection against high out of pocket health
       expenditures for vulnerable populations outside Phase I Governorates.

   2. The Project supports the Government of Egypt (GoE) ambitious Healthcare transformation agenda. In 2018, the
      Government passed the Universal Health Insurance Law No 2/2018 to launch a major transformation to its
      Universal Health Insurance System (UHIS) - to roll out health insurance nationwide in six phases over a 15-year
      period. Phase I of the roll out covers six Governorates (Port Said, Ismailia, Luxor, South Sinai, Suez and Aswan) and
      started in 2019 with a pilot in Port Said. The program is currently operational in 3 other governorates (Ismailia,
      Luxor, South Sinai). Additionally, four new public agencies have been established to implement the UHIS: (i) the
      Universal Health Insurance Agency (UHIA) - the purchaser; (ii) the Egypt Healthcare Authority (EHA) - the provider;
      (iii) the General Authority for Healthcare Accreditation and Regulation (GAHAR) - for quality accreditation and
      regulation; and (iv) the Egyptian Authority for Standard Procurement and Medical Technology Management
      (UPA). The Project supports the Ministry of Finance (MOF) and the four newly created UHIS organizations t
      agencies to roll out UHIS in Phase 1 Governorates.

   3. Achievement towards the PDO and Implementation Progress are rated Moderately Satisfactory. As of January
      2024, one out of the five PDO indicators met the year 3 target (enrollment and empanelment of population into
      UHIS - 2,542,666 out of 2,328,000 beneficiaries) and is on track to meet the end project target. Project reports on
      three PDO indicators: (i) per capita annual PHC visits by UHIS enrollees in Phase I Governorates (2.05 out of 2.15);
      (ii) percentage of contracted public provider entities accredited by GAHAR (86 percent out of 90 percent); and (iii)
      number of other vulnerable people benefiting from “Payment for Treatment at the Expense of the State” (PTES)
      in other Governorates (87 percent out of 100 percent) show that they achieved at least 80 percent of the year 3
      targets. There is no data to accurately measure the last PDO indicator on annual percentage of payments by UHIA
      made to contracted providers within 60 days of claims submission, though all the backlog of claims for years 2022
      and 2023 has been recently cleared and 75 percent of payments are paid in advance to the public providers upon
      receipt of claims.

   4. There has been some implementation progress and the building blocks of UHIS are gradually being put in place.
      As of January 2024, about 3.1 million people have been enrolled into UHIS, of which over half a million of them
      are poor and vulnerable populations whose premium are being subsidized by the Government. This enrollment

       number represents over 80 percent of the population of the four Governorates where UHIS is presently
       operational. The Prime Minister’s Decree Number 4586/2023 passed in November 2023 expands and clarifies the
       definition of the vulnerable groups and will ensure more poor and vulnerable people are enrolled into UHIS.
       GAHAR has developed national accreditation standards for hospitals, primary health care centers and units,
       laboratories, ambulatory service providers, radiological centers and pharmacies, and UHIA has contracted over
       286 of such accredited providers. A revised provider payment mechanism was adopted for UHIS, and it is being
       implemented, though partially. The Prime Minister issued a Decree in September 2023 to establish a high-level
       Committee to steer the affair of UHIS. There has been recent progress with the UHIS IT system, with one of the 4
       modules (Supply Chain Procurement module) completed and in use. The beneficiary and provider modules of the
       IT system are in advance stages and expected to be fully in use in the coming months. The claims module will likely
       be completed by March 2025, though it can currently process outpatient claims. Notwithstanding these advances,
       there are also significant challenges with the Governance of the system, the pace of implementation of the reform
       and the legal and constitutional constraints with defining a prioritized list of benefit package.

   5. Disbursement: The Project has currently disbursed 36 percent (US$ 143.7 million) of the total financing amount.
      In addition, results have been achieved, submitted and verified for US$ 35.1 million and expected to be processed
      and disbursed within the coming weeks when the notice of achievement and withdrawal application will be
      submitted and processed. This will take disbursement to US$ 178.8 million (45 percent of loan amount). In
      addition, the Government has submitted results for achievement of PBC 9 (number of other vulnerable people
      benefiting from PTES) worth about US$ 45 million (subject to verification). If fully verified, this will improve the
      disbursement to US$ 223.8 million (56 percent).

   6. Fiduciary, safeguards, and INT compliance: As of May 2024, Procurement, Financial Management, and
      Environmental and Social Safeguards are all rated Moderately Satisfactory. There are no outstanding audits
      reports and all previous Interim Financial Reports and audit reports have been received, reviewed and deemed
      acceptable. No change in procurement arrangements. There are no open INT investigations related to the sector
      or Project and the team also confirms that no contract has been awarded to or is in the process of being awarded
      to a debarred firm(s).

B. RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

   7. An extension of Project closing date by 23-months will allow for improved performance and impact, improved
      disbursement, and will enable the Project to achieve its PDO results within the proposed Project extension
      period. The Project design started in 2019 when the newly created UHIS institutions were still in their early stages
      of development, thus these implementing institutions only had limited inputs at the Project preparation stage.
      Project effectiveness was delayed after the World Bank's Board approval, from June 2020 to August 2021. The
      COVID-19 pandemic also significantly contributed to the delay in initial Project activities, including the hiring of an
      independent verification agent. The Project had a three-year implementation timeline, which is not adequate for
      the scale and ambition of the reform agenda. The UHIS agencies are now better established and functional and are

      able to implement project activities more than previously. They also have more buy-in into the Project design and
      have proposed some changes to better address implementation bottlenecks and align with the country’s emerging
      needs, as implementation progresses. These proposed changes will be addressed through a proposed technical
      restructuring of the Project, which is expected to be sent to the World Bank for approval. Consequently, the
      proposed extension of the Project closing date will provide the Government additional time to finalize a proposed
      technical Project restructuring, and accelerate disbursement, which is now showing signs of improvement as a result
      of improved Project performance. Additionally, the extension of the Project closing date will immediately allow for
      the Project to continue with critical activities that will go beyond the current closing date of October 2024 such as
      engagement of firms that will help with the development of the UHIS roadmap and fill current staffing gaps that are
      required for successful implementation.

  8. Closing the UHIS Project at this stage is likely to have severe negative impact on the transformational agenda of
     the Government and derail achievement of the World Bank Country Partnership Framework objectives for FY23-
     FY27 which prioritizes improving access to healthcare services and strengthening financial protection from out-
     of-pocket expenditures. Global evidence is that reforms of this nature require time. The World Bank is a critical
     financial and technical partner to the Government of Egypt on this ambitious UHIS reform and is the primary
     development partner supporting the UHIS, not only in lending operation, but also with an array of advisory and
     analytic services as well as policy dialogues and aligning partnership around the agenda. JICA and AFD have
     developed projects and technical assistance to support Egypt’s UHIS, in line with the World Bank’s financing and
     technical support.


II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

  9. The proposed restructuring is to extend the Project closing date by 23 months to September 30, 2026. The
     extension of the closing date to September 30, 2026, rather than October 31, 2026, as requested by the
     Government was agreed and communicated to the client.



Summary of Performance of E&S Risk Compliance

Compliance with the ESCP: The PMU is working towards meeting all the requirements of the ESCP. They have hired a
new environmental specialist and are in the process of filling the vacant position of the Social Specialist. The PMU has
agreed to send progress reports to the Bank team, covering the period from December 2022 till the last quarter, in
October 2023. The reports should reflect the actual progress on the Environmental and Social aspects and include
analysis of common challenges and suggestions for improvement. The PMU will also ensure that all relevant
stakeholders have copies of the ESCP, SEP, and SIA. Field Visit in Ismailia governorate: The Bank team visited two
health care facilities that are registered with GAHAR. The facilities were observed to effectively implement GAHAR's
standards for environmental protection, fire protection, and waste management. They had dedicated infection
prevention control teams and waste management staff. Training on IPC measures and waste management was
provided to both medical and non-medical staff. The facilities recorded the amount of generated waste and had proper
storage areas. They also conducted regular emergency drills and had contracts with licensed contractors for hazardous

   waste disposal. Grievance Mechanisms and Beneficiaries' Satisfaction: The visited facilities have clear channels for
   grievance mechanisms and disseminate information about them. The social specialist resolves most complaints, and
   unresolved ones are referred to related entities and Facility Managers. The facilities also have Beneficiaries'
   Satisfaction Units and display the patients' rights charter. Reports on patients' complaints and stakeholder engagement
   are regularly submitted to the EHA office at the Governorate level. The Bank team suggested better communication of
   these reports to the PMU team. A new mobile application was launched for healthcare workers to report complaints,
   which should be reflected in the ESF reporting. Improving E&S Performance: The Bank team commended the facilities'
   good E&S performance on grievance mechanisms and stakeholder engagement. However, some patients expressed
   dissatisfaction with the registration process, staff training, and the hotline for reservations at El Salam PMC. The Bank
   team advised the PMU to utilize the SEP and work with the facilities to improve information sharing and streamline
   registration requirements. Capacity Building Activities: The PMU E&S team appreciated the support offered during a
   workshop in May 2023 to improve ESP and GRM templates. The PMU requested support from the Bank in conducting a
   2-day workshop to discuss the ESP and user satisfaction survey, as well as the dissemination of findings to citizens and
   key stakeholders. The workshop is planned to be held in late November or early December 2023. The following actions
   were agreed during the last mission: 1- Continue the efforts to streamline the different GM systems and ensure
   consistency in the collected information both for GM and for stakeholder engagement activities 2- Workshop on user
   satisfaction survey and modalities of dissemination of findings to the citizens and to the key stakeholders
