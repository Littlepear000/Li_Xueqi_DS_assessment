4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  The overall progress of implementation is deemed satisfactory (S), and the progress towards the achievement of the
  Project Development Objective (PDO) continues at a moderate pace (Moderately Satisfactory – MS). These ratings will be
  reviewed and potentially revised after the next implementation support mission, scheduled for May 20-24, 2024. The
  disbursement ratio for FY24 stands at 24.37%, with the overall disbursement rate reaching 63.95%, equivalent to US$
  127.89 million. This represents a substantial increase from the last ISR in November 2023, which recorded US$ 106.61
  million due to the finalization and initial disbursement towards the large-sized contracts signed, including the Kumasi
  EPC, Project management consultancy for the EPC and ORC's new e-Registration Software, among others. It is anticipated
  that the client will submit withdrawal applications totalling at least US$ 11.0 million before the end of FY24. These
  applications are to cover payment of US$ 4.0 million to the Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (EPC) contractor
  for the Kumasi Special Economic Zone (SEZ) site and to finalize the establishment of new funds, estimated at US$ 7
  million under the Venture Capital Trust Fund (VCTF). These actions are expected to bring FY24 disbursement to
  approximately US$ 32.5 million.

  During this ISR period, several result indicators requiring clarification in their definitions, baselines, and targets have been
  addressed to ensure proper attribution to the project's activities and investments. These include PDO indicators such as
  the target and scope for measuring the turnaround time for obtaining construction permits; the scope, baseline, and
  target for the time to resolve commercial disputes; and reaching an agreement with GIPC and the PCU on measuring
  Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), which directly contributes to the PDO indicator of private sector investments leveraged
  through project activities and one of the PBCs. Additionally, citizen engagement in rulemaking improved through the
  Business Regulatory Reform Portal, a PBC, which was also finalized, and its definition, measurement, and baseline were
  established. Three out of four PDO indicators are on track, including the turnaround time to obtain construction permits,
  private investments leveraged through project activities and increase (additional) in average value of gross sales by firms
  supported by the project and are expected to be achieved by project closure. The remaining indicator, the time it takes to
  resolve commercial disputes, requires close monitoring and additional investment in a new district court automation
  system to improve efficiency and enable court processes, for which the team is working closely with the Judicial Services
  of Ghana (JSG). Six intermediate results indicators have already met or exceeded their end targets; seven are on track to
  meet their end targets; and three are considered achievable by the project's end.
