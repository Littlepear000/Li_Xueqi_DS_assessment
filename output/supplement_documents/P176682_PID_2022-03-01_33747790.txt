     B. Introduction and Context

     Country Context

 1.      Tanzania is a geographically large, culturally diverse, and well-endowed lower middle-income country
 (LMIC). Out of 54 African countries, Tanzania is the fifth largest in terms of population, ninth largest in terms of
 the size of economy, and thirteenth largest in terms of geographical area. Solid income growth over two decades
 led the country to reach LMIC status in July 2020.

 2.       The graduation from low-income status reflects sustained macroeconomic and political stability as
 well as the country’s rich natural resources endowment and strategic geographic position. Macroeconomic
 stability has been crucial to Tanzania’s growth; inflation rates have been low – below five percent since 2018 –
 and sustainable fiscal and current-account deficits have been financed by a combination of domestic and
 external sources. Over the past two decades, investment has been a key driver of economic growth. The rise in
 overall investment translated into a sustained accumulation of capital stock and has consistently accounted for
 roughly two-thirds of real gross domestic product (GDP) growth.

 3.     The COVID-19 pandemic has negatively affected Tanzania’s macroeconomic performance—
 decelerating GDP) growth in 2020—although Tanzania is one of the few economies in the region that avoided
 recession.1 The global economic slowdown adversely affected Tanzania’s economy as shocks to export-oriented
 sectors such as tourism and manufacturing and related services diminished business revenue and labor income,
 which adversely affected domestically oriented firms of all sizes across all sectors. The exception is gold mining,
 which has benefitted from rising prices since the onset of the pandemic. Although the Government did not
 impose a lockdown, the pandemic initially spurred precautionary behaviors that slowed down domestic
 economic activity.

 4.     Tanzania’s vulnerability to the global pandemic remains high. Tanzania has had two surges of COVID-
 19 (March-June 2020 and December 2020-September 2021) with 26,115 cases and 725 deaths reported so far.2
 The Government of Tanzania (GoT) implemented critical measures aimed at containing the spread of COVID-19
 and encouraged people to avoid unnecessary movements, practice preventive behaviors including hand
 washing, use of masks and social distancing, besides operationalizing several public and private hospitals to
 provide services for people infected with COVID-19. In February 2021, the COVID-19 response strategy was
 revised in line with global standards and epidemiological trends, with emphasis on encouraging people to
 practice preventive behaviors, continued education of the population, rolling out COVID-19 immunization to

 5.     The poverty rate in Tanzania has been declining gradually. The national poverty headcount has
 improved to 28.2 percent in 2012 from 34.4 percent of population in 2007, and further to 26.4 percent in 2018.4
 However, the number of poor people has increased due to population growth. 5 Despite Tanzania’s impressive
 GDP growth between 2012 and 2018, poverty reduction slowed, and growth has become less inclusive.
 Inequality has also risen during this period. The international poverty headcount (US$1.90/day in 2011
 purchasing power parity) remained high and unchanged during this period at 49 percent.

 6.     Tanzania is heavily reliant on natural resources, although climate change is threatening the livelihoods
 of many. Climate change affects coastal zones, public health, energy supply and demand, infrastructure, water
 resources, agricultural production, and availability of goods and services. Approximately 25 percent of the
 Tanzanian population live along the mainland coast and are susceptible to accelerated sea level rise. It is
 estimated that the hazard level for floods is high, and the hazard levels for water scarcity and extreme heat are
 medium.6 Research shows that intensifying disasters due to climate change are the second most cited reason
 for tenure insecurity in Tanzania,7 and Tanzania’s National Climate Change Strategy (2021) identified land use
 as one of the key intervention areas to combat climate change.

 7.      Tanzania’s cost of adaptation to the current climate impacts is estimated at approximately US$500
 million annually and, by 2030, could increase to US$1 billion a year if no action is taken .8 Individual annual
 events have economic costs in excess of 1 percent of GDP. Recognizing its vulnerability to climate change
 impacts, the Government of Tanzania adopted the National Climate Change Strategy in 2012, a plan that
 identifies climate change risks for 18 sectors and 12 cross-cutting areas, where it proposes over 200 strategic
 interventions to mitigate risks. However, the implementation of climate-resilient approaches and its integration
 in projects has been insufficient. Currently, there is a lack of action plans to address climate change for most
 sectors, including transport infrastructure, and the country is already experiencing problems related to inaction.
 For example, abnormal flooding has recently been causing railway closures along the central railway line
 (especially in Morogoro region) for around three to four months annually with massive economic losses.

 Sectoral and Institutional Context

 9.     Tanzania is an important gateway serving its own hinterland as well as the six landlocked countries of
 Malawi, Zambia, the Democratic Republic of Congo, Burundi, Rwanda, and Uganda. The country of 945,500
 km2 area has a total road network of 145,308 km, out of which 13,590 km is paved and the rest unpaved gravel
 and earth. The roads are being managed by the Tanzania National Roads Agency (TANROADS) and the Tanzania
 Rural and Urban Roads Agency (TARURA). There are two railway systems with a total of 3,682 km: the Tanzania
 railway (2,707 km) – a meter gauge railway (MGR) - under the Tanzania Railways Corporation (TRC) and the
 Tanzania-Zambia railway line (975 km) – a cape gauge railway (CGR) - under the Tanzania Zambia Railway
 Authority (TAZARA). Also, there is a standard gauge railway (SGR) that is under construction along the central
 corridor between Dar es Salaam and Mwanza (1,219 km) with works already under way along Dar es Salaam to
 Makutupora (722 km). The maritime transport system comprises coastal seaports of Dar es Salaam, Tanga, and
 Mtwara as well as lake ports at lakes Victoria, Tanganyika, and Nyasa under the Tanzania Ports Authority (TPA).
 The country also has 59 airports under the Tanzania Airports Authority (TAA) and about 200 airdromes operated
 by other agencies including the private sector.

 10.    Regulatory arrangements are in place for regulating transport operations. Surface transport (road and
 railway transport) is being regulated by the Land Transport Regulatory Authority (LATRA), while maritime
 transport operations by the Tanzania Shipping Agencies Corporation (TASAC). The aviation operations are being
 regulated by the Tanzania Civil Aviation Authority (TCAA).

 11.     The Tanzania railway system is yet to operate to its full potential due to infrastructural and operational
 challenges. The design of the annual freight capacity for both the central MGR and the TAZARA CGR lines is 5
 million tons each. However, the historical annual peak freight volume carried by the 100-year-old MGR and the
 TAZARA railway was 1.2 million tons and 1.5 million tons, respectively, in 2002 and 2005. Thereafter, the freight
 volumes have been declining, mainly due to deteriorated infrastructure and inadequate investment for repairs
 and/or replacement of rolling stock that led to unreliable services. The freight carried in the year 2020/2021 by
 MGR and TAZARA was 316,000 and 217,661 tons, respectively. The railway has therefore been losing its modal
 share to its main competitor—roads running parallel to the railway lines in both cases.

 19.    The Government of Tanzania has embarked on the construction of a new SGR along the central

  D. Concept Description

 21.     The proposed project is a follow-on project to TIRP to strengthen the safety and operational efficiency
 of the central corridor railway from Dar es Salaam port to Isaka. With an investment of US$150 million, the
 project is planned to strengthen the railway infrastructure, enhance operational efficiency, develop and deploy
 robust safety processes, and ensure institutional strengthening for safe open access operations. The project is
 also proposed to support the updating of the Business Plan, commercial plan and regulation for TRC to operate
 as both an infrastructure manager as well as a railway operator. The project is also expected to support the
 capacity strengthening of TRC through management partnering with well-established operators. This will be
 accomplished whilst also addressing environmental concerns and empowering women to further participate in
 the railway sub-sector.

 22.    The project will contribute to Tanzania’s decarbonization agenda by attracting more cargo to the
 railway along the central corridor and thus moving cargo to a more energy-efficient mode of transport. The
 transport sector is responsible for about one-quarter of global CO2 emissions from fuel combustion. At the same
 time, despite the relatively large volume of traffic it carries, rail transport consumes only 2 percent of total
 transport energy demand worldwide.19 In the United States, where rail transport is heavily tilted toward
 movement of freight, only a small fraction of its rail network is electrified, with a greater reliance on diesel-
 powered locomotives. Even so, rail transport provides significant energy efficiency gains when compared to
 highway transport. In 2018, reported numbers for a major US rail carrier indicated a rail energy consumption
 metric of 492 ton-miles per gallon of diesel (190 ton-km per liter).20 This is nearly four times the efficiency of
 moving freight by highway trucks with a typical energy consumption metric of 134 ton-miles per gallon (52 ton-
 km per liter).

 Component 1 - Strengthening of Infrastructure

 24.     The proposed strengthening of infrastructure will enable the railway track to be able to carry 18.5-ton
 axle load between Dar es Salaam port and Isaka. The project will upgrade bridges below 18.5-ton axle load,
 rehabilitate the Tabora - Isaka (130 km) section, and refurbish the tracks to selected sections of about 350 km
 between Dar es Salaam and Tabora. Strengthening of the infrastructure will also facilitate efficient block train
 operation which is currently precluded by axle loading limits in some areas of the network.

 25.     The Tabora-Isaka section that is to be rehabilitated is currently the weakest link for the container block
 train operations. The railway track and bridges are weak and this is the only section between Dar es Salaam and
 Isaka with 56/60 pounds per yard rails and therefore a maximum axle load capacity of 15 tons. The Dar es
 Salaam-Tabora section is currently laid with 80 pounds per yard continuously welded rails (CWR) and can thus
 support an 18.5-ton axle load capacity. Therefore, currently, the 3,000 hp container block train locomotive can
 only operate between Dar es Salaam and Tabora. The block train must be split at Tabora for the shunting
 between Tabora and Isaka to be done by two 2,200 hp locomotives. The double handling causes delays and
 affects the reliability of and efficiency of services. The proposed rehabilitation is planned to address this
 bottleneck by rehabilitating the section through complete renewal of tracks, installation of CWR of 80 pounds
 per yard, and rehabilitation of bridges. The improvements will upgrade the section to an 18.5-ton axle load
 capacity and enable continuous operation of the block train (using a 3,000 hp locomotive with 40 container
 wagons) uninterrupted from Dar es Salaam to Isaka.

 26.    The refurbishment/repair of the track and bridges between Dar es Salaam and Tabora will stabilize
 the infrastructure to attain an average operational speed of 30 kph. The refurbishment is planned to be
 conducted at Munisagara - Msagali (84.3 km), Kikombo - Bahi (74.8 km), Makutupora - Manyoni (33.6 km), and
 Kitaraka - Igalula (149 km) and will include strengthening works that were not implemented under TIRP due to
 budget limitations. The proposed works include CWR, replenishment of ballast, and formation repair and
 strengthening.

 27.     The proposed infrastructural activities are summarized in a strip map in Annex 1 to provide a clear
 distinction from implemented TIRP activities.

 28.    Bridge repairs and rehabilitation. In this activity, culverts will be replaced by box culverts and riverbed
 protection and river flow improvement will be done to bridges. A total of 156 bridges will be repaired, and two
 new bridges will be constructed at Ruvu.

 29.     Infrastructure safety improvements at level crossings and safety improvements in settlement areas.
 This activity will involve erection of railway crossings sign boards and warning electric sirens at several towns
 (Dar es Salaam, Morogoro, Dodoma, and Tabora) and erection of chain-link fence to deter trespassing.

 30.    Improvement of intermodal terminals. The two intermodal terminals of Ilala and Isaka that were not
 covered under TIRP are proposed to be implemented under the project. The third intermodal terminal at the
 Dar es Salaam port is being implemented under the Dar es Salaam Maritime Gateway Project (P150496). The
 completion of realignment of rail and loading platform at the three intermodal terminals will reduce train in-
 and-out maneuvers and address cargo loading and unloading delays at the intermodal terminals.

 31.    Recovery from the annual flooding of the Kilosa - Gulwe segment is important to continued
 operational efficiency. The project will therefore include provisions to plan and support the emergency rapid
 response along Kilosa-Gulwe as well as a response mechanism to any other unforeseen disaster on emergency
 basis within the Dar es Salaam to Isaka section with the inclusion of a CERC (see Component 3 below).

 32.     The project is also proposed to support key transport studies. The Kilosa-Gulwe-Igandu segment of the
 Dar es Salaam-Isaka section is subject to two to four months of closure annually due to flooding of the adjoining
 river. The project is proposed to support a study for a permanent solution. Further, the project is proposed to
 support studies for designs for rehabilitation of the rest of the central railway. The proposed sections to be
 designed include Kilosa-Kidatu, Tabora-Kigoma, Isaka-Mwanza, and Tanga-Arusha.

 Component 2 – Institutional Safety and Operational Support

 33.      The development of a safety culture in the railway network and the supporting regulatory agencies is
 critical for enhancement of safe operations and reduction/elimination of safety incidents. Institutional safety
 support is proposed to include updating of the Safety Management System (SMS); updating of the operational
 manual to reduce the likelihood of human errors; guiding planned improvements in level-crossing protection
 and the deployment of new electronic warrant train control system; guiding the Project Implementation Team
 (PIT); a study for the transformation of TAZARA; and training of TRC, LATRA, and Ministry of Works and Transport
 (MoWT) staff.

 34.    The goal of increasing freight and passenger productivity of the line and the move to an open access
 operational mode requires a number of key transformations. Operational support is proposed to include
 support for the TRC to implement its business plan; introduction of open access operations, deployment and
 operation of a new track recording car, and the switch to condition-based maintenance as part of enterprise-
 wide asset management transformation.

 Component 3 - Contingent Emergency Rapid Response (CERC)

 35.    This zero-dollar component will allow for swift reallocation of credit proceeds from the other
 components to provide immediate emergency recovery support following an eligible crisis or emergency. This
 CERC was developed to be triggered only for emergencies and/or disasters that affect the railway track along
 the Dar es Salaam – Isaka railway section.

 Cost Estimates

 36.     The Government request for the project was for US$144.9 million; however, initial discussions show
 that the activities for safety strengthening of the central railway system are estimated to cost around US$200
 million. As only about US$150 million of IDA fund is allocated, the team will discuss with the Government
 counterparts the possibility for counterpart funding and/or exemption of value added tax (VAT), payable by the
 Government during project preparation.

 37.     The overall risk is Moderate at the project preparation stage. The Government is implementing the SGR
 in parallel to the central railway corridor. However, although the SGR project has been under implementation
 since 2017, only two out of four lots between Dar es Salaam and Isaka have secured funding commitment from
 the Government, while implementation of one package beyond this project area between Isaka and Mwanza
 has only commenced in 2021. Irrespective of the implementation of the SGR, the Government informed the
 World Bank in 2016 that the current MGR line will continue with operations even when the SGR line is
 completed. There is also a risk of commitment for timely release of counterpart funding for addressing the
 estimated US$50 million financing gap. The risk will be mitigated during project preparation and concluded
 during appraisal. There is also a risk of lack of capacity for project preparation, and implementation. This will be
 addressed by maintaining the existing Project Implementation Team (PIT) for TIRP to support preparation and
 implementation of the second project. Finally, there is low risk for noncompliance to Environmental and Social
 Framework (ESF) requirements as the project will be implemented along the same corridor where
 environmental and social instruments for TIRP applied. The inadequate ESF knowledge by the implementing
 agency staff is to be addressed through continuous training of the environmental and social unit.

 project, the civil works and activities will take place within the same footprint of existing Railway infrastructure and will
 not extend beyond the track corridors except for sourcing materials and new tracks. While the objective of the project is
 to strengthen the safety and operational efficiency of the central corridor railway from Dar es Salaam port to Isaka,
 construction phase of the project will entail (1) labor and working conditions including (Occupational health and Safety
