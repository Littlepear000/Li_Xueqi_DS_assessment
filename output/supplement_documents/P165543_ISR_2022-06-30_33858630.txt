 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The project will have a restructuring to reduce and add certain scope of activities and to accelerate the implementation. It is expected that the
  project will operate in 380 districts to cover 66,496 villages in Year 2022-2024. During this year, several activities have been completed such as
  series of workshops to define the competencies and standards of various village government officials, an assessment to inform potential
  modifications, adjustments or additional support systems needed to roll out capacity building programs in lagging regions, development of desa
  inklusif guidelines, and so on. Furthermore the project plans to carry out series of training in 15 districts to improve technical guidelines and develop
  an Akademi Desa online video platform which will be used to store and publish the community produced videos.

 Overall Comments PDO

  Citizen Engagement

  Overall, the Project (P3PD) aims to strengthen institutional capacity of the village governments so as to improve quality of spending in the
  participating villages. The project's theory of change outlines several pathways to achieve these results and it will invest in technology-driven
  systems and will rollout support for customized village capacity plans, increased citizen access to empowerment and technical support
  services, village performance systems, and national coordination. The project anticipates that when these interventions are in place as
  measured in the Results Framework, they will lead to improved institutional capacity in three core areas: i) data-driven and results-oriented
  development plans and budgets; ii) active citizen engagement in planning, implementation, and oversight; and iii) improvements in
  transparency and accountability systems.

  Under the Restructuring packages that are expected to soon take place, the project will comprise three components, ie., Component 1 focuses
  on the strengthening of village governments capacity and their accountability, Component 2 focuses on strengthening community participation
  and social inclusion and accountability systems for village development, and Component 3 focuses on national coordination, monitoring and
  policy.

  The project went effective in February 2020 slightly before the pandemic begun. The pandemic along with other aspects related to the
  operating environment have contributed to the delays of the project. We started to see some progress of implementation only in 2021, albeit
  relatively small progress and at different pace across different components. This has resulted to the fact that most of the RFs are still at their
  baseline value and/or monitoring and evaluation plans are still underway.

  1. Citizen engagement on project activities

  As noted above, when the overall project’s theory of change is able to be executed as planned, the project hopes to among others improve
  citizen engagement in overall village governance cycle, from planning, implementation and oversight, and ultimately this will contribute to the
  achievement of the PDO. In particular, under Component 1, it is expected the investment being made to, among others, improve the capacity
  of the village governments to be more willing and open to greater citizen engagement in policy making processes. Under Component 2, most
  of the activities are to improve the operating environment at village level so that, there are more safe space for civic engagement and more
  meaningful citizen participation at decision making processes. Under Component 3, investments are being made to ensure all of the planned
  changes can be better coordinated beyond project’s life cycle.

  2. Grievance mechanism

  The grievance mechanism for the project uses the Government’s existing complaint handling systems SP4N LAPOR! System. It is an
  integrated “one stop” online public complaint management system. This is aligned with the Government of Indonesia’s vision for an integrated
  system for good governance that is centrally managed. The P3PD will focus on further strengthening the access to the SP4N LAPOR! System,
  managed by theMinistry of Administrative and Bureaucratic Reform (MenPANRB).




  The system is publicly accessible through http://kemendagri.lapor.go.id/ and currently connects 34 ministries, 96 central agencies and 493
  local governments in the country. The system records complaints received through the website, as well as through SMS, twitter and mobile
  applications. The system application can be downloaded in mobile phone from Google Play and the App Store.




  The SP4N LAPOR! dashboard is publicly accessible and presented updates on the received complaints. To manage, monitor and follow-up the
  complaints related to P3PD programs activities, MoHA will assign personnel and develop a Standard Operation Procedure (SOP) that sets
  clear procedures and mechanisms focused on complaint handling. As part of the Project restructuring, it is expected that CPMU MoHA can
  accelerate the procurement of NMC package within which a Complaint Handling Specialist (consultant) will be recruited to manage and
  handling the complaints received.




  3. Beneficiary feedback indicator in the Results Framework

  The indicator ie, “Beneficiaries feel that village investments meet their needs (by gender)”, was selected to measure citizen engagement (CE)/
  beneficiary feedback across the program. Current status of the indicator are still at their baseline value this was due the overall delay of the
  project as noted above. This indicator will be tracked using the Baseline, Midline, and Endline surveys led by PIU Bappenas as part of the IE
  study. The Baseline survey has only kicked off in March 2022 and is estimated to be completed by end of 2022. As such, at the time of this ISR
  reporting, the task team does not have the data to report on any progress of the indicator. It is expected that the result of the Baseline survey
  will be reported at the next MTR Mission in December 2022 and, among others, generate some findings that will inform the progress of the BF
  indicator.
