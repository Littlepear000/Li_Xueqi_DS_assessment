I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING
    1. This paper seeks approval from the Country Director for a Level II restructuring
       of the Madagascar Economic Transformation for Inclusive Growth Project (PIC3),
       Credit No. 6929-MG, which will reallocate 25 million USD of funds towards activities to
       support Madagascar Airlines (MA) – which will be included in a new disbursement category
       - and add two intermediate indicators to track impact of this support. This is the second
       restructuring for this project.
   2. Urgent support for Madagascar Airlines was requested by the Government of
      Madagascar. At the request of the Government of Madagascar, the PIC3 agreed to reserve
      in late December 2023 US$25 million of the project budget for a group of activities to
      support on an emergency basis the operation of Madagascar Airlines. Tourism is one of the
      three target sectors of the PIC3 and having a viable airline is key for the continuation of
      tourism activities in the country and will ensure that SMEs in the sector can continue to
      operate and grow. The set of activities approved will contribute to keep Madagascar Airlines
      operating their domestic flights, which are key to maintaining intra-regional connectivity
      and supporting the functioning of the tourism sector in Madagascar. A specific operation
      to provide much larger and comprehensive support is being prepared by the World Bank
      and is expected to be presented to the World Bank Board in first half of FY25. As soon as
      this new operation is effective, any activities previously planned under PIC3 that are not
      already under procurement will be migrated to the new operation. Therefore, PIC3 support
      to Madagascar Airlines is limited in both time and budget. The team will review and propose
      more comprehensive revisions to PIC3 (including indicators and targets) based on the
      project’s upcoming Mid-term Review.
   3. Background: PIC3 is an Investment Project Financing (IPF) that was approved on June 18,
      2021, in the amount of US$150 million equivalent, and became effective on November 12,
      2021. In May 2024 the project was asked to cancel US$20 million of unused funds, at the
      request of the Government of Madagascar. This cancellation was formalized through a level
      2 restructuring which was approved by the World Bank Country Director on May 31, 2024.
      The project closing date is January 31, 2027.
   4. The PDO is to “increase the growth of Small and Medium Enterprises in Target
      Regions in Madagascar”. The project’s target regions are Diana, Atsimo-Andrefana,
      Anosy, Analanjirofo, Atsinanana, Fitovinany, Vatovavy, and Atsimo-Atsinanana. PIC3
      consists of five components: (1) Attracting and Retaining Private Investment and Removing
      Key Constraints to Support Economic Recovery from the COVID19 crisis; (2) Removing
      Constraints to Private Investment and Enhancing Local Economic Competitiveness to
      Support Recovery in Target Regions and Target Sectors; (3) Supporting SMEs and
      Entrepreneurship Recovery and Growth in Target Sectors and Target Regions; (4) project
      implementation; and (5) contingent emergency response component (CERC).
   5. Project performance to date: Progress towards achieving the PDO and implementation
      progress are both rated Moderately Unsatisfactory (MU). Financial Management (FM) and
      procurement are rated Moderately Satisfactory. There are no pending audits. All legal
      covenants to date have been complied with. The project's environmental and social
      management performance is rated as "Moderately Satisfactory," (MS) supported by an
      operational E&S institutional arrangement. Overall risk remains Substantial.

       6. PIC3 has disbursed US$ 39 million1 (out of the US$ 129.8 million budget),
          representing 30 percent of the total budget. The reason for the downgrade to MU
          after the supervision mission of October 2023 was the low disbursement performance that
          the project had starting at the end of 2022 and throughout 2023. These challenges were
          mainly caused by the delays in processing the “régularisation budgétaire” (a national
          procedure which was a prerequisite for call of funds and withdrawal applications
          clearance). The delays with the régularisation budgétaire were due to heavy procedures at
          the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) but were also a consequence of important
          delays at the PIC PIU, reflecting a diminished FM capacity at the Project Implementation
          Unit (PIU). Because of these delays, the project was not able to receive funds into its own
          accounts, which significantly delayed payments of suppliers and contractors of goods and
          services. As a consequence, the project accumulated significant arrears and disbursed
          lower than expected amounts, leading to delays and even temporary suspension of the
          implementation of many committed activities that eventually put the project in problem
          status.
       7. The fiduciary situation has significantly improved since then, albeit FM capacity
          at the PIU continues to be a challenge. Over the past 6 months (January-June 2024),
          the project has been able to disburse more funds than over the previous 2 years. The World
          Bank supervision team and the PIU also agreed on a list of contracts/activities that have
          been prioritized in terms of implementation. More efforts have been dedicated to improve
          the communication with PIU and increase the frequency of technical meetings to support
          the PIU in resolving issues with contracting. The recruitment of key positions in the PIU is
          ongoing. Progress on priority activities and main issues are followed weekly during a
          meeting between World Bank and PIU. The above actions including this restructuring are
          all proactive actions taken to remove this project out of problem status.


II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

       8. The proposed changes under this restructuring are the reallocation of funds across
          subcomponents to provide the budget of US$25million for the support to Madagascar
          Airlines, and the addition of indicators to track impact of these activities. In addition, a new
          category of expenditures is being added (category 4) to insulate the spending related to
          MA.
       9. Activities to support Madagascar Airlines will be inserted under Subcomponent
          2.1 (specifically under a new line of activities 2.1. (e)), aligned with the
          objectives described in the PAD of promoting improved access and enhancing
          competitiveness of key regional tourism destinations and is not expected to alter the
          project PDO. Activities include maintenance and repairs of key equipment (engine, brakes),
          purchase of spare parts, and digitalization of key services (including relaunch of main B2C
          website, and capacity building and training)2.

      work to process and implement activities to benefit Madagascar Airlines has added a
      significant burden to an already stretched PIU. An additional procurement specialist is
      being hired to specifically aid the PIU process contracts and activities related to this
      support.
  15. Addition of two intermediate indicators. Given the significant budget that is being
      allocated to supporting Madagascar Airlines, two new intermediary indicators are being
      added to help track the impact of the $25 million worth of activities that will be
      implemented through PIC. These indicators have been discussed and agreed with the
      Transport Global Practice (GP) team (internally), and the PIU and Madagascar Airlines. Two
      indicators to track progress and impact of support to Madagascar Airlines are being
      proposed. The indicators are:
         a. Domestic Available Seat Kilometers (million): Sum product of number of seats
            and distance flown of each flight, indicative of the capacity.
         b. On-line sales (percent): Level of penetration of on-line sales as percentage of
            total sales.
   16. These indicators give a measure of seat capacity of the flights, and thus of
       continued service to the tourism sector, and also digitalization of services, which
       should help with increased efficiency and ultimately productivity (and ease of
       customer experience). They are also directly related to activities that the PIC3 is
       financing for Madagascar Airlines. Moreover, these are indicators already planned to be
       tracked by the larger Transport GP loan currently under preparation. The Transport GP
       team already has baseline data for them, and targets for 2026 are being proposed to fit
       within the timeline of implementation for PIC3. The PIC3 PIU will work with Madagascar
       Airlines to track and report data related to these two indicators, and they will become part
       of the Results Framework (as intermediate indicators) for the project and will be reported
       to the World Bank along with the other existing indicators.
   17. The previous cancelation of funds and the current reallocation towards new
       activities related to MA, would necessitate a more comprehensive review of the
       indicators and targets that were originally envisaged for the project. Given that
       there is an upcoming Mid-Term Review expected in this FY, a review of project components,
       indicators and targets will be undertaken as part of that process and a proposal for
       adjusting the project (including its targets) will be submitted at that stage.
   18. Capacity building for MEF. The Bank and the government recently agreed to initiate a
       program of capacity building for the MEF to improve its ability to monitor and support the
       implementation of the Bank portfolio, to be financed from several projects. Accordingly,
       component 4 of PIC3 (Project implementation) will finance $0.5 million to support
       institutional capacity building for the MEF.

IV. DETAILED CHANGE(S)

Summary Description
Operation Abstract: Summary Description of Proposed Operation
The Project Development Objective (PDO) for Madagascar Economic Transformation for
Inclusive Growth Project (named “PIC 3”) is to increase the growth of Small and Medium
Enterprises in target regions in Madagascar. It will focus on three key growth sectors:
agribusiness, tourism and digital. The PIC 3’s integrated, multi-sectoral approach to job creation
and inclusive growth is fully aligned with the objectives of the government’s development plan
(Plan emergence Madagascar – PEM) and the World Bank’s 2017-2021 Country Partnership
Framework. The project specifically supports the two Focus Areas of the CPF, namely: i)
increase resilience and reduce fragility; and ii) promote inclusive growth. This project is
presented as the first in a Series of Projects (SOP), which will share the same PDO as the
proposed first project. This new series will build on the successful model implemented under the
preceding Integrated Growth Pole (PIC) operations (PIC1, PIC2-1 and PIC2-2). The current
project, PIC3-1 (Phase I), will notably support the economic recovery from the COVID-19 crisis,
the expansion of the PIC model to new regions and sectors, while also piloting novel approaches
that if successful can be scaled under the PIC3-2 project.


This project will have the following five (5) components:
Component 1: "Attracting and Retaining Private Investment and Removing Key Constraints to
Support Economic Recovery from the COVID-19 crisis"


The objective of this component is to support policy, administrative and regulatory reforms that
will facilitate investments and business operations in Madagascar, essential for a rapid
economic recovery. Given the uncertainty around the COVID-19 pandemic and to ensure swift
recovery, this component will support the Government of Madagascar to implement investor
retention mechanism, and overall and sector-specific investment promotion strategy. To ensure
private sector engagement and input to the reforms implemented under this component, the

project will use established public-private dialogue platforms, or create ad-hoc working groups
for discussions on the reforms being proposed, and support implementation and communication
of the reforms and activities being undertaken.


Sub-Component 1.1: Attracting and Retaining Private Investment.
The objective of this subcomponent is to support the government’s efforts to retain and expand
existing investments while attracting new ones. This will be achieved by the implementation of
activities that seek to improve the policy and legal framework for investments, strengthening
the coordination and implementation of investment promotion activities, and by expanding the
existing investment retention program.


Sub-component 1.2: Strengthening the business environment to contribute to recovery.
This sub-component will contribute to improving the enabling environment to facilitate business
entry and operation. This will be achieved by supporting activities to: (i) reinforce institutional
capacity and public-private dialogue (PPD) for more effective and conducive policy making; (ii)
streamline, automate (whenever possible) and enhance the transparency of the interactions
between government institutions and businesses , especially firm registration, and the granting
of licenses, permits, approvals and other key documents (such as property registration) needed
for a business to enter and operate in Madagascar; and (iii) expand the outreach of the
commercial justice reforms by building on achievements supported by the previous operations.
The sub-component will finance TA, capacity building, equipment and small works
(improvements to offices and working environment).


Component 2: "Removing Constraints to Private Investment and Enhancing Local Economic
Competitiveness to Support Recovery in Target Regions and Target Sectors".


This component will facilitate local private sector growth through targeted investments in the
target regions in local human capital, institutional capacity, and infrastructure investments, as a
continuation of similar activities undertaken under the PIC2-2.


Subcomponent 2.1 "Technical support to strengthen the competitiveness of value chains in the
targeted regions". This subcomponent will strengthen sector capacity in tourism, agribusiness



                                                                                              Page 8
      The World Bank
      Economic Transformation for Inclusive Growth Project (P174684)


and digital sectors to facilitate recovery and increase competitiveness in the project’s target
regions. In the post-COVID context, this subcomponent will focus for the tourism sector on
providing technical assistance to manage and promote tourism sites; adopting innovative
technologies to increase sector productivity and competitiveness; carrying out skills
development of key sector service providers; carrying out studies to inform local
competitiveness strategies, regulations and policies. Agribusiness sector support will improve
agriculture production through capacity building for farmers and community level agro-
processors ; support research into, and the renewal of, plantation and seed selection to adapt
to local agroecological conditions and climate change; support to the greening of selected
agribusiness value chains; support certification and traceability; and promote training curricula
to fill key skills gaps. For the digital sector, the subcomponent will finance TA, capacity building
and equipment (where needed) to strengthen local and regional level capacity to promote
adoption of digital technologies in the targeted sectors. The project will also support
Madagascar's tourism sector through maintaining intra-regional connectivity by financing of
Eligible Madagascar Airlines Expenditures.


Subcomponent 2.2. "Strengthening local infrastructure to support the growth of target sectors
and business development". This subcomponent will support private sector expansion in the
project sectors through provision of targeted TA, equipment and investing in/leveraging private
investment in small-scale, local infrastructure. These will include activities such as supporting
investments and services, including in logistics infrastructure, key urban and rural roads,
energy and water supply, small port handling equipment and facilities, small scale
infrastructure to improve access to internet for clusters of businesses, warehouse and cold-
storage facilities. Particular emphasis will be placed to increase climate resilience and women
participation in economic activities in the Target Regions. These improvements in local small-
scale infrastructure and services in the target regions will in turn create a more favorable
environment for citizens and tourists and to enhance the attractiveness for investment in the
project sectors


Subcomponent 2.3. "Strengthening local governance to support the growth of target sectors
and business development". Building on previous successful experience under PIC 2.1 and 2.2
to support local governments through activities aimed at strengthening the capacity of

      The World Bank
      Economic Transformation for Inclusive Growth Project (P174684)


Decentralized Territorial Local Authorities (DTLA), this sub-component will finance TA, capacity
building, equipment, and small civil works to: enhance the institutional capacity of local
governments to improve revenue mobilization and public funds management for infrastructure
investments and maintenance (ii) strengthen the coordination capacity of decentralized
technical services (DTS) to provide hands-on and in time advisory support to DTLA, (iii)
elaborate/update regional, intercommunal and commune-level development plans and sectoral
investment strategies, (iv) rehabilitate key local government offices and infrastructures to
improve service delivery; (v) providing financial and technical support for investments in
communes or group of communes to promote the local attractiveness and competitiveness of
targeted value chains. These sets of support will be calibrated to each Region and Commune’s
needs and challenges, starting incrementally in the new project regions.


Component 3: "Supporting SMEs and Entrepreneurship Recovery and Growth in Target Sectors
and Target Regions"


The objective of this third component is to strengthen the capabilities and “COVID-19 recovery”
growth opportunities of agribusiness, tourism and digital SMEs and entrepreneurs in the
project’s target regions through the provision of financial and non-financial support.


Subcomponent 3.1.: "Direct Support to Start-ups and SMEs in target sectors and regions". The
component will provide competitively allocated direct financial and non-financial support
through a tiered and targeted manner to growth-oriented start-ups, microenterprises, and SMEs
in the tourism, digital and agribusiness sectors in the project’s target regions, further building
on existing programs under PIC2.2. The financing and associated TA provided to firms will be
performance-based and enable firms at each stage of development to achieve key milestones
in the recovery, development, acceleration, and growth of their businesses. There will be three
windows of direct support: (i) Start-up / Early stage (Window 1); (ii) Pilot Scale-up stage
(Window 2); (iii) Growth stage Catalytic Grants (Window 3), based on the maturity and growth
stages of the firms. The subcomponent will also seek to leverage additional capital and
investment support, through the piloting of a co-investment mechanism with up to US$5 million
set-aside for coinvesting in growth-oriented SMEs and entrepreneurs under window 2.

Subcomponent 3.2: "Strengthening Firm Capabilities through Ecosystem Upgrading:. This
subcomponent will provide indirect support to firms, through strengthening the quality of the
organizations providing support to entrepreneurs and SMEs, including in the Analamanga region
for digital SMEs, thus also building the sustainability and reach of the overall program over the
medium-term. The subcomponent will notably provide financial and technical support through
provision of competitively allocated performance contracts to these organizations to scale up
the quality of their support to firms. This component will have an explicit gender focus,
including through tailored support and specific programs provided to women-led firms.


Component 4: Project Implementation.


This component will finance the PIU and allow it to implement the project, comply with fiduciary
rules and the environmental and social framework, and fulfill monitoring and evaluation (M&E)
commitments. Specifically, it will fund: (a) the continued operation of the National Project
Secretariat PIU based in Antananarivo, as well as decentralized technical units in the project’s
Target Regions; (b) the SME and Entrepreneurship Unit Coordinator under the Secretariat of the
PIU; (c) the project’s M&E system; and (d) the preparation and implementation of all ESF
processes and documentation.


Component 5: Contingent Emergency Response Component


A “zero-dollar” contingent emergency response component (CERC) will allow for the reallocation
of financing to provide immediate response to an eligible crisis or emergency, when/if needed.
In the event the component triggered, a CERC manual will be developed for activities under this
component , detailing streamlined FM, procurement, safeguard, and any other necessary
implementation arrangements. In the event the component is triggered, the Results Framework
would be revised through formal restructuring to include appropriate indicators related to the
emergency response activities.
