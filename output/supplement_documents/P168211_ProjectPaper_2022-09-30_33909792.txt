I. PROGRAM STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. PROGRAM STATUS


1.      Implementation progress. The ratings towards achievement of the Project Development
Objective (PDO) and Overall Implementation Progress are rated as Moderately Satisfactory given the
overall impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the financial standing of Barqi Tojik (BT). An Additional
Financing (AF) and restructuring for the Program was approved by the Board on June 30, 2022, adding
47 Disbursement Linked Results (DLRs) bringing the total number of DLRs to 96. Out of the 96 (ninety-
six) DLRs, 10 (ten) have been achieved, 4 (four) are under review, and 13 (thirteen) are expected to be
completed by the end of December 31, 2022. The remaining 69 (sixty-nine) DLRs are expected to be
achieved at various times during the project implementation period. The overall disbursement rate
(including original Grant and AF) is 29 percent and will reach over 45 percent by January 30, 2023, if the
Recipient meets the agreed DLR deadlines. The Financing Agreement for the AF has been countersigned
on September 22, 2022, and effectiveness is expected within 6 months of countersignature. The
effectiveness conditions are: (i) Subsidiary Agreement has been revised and executed on behalf of the
Recipient and BT; and (ii) Shabakahoi Intiqoli Barq (SIB) – the national electricity transmission company,
and Shabakahoi Taqsimoti Barq (STB) - the national electricity distribution company - have each entered
into an Implementation Agreement with the Recipient and BT.

2.      The Program has made progress with implementation of the policy actions and other operational
and technical measures to improve the financial viability, increase the reliability of electricity supply,
and strengthen corporate governance resulting in: (i) successful completion of the unbundling process
of BT into BT, SIB and STB and establishing Supervisory Boards and Audit Committees at SIB and STB;
(ii) adequate purchases of electricity from and payments to Sangtuda-1 Hydropower Plant (HPP); (iii)
reduction of power outages supported by timely repair and maintenance of the T&D assets; and (iv)
revisions drafted for the Group 2 Subsidiary Agreements (SA) between BT and MOF to convert into BT’s
equity the outstanding balance of loans under target SA and the associated fines.

3.     The Government decided to update its program during implementation, mainly due to the
complex process of BT’s unbundling, the macroeconomic impacts of COVID-19, and the spillover effects
of the ongoing war in Ukraine (see Annex 3). This resulted in the AF under which the Bank also
restructured the original project to reflect the updated government program. The main changes
included (i) extension of the closing date of the project from August 30, 2026, to August 31, 2032; (ii)
changes in the results framework, target dates of indicators and the DLRs to allow sufficient time for
their completion; (iii) addition of two new DLIs (DLI 10 and 11) aimed at optimization of electricity
generation costs and liabilities, to achieve financial viability earlier, i.e. by 2030; and (iv) SIB and STB
entering into project implementation agreements with BT through MEWR and MoF.

4.     The summary of the progress of achievement of DLRs (and associated delays) is provided as
follows:

a.   Cost-recovery tariffs and tariff adjustments: The dates for the achievement of the DLRs
     related to tariff increase (DLR 1.1), annual adjustment of generation, transmission,
     distribution and end-user tariffs (DLR 1.2); approval of the Electricity Tariff Paper, and
     establishment of a Tariff Unit (DLR 1.7) were extended through a restructuring at the time
     of Board approval of the AF to allow more time to complete the allocation of the fixed
     assets among BT, SIB, and STB that would allow computation of tariffs for each separate
     energy company. The distribution of assets and separation of accounts between the three
     companies (BT, SIB and STB) have been finalized and the Government approved a Decree
     (#449, dated August 31, 2022) on tariff increase (weighted average by 17%) for power
     generation, transmission and end-users to be effective from October 1, 2022. One more
     Government Decree is expected to be issued on approval of the Electricity Tariff Paper to
     be prepared based on the new tariffs by September 30, 2022. This DLR (DLR 1.7) would
     need to be revised with the deadline extended to December 31, 2022, to allow for adequate
     time to ensure its completion.

b.   Revision of the Subsidiary Agreements (SAs): The revision of 28 Group 2 SAs between BT
     and MoF has been finalized and all related amendments and modifications following the
     clauses in their respective Financing Agreements have been approved by their respective
     IFIs to align the terms of these SAs with the terms in the respective legal agreements
     between MoF and IFIs. The revised SAs are currently being reviewed by MoF. Two
     associated DLRs (DLRs 2.6 and 2.8), which were due by September 30, 2022, have been
     delayed and will require additional time to be completed. Thus, in order to give more time
     for their completion they are being extended to December 31, 2022.

c.   Reduction of BT cash deficit: The reduction of 7% in 2020 and 22% in 2021 in BT’s cash
     deficit (DLRs 3.1 and 3.2) was not possible due to the COVID-19 impacts and other
     constraints including: (i) lower collection rates and non-payments from Afghanistan for
     electricity exports; (ii) depreciation of the local currency against the US dollar that increased
     the amount of BT's US dollar-denominated commercial loans and liabilities to Sangtuda-1
     HPP; and (iii) domestic tariffs not being increased in 2020-2021. The Government increased
     the average tariff in September 2022 (effective from October 1, 2022), which will
     significantly improve the sector’s cash flows, especially if accompanied by the revision of
     the on-lending terms under the SAs above, which in turn will further reduce the debt service
     costs of the sector and free up additional cash for repayment of BT’s overdue liabilities. The
     associated DLR for the year 2022 is expected to be achieved by December 31, 2022 (DLR
     3.1).

d.   Preparation of a Generation expansion plan (GEP): While significant progress has been
     made with preparation of the GEP, some delays have materialized due to the process of
     harmonization and approval of assumptions and scenarios. For this reason, the associated
     DLR (DLR 7.1) is now expected to be completed by December 31, 2022 (a two-month delay).

               adequate with delays caused primarily by COVID-19. As mentioned above, additional
               attention is needed to improve the Fiduciary Systems. Nevertheless, elements of good-
               practice corporate governance were introduced with internal audit functions improved
               and corporate-level environmental policies adopted at BT, SIB, and STB.

5.      The Program has no overdue audit reports. Financial management and procurement
performance of the Program in the latest ISR is rated as Moderately Satisfactory due to (i) lack of
sufficient evidence on implementation progress of procurement actions as defined in the PAP requiring
further improvement in the bidding documents and creation of a complaint monitoring section at the
relevant websites of BT, SIB and STB to share information on handling procurement complaints; and
(ii) lower disbursements caused by external shocks delaying the achievement of some of the
disbursement linked results. The rating for DLIs is Moderately Unsatisfactory as the finalization of some
of the targets to be achieved by the end of September 2022 will not be met.



B. RATIONAL FOR RESTRUCTURING

6.       The Financing Agreement for the AF was signed on September 22, 2022. The project is expected
to be ratified and declared effective within six months. However, there are seven DLRs which will not
be achieved by their due dates, mainly because of the lengthy processes required by several institutions
and bodies to review the associated documents before issuing their approval, requiring more time than
initially expected. The affected 7 (seven) DLRs are: DLRs 8.1 and 8.2 (due date June 30, 2022); DLRs 1.7,

         time for their achievement.
