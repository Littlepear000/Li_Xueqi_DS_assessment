  I. BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING

   A. Introduction

       1. This Project Paper seeks the approval of the Executive Directors to provide an additional grant of
       Special Drawing Rights (SDR) 3.5 million (US$5.0 million equivalent) to be financed through the Crisis
       Response Window (Fast Track COVID-19 1 Facility) to the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) for the
       South Sudan Provision of Essential Health Services Project (PEHSP) (P168926). The proposed Additional
       Financing (AF) will replenish the US$5.0 million which was reallocated from Component 1 to PEHSP’s
       Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC) on April 6, 2020 for the COVID-19 response in South
       Sudan. As the proposed AF aims to fill the financing gap created by the activation of the COVID-19 CERC, the
       same implementation arrangements for the parent project approved by the Board of Executive Directors
       will apply, without any additional or new activities to those originally planned. In addition to the AF, the
       project will also be restructured to: (i) modify the Project Development Objective (PDO) and Results
       Framework to reflect the CERC activation responses to Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) in September 2019 and
       COVID-19 in April 2020; and (ii) allocate the US$5 million in funding to Component 1 to replenish the CERC
       for COVID-19 under the Fast Track COVID-19 Facility.

       2. The PEHSP was approved on February 27, 2019 in the amount of SDR 75.9 million (US$105.4 million
       equivalent), including SDR 52.8 million (US$73.4 million equivalent) to UNICEF and SDR 23.1 million (US$32
       million equivalent) to the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC). The project became effective on
       March 11, 2019 and is expected to close on December 31, 2021. The original PDO is to increase access to an
       essential package of health services in the Republic of South Sudan, with a particular focus on the former
       states of Upper Nile and Jonglei. As of December 16, 2020, US$94.8 million was disbursed representing
       90.33 percent of the total funds.

       3. The project supports strengthening South Sudan’s health system by responding to critical needs
       including support to delivering health services in some of the most challenging environments in the country,
       strengthening public health emergency preparedness and response systems, and contributing to building a
       more robust and harmonized national monitoring and evaluation system. It includes four components: (1)
       Delivery of Essential Health Services (US$93.0 million equivalent); (2) Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning
       (US$4.0 million equivalent); (3) Emergency Preparedness and Response (US$3.0 million equivalent); and (4)
       Repayment of Project Preparation Advances (US$5.4 million equivalent). Although the project has a focus
       on the states of Upper Nile and Jonglei, it has built the maximum flexibility for a wider geographical coverage
       given the evolving situation.

       4. Project performance. Progress towards achievement of the PDO and overall implementation progress
       have both been rated Satisfactory for the last 12 months over the past three Implementation Status and
       Results Reports (ISR) (Nov. 2019 – Nov. 2020). Combining results reported from both implementing
       agencies, UNICEF and ICRC, all nine PDO indicators are either achieved or on track to be achieved, indicating
       that the PDO is likely to be met by the project’s Closing Date. Further, performance in financial management
       (FM), project management and procurement are all rated Satisfactory. The project has faced
       implementation challenges, particularly due to insecurity. The challenges include: (i) security incidents in

       5. The CERC activation and progress. The first CERC activation was on September 9, 2019 to reallocate
       US$7.9 million from Component 1 to Subcomponent 3.2 to support EVD preparedness activities. The second
       CERC activation was on April 6, 2020 to reallocate US$5.0 million from Component 1 to Subcomponent 3.2
       and reprogram US$2.6 million remaining from the previously triggered CERC for EVD preparedness towards
       the COVID-19 response (total of US$7.6 million). The CERC was activated to support the National COVID-19
       Preparedness and Response Plan in coordination with other development partners. World Bank support has
       been focusing on the following pillars (i) Risk communication and community engagement (RCCE); (ii)
       Infection Prevention and Control (IPC); (iii) Points of Entry (POE); and (iv) Operations and procurement. This
       support was implemented by UNICEF given their important role in the National Task Force and its technical
       working group (TWG). As of November 19, 2020, the allocated amount of US$7.6 million for COVID-19 was
       fully utilized on the planned activities.

       6. The overall risk rating is High. The residual risk rating for all risk categories is High as of the most recent
       ISR (November 2020) of the parent project and has been since project approval. This project supports health
       services delivery in one of the most challenging fragile, conflict and violence (FCV) contexts in the world and
       the security environment remains highly unstable and unpredictable.

     B. Rationale for Additional Financing

       7. South Sudan is facing a major health emergency. An outbreak of COVID-19 caused by the 2019 novel
       coronavirus (SARS-CoV-2) has been spreading rapidly across the world since December 2019, following the
       diagnosis of the initial cases in Wuhan, Hubei Province, China. On March 11, 2020, the World Health
       Organization (WHO) declared a global pandemic as the coronavirus rapidly spread across the world. As of
       January 7, 2021, the outbreak had resulted in an estimated 87 million cases and more than 1.8 million deaths
       in 218 countries and territories. 2 COVID-19 is one of several emerging infectious disease outbreaks that have
       emerged from animals in contact with humans, resulting in a major pandemic with significant public health
       and economic impacts. In response, South Sudan developed a National COVID-19 Preparedness and
       Response Plan, in accordance with WHO guidelines, with the support of development partners. This first

       plan was finalized and endorsed by the Ministry of Health (MOH) on March 24, 2020. Its total preliminary
       budget was US$10.9 million, with contributions mobilized from the government and other development
       partners.

       8. The number of COVID-19 cases in South Sudan have been increasing quickly. Since the first confirmed
       case was reported in South Sudan on April 5, 2020 the trajectory of cases took a steep upward trend despite
       the limited testing capacity with the number of cases reaching 3,589 as of January 7, 2021.3 The National
       Steering Committee and its TWGs have been updating the National COVID-19 Response Plan to cater to the
       needs of a country with an already ailing health system. In response, the government adopted the following
       measures in April 2020, among others: (i) suspension of travel to and from other countries except for certain
       flights; (ii) closure of schools and non-essential businesses; (iii) limited movement from 7pm to 6am; (iv)
       communication campaign on social distancing and hand washing; and (v) limiting of mass gathering
       including in churches and mosques. These measures were gradually eased by the government since July
       2020. The spread of the epidemic in countries with close trade ties with South Sudan and the restriction of
       travel to such countries, including Uganda, have caused an economic and food crisis for South Sudanese.

       9. In response to the Government of South Sudan’s request for the World Bank’s support to the National
       COVID-19 Response Plan, the World Bank activated the CERC, in an amount of US$5 million, on April 6,
       2020. The World Bank reviewed the Government’s request for activating the CERC and supporting
       documentation (i.e. Emergency Operational Manual for the CERC, Action Plan/CERC Implementation Plan
       (CERIP), updated ESMF, Procurement Plan) and provided approval on April 6, 2020. The CERC finances a
       subset of the National COVID-19 Response Plan on the basis of a positive list in the CERC operational manual.
       Table 1 provides a summary of the COVID-19 activities supported by the project under the CERC. The
       detailed CERIP is in Annex 1.

       10. The CERIP was completed and approved two weeks after the endorsement of the COVID-19 National
       Response Plan. The World Bank responded swiftly to provide financial support in order to ensure timely
       preparedness and response to COVID-19. The COVID-19 response plan included the following pillars: (i)
       Country Level Coordination, Planning and Monitoring; (ii) Risk Communication and Community
       Engagement; (iii) Surveillance, Rapid Response and Case Investigation; (iv) Points of Entry; (v) National
       Laboratories; (vi) Infection Prevention and Control; (vii) Case management; and (viii) Operational support
       and logistics. Development and resource partners such as the Office of U.S. Foreign Disaster Assistance,
       European Civil Protection and Humanitarian Aid Operations, U.K. Department for International
       Development, European Union and GAVI have contributed towards the pillars of this plan.

       11. World Bank support to the COVID-19 response was implemented by UNICEF. As outlined in the
       national response plan, the major pillars supported are: Risk Communication, Community Engagement,
       Social Mobilization and Psychosocial Support (RCCESMPS), IPC and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH),
       POE Agreement, and Operations and Logistics. UNICEF co-led the RCCESMPS and the IPC TWGs at national
       level, supporting coordination of partners, finalization of guidelines, harmonization of training materials and
       coordination of activity implementation. Specific activities to operationalize the national plan, executed by
       UNICEF included: (i) engaging seven non-governmental organizations (NGOs) to support scale up IPC/WASH
       activities in at-risk communities and prioritized health facilities in seven priority towns; (ii) supporting 250

       13. This AF replenishes the funds reallocated to the COVID-19 CERC to enable continuation of PEHSP and
       ensure that originally planned activities will be adequately financed and implemented. Specifically, the
       CERC activation for COVID-19 response created a financing gap for principal activities under Component 1.
       Without replenishing the funds, the financing gap would affect the delivery of the following essential health
       services: (i) Maternal Neonatal and Child health services (ii) Basic and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric
       and Newborn Care (iii) Sexual and Gender-based Violence (SGBV) services (iv) Procurement and distribution
       of medical and non-medical supplies (v) Emergency Preparedness and Response (vi) Disease Surveillance
       and Outbreak Response and (vii) Quality improvement and supervision. This AF will close the financing gap
       under Component 1 to ensure the achievement of the PDO.

       14. Triggering Paragraph 12, Section III of the World Bank Investment Project Financing (IPF) Policy. Since
       funds for this AF are being sourced from the Fast Track COVID-19 Facility , paragraph 12, Section III of the
       World Bank IPF Policy has been triggered to allow processing of the AF following the condensed procedures
       for situations of urgent need as part of the emergency response to the COVID-19 pandemic.

  II. DESCRIPTION OF ADDITIONAL FINANCING

       15. This AF will help fill the financing gap created by triggering the CERC related to COVID-19 support.
       Specifically, the AF will support the replenishment of the project funds to ensure some critical originally
       planned activities are financed and implemented. Through this AF, financing will be provided back to
       Component 1 that was used to finance the CERC activation and COVID-19 emergency response. No changes
       to the activities or the implementation arrangements are envisaged. However, a financing gap still remains
       related to the amount allocated from Component 1 to the EVD CERC in September 2019.

       16. A project restructuring is also proposed. This is to: (i) modify the PDO and Results Framework to reflect
       the CERC activation responses to EVD in September 2020 and COVID-19 in April 2020; (ii) allocate the US$5
       million in funding to Component 1 to replenish the CERC for COVID-19 under the Fast Track COVID-19

       Facility; and (iii) reallocate an amount of US$1.169 million from Component 2 to Component 1.1 based on
       a revised budgeting. Further details are provided below:

            i.    Revision of the PDO. The PDO of the original project does not reflect the CERC activities. Hence,
                  the PDO will be amended as follows: “to increase access to an essential package of health services
                  and respond to health emergencies in the Republic of South Sudan, with a particular focus on the
                  former states of Upper Nile and Jonglei.”

            ii.   Modification of the Results Framework of the project. The results framework will be adjusted to

                  a beneficiary feedback indicator has been added to the results framework.

  III. KEY RISKS

       17. The overall risk rating is High. This operation supports health services delivery in one of the most
       challenging FCV contexts in the world. The project targets some of South Sudan’s most conflict affected

       areas and is implemented in one of the riskiest contexts in the World Bank’s portfolio. Although the signed
       peace agreement and the transitional government have resulted in some gains in stability, the security
       environment remains highly unstable and unpredictable. This results in greater risks than those found in
       non-FCV environments. More specifically, the acuteness of the violence and instability set South Sudan
       apart from other FCV environments with even greater levels of risk.

       18. There are significant risks to implementing a project in South Sudan, as evidenced from the ongoing
       PEHSP and the previous South Sudan Health Rapid Results Project (P127187). Throughout the
       implementation period of these projects, a number of facilities were attacked and looted leading to deaths
       of patients and health workers. These risks remain a reality for the World Bank and partner agencies
       providing support to health services across South Sudan, particularly in the two states of Upper Nile and
       Jonglei. Every effort has been made, however, to mitigate potential risks through the recent update of the
       security management plan of PEHSP along with its implementation arrangements.

       19. Political and governance risks are High. The Report of the Commission on Human Rights in South Sudan
       from February 2018 highlights atrocities including widespread violence against civilians nationwide. Thirty-
       three new armed groups, currently estimated at 40, continue to emerge, mainly as a result of the spread of
       the conflict to the Equatorias and the northern part of Upper Nile State. The Revitalized Peace Agreement
       signed in September 2018 along with the recently formed transitional government, provides an opportunity
       for potential progress, yet the effects of the protracted conflict still affect the majority of the country’s
       population. The intercommunal clashes in Jonglei resulted in a number of causalities under PEHSP,
       evacuation of health workers, and suspension of project disbursements or activities in some occasions. The
       Security and Significant Events Management Framework (SSEMF) was developed to help mitigate security
       risks to project implementation.

       20. Macroeconomic risks are High. South Sudan’s economy has been hit hard by the triple effect of COVID-
       19, deteriorating oil prices, and the locust infestation. South Sudan has one of the least diversified
       economies in the world, a result of being extremely oil dependent. This has made the sector mainly
       depending on the donor funding with irregular payment of salaries to civil servants including health workers.
       To mitigate the risk of irregular health worker payments from the Government, most NGOs hire health
       workers who they pay regularly. In addition, the World Bank and UNICEF engage in discussions with the
       Government on ensuring timely and regular payments of health worker salaries.

       21. The residual risk rating for Sector Strategies and Policies is substantial. South Sudan has a number of
       health sector strategies and policies, however full implementation of these is beyond the capacity of the
       Government as well as available resources. Key strategies include: South Sudan’s Basic Package of Health
       and Nutrition Services in Primary Health Care (BHNPS-PHC), which and presents a broad health service
       package; the National Action Plan for Health Security (NAPHS 2020-2024), which captures health security
       priorities through an ambitious agenda; and The South Sudan Health Systems Stabilization and Recovery
       Plan (HSSRP) 2020-2022, which was adopted as part of the Country’s peace agreement. The HSSRP’s
       ambitious nature within a short timeline and limited resources leave it unlikely to be fully implemented. To
       mitigate the unachievable nature of these strategies, given available resources, the PEHSP utilizes a
       prioritized package of health services based on the plans, primarily the BHNPS-PHC, aligned with the
       package of health services agreed with the Health Pool Fund who are providing health services in the other
       eight states of the country and the MOH.

           Climate vulnerabilities, mitigation solutions and adaptation measures

       33. The parent project was screened for climate change during its preparation. The overall assessment of
       potential risks in the Summary Climate and Disaster Risk Screening Report was “high” due to extreme
       precipitation and flooding, as well as drought. These remain likely to impact target beneficiaries in the
       project’s locations. Women and children were identified as particularly vulnerable to impacts from climate
       change. South Sudan in general, and the states of Upper Nile and Jonglei in particular, have both dry and
       rainy seasons. During the dry season, temperatures can become extreme and affect mobility and operation
       of both service providers and target beneficiaries. Conversely, during the rainy season there is extreme
       precipitation and flooding which affects accessibility to health service providers. These increases in rainfall
       and flooding may encourage the range and prevalence of infectious disease vectors including malaria. Of
       note, extreme weather patterns are undermining already vulnerable livelihoods and existing coping
       strategies, negatively affecting food security and nutrition of the communities in the catchment areas.

       34. The AF intends to address these vulnerabilities through the same mitigation and adaptation measures
       followed by the parent project. The excessive reliance on fixed health facilities combined with the low
       density of these, together with the low numbers of qualified health workers, presents a significant challenge
       to service delivery. These challenges are exacerbated by seasonal flooding and droughts, which together
       with conflict, has led to an increase in displaced peoples and refugees. The AF will implement mitigation

       solutions to this climate challenge by strengthening outreach activities, particularly during the dry season.
       In addition, by strengthening the accessibility of primary health care services as well as delivery pathway to
       secondary health care. The estimated savings will be significant. Further mitigation benefits will be realized
       through improved procurement practices that will seek to reduce the embedded carbon in medications and
       other medical supplies. In terms of adaptation measures the AF will continue to provide access to health
       service in areas with intermittent periods of access, due to drought and floods, the project will increase the
       resilience of community’s both in terms of the direct provision of health services including nutrition as well
       as improving the wider environmental and social determinants of health.

          Annex 2: World Bank Group Response to COVID-19 in South Sudan

                  I.    Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the country and government response

          1.      The COVID-19 crisis has had significant health impacts for South Sudan. Since the first confirmed
          case was reported in South Sudan on April 5, 2020, the trajectory of cases took an upward trend despite
          the very limited testing capacity with the number of cases exceeding 3,000 as of December 2020. 6 In
          addition to direct impacts, there is also substantial evidence that the pandemic has resulted in reduced
          health seeking behavior, including fewer outpatient consultations, child immunization and reduced
          antenatal care usage by pregnant women. Travel restrictions put in place to limit the disease’s spread
          created delays in supply and staff movements, inhibiting health service delivery and hampered the
          delivery of the humanitarian program. Measures to prepare health facilities to prevent the spread of cases
          and treat COVID-19 patients further affected the fragile health system.

          2.      The Government has taken measures to limit the spread of COVID-19. The government adopted
          measures in March 2020, most notably: (i) suspension of travel to and from other countries except for
          certain flights; (ii) closure of schools and non-essential businesses; (iii) limited movement from 7pm to
          6am; (iv) communication campaign on social distancing and hand washing; (v) limiting of mass gathering
          including in churches and mosques; (vi) launching a risk communication and education campaign on
          prevention of COVID-19 including where to seek help, social distancing, and hand washing, as well as the
          launch of the 6666 COVID-19 helpline; (vii) imposition of a 14 day quarantine on travelers between states
          and travelers coming from outside the country; and (viii) reduction of the number of passengers in public
          vehicles to half the vehicle’s capacity. Enforcement of the measures was poor, and the Government
          gradually eased the measures since July 2020. On the economic front, the authorities took action to
          reduce both the Bank of South Sudan (BoSS) refinancing Rate (15 percent to 13 percent) and Reserve
          Requirement Ratio (20 percent to 18 percent) in April 2020. Additional measures reduced the BoSS Rate
          by a further 3 percentage points, down to 10 percent, and suspended the regulation of higher minimum
          paid-up capital for commercial banks 7. In addition, South Sudan has created two special committees to
          oversee critical economic and public financial management reforms in response to COVID-19.

          3.       Notwithstanding the Government’s response, the pandemic, associated containment
          measures, and global downturn have had severe economic impacts. COVID-19 effects have been
          transmitted through lower oil prices, an increase of the prices of basic commodities due to trade
          disruptions, lower remittances receipts, and restrictions on humanitarian operations on the ground. The
          spread of the epidemic in countries with close trade ties with South Sudan and the restriction of travel to
          such countries, particularly Uganda, have had a significance impact. Following a rebound in FY2019/20
          with real Gross Domestic Product (GDP) growth estimated at 9.3 percent in FY2019/20, growth projections
          for FY2020/21 are that the economy will contract by -3.4 percent. The oil sector is expected to contract
          by -5.8 percent with production declining to 58.4 million barrels in FY2020/21 from 62.1 million barrels in
          FY2019/20 as COVID-19 restrictions impacted movement of machinery and Organization of Petroleum
          Exporting Countries (OPEC+) production cuts affected production. With COVID-19 restrictions delaying
          new investment, activity in the oil sector is not expected to improve until FY2022/23, at which time oil

          4.       Limited data points to COVID-19 having had a significant impact on households. Rapid phone
          surveys conducted by the World Bank in June 2020 indicate lower purchasing power and a likely increase
          in poverty and vulnerability as 52 percent of respondents reported losing either some or all their income
          from their main income source post-COVID. In addition, 46 percent were unable to buy their main staple
          foods due to lack of money (44 percent) or higher food prices (11 percent) and 42 percent of domestic
          remittance-receiving households reported a decline, with 16 percent reporting a cessation of this income
          source. Over 80 percent of people surveyed reported that they or someone in their household were
          worried about food security due to lack of income. The reduction in household and business income as
          well as remittances lead to a projected increase in the poverty rate to 82 percent in FY21 from 76 percent
          in FY19 (at $1.90 per person per day).

          5.       An already difficult fiscal situation has worsened owing to COVID-19 impacts. Government
          revenues as a ratio to GDP in FY20/21 are estimated to decline by about 15 percent relative to the pre-
          pandemic projections, following the decline in both oil prices and oil production. Combined with a
          significant reduction in available financing, this would contribute to a fiscal financing gap of about 4
          percent of GDP. The impact of the pandemic has created immediate and large external financing needs.
          An urgent balance-of-payments need of US$272 million, or 6.4 percent of GDP, is expected in FY20/21. At
          the same time, the budget process has been significantly delayed and the draft budget includes a cash
          deficit of 13 percent of GDP despite the authorities’ commitment to contain the fiscal deficit in FY20/21
          at 2.5 percent of GDP. Monetization of the fiscal deficit resulted in sharp exchange rate depreciation
          leading to a surge in inflation. South Sudan faces significant weaknesses with the availability of debt data
          and has not participated in the Debt Servicing Suspension Initiative. As an expected recipient of an IDA
          Remaining Engaged in Conflict Allocation (RECA), South Sudan has not been required to submit
          performance and policy actions under the Sustainable Development Finance Policy.

                  II.   World Bank Group Programming Response to the COVID-19 Crisis

          6.       The World Bank responded quickly with adjustments to the portfolio to better support South
          Sudan deal with the impacts of COVID-19. The WBG’s strategy outlined in its Country Engagement Note
          had focused on resilience and was therefore smoothly adapted to addressing COVID-19 impacts. A CERC
          of the PEHSP was triggered in May 2020 to provide emergency support to the National COVID-19
          Response Plan. Combined with accessing financing from the Pandemic Emergency Financing Facility, a
          total of US$7.6 million financing was provided for activities including risk communication, community
          engagement, infection prevention controls at points of entry, and logistics and operations for
          procurement of required items. The US$40 million South Sudan Safety Nets Project (SSSNP; P169274),
          approved in April 2020, adjusted its implementation plan to follow a phased approach, initially prioritizing
          Juba in recognition of the increasing and deepening socio-economic vulnerabilities of the poor urban
          households. Requirements for public works have been waived to avoid crowding and physical interaction
          and additional activities, particularly related to WASH and nutrition messaging, have been enhanced with
          COVID-19-related risk prevention guidance. While these two operations are implemented via third

          7.      The World Bank’s technical advisory services and analytics (ASA) have also been adjusted in
          response to COVID-19. In light of South Sudan’s still weak systems for data, statistics, and evidence-based
          policymaking, the World Bank has carried out a series of high frequency phone surveys to monitor the
          impact of the current COVID-19 crisis on households and businesses. The World Bank has also focused its
          macroeconomic monitoring on impacts of COVID-19. ASA in the education, health, and social protection
          sectors have included a focus on the implications of COVID-19 for service delivery. Finally, on the basis of
          renewed demand from the Government, the World Bank has increased technical support for the
          development of core governance systems, particularly public financial management. These ASA will also
          contribute to strengthening policies and institutions to help the Government address the COVID-19 crisis.

          8.      The World Bank’s lending pipeline is also aligned to respond to the COVID-19 health impacts
          and build resilience. The US$45 million Enhancing Community Resilience and Local Governance Project
          (P169949), which had been under preparation prior to the pandemic and was approved in July 2020,
          focuses on support for local infrastructure, including for WASH. The South Sudan Emergency Locust
          Response Project (P174546) is being prepared for Q3 FY21 under the Emergency Locust Response Multi-
          Phase Approach (MPA) that includes social protection measures which will assist rural households facing
          shocks owing to the locust infestation as well as COVID-19. The pipeline for FY21-22 includes further
          additional financing to the PEHSP, including compensating for the financing utilized by the CERC (and
          financed by the COVID-19 Emergency Response MPA), as well as additional financing for the South Sudan
          Safety Nets Project (P169274). Additional Financing for the PEHSP is expected to include improved
          emergency preparedness as well as participation in the planned supplemental COVID-19 MPA for
          vaccines. A World Bank Group Country Engagement Note for South Sudan is under preparation with
          expected approval in Q3 FY21 laying out a COVID-19 responsive program.

          9.      The World Bank’s efforts are part of a broad development partner effort to support South
          Sudan’s response to COVID-19 impacts. The World Bank’s US$190 million operational portfolio and ASA
          provide an important complement to other development and especially humanitarian agencies active in
          South Sudan. World Bank activities are particularly valued for providing medium to long term perspective
          to other agencies along the humanitarian-development nexus. With respect to health impacts, the PEHSP
          is coordinated with analogous service provision financed by the United Kingdom to provide for country-
          wide support. The SSSNP is coordinated with analogous cash and in-kind transfer as well as workfare
          programs supported by agencies from the United Nations system, particularly the World Food Program,
          and international NGOs. The World Bank is coordinating particularly closely with the International
          Monetary Fund (IMF) in providing technical assistance on the public financial management agenda. In
          addition, the IMF has provided US$52 million in budget support to the Government of South Sudan
          through its Rapid Credit Facility.
