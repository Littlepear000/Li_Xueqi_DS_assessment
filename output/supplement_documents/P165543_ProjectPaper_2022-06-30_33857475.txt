    I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

Introduction

1.      This Project Paper seeks the approval of the Country Director to make changes to the Institutional
Strengthening for Improved Village Service Delivery (P165543), IBRD Loan No. 8941-ID. The restructuring will: (i)
modify the results framework to include revision of targets for select indicators, (ii) drop Component 3: Village
Performance Awards, as the Government will implement and finance it through their own mechanism, separate from
the project intervention, (iii) modify selected scope of activities in Components 1,2 and 4, (iv) initiate a partial
cancellation in an amount of US$49.98 million, (v) update the implementation arrangements to add the Directorate
of Regional Administration, as a new Project Implementing Unit (PIU), (vi) modify costs between components and the
disbursement schedule, and (vii) reallocate proceeds between disbursement categories. This is the first project
restructuring.

Background and rationale for project financing

2.        In 2014, the Government of Indonesia (GoI) passed the landmark Village Law (Law No. 6 of 2014),
significantly increasing fiscal resources for development and community empowerment in Indonesia’s 74,954 rural
villages. Between 2014 and 2018 annual fiscal transfers to villages increased from approximately IDR 16.8 trillion ($1.2
billion) to IDR 119 trillion ($8.4 billion). The fiscal transfers are large and in 2018 they accounted for approximately six
percent of the national budget, and around 0.5 percent of GDP. On average, a village now receives approximately IDR
1.6 billion ($113,500) every year, although the amount on a per capita basis varies significantly because the allocation
formula includes a large, fixed allocation and village population sizes vary greatly.1 The new Village Law also expanded
the roles and responsibilities of villages in relation to village administration, infrastructure, basic service delivery and
community empowerment.

3.       The central government, as well as provincial and district governments, realize villages require support to
maximize the development impact of the Village Law. Since 2015, the central as well as provincial and district
governments all contribute to financing various programs and systems to improve coordination, village
administration, village development and community empowerment, and accountability. At project appraisal, the
quality of these support systems was assessed to largely be in place, but they were costly, their effectiveness was
mixed, and the efficiency of these systems was low. The four core areas of support included village administration,
village development and community empowerment, inter-village development, and accountability—which are the
responsibility of three main ministries: the Ministry of Home Affairs (MoHA), MoV, and the Ministry of Finance (MoF).
One overall weakness of the support systems is that they make little use of new technologies to increase the efficiency
and effectiveness of capacity building, knowledge sharing and accountability. It was also deemed critical that the
support systems more systematically help districts to deliver capacity building and empowerment to villages and
community organizations. Key district government entities involved in implementing the Village Law include: (a) the

Department of Community and Village Empowerment (DPMD), under MoV, which is responsible for village support

or fiscal transfers to effectively manage these responsibilities.2 Finally, national coordination of the village support
system continues to be weak. Current systems for national coordination are ad hoc and lack the institutional mandate
to effectively enforce decision-making.

4.       In 2019, the World Bank approved the Institutional Strengthening for Improved Village Service Delivery
project. The project’s development objective is to “strengthen institutional capacity for improved quality of spending
in participating villages.” This will be achieved through investments that improve and modernize the Government’s
own systems and institutions that build capacity of village institutions, enhancements to and integration of
information systems, and aligning these with fiscal transfers to focus on performance and results. The project
implementation spans across five ministries. The Ministry of Home Affairs is the Executing Agency, with the Ministry
of Villages, National Planning and Development Agency, Ministry of Finance and Coordinating Ministry of Human
Development as implementing agencies under the project.

Rationale for Restructuring.

5.        Since project approval, several factors have contributed to a change in the operating environment of the
country. In August 2019, the country held national elections, with subsequent delays emerging with the establishment
of a new government. As a result of the elections, several Ministries saw both institutional restructuring of
departments and mandates, as well as widespread new staff appointments. For example, after the election, the
Ministry of Villages reorganized all its Directorates with new names and mandates and appointed new staff to Echelon
1 and 2 levels. Kemenko PMK also saw internal reshuffling of mandates under Directorates, and a subsequent merging
of Village Law coordination mandates with others. All four implementing ministries saw appointments of new
Ministers, where it took time to brief incoming Ministers, advisory staff, and newly appointed staff on the program,
its objectives and design. Both Kemenko PMK and Bappenas Ministers indicated that their activity contributions would
initially come from the Government budget (Rupiah Murni), although recently Kemenko PMK has advised that it has
obtained approval to utilize the loan proceeds for its activities. In MOHA, a series of new appointments and staff
turnover in Bina Pemdes also led to delays because of briefings to new staff on the program objectives, activities and
MOHA’s role in coordinating this cross ministerial program.

6.       COVID-19 Pandemic. In March 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic broke out in Indonesia. The Government of
Indonesia acted quickly to reallocate budget towards combatting the spread of the virus and to supporting economic
fallout from widespread containment measures. While overall national budget allocations (Dana Desa) remained
unchanged (at around IDR 70 trillion, or US$5 billion), the Government announced new measures and priorities for
Village Fund use, including a new cash transfer program to be financed from village budgets (Bantuan Langsung Tunai

Desa, or BLT Desa). The introduction of this program meant both MOHA and MOV were busy with amending the village
chart of accounts and issuing relevant regulations and guidelines for rapid implementation. During much of 2020, the
Government counterparts were occupied with the response to the pandemic, including shifting priorities towards

pandemic, the Government is increasingly looking to the role that villages will play in the economic recovery. The
Government and key implementing agencies MOHA and MOV are expected to play a key role in improving data and
monitoring systems for villages, as well as to step up support services for local economic development, ensure the
inclusive recovery for all, and continue to improve utilization and quality of spending of the village funds. These
emerging priorities are in line with activities that are being financed under the loan, albeit some adjustments and
modifications will be required to more fully align these with the current set of government priorities.

7.      Proposed changes for restructuring. Based on the above-mentioned changes, the Government of Indonesia
requested that the current scope of the project would need to be restructured to (i) modify key activities to more fully
align these with emerging priorities of the Government for Village Law implementation; (ii) update implementation
arrangements, including clarifying coordination, with new institutional arrangements put in place since 2019; and (iii)
drop activities that are no longer priorities for the Government. It was agreed that the Project Development Objective,
“to strengthen institutional capacity for improved quality of spending in selected villages” remains relevant and
unchanged. The restructuring would not seek any modification to the closing date. Between April and September
2021, the CPMU led an exercise for all PIUs to submit Terms of Reference outlining their activities between 2021-
2024, the proposed sequencing of implementation, and clarifying the financing from the loan. These were then
reviewed jointly with the World Bank to (i) confirm the relevance and continued inclusion of the activity within the
loan; and (ii) ascertain the level of financing required to complete these activities by 2024. A review of the project
implementation arrangements was also conducted and identified the need to clarify coordination between
components, to clarify project management arrangements within Component 1, and to update and align the
nomenclature for units in Component 2 after the Ministry of Villages underwent its own internal restructuring in 2020.

Moderately Unsatisfactory. The project remains behind schedule on the implementation of key activities and in the
recruitment of several key consultancy packages. In recognition of the current ground realities, it was agreed that a
project restructuring could modify the scope and make appropriate adjustments to the project activities, and the

project management structures, with the objectives of streamlining and improving implementation progress. It was
agreed that the overall project objectives and key activities continue to remain relevant in the current context. It is
expected that the corrective actions introduced under the project restructuring would bring a streamlined set of
activities that would be able to achieve their objectives by the closing date of the project. A Mid Term Review of the
project, currently scheduled for December 2022, would further assess implementation progress against the revised

10.     Fiduciary. There are no overdue audits for the project.

11.      Environment and Social Safeguards. The restructuring does not seek any changes or modifications to the
safeguards policies triggered. The implementation of the project safeguard measures are to be supported in MOHA
with the support of a National Management Consultant, who has yet to be recruited. To date, no field level activities
have been conducted, and would commence only after the NMC and requisite technical support at the sub-national
level is in place to support rollout of new systems and institutions. MOV has recruited environment and social
specialists to support on mainstreaming activities into the facilitator capacity building program, and other activities.
Given the delays in recruitment of the NMC, the performance on implementation of Environment and Social
Safeguards is Moderately Satisfactory. Revisions to the implementation structure in Component 1 is expected to
address the bottlenecks in recruitment of key consultant packages, including the NMC, and it is expected that once
the consultants are recruited, MOHA would have the requisite support to implement project activities in line with
requisite Environment and Social Safeguards. The Environment and Social Management Framework has been revised
to reflect the updated and streamlined component structure. Technical consultations were completed with all
implementing agencies reflecting continued agreement to mainstream environmental and social standards into village
governance. The revised ESMF was approved by the World Bank on April 27, 2022, and publicly disclosed on June 8.
2022.

12.     Grievance Redress. Given the project focus on strengthening Government systems, complaint handling will
continue to utilize the SP4N LAPOR! System, a national complaint handling system managed by the MenPANRB, which
connects 34 ministries, 96 central agencies and 493 local governments in the country. The system is publicly accessible
through http://kemendagri.lapor.go.id/. The NMC will support project PIUs to follow up and manage any complaints
related to the project, in line with clear procedures and mechanisms on complaint handling.




 II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

13.     Overall. The scope of revised activities continues to align with the PDO, which remains relevant and
unchanged. No extension of the closing date is being sought at this time. Activities were revised in scope to align with
new institutional structures and priorities for Village Law implementation and revised to reflect feasibility of
implementation in the remaining project period. Between April and September 2021, MOHA led an exercise to review
and revise project activities in line with a proposed restructuring, with proposed modifications endorsed by the Project
Steering Committee in October 2021. The Government formally requested the project restructuring in a letter dated
June 29, 2022.

         Institutional Strengthening for Improved Village Service Delivery (P165543)




Proposed Changes

14.      Overall, the changes propose to scale back and streamline the design of the program, given the current
political economy and Covid 19 response realities to adjust to what is achievable in the current context. The
restructuring is an opportunity to consolidate activities to reflect both what is feasible with current Government
priorities, including in response to the Covid 19 pandemic and to align to Government capacity. There is no proposed
change to the PDO, and only minor modifications proposed to the Project Results Framework (Annex 3). Proposed
changes to component activities and costs reflect both the policy context and implementing partner priorities and
capacity. Component 3 will be dropped as agreed at the Project Steering Committee. Project Components 1, 2 and 4
will be modified, and Component 4 will be renamed as Component 3.

15.      Project Development Objective: The objective of the Project remains unchanged and is to strengthen
institutional capacity for improved quality of spending in participating villages.



Project Description

16.     The Project will be restructured to have three components, with modifications in the scope of activities as
outlined below:

17.      Component 1: Strengthening Village Government Institutions. The objective of this component is to improve
the institutional strengthening system for village governments within MoHA, and to support the rollout and adoption
of technology-based tools and approaches at the subnational level. The component will continue to support the
development of a Learning Management System (LMS) for village governments that is flexible and adaptable to their
varied capacity needs. It will also support the rollout of the LMS platform with the aim of handing over implementation
to provincial and district governments and subdistricts in a phased manner. The component will allow social and
environmental standards, awareness of climate change hotspots and impacts on village livelihoods, and potential
responses to be integrated in village plans, strengthening inclusion and participation in planning and budgeting, and
upgrading and supporting best practice in procurement systems. Key changes to the components agreed as a part of
the restructuring as follows:

        a.       Sub-Component 1A: Supporting Institutional Strengthening Systems for Village Governments.
        Activities remain largely as approved, with clarifications reached on ensuring that specific content would be
        developed for village councils and to promote women’s empowerment, and that the project activities would
        support the development of specific assessments and support strategies for lagging regions. The curriculum
        and modules for women’s empowerment would focus on village level rollout to strengthen women’s
        participation in the planning and budgeting process and would be executed in close collaboration and
        coordination with the PKK unit in MOHA.
        b.      Sub-Component 1B: Rollout of Institutional Strengthening Systems. No changes are being sought to
        this subcomponent.

         Institutional Strengthening for Improved Village Service Delivery (P165543)


        c.      Sub-Component 1C: Improving Oversight and Management. Activities remain largely as approved,
        with clarifications reached on the specific activities related to strengthening MOHA’s village-level data
        systems (Prodeskel) and improving and expanding village financial management training and consolidation
        applications. The restructuring will also add a specific activity to developing an integrated data system and
        dashboard. The sub-component has also clarified activities related to strengthening village audits,
        implementation of which would be coordinated in close collaboration with MOHA’s Inspectorate General.
        d.      Sub-Component 1D: Frontline Service Delivery. This is a new sub-component that will pilot test an
        approach to improve frontline service delivery in participating village governments. The pilot program will be
        undertaken in 60 districts. The total financing allocated to this was agreed to be US$6 million. Findings of the
        pilot could inform a future scale up of the approach. This subcomponent will be implemented by the
        Directorate of Regional Administration (Adwil) under MOHA.

18.      Component 2: Promoting Participatory Village Development. This component will continue to strengthen
participation and social accountability in village governance systems by leveraging systems of capacity support within
MoV—both the existing facilitator structure and several other programs that support community empowerment and
village development. These programs will be supported to be more flexible and adaptable to better respond to needs,
and to facilitate innovation and cross-learning for community capacity. The key changes to component activities are
as follows:

        (a)      Sub-Component 2A: Facilitator Capacity Strengthening. This sub-component will drop the activity
        related to the Village Digital Platform (GoDesa) and will be renamed to reflect the modified activities. The
        activities under this component will now include (i) the development training modules related to social
        accountability, community empowerment and participatory planning and the integration of these modules
        into the facilitator capacity building program and training rollout; (ii) the development of a digitally enabled
        Learning Management System to transition the training program to a platform-based approach starting in
        2022; and (iii) the development of a digital marketplace platform, as well as associated technical guidelines
        and regulations for technical service providers (P2KTD).
        (b)     Sub-Component 2B Community Capacity and Social Accountability Systems. No changes are being
        sought to this sub-component.
        (c)     Sub-Component 2C: Digital Innovation and Community Learning. The activities remain largely as
        approved, with clarifications reached on financing the community learning part under the Ministry’s Akademi
        Desa 4.0 platform, highlighting a peer-to-peer learning approach. This sub-component will also finance the
        development and deployment of a pilot program entitled “Smart Villages” in 3,000 villages, in partnership
        with the Ministry of Communications. No changes are being sought to the scope of the component.
        (d)   Sub-Component 2D: Technical Support and Program Management. No changes to this sub-
        component are being sought.


19.     Component 3: Village Performance Awards. This component is proposed to be dropped from the loan. The
component represents a zero-financing component, with the view to introduce performance monitoring and award
systems with the Ministry of Finance. The component is no longer considered to be a priority in a streamlined set of
project activities.

20.     Component 4: National Coordination, Monitoring, and Policy. The objective of this component is to improve

Plan (RPJP) for 2025-2045.

        (a)      Sub-Component 4A Development of Integrated Village Data System. No changes are being sought

Implementation Arrangements

23.      The project anticipated supporting the Government of Indonesia in the establishment of a National
Coordination Platform for Village Law, which would support improved coordination, monitoring and implementation
of village level investments. In particular, the platform would support the overall objective for improving the utilization
of village funds by ensuring alignment and improving synergies of Government spending at the local level. Delays in
the establishment of this platform are due, in part, to a changing political landscape between the time that the project was
approved, elections and subsequent reshuffling of the Cabinet and other key posts. At this stage, the establishment of the
NCP is delayed, and is unlikely to be achieved. This is due, in part, to a changing political landscape between the time
         The World Bank
         Institutional Strengthening for Improved Village Service Delivery (P165543)


that the project was approved, elections and subsequent reshuffling of the Cabinet and other key posts and positions,
and the implications of the COVID-19 pandemic on the attention and priorities for the Government in the short term.
Thus, it is proposed that the project focus on strengthening the existing coordination functions within Kemenko PMK
and develop a broader strategic vision and institutional arrangements for national coordination under Village Law as
a key output of the project. In light of the modification in scope of the activity, the Borrower’s covenant on the
establishment of an NCP is proposed to be deleted.

24.      A second change to the project’s implementation arrangements will be establish separate PIUs within MOHA
to oversee and administer Components 1A, 1B, 1C and 1D of the project. All of these PIUs would report to the
Coordinating Project Management Unit (CPMU) which has the dual function of coordinating activities within MOHA
and Component 1, as well as across the different implementing agencies. The role of the CPMU has been further clarified
in specifying its role in oversight and coordination of the implementation of the activities of Component 1. The PIUs in
MOHA will be accorded the authority and budgets to administer their respective activities. The restructuring would
also update the nomenclature for MOV in the Project Operations Manual that resulted from internal changes and
reorganization of the Ministry of Villages departments. These changes have no impacts on the scope or agreed project
administration activities as outlined in the project’s Legal Agreement. The revised management structures, with
numbers of PIUs and with designated scope of responsibilities, is summarized in a ministerial decree, subject to
arrangements satisfactory to the Bank.

25.      Finally, the project would revise the subnational implementation arrangements in the Loan Agreement by
removing specific references to the establishment, scope and roles and responsibilities of subnational implementation
arrangements as the project would continue to strengthen government implementation systems for Village Law. As
such, the establishment of subnational implementation units are proposed to be deleted as legal covenants.
Incremental operating costs incurred in line with the implementation of project related activities continue to be
eligible for Bank financing.


Results Framework

26.     Changes to the results framework include: (i) dropping and/ or modifying result indicators that are no longer
relevant due to the cancellation of Component 3 and Subcomponent 2C; and ii) adjusting the 2021 and 2022 targets
given the 2-year implementation delay. the modification and adjustment of outcomes based on current status and
performance, as well as dropping several indicators that are associated with activities to be dropped under the loan.
The revised results framework is provided as an annex to this Restructuring Paper.
