 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The project was approved by the National Assembly’s borrowing plan in November 2021. Implementation progress for the project includes:
          All the administrative steps to allow disbursements, including legal covenants, were finalized, and disbursement conditions (on
           construction) met. As such, most project activities have commenced. Disbursements are taking place, while some states are more
           advanced than others.
  Component 1. Creating safe and accessible learning spaces
  Sub-Component 1.1 Creating new learning spaces in secondary schools
      a. Status: Construction of 400 JSSs and 266 SSSs is planned in six states. Sample architectural drawings have been finalized by the NCPU
           architect. The type designs of all buildings, bill of quantities, cost estimates, technical specifications, and construction manuals have been
           finalized and are now ready for the States to use.

      b. Next steps: Signing of supervision contracts is expected by August/September; award of contracts by September and school
         construction to commence in October 2022.
  Sub-component 1.2. Renovation and rehabilitation of schools
      a.  Status: 10 sample SIPs for 7 states were approved clearing the way for States to accelerate the preparation of SIPs. All states have
         agreed to complete disbursement of small grants in 50% of schools in the next 1-2 months and the remaining 50% in the following 2-3
         months. All states have trained their School-based Management Committees (SBMCs) on renovation activities including record keeping,
         financial management and monitoring. Contracts were awarded for school renovation and works are underway. There is good progress
         for all states, except for Ekiti and Plateau.
      b. Next steps: Finalization of sample School Improvement Plans for large grants is expected by July 7, 2022.
  Sub-component 2.1. Promoting social and behavior change through traditional rulers, campaigns, and advocacy.
      a. Status: The states held community visits to Religious and Traditional leaders to sensitize them on the proposed social norms campaign.
         The NPCU has developed the media campaign framework. ToR for engagement of audience research firm was finalized for all states, and
         two states have already hired media firms and developed media content. Plateau and Borno are again lagging behind. To improve their
         implementation capacity, consultants were hired to support them on specific areas.
      b. Next steps: Commencement of campaigns is expected by mid-July 2022.
  Subcomponent 2.2.a Providing girls with critical life skills
      a. Status: Pilot schools were selected for all the states and a workshop was conducted for Katsina state. The TORs for 6 participating states
         were approved in STEP and 3 states have issued REOI to selected firms. A life Skills Workshop took place with J-PALto build PIU
         capacity on implementation and meet context-specific objectives.
      b. Next steps: The Expression of Interest for other states is expected by the end of June 2022 contract planned to be awarded and actual
         training expected to start in September 2022, following the hiring of the consultant.
  Subcomponent 2.2.b. Digital skills and remote learning platform
      a. Status: States A workshop on digital skills was conducted on March 7 and 8. It was agreed that the UNESCO framework will be used as
         a reference for the curriculum. Schools were selected and gaps assessments were finalized for all the states. ToRs for technical partners
         were cleared for all states. EOI was released for 3 states. Borno developed a curriculum in alignment with UNESCO digital skills
         framework. All states have defined the technical specifications for the procurement of devices.
      b. Next Steps: The onboarding of technical partners should be finalized by August 2022. The curriculum and the training manual are to be
         developed by September 2022. The procurement of technology devices should commence by June 2022. The training of students is
         expected to start by September 2022.
      c. Subcomponent 2.3. Providing financial incentives to the poorest households
      d. Status: SPIUs identified beneficiaries in poor vulnerable households in the Local government Areas with the lowest education transition
         for girls, using the poverty mapping register developed by NASSP.
      e. Next steps: 2 states have made progress in the CCT targeting process, the first disbursement to beneficiaries is expected by the end of
         June 2022. Kano state is expected to disburse CCT to the 14 selected eligible LGAs quickly (June/July) before the cohort of beneficiaries’
         transit to the next grade (or they drop out of school due to binding constraints). Katsina state plans to review the 32 selected LGAs to
         ensure compliance with the stipulated targeting guidance before disbursement (b) Borno, Kaduna, Kebbi, and Plateau to follow the
         implementation pathway of Kano and Katsina to ensure accuracy of targeting and overall implementation in time before the academic year
         is over. All SPIUs to strictly comply with the harmonized targeting strategy, use harmonize Financial Service Provider agreement strategy
         and report on the harmonized CCT template. Disbursement of CCT by Kano and Katsina to be done by June/July 2022 before the end of
         current school year.
      f. Component 3. System Strengthening and M&E
      g. Status: The Third-party Monitoring (TPM) firm contract was signed in May 2022. The monitoring and evaluation contract was signed in
         May 2022. Five states are procuring a firm/consultant to support their activities, Borno and Plateau are lagging.
      h. Next steps: It is expected that all SPIUs will have their firms on board by June/July and will synchronize their activities with those at the
         federal level. The first Monitoring Implementation Report of the Project is expected by July 30.
