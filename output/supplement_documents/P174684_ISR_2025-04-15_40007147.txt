4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  This Implementation Status Report (ISR) includes the results of the Mid Term Review (MTR), conducted in November
  2024 as well as a more recent implementation support mission, conducted in March 2025.
  The project is progressing well with a rating of Moderately Satisfactory. The MTR found that the project has achieved a
  number of important results. The project’s work continues to be well received by beneficiaries, including citizens in the
  project's intervention regions, the private sector and public sector authorities, at both local and national levels. The PIU
  and the Bank teams have proactively worked to advance project activities and disbursement.
  The MTR found that project management should be improved, particularly with regard to: (i) improving staff
  recruitment and management practices, (ii) improving procurement and contract management practices for staff and
  activities, (iii) improving financial management practices, (iv) strengthening E&S risk management practices, including the
  operationalization of the Grievance Mechanism (GM), and (v) improving project monitoring and evaluation. Since the
  MTR, significant efforts have been made to improve project management (human resources management, procurement),
  but several areas still require further attention: (i) improving financial management and (ii) improving data collection for
  the results framework.
  Additional efforts are needed to implement the business support component (component 3), which is at least a year
  behind schedule. As the PDO indicators are directly linked to this component, the MTR recommended stepping up efforts
  to implement those activities that directly support businesses, so that results are visible over the life of the project. Since
  the MTR, progress has not been sufficient, and the most recent mission recommended for the Project Implementation
  Unit (PIU) to engage external technical assistance to support the implementation of the Madagascar Business Investment
  Facility (MBIF) program under component 3.
  The project is experiencing a financing gap in the amount of around US$70 million. Two past restructurings resulted in
  the cancellation of US$20 million and the redirection of US$25 million towards urgent support required for Madagascar
  Airlines, at the request of the government. In addition, the project has experienced exchange rate losses (US$12 million),
  and the addition of several activities that were uncompleted from previous projects (US$ 15 million).
  To achieve its stated objectives and results, an additional financing (AF) of US$71 million has been proposed to cover
  the financing gaps, replace funds cancelled or redirected, and ensure originally planned activities will be financed and
  implemented. This AF will also include a level-two restructuring of the PIC3 project to: (i) extend the project closing date
  by one year; and (ii) adjust the results framework to better capture results, align with corporate scorecard and update
  baseline and targets. No change on the PDO is proposed.
