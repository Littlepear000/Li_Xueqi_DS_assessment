 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING
A. Project Status
     1. The Strengthening the Statistical System of Uzbekistan (SSSU) Project was approved on February 16, 2022, for an
        amount of $50 million and became effective on September 15, 2022. However, implementation only commenced after
        the government approved Presidential Decrees No. 113 and 114, on March 4, 2024, which authorized spending for the
        SSSU project and determined the legal status of the implementation agency. To accelerate disbursements and project
        implementation after this long delay in starting project implementation, the initial activities agreed upon with the
        Implementing Agency (then called the Agency for Statistics under the Office of the President of Uzbekistan – Statistics
        Agency in short), primarily focused on launching the procurement of goods to support the implementation of the
        Population and Housing Census which at the time, was being planned for July/August 2026, and underpins other data
        production activities. This plan also included kick-starting institutional strengthening activities and reviewing progress
        in the implementation of the National Strategy for the Development of Statistics (NSDS 2020-2025) which anchored the
        project design. The NSDS review aimed to identify activities that were already implemented with government financing
        during the two-year project delay and those activities still relevant for the project to finance.

    2. The implementation of the initially agreed key activities went off track and priorities shifted in February 2025, further
       delaying implementation. In February 2025, the government reconsidered the implementation modality of Population
       and Housing Census, while the Presidential Decree on the Census implementation was being finalized. The Decree will
       dictate both the timing and modality of the census. Meanwhile, the implementing agency had to revisit the initial needs
       assessment and specifications for the procurement of goods in view of Presidential Decree 162 (April 19, 2024)
       proclaiming that all government functions will be relocated to New Toshkent City within the next two years. This
       delayed the procurement processes. Furthermore, the government changed the leadership of the Statistics Agency and
       reorganized it under the Presidential Decree 75 (February 24, 2025) accompanied by the Presidential Resolution 27,
       changing its status from an Agency under the President’s Office to an autonomous National Statistics Committee (NSC)
       of Uzbekistan, with key priorities for the modernization of the Uzbekistan's statistical system. Key decisions affecting
       project procurement were delayed during this reorganization period in February and March 2025. These changes have
       necessitated the restructuring of the project, which has been agreed with the government. As of May 2025,
       disbursement stands at 3% and the project remains in problem status since (23 August 2023 – put the date of the ISR
       that downgraded it).

B. Rationale for Restructuring

    1. Restructuring the project leverages the appetite for statistical modernization by the new leadership of the NSC, the
       urgency of implementation the new leadership has brought with high-level and broad political support for the
       statistical modernization and transformation, while rebalancing project expenditures away from procurement
       activities that so far had been a bottleneck for implementation, to faster disbursing activities. The NSC now reports
       to a Board chaired by the Deputy Prime Minister who is also the Minister of Economy and Finance. The Board also
       includes the Advisor to the President, Chairpersons from the Budget and Economic Issues Committees in the Senate
       and Legislative Chamber of the Oliy Majlis of the Republic of Uzbekistan, Ministers from key Ministries identified for
       administrative data integration as well as government think tanks and academia. Furthermore, supporting the census

    under the new implementation modality, which has gained traction with the government due to the cost savings it
    generates, puts the project on track to achieve all PDO outcome indicators.

2. Proposed changes in the modality of the Population and Housing Census impact both the overall financing
   contribution of the project to the census and the composition of the supported expenditures. The government has
   decided to (i) use a combination of Web-based Interviews (CAWI) and face-to-face enumeration using tablets (CAPI)
   conducted by 40,000 enumerators (mahalla officials, schoolteachers and school principals); (ii) combine the population
   and housing census and the agriculture census, which were two separate, sequentially implemented sub-activities
   under the project and, (iii) supplement the census questionnaire with administrative data to shorten the questionnaire.
   The NSC envisages conducting the combined census at the end of 2025 or early 2026 with technical assistance from
   UNFPA and FAO. The government will use existing tablets from the mahalla authorities, requiring fewer tablets from
   the project and intend to use project funds for operational expenses instead (for census preparations, data collection
   costs and communication campaigns and dissemination). The new modality reduces the overall financing needs of the
   population and housing and agriculture census by about $8 million, which is being reallocated to other activities within
   the project. Government clarity on this methodology also removes a key source of uncertainty that held up the
   implementation of the project and reduces the procurement risks tied to the purchase of a large number of tablets.
   Though the shorter preparation timeline introduces significant risks regarding completion of preparation activities, it
   also reflects renewed urgency to conduct the census of previous postponements. Furthermore, combining the
   Population and Housing Census and the Agriculture Census means that all household-based census activities can now
   be completed within the current project period. This will improve the Statistical Performance Index significantly enough
   to surpass the first PDO outcome indicator, while automatically achieving the third PDO outcome indicator. This puts
   the project on track to achieve all the PDO outcomes indicators by the project closing date.

3. The government plans to reprogram the savings from the census budget to finance the statistical modernization
   and instructional modernization agenda as per the new mandate of the NSC. The specific priorities under the
   new direction are to:
   a. Modernize and transform the institutional set-up of the National Statistical Committee to produce high quality and
       timely statistics.
   b. Enhance primary data collection by transitioning from aggregate statistical reports to sample surveys, thereby
       improving the accuracy and reliability of statistical information.
   c. Optimize statistical surveys by expanding the use of administrative data integration and reduce redundancy,
       especially on enterprise and agriculture production statistics using data from the Tax Committee, Ministry of
       Agriculture, Ministry of Employment and Poverty Reduction’s “mahalla” level data and the Cadaster Agency. The
       NSC has been directed to take measures to improve the qualifications of employees responsible for forming
       statistical and administrative data.
   d. Harmonize the national statistical system’s methodologies with international standards to ensure consistency and
       comparability of data. The NSC has therefore been tasked with organizing sectoral reviews to assess the
       introduction of advanced quality management systems into the national statistics system and in cooperation with
       the Ministry of Justice, implement the state registration of individual entrepreneurs based on the types of activities
       they can engage in and the national classifier of economic activities of the Republic of Uzbekistan from July 1, 2025.

       e. Expand digital capabilities by investing in and utilizing AI and big data technologies to improve data analysis and
          forecasting, enhancing the overall efficiency of the statistical system. A key priority is the integration of information
          systems of state bodies and organizations to expand the scope of statistical indicators and regionally disaggregated
          statistics. This targets integration of administrative data from 17 Ministries, Departments and Agencies, including
          on enterprise and agriculture production statistics using data from the Tax Committee, Ministry of Agriculture,
          Ministry of Employment and Poverty Reduction’s “mahalla” level data and the Cadaster Agency. Furthermore, the
          NSC has been tasked with making the necessary technological, methodological, and human investments to use
          Computer Assisted Telephone Interviews (CATI).

   4. Reflecting these priorities, the NSC has been tasked with developing the next National Strategy for the Development
      of Statistics (NSDS) incorporating modernized statistical production, management, and use. This will supplant the
      NSDS 2020-2025 which underpinned the current project design. This new NSDS will enhance the focus on integrating
      data innovations using digital transformation and Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies, transitioning from aggregate
      reporting to sample-based surveys while incorporating new methods and techniques that complement traditional
      methods for statistical work, compliance with international standards and support institutional reforms. It will also aim
      to improve cooperation between users and providers of statistical data and introducing mechanisms for preparing high-
      demand data and analytical information.

   5. Restructuring the project is necessary to reflect these changes, leveraging the appetite for statistical modernization
      by the new leadership of the NSC as well as the high-level and broad support for the statistical modernization and
      transformation that the NSC now has. The NSC now reports to a Board chaired by the Deputy Prime Minister who is
      also the Minister of Economy and Finance. The Board also includes the Advisor to the President, Chairpersons from the
      Budget and Economic Issues Committees in the Senate and Legislative Chamber of the Oliy Majlis of the Republic of
      Uzbekistan, Ministers from key Ministries identified for administrative data integration as well as government think
      tanks and academia. Furthermore, supporting the census under the new implementation modality which has gained
      traction with the government due to cost savings it generates, puts the project on track to achieve all the PDO outcome
      indicators.

II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

C. Project Scope and activities
   6. The overall allocation of the budget across the components will be maintained, but substantial changes are being
       made to sub-components. These changes are mostly within the Data Production component to reflect the new census
       implementation modalities and reprogram funds from the census to new priorities dictated under the Presidential
       Decree 75 (February 24, 2025) and Presidential Resolution 27.

   7. Limited changes have been made to Component 1: Institutional Reform and Capacity, as the current scope can
      support the transformation of the National Statistics Committee, and the national statistical system.
      a. Sub-component 1.1: Institutional Reforms will include the development of the new National Statistics Development
          Strategy, which will reflect recommendations from the preparation of a change strategy for the National Statistics



                                             Official Use Only                                                             Page 3
        The World Bank

          Committee and serve as a tool for enhancing statistical coordination among government institutions and
          development partners.

8. Comprehensive changes are being made to the sub-components of Component 2: Data Production. These reflect the
   significant changes in the census activity and reallocation of financing towards the production of sample surveys,
   integration of administrative data and adoption of digital technologies in statistics production as part of the statistical
   modernization agenda.
   a. Sub-component 2.1: National Accounts Statistics and Business Census - will include support to (i) the development
       and implementation of the classifiers for institutional units; (ii) improving methodologies, data sources and
       upgrading the necessary technologies to adopt the SNA 2025 in compilation of national accounts in Uzbekistan; (iii)
       conducting sample surveys for private sector housing construction; (iv) improvement and piloting enterprise
       sample surveys, including through use of Computer Assisted Telephone Interviews and (v) operational expenses
       for conducting business census. The implementation of an informal sector survey will be dropped from the project.

   b. Sub- component 2.2: Well-being monitoring – will exclude redundant activities already completed with government’s
      own financing and focus on further improvements in areas requiring additional support from the project. For
      example, Uzbekistan has already participated in the last International Comparison Program (ICP) round and
      modernized the Household Budget Survey (HBS) by expanding the sample size and fully transitioning to electronic
      data collection using its own resources. The ICP participation activity will therefore be excluded from the project.
      The project will continue to support the HBS modernization and household surveys more broadly focusing on (i)
      sample survey methodology development and training, including development of a master sample for household-
      based surveys including the HBS, drawing on the new population, housing and agriculture census; (ii) purchase of
      tablets to support survey data collection. Project support on price statistics will now focus on the use of big data in
      calculating price indices, prioritizing the calculation of the Consumer Price Index. This will reflect the ambition to use
      digital technologies and AI in statistical production. Support for improving local data management and integration
         systems for the "mahalla summary" database with the Ministry of Employment and Poverty Reduction,
         Association of Mahallas and Ministry of Interior, including the integration of mahalla-level statistics with the NSC
         statistical reporting shall be maintained.

   c.    Sub-component 2.3: Population, Housing and Agriculture Census - is being broadened in line with the revised census
         modality to provide support to a combined Population, Housing and Agriculture Census. The combination of the two
         censuses into one activity, mostly utilizing already existing mahalla tablets and local level officials for enumeration,
         reduces the budget needs of census support under the project by around $8 million. It also shifts the support needs
         from being heavily on hardware procurement to operational expenses instead. The main risks for implementing a
         population census pertain to (i) under coverage of certain population groups for political reasons, (ii) methodological
         approach to the census and technical capacity of enumerators and (iii) inadequacy of logistical arrangements for all
         aspects of data collection, processing, and reporting. The first source of risk is low in Uzbekistan reflected in the
         moderate political and governance risks, that ESS 7 has not been triggered in this project and the use of mahalla
         officials in a country with a tradition of strict household registration system. The second risk is high but can be
         mitigated with ongoing technical support on the methodological design. The use of local officials in their own
         communities reduces logistical risks, mitigates gaps in the cartography maps, and makes updating the cartography

                                           Official Use Only                                                             Page 4
     The World Bank

       maps easier by using people familiar to the enumeration areas. However, there is a major risk from the fact that the
       accelerated implementation timeline of the census provides little time for establishing other logistical infrastructure
       and pilot testing. The planned project support therefore places a strong emphasis on supporting investments to
       reduce these risks and improve the quality of the implementation of the census. About 40 percent of the census
       support budget under the project will be spent on preparatory activities, as per government restructuring proposal
       – in particular, questionnaire finalization, administrative data assessments, software development, stress testing,
       piloting the census and enumerator training. A legal covenant will be added requiring an independent certification
       that preparatory activities, including full pilot and stress testing of the IT system, have met minimum international
       standards prior to commencement of data collection. Third-party verification and establishment of quality assurance
       mechanisms will be reflected in an updated operations manual. The project will also finance support for a post-
       census enumeration. This is critical for ensuring the integrity of the census.

    d. Sub-component 2.4: Agriculture Production Monitoring System - now excludes a stand-alone agriculture census
       since it has been merged with the population and housing census in sub-component 2.3. This sub-component will
       instead focus on modernization of agriculture surveys through developing and piloting the methodology for
       agriculture sample surveys and use of administrative data for monitoring agricultural statistics.

    e. Sub-component 2.5 – Methods, Standards and Registers’ overall budget allocation will increase by $5 million to
       raise the ambition of activities planned under this sub-component in accordance with the elevated priority of
       modernization of statistical production. The planned results will be expanded beyond harmonization of standards,
       classifications of existing standards, and classification. The ambition is being raised to include (i) development of
       additional standards and classification based on international standards, including updating and expanding gender
       statistics methodologies and the development of methodologies for energy and investment efficiency indicators,
       (ii) updating rules and standards for metadata production and (iii) strengthening the data quality and technical base
       of ministries and other government bodies involved in the production of statistical data to improve registers.
       Methodologies and tools will also be enhanced by utilizing AI and other digital technologies to process
       administrative data to produce statistical indicators.

9. Minor modifications to Component 3: Improvement of Equipment and Dissemination are being made, primarily to
   strengthen support for open data access and production and dissemination of statistical reports.
   a. Sub-component 3.2. Analysis and Dissemination will provide more resources to support (i) the development of a
       comprehensive data dissemination policy in order to implement international data dissemination standards, (ii)
       improvement of annual statistical yearbooks and production of statistical reports and (iii) upgrading of the printing
       facilities at the NSC.

10. No changes are being made to Component 4: Project Management.

11. Implementation of the proposed activities will accelerate disbursements of the project. Completion of all activities
    related to the integrated population, housing and agriculture census will disburse $14.5 million within the next year,
    while procurement of the data center, with a bid announcement is expected in June 2025, will disburse an additional
    $7.5 million and institutional reform, capacity building and other data production activities will bring the total

       disbursement for FY26 to around $25 million. The disbursements for FY27 would be anchored in the implementation
       of the business census activities ($4.5 million), administrative data integration ($4 million), other production activities
       on enterprise surveys, HBS, and agriculture surveys ($1.0 million), purchase of equipment for regional offices ($6.7
       million) and reallocated spending to component one for capacity building and statistical transformation.

   12. Implementation of the proposed activities will accelerate disbursements of the project. Completion of all activities
       related to the integrated population, housing and agriculture census, installation of the data center and implementation
       of institutional reforms, capacity building and other data production activities within the next year will raise
       disbursement to more than 50 percent by the end of FY26. The disbursements for FY27 would be anchored in lumpy
       activities like the purchase of equipment for regional offices, the implementation of the business census activities and
       completion of administrative data integration for up to 17 agencies. This and completion of other data production
       activities on enterprise surveys, HBS, and agriculture surveys and reallocated spending to component one for capacity
       building and statistical transformation is expected to lead to significant disbursements in FY27.

D. Results framework
   13. The PDO level outcome indicator on Completed census and publication of comprehensive reports has been revised
       to “Complete Population, housing and agriculture census fieldwork.” This reflects the new census implementation
       modality combining the Population and Housing Census and the Agriculture Census.

   14. Intermediate Results by Components have been revised to reflect changes in sub-activities within components.
       a. Two new intermediate indicators under Component 1: Institutional Reform are being added, namely (i) adoption of
            a new NSDS and (ii) Adoption of the revised regulatory framework and institutional structure of the Statistics
            Committee system in Uzbekistan.
       b. Some intermediate indicators under Component 2: Data production are being dropped and new ones adopted in
            their place, while some are being revised to reflect the substantial changes to this component.
            i. Three indicators are being dropped, namely (i) conducting a cartography for housing and population census
                (which was completed without using project funds, but likely to be updated manually); (ii) Participation in the
                ICP round (also already completed without project funds) and (iii) completed agriculture census fieldwork (since
                the agriculture census will now be merged with the population and housing census as opposed to a stand-alone
                agriculture census)
           ii. The intermediate indicator on completion of the population and housing census is instead being revised from
                “complete housing and population census indicator” to “complete population, housing and agriculture census”
                to reflect implementation of an integrated census.
          iii. The following three new intermediate indicators are being added under this component: (i) completion of a full
                pilot for census enumeration (which is a critical milestone for ensuring a quality census is implemented); (ii)
                number of statistical dimensions with application of digital technologies (to reflect emphasis on modernization
                of data production) and (iii) number of information systems from Ministries, Departments and Agencies
                integrated with the NSC statistical production systems (to reflect additional emphasis on administrative data
                integration).
        c. An intermediate indicator on “establishment of the data center” has been added under Component 3: Equipment
              and Dissemination. The target number of personal computers and tablets procured has also been revised.
