 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The dated covenant stipulated in the Legal Agreements was finally met and lifted in June 2022 through the adoption and signature of the
  project institutional decree. This decree frames the project institutional arrangement which is comprised of the Project Steering Committee (PSC),
  the Technical Committee (TC) and the PIU. The Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) remains the head of the PSC and TC. The PSC has been
  extended to include three new Ministries (the Ministry of Culture and Communication, the Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development
  and the Ministry of Mining) without prior consultation with the WB. The expansion of the PSC, which is now composed of 13 Ministries, can slow

  decision making. The PIU has been maintained but the Government has decided to pursue the recruitment of a new National Coordinator for the
  PIU through a competitive selection process.
  Following the lifting of the dated covenant, the project was able to draw its first disbursement and actually start implementation. The first
  disbursement amounting to 5 million was done in July 2022, a month following the lifting of the dated covenant. This positive progress has also
  given an impulse to project implementation (with the acceleration of procurement activities) and has also pushed the TC to prepare the 2023 Annual
  Work Plan and Budget proactively and collectively. For example, the preparation of the first cohort for the MIARY digital incubation program is
  advancing well with the launch expected to happen in November 2022, while the study for the rehabilitation of the Manangareza port (in a new PIC
  zone in the East coast) will be launched soon. Discussions around other specific activities including infrastructure investments are also continuing,
  particularly for the new poles.
  The process of hiring the new National Coordinator (NC) has faced some procedural issues, following the Bank’s review. The process for
  hiring the new NC was started in June 2022 and an evaluation committee was set up, composed of the Ministry of Economy and Finance, the
  Ministry of Tourism, the Ministry of Agriculture, the Ministry of Industrialization and Trade, the Ministry of Interior and Decentralization, the Ministry of
  Spatial Planning and Land Services, and the Ministry of Public Work. The results of the evaluation were submitted to the World Bank for clearance
  in September 2022. Following the Bank’s review, a shortcoming in the evaluation procedure was identified: the evaluation report did not follow
  standard WB procurement procedures for this type of evaluation. The WB has requested for the evaluation committee to work with the PIU
  Procurement Officer to resubmit the evaluation report according to the usual WB procurement requirements. The WB team will continue to closely
  monitor the evaluation process. Nevertheless, the Bank agreed with the MEF that a transition period of three months will be followed to allow for
  overlap and transfer of knowledge between the two coordinators.
  Recruitment of other key staff is also ongoing, particularly in the new poles. Recruitment of regional coordinators in the new poles is
  progressing well. The hiring of key regional technical staff has also started, including: a digital entrepreneurship specialist, agribusiness technical
  officers, MBIF program officers, private sector technical officers, and local governance specialists.
