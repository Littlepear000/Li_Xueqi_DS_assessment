 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  1. African Union (AU):
  Implementation status: The FA became effective on November 25, 2020 and all the declaration of effectiveness conditions, including the
  preparation of a Project Operations Manual (POM) and legal opinion were met. The implementation support mission was conducted on November
  24-25, 2020.

  Institutional arrangement: The AUC’s STATAFRIC based in Tunis is responsible for the execution and management of activities under project
  Subcomponent 1.1 which includes: supporting the monitoring of SHaSA2; and supporting the production of harmonized methodologies, manuals
  and guidelines applicable across the continent. The Project Implementation Team (PIT), based at the STATAFRIC is responsible for the day-to-day
  management of this project sub-component 1.1, as well as the preparation of annual operational, procurement, and financial plans, the drafting of
  contract documents, and the preparation of a consolidated implementation report. As part of the core PIT members, qualified procurement specialist
  and FM officer will be hired soon.

  FM and Procurement: The AUC’s Programming, Budget, Finance and Accounting Directorate (PBFA) will be responsible for the overall fiduciary
  management of project funds, including payment authorizations and internal control mechanisms, and will provide quarterly financial reports to the
  World Bank as per the FA. The Procurement and Travel Services Division (PTSD) under the AUC’s Administration, Human Resource Management
  Directorate (AHRM) will overlook the procurement aspects of the project.

  A draft annual work plan and budget (AWPB) of the project for the year 2021 was submitted the World Bank for No objection. The Bank team
  reviewed the project cost and implementation schedule for each proposed activity and provided No objection.

  The project’s M&E activities will be carried out by STATAFRIC’s statistician.

  Steering Committee: During implementation, a Steering Committee will be established for the supervision and monitoring of the project
  implementation activities.

  Virtual project launch was conducted on December 17, 2020 at the 13th Committee of Directors Generals of National Statistics Offices (CoDGs)
  Meeting organized by AU. World Bank project team members attended virtual project launch meeting virtually.

  Overall, all major steps of project implementation start-up activities are on track.

  2. Burkina Faso:

  Implementation status: The FA became effective on October 9, 2020.

  Institutional arrangement: The “Institut National de la Statistique et de la Demographie” (INSD) is the technical implementing agency of the
  Project and coordinating all project activities with other beneficiary government agencies. Delays are however recorded in the process to set up the
  Project Implementation Unit within the INSD. The project coordinator has been nominated. .

  Disbursement: The disbursement has not yet started. The first request disbursement which is in the approval process within the government
  officials is expected to be approved soon. The project Oversight Committee (OC) which is responsible for oversight and governance of the project,
  and the project Technical Committee (TC) have been nominated.

  The first implementation support mission for the project was conducted on December 16, 2020. A draft annual work plan (AWP) of the project for
  2021 was presented to the World Bank team during the mission. The team reviewed the project cost and implementation schedule for each
  proposed activity and provided some comments. The main activities planned include the analysis of the recently completed fifth population and
  housing census, the agriculture census, the demographic and health survey as well as improvement of price and national accounts statistics.

  3. Cabo Verde:

  Implementation status: The FA became effective on September 28, 2020.The implementation support mission (ISM) was conducted on November
  12-17, 2020. The ISM provided an opportunity to jointly review project activities and to identify next steps and agreed actions prior to the actual start
  of project implementation. This review covered technical considerations, as well as administrative, financial and risk management aspects.

  Technical aspects: Following project effectiveness, the Borrower conveyed an interest to reassess project activities, i.e., to replace some new
  activities and propose to remove some of the activities initially agreed. The objective is to better align project activities, to the extent possible, with
  the Government’s reprioritization to accelerate implementation plan to improve the quality and use of administrative data systems against the
  backdrop of the ongoing Covid-19 pandemic and fiscal pressure. The World Bank project team has agreed to consider new activities but
  emphasized these would need to be assessed as both in adherence with international standards and consistent with the project development
  objectives. The client has agreed to work closely with the World Bank team to provide the required information to undertake a technical evaluation
  of the new proposed activities. Both teams have agreed that in the meantime the implementation of project activities which are not the subject of
  discussion for changes will be commenced as soon as possible.

  Administrative, financial, and risk aspects :The Procurement plan and the PPSD have been already developed but they will be updated later. A
  general procurement notice will be lunched based on activities that implementation will commence in the coming months. The process of recruiting
  a project Manager is already launched. The Project Implementing Unit – UGPE -, which manages all World Bank’s projects, has set up an internal
  organization based on a rationalization of accountants. Thus, the accounts of the HISWA Project will be handled by an accountant already recruited
  in one of the active projects funded by the World Bank. An internal auditor is expected to be recruited by soon. UGPE intends to rely on its team for
  the management of contract files. Hence, the recruitment of an additional procurement specialist initially planned will be cancelled. The results

  4. Cote d'Ivoire:

  Implementation status: The project become effective on August 31, 2020.

  Institutional arrangements: The first meeting of the steering committee of the project is needed to approve the 2021 work program of the project.
  However, the inter-ministerial decree establishing the steering committee and the technical committee has not be signed. The delay in signing the
  inter-ministerial decree did not allow the organization of the first meeting of the steering committee. The signing of this decree should allow
  compliance of the legal covenants that expires on February 28, 2021.

  Disbursement: The first disbursement of USD 4.6 million was made on January 13, 2021. The government has designated a financial auditor and
  an account for the project. However, the process of hiring the member of the PIU including a coordinator, a procurement specialist, a financial
  management specialist and the monitoring and evaluation specialist is ongoing. The Terms of References of the PIU members are drafted and the
  procurement plan submitted by the client is under review. Meanwhile, the day-to-day activities of the project is being managed by the
  implementation unit of the project “ Enhancing Government Effectiveness for Improved Public Services - (P164302)”. This arrangement was made
  during the negotiations to avoid that the implementation of the project be hindered by a delay in the hiring the project’s PUI members.

  Implementation of the project activities: The population census represent approximately 20 percent of the total budget of the project. The
  cartography of the census was completed in January 2020 the enumeration that was initially planned for May 2020, has been postponed to June
  2021 (to be confirmed by the government) because of the Covid19 pandemic, the presidential elections of October 2020 and the legislatives
  elections planned for March 2021. According to international standards the enumeration should be completed not later than six month after the
  cartography. Therefore, the national institute of statistics has to redo the cartography in some selected regions to update the 2020 cartography data
  . The update of the cartography will increase the initial budget of the census. Furthermore, because of the COVID-19 pandemic, the initial budget
  will increase to account for additional cost related to social distancing and the precautionary measures needed for field operation. Once the revised
  budget is completed, all stakeholders including the World Bank will discuss the financing options.

  Implementation status: The FA became effective on August 13, 2020. All major steps of project implementation start-up activities are on track.

  The audit firm recruitment should start shortly as it is a covenant and the Bank team is keen to provide full assistance to ECOWAS for this purpose.

  ECOWAS has created a US Dollar project account but still need to create a local currency (Naira) account in Commercial Bank soon.

  6. Ghana:

  PPA implementation: A PPA is supporting the Ghana Statistical Service mainly planned for the preparatory activities for the Population census.
  The population census was initially planned to take place in June 2020. However, due to Covid-19 pandemic the enumeration has been postponed.
  It will now take place in April/May 2021. To that end, GSS is implementing all the preparatory activities, including procurement of tablets, servers,
  trainings of trainers and advertising gadgets. The last stage of the office work of the cartography after the field work is now fully completed. GSS

  has also used the PPA facility to initiate the work on the Producer Price Index rebasing to prepare for the monthly price data collection phase that
  will start in 2021.

  Implementation status: The project declared effectiveness on July 13, 2020.

  Institutional arrangement: The Ghana Statistical Services (GSS) is the implementing agency and the Coordination and Program Management
  Directorate (CPMD) within GSS is coordinating all project activities with all beneficiary agencies, including financial and procurement management.
  The implementation arrangement in Ghana is similar to that of the previously World Bank supported statistics project. The project Oversight
  Committee (OC) will be responsible for oversight and governance of the project, and the project Technical Committee (TC), comprised of technical
  representatives from GSS and participating MDAs, will be tasked with reviewing progress; addressing any critical problems; and, preparing and
  reviewing project work programs, budgets, procurement plans, and financial, and monitoring and evaluation reports.

  The main ongoing activity of the project is the population census. A Project Preparation Advance (PPA) of $6 million and a retroactive financing for
  $45 million were approved to support this activity. However, because of the COVID-19 pandemic, the census enumeration planned to take place in
  June 2020 was postponed. Thus, the government didn’t advance any funding to be claimed as part of the retroactive financing. But, the resources
  from the PPA supported the census activities with the procurement of tablets and finalization of the cartography. These resources have also
  supported the ongoing rebasing and modernization of the Production Price Index.

  A draft annual work plan (AWP) of the project for 2021 was presented to the World Bank team during the mission. It encompasses activities to be
  implemented by the GSS and the 12 MDAs which will receive direct support by the project. The team reviewed the project cost and implementation
  schedule for each project activity to be implemented in 2021 and requested GSS to send to the Bank a revised AWP as soon as possible. The
  postponement of the census to 2021, due to the COVID-19 pandemic as well as the precautionary measures needed now for field operation (limited
  number of staff to transport in one vehicle, personal protective equipment, etc.) have increased the cost of the census from $83 million to $91.3
  million, leading to a financing gap of $12.6 million. In this regard, the GSS met with development partners on November 5, 2020 to discuss on any
  contributions to help fill this financing gap, but it was not successful. The current option is to have further discussion with the government for more
  national budget contribution.

  7. Liberia:

  PPA Implementation: Due to COVID-19 pandemic, some of the PPA activities were put on hold, particularly those that involve GIS mapping
  and cartography for the population census. However, LISGIS has been put the process together in order to undertake some of the activities,
  including: Project management and operation activities; workshop on administrative data, adoption of curricula for the Statistics Degree and
  Certificate - CEST - Programmes at the UL and preparation of a MOU between the two institutions aimed at establishing a framework for
  strengthening operational partnership between them.

  The on-going activities primarily focus on preparation for the upcoming Population and Housing Census. The current priority is to produce
  cartography maps for the Census, which is expected to take up to 10 months and to be completed by October 2021. Accordingly, the Census
  enumeration will likely take place in early 2022. Other activities included the preparation of a degree and certificate program in Statistics at
  University of Liberia and initial consultation for the production of consistent administrative data by ministries, agencies, and commissions (MACs)
  within the Government of Liberia.

  Implementation status:The FA became effectiveness on December 2, 2020. Members of Project Steering Committee (PSC) and Project Technical
  Committee (PTC) are selected. PSC will be responsible for the supervision and monitoring of the project. It is comprised of the Directors of statistics
  at the Ministries, Agencies and Commissions (MACs) within the National Statistical System (NSS). The PSC is expected to meet at least twice a
  year or as needed to approve the annual work plan and budget. PTC is composed of Directors and Heads of Departments within LISGIS and a
  representative from the Aid Management Unit (AMU) and will be responsible for quality control of the project activities.

  The main focus of the project in 2021 is on the preparation for the Population and Housing Census and other activities such as sample-based
  surveys and rebasing of National Accounts need to be sequenced accordingly in order to fully utilize the renewed sampling frame expected out of
  the Census. Therefore, the project implementation team will submit a revised annual work plan and budget to the World Bank. The preparatory
  activities of the population census are underway with the goal to start the cartography field work by March 2021 to prepare for the enumeration
  planned to take place in early 2022.

  Fiduciary aspects: Financial Management and Procurement activities are on track since some of the project activities have been started through
  the PPA.

  Environmental and Social Safeguard: A Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) and action plan will be developed to establish GRM that includes
  an operation manual clarify roles and responsibilities as well as timelines.

  Ongoing Activities and 2021 Work Plan: The Statistician General underscored the great importance of conducting the mid-term census on 19
  April 2021, as per the date announced by His Excellency, the President of Sierra Leone. He expressed concern that Stats SL might lose credibility
  if it did not deliver on this. Based on a technical assessment of preparation progress made to date, the mission team considers it unlikely that the
  census could be conducted this soon and in conformity with good quality standards. However, Stat SL could start with activities as per the Annual
  Work Plan (AWP), and that PFMU would fast track the procurement of key items for the census cartography while conforming with project
  procurement provisions. The TTL provided his no objection to the 2021 Annual Work Plan on November 11, 2020. It was communicated that this is
  a living document and can be revised as needed. Plans for activities in 2021 apart from the census were discussed, particularly the Consumer
  Price Index (CPI), National Accounts (NA), trade statistics, training, National Strategy for the Development of Statistics (NSDS), and data
  dissemination. The ongoing support of Afritac West (IMF) to the CPI and NA work was noted. They will continue to lead from the technical side and
  the project will allocate funds to complement this technical support. The African Development Bank is supporting Stats SL in the development of
  the next NSDS, again, project funds are available to support NSDS dissemination or other activities as needed. Stats SL was advised to detail what
  specific activities they want to carry out in these areas for 2021, and to submit a revised AWP including these activities as soon as possible.

  Procurement: A procurement plan was approved with activities mainly for mid-term census and there is a need to fast-track implementation in line
  with project procurement rules. The team was advised to try to minimize the procurement lead time where it is under control of the procuring entity
  and apply possible quick market approaches to achieve the targeted timeline. The procurement of key items for census cartography is underway by
  PFMU, with additional items to be procured directly by the GoSL out of their own funding. It was recommended that the project finalizes the
  recruitment of a procurement specialist and the Ministry of Finance to provide official grades for procurement staff and requirements for each grade.
  It was noted that Senior Procurement Specialist position that is proposed for this project is only applicable for integrated projects as per official
  harmonized salary scales issue by Ministry of Finance.

  Financial Management: The overall FM performance for the Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa Project is rated as satisfactory
  while the FM risk rating remains substantial. The existing financial management procedures and systems at the Project Fiduciary Management Unit
  meet the minimum Bank requirements for financial management. The first interim financial report (IFR) and audit report should be submitted to the
  Bank not later than February 14, 2021 and June 30, 2022 respectively. It has been agreed that the first audit of the project will cover the first 14
  months of implementation (Nov 2020 to Dec 2021) and be done in early 2022.

  Social and Environmental Safeguards: The mission received briefing on the progress in implementing the social safeguards requirements under
  the project. The mission notes that, the questionnaire for the Mid-term Population and Housing Census includes the Washington Group of short
  question to enhance disability data collection. Establishing the project Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) is however yet to be initiated and
  there is currently no communication or stakeholder engagement plan to guide the project information dissemination efforts. Rehabilitation of the
  statistics Sierra Leone Office has not started as current efforts is on rolling out the census. Necessary protocols are required to prevent COVID-19
  spread and maintain the health and safety of project staff, enumerators and the public during the census survey. The mission identified the need for
  an E&S officer to manage and oversee the E&S requirement under the project. The social risk is rated as Moderate and social safeguards
  performance is rated Moderately Satisfactory (MS).
