 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The Gambia Fiscal Management Development Project became effective on July 28, 2020. The five-year project is financed through a US$35 million
  International Development Association (IDA) Grant.
  The Project Development Objective is to increase fiscal space and improve the effectiveness of public resources management. This will be
  achieved by: (a) enhancing the Gambia Revenue Authority’s (GRA) compliance management, revenue risk analysis, and enforcement capacity, for
  broadening the tax base and improving voluntary compliance; (b) introducing electronic Government Procurement (e-GP); and (c)
  improving financial and operational performance of State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs) and reducing the telecom-related fiscal burden.
  The project responds to a context in which SOE's fiscal burden is unsustainable for public finance, and in which a significant tax potential gap (5-7
  percent of GDP) constrains revenue collection. The Fiscal Management Development Project (FMDP) is aligned with the strategic objectives of the
  Government’s National Development Plan (NDP) and the World Bank Group Country Engagement Note for the Republic of The Gambia for FY18-
  FY21 (CEN n° IDA/R 2018-0139).

  Progress towards the achievement of the PDO and overall Implementation Progress are Moderately Satisfactory. After the declaration of the
  Project's effectiveness on July 28, 2020, the procurement processes of key capacity building and technical assistance contracts have been
  launched. All Project Implementing Unit staffing recruitment is completed with the award of the contract to fill the Project Manager position in March
  2021. In addition, on March 9, 2021, the Ministerial Cabinet approved the establishment of a Tax Policy Unit which paves the way for the
  strengthening of the capacity of the Unit. The GRA business review and the technical assistance for e-GP readiness review contracts have also
  been awarded. The disbursement rate has increased from 4.6 percent (September 2020) to close to 9 percent (May 2021). Nonetheless, critical
  reforms embedded in the SOE Bill and the restructuring of the Telecom SOE sector have been significantly delayed. The Government counterpart
  and project team are monitoring these two reforms' actions to accelerate the implementation momentum.
