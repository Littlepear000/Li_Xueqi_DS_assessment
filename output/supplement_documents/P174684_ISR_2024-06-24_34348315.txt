4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  This ISR covers three key issues: (i) cancellation of 20 million USD of project funds at the request of the Government, to
  free up resources to fund emerging priorities in the WB country portfolio; (ii) inclusion of support to Madagascar Airlines
  (MA) through PIC3, on an emergency basis; and (iii) actions taken by project to improve disbursement performance.

  Cancellation of 20 million USD. The Government requested that funds from several projects be reallocated and the PIC3
  was requested to cancel 20 million of unused funds. This cancellation was formalized through a level-2 restructuring
  which was approved by the World Bank Country Director on May 31, 2024. Funds for the cancellation came from
  Component 3 where some of the activities have been slower to get started, due to the budgetary challenges the project
  has faced over the past 12 months. The budget remaining should allow for the launch of all planned activities under this
  component, albeit with a reduced number of cohorts possible (for pre-incubation, incubation and acceleration) given the
  time remaining for project implementation.

  Reallocation of 25 million USD of project resources for urgent support to Madagascar Airlines. At the urgent request of
  the Government of Madagascar, the PIC3 was asked to allocate in late December 2023 US$25 million of the project
  budget for a group of activities to support Madagascar Airlines to continue operations. The set of activities approved will
  contribute to keep Madagascar Airlines operating their domestic flights, which are key to maintaining intra-regional
  connectivity and supporting the functioning of the tourism sector in Madagascar. Activities include maintenance and
  repairs of key equipment (engine, brakes), purchase of spare parts, and digitalization of key services (including relaunch
  of main B2C website, and capacity building and training). A specific operation to provide much larger and comprehensive
  support is being prepared by the World Bank and is expected to be presented to the World Bank Board in early FY25. As
  soon as this new operation is effective, any activities previously planned under PIC3 that are not already under
  procurement will be migrated to the new operation. Therefore, PIC3 support to Madagascar Airlines is limited in both
  time and budget. This support will be formalized through a second Level-2 restructuring of the project, to clarify the
  objective of the activities being added to PIC3, reallocate the funds between subcomponents as needed, and to add
  indicators to track the impact of the activities to be funded for Madagascar Airlines. This additional support to MA is
  aligned with the support to enhance competitiveness of regional tourism destinations (under Component 2) and is not
  expected to alter the project PDO. A Mid-Term Review of the Project is also expected to take place over the next 12
  months where specific actions will be addressed to improve Component 1 and Component 2 ratings. These should
  include a better tracking of activities results.

  Procurement and FM considerations for MA. Existing procurement and FM procedures are being followed. A few of the
  early contracts that were sent for the PIC3 to support were retrofitted, meaning the contracts were amended to follow
  World Bank requirements, including the inclusion of the fraud and corruption clause.
  Given the urgency of this support, the additional work to process and implement activities to benefit Madagascar Airlines
  has added a significant burden to an already stretched PIU. An additional procurement specialist is being hired to

  procurement specialist performs well, the person will be migrated to the new aviation operation.

  Addition of two intermediate indicators. Given the significant budget that is being allocated to supporting Madagascar
  Airlines, two new intermediary indicators will be added during the restructuring to help track the impact of the $25
  million worth of activities that will be implemented through PIC3. The indicators are:
  a. Domestic Available Seat Kilometers (million): Sum product of number of seats and distance flown of each flight,
  indicative of the capacity.
  b. On-line sales (percent): Level of penetration of on-line sales as percentage of total sales.

  These indicators give a measure of seat capacity of the flights, and thus of continued service to the tourism sector, and
  also digitalization of services, which should help with increased efficiency and ultimately productivity (and ease of
  customer experience). They are also directly related to the activities that the PIC3 is financing for Madagascar Airlines.
  Moreover, these are indicators already planned to be tracked by the larger Transport loan currently under preparation.
  Baseline data is already available for these indicators, and targets for 2026 are being proposed to fit within the timeline
  of implementation for PIC3.

  Improvement in disbursement performance. The fiduciary situation has significantly improved since the last ISR
  (December 2023), albeit FM capacity at the PIU continues to be a challenge. Over the past 5 months (January-May 2024),
  the project has been able to disburse more funds than over the previous 2 years. Disbursement rate is now 27 percent,
  while in December 2023 it was 9 percent. This is due to changes at MEF on the treatment of disbursement requests for
  externally financed projects such as World Bank’s financed projects, which have eliminated many of the delays previously
  experienced. The World Bank supervision team and the Government and PIU also agreed on a list of contracts/activities
  that have been prioritized in terms of implementation. These priority contracts/activities were selected to help improve:
  (i) project financial performance, both commitments but especially disbursement rates; and (ii) project results. Progress
  on these priority activities/contracts are followed weekly during a meeting between World Bank and PIU. The World Bank
  technical team and the PIU have also worked closely with World Bank procurement specialist and FM teams to identify
  and agree on the most efficient procurement methods and to facilitate and decide when direct payments should
  be pursued to expedite some key payments. The above actions, in addition to the cancellation of funds recently
  approved, are all part of the actions taken to remove this project out of problem status.
