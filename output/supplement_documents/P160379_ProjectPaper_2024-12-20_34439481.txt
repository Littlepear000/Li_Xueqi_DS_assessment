I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

  1. Project Background: The Innovation in Solar Power and Hybrid Technologies Project (ISHTP) was approved by the
     World Bank’s (the Bank) Board on March 29, 2019. The Project was originally financed by an IBRD Loan (US$150
     million), CTF Loan (US$28 million), and CTF Grant (US$21.81 million), whose Agreements were signed on
     December 14, 2022, and became effective on March 7, 2023. On February 14, 2023, the financing amounts were
     revised downwards to US$67 million for the IBRD Loan, US$13 million for the CTF Loan, and US$9 million for the
     CTF Grant. The Borrower/Recipient and the implementing agency for ISHTP is the Solar Energy Corporation of
     India Limited (SECI), which is a central sector public utility that plays a strategic role in India’s renewable energy
     initiatives.

  2. Project Development Objective (PDO): The PDOs are to demonstrate the operational and economic feasibility of
     utility-scale innovative renewable energy (RE) technologies and battery energy storage solutions (BESS), and to
     strengthen institutional capacity to facilitate scale-up of such technologies on a commercial basis in India.

  3. Previous restructuring: The Project has been restructured twice in response to changing implementation
     conditions.

          •   The first restructuring was conducted on March 11, 2022, to change the PDO Indicators, Intermediate
              Result Indicators and Disbursement Estimates, because of unforeseen delays in loan signing and
              effectiveness. In addition, the legal covenants were updated to reflect the change in the first subproject
              (solar-wind hybrid with BESS subproject at Ramagiri, Andhra Pradesh), after the new state government in
              the off-taking state declined to buy power from this subproject.

          •   The second restructuring was conducted on February 14, 2023, to effect a partial cancelation of
              US$110.81 million, hence reducing the project size from US$199.81 million to US$89 million. This was
              done since SECI had decided to limit the financing to the two subprojects instead of three, since the first
              subproject at Ramagiri, Andhra Pradesh could not be taken forward.
          •   The current proposed restructuring is the third restructuring of the project, as described below.

  4. Current status of the project: The Project supports two subprojects and strengthens the institutional capacity of
     SECI:

      (a) 100 Megawatt (MW) Solar Photovoltaic (PV) with a 40 MW/120 megawatt-hour (MWh) Battery Subproject at
          Rajnandgaon, Chhattisgarh: This subproject was commissioned on February 1, 2024. It is the largest battery
          project commissioned in India to date and was formally inaugurated by the Honorable Prime Minister of India
          on February 24, 2024. It is also the first solar-with-battery project commissioned for the World Bank globally.
      (b) 100 MW Floating Solar PV Subproject in Getalsud, Jharkhand: The contract for this subproject was awarded
          in May 2024. As per the contract, 18 months have been provided to the contractor to design, install, and
          commission the subproject. This timeline will extend beyond the current project closing date of December 31,
          2024.

  5. Project Ratings: Both the progress towards the achievement of PDO and the implementation progress (IP) have
     been rated as Moderately Satisfactory given the progress made on both the subprojects, despite initial delays.
     There are no overdue audit reports for the project.

  6. Rationale for Restructuring: This restructuring paper proposes the extension of the Project Closing Date. Since the
     second subproject, 100 MW Floating Solar PV Subproject in Getalsud, Jharkhand will require 18 months for design,
     installation, and commissioning, the Project closing date needs to be extended by 12 months from December 31,
     2024, to December 31, 2025. Update to the Results Framework is also required to reflect the proposed extension
     of the closing date. A subsequent restructuring to reflect the downward revisions in the results matrix (upon
     downsizing of the project amount as part of second restructuring) will be undertaken later.

II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

  1. Change in closing date: The contract for the 100 MW Floating Solar PV Subproject in Getalsud, Jharkhand was
     awarded in May 2024. As per the contract, the contractor has 18 months for the design, installation, and
     commissioning of the subproject. Consequently, it is proposed that the loan closing date be extended by 12
     months from December 31, 2024, to December 31, 2025. This is the first request for extension of the project
     closing date.
  2. Change in Results Framework: The Results Framework of the Project needs to be updated to reflect the extension
     of the project closing date by 12 months from December 31, 2024, to December 31, 2025. Further, in line with the
     institutional mandate of periodic reporting on the World Bank’s corporate scorecard, the indicators ‘Generation
     Capacity of Renewable Energy (other than hydropower) constructed (MW)’ and ‘Renewable Energy Generated
     (megawatt-hour (MWh))’ will now also be reported as Corporate Results Indicator (CRI) - Renewable Energy
     Capacity Enabled. The proposed changes to the Results Framework are detailed in Table 1. A subsequent
     restructuring to reflect the downward revisions in the results matrix (upon downsizing of the project amount as
     part of second restructuring) will be undertaken later.
