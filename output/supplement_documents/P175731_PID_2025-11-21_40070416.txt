B. Introduction and Context

1.       The SADC Regional Statistics Project, approved on June 8, 2023, seeks to modernize participating countries’
data ecosystems by addressing gaps in data production, harmonization, and use. The PDO is to strengthen the
institutional capacity of SADC and Participating Countries to produce, disseminate and use quality statistics while
increasing regional harmonization and collaboration. The project includes seven Recipients: Comoros (US$1.5 million),
Madagascar (US$45 million), Malawi (US$15 million), Zambia (US$30 million), São Tomé and Príncipe (US$3 million),
Mozambique (US$60 million) and the SADC Secretariat (US$10 million), for a total amount of US$164.5 million.

2.    Project description. The closing date of the project is October 31, 2028. The project comprises the following
components:

          • Component 1: The Regional Component supports the implementation of continental and regional
            agreements on statistics – specifically, SADC’s Regional Strategy for Development of Statistics (RSDS) 2020-

              of core social and economic statistics.
            • Component 2: The Country Component finances key data collection and production (such as National
              Accounts, CPI, Household and Expenditure Surveys and Agriculture and Livestock Censuses), as well as
              statistical capacity building and data use activities.
            • Component 3: Project Management ensures the implementation of the project in each participating entity.
            • Component 4: Contingency Emergency Response Component (CERC).

    3.      The proposed Comoros Additional Financing (AF) for the SADC Regional Statistics Project in the amount of
    US7 million will scale up activities under this project. Over the past decade, the Government has worked to strengthen
    national statistical capacity, particularly through the National Statistical Institute - Institut National de la Statistique et
    des Etudes Economiques et Démographiques (INSEED), recognizing the strategic role of statistics in decision-making and
    public policy. The national statistical system undertook key sectoral surveys in health (MICS 2021), agriculture (RGA2
    2025), education (2025), poverty (Household Budget Survey 2024), and economic statistics (national accounts and price
    data 2025), with the poverty and economic statistics supported by the project. Building on the project’s success, the
    additional financing will deepen data on agriculture and economic statistics, expand coverage to private sector and
    demographic trends, and further develop capacity for data-driven decision-making.


    Sectoral and Institutional Context

    4.      Support for building a strong monitoring and evaluation system for the Plan Comores Emergent (PCE). The
    PCE is the national development strategy for 2019-2030, approved in December 2022, which aims to achieve
    sustainable, strong, inclusive and equitable economic growth in Comoros and requires a strong monitoring and
    evaluation system with reliable indicators to guide public action. Despite progress, the statistical system still struggles
    to meet growing demands, especially due to budget constraints. The activities supported under the additional financing
    would provide critical insights for guiding public action.

    5.      Strong national data systems support job creation and economic growth by improving the quality, timeliness,
    and credibility of the information that governments and firms rely on. Regular, reliable household surveys, business
    censuses, and price statistics enable policymakers to target social programs, design labor market interventions, and
    manage inflation with less uncertainty. Better data also lower the cost of capital—credit markets can price risk more
    accurately—and help firms identify opportunities, which expands formal employment and raises productivity.

    6.       Supporting the production and use of data to inform decision-making is aligned with the World Bank strategy.
    The World Bank has been a critical partner in developing data to guide public action through the Comoros Statistics
    Project (P159437), closed in 2020, and the current project. The importance of strengthening the statistics system to
    enable evidence-based decision making is recognized in the current Country Partnership Framework for Comoros.1 In
    particular, the proposed scale-up activities are in line with the CPF objectives to increase country capacity to gather and
    release statistics in accordance with international standards.

 D. Project Description

7.      Proposed changes. The AF will continue supporting ongoing activities (national account and prices statistics) and
adding new activities (population census, economic census, thematic agricultural surveys, and refurbishments of offices)
aligned with the project objectives. The targets for results indicators will be revised to reflect AF activities. The
implementation of the AF will maintain current achievements and expand statistical production to additional areas,
including social and demographic sectors.

Subcomponent 2.1 Close data production gaps

8.      The AF will support the 2027 General Population and Housing Census (RGPH5), following the last census
conducted in 2017 with World Bank support. This Population Census is part of the 2020 round recommended by the
United Nations to help Comoros maintain the 10-year census cycle, and it is expected to be fully digital: for both
cartography, general enumeration, and post-census survey. The AF will secure financing for the core activities of the
census (preparatory pilots and main data collection for both cartography and household enumeration, and production of
preliminary results). The Government will contribute funding towards sensitization and coordination, and UNFPA will
provide technical assistance. Financing for the Post Census Survey is expected to be covered by other donors.

9.      Additionally, an Economic/Business Census will be carried out to generate essential data on the structure and
size of the national economy, including the informal sector. The Plan Comores Émergent (PCE) requires a robust
monitoring and evaluation system, supported by reliable indicators, particularly in the field of economic statistics.
However, to date, the structure of the national economy is only partially known: the business register, industrial
production indices, and statistical and tax declarations remain either non-existent or unused, and the prevalence of the
informal sector still high.

10.     The project will also fund one round of Annual Agricultural Surveys to complement the ongoing General Census
of Agriculture (RGA), providing thematic data to support agricultural policy, sectoral planning, and monitoring of the
Sustainable Development Goals. INSEED has conducted the Comoros Second Agriculture Census (RGA-2), and final results
are expected by December 2025. This operation was fundamental in providing a statistical inventory of all resources,

farms, practices, and constraints in the agricultural sector, including fisheries. However, the RGA provides only a general
reference for the agroecological environment, infrastructure, rural organizations, land practices, climate risks, and support
services. To enhance its usefulness, it will be complemented by Agriculture Surveys — in-depth thematic surveys that can
support sectoral planning and formulation of accurate agricultural policies, as well as a robust dashboard for monitoring
and evaluating national and international development agendas such as the SDGs. INSEED plans to do four rounds of
Agricultural Surveys, and the AF will cover one of these rounds.

11. Continuing activities under this subcomponent include support for National Accounts and Consumer Price Index
(CPI). For National Accounts, the additional funding will consolidate the newly rebased accounts developed during the
project’s first phase and ensure regular updates. This will also enable the production of supplementary tables such as
input-output tables. For the Consumer Price Index (CPI), the project will build on price data collected earlier to help INSEED
finalize a new harmonized CPI using 2024 as the base year, improving the accuracy and consistency of inflation
measurement across the country.

Subcomponent 2.2: Strengthening Institutional Capacity.

2.2.1 Enhance institutional frameworks and statistical capacity

12.     Under this subcomponent, the AF introduces new activities aimed at building long-term statistical capabilities
in Comoros. One key initiative is the training of statistical assistants, which seeks to transform the University Institute of
Technology’s Statistics Department into a national center of excellence. This responds to the growing demand for skilled
technicians and data analysts. Other new capacity building activities include support to the Economic Reform Directorate
of the Ministry of Finance, enhancing its technical, material, and human capacity to better formulate, monitor, and
evaluate national policies through an improved command of macro modeling.

13.     In addition to these new efforts, several ongoing activities in this subcomponent will continue to receive
support. Scholarships for RESA (Reseaux des Ecoles de Statistique Africaines) students, supported by the project, will help
develop statistical talent, with INSEED planning to implement contractual agreements requiring beneficiaries to work
within the agency for two to three years after completing their training. The project will also continue to provide support
to the General Commissariat of Planning by enhancing its technical, material, and human capacity to better plan, monitor,
and evaluate national policies.

14.     Capacity building, mainstreaming international standards and harmonization of methodologies. The AF will
include funding for participation in regional capacity-building events and technical assistance from global experts. Given
the SADC level 4 restrictions on Comoros affecting attendance of INSEED in technical workshops and trainings organized
by SADC, the PIU is engaged in establishing a specific action plan aiming to compensate this gap. The Action Plan is built
from the SADC Capacity Building Program in order to ensure (i) a similar level of capacity as in other SADC participating
countries, and (ii) harmonization of methodologies at regional level, as well as compliance with international standards
when implementing censuses and surveys. INSEED will seek entities that can provide the same TA, such as IMF/AFRITAC
SOUTH, and other academic and research institutions from francophone regions in west and central Africa.

2.2.2 Upgrade equipment, operational statistical inputs, and ICT

15.      Rehabilitation of INSEED office. Another new activity is the rehabilitation and extension of the INSEED building,
which will upgrade the current colonial-era facility that is vulnerable to climate hazards (seasonally floods, cyclones) to
ensure a safer and more functional space for the national statistics office. The current building no longer meets
requirements for security, data storage, and housing of technical equipment. Renovating the INSEED building would
contribute to the overall performance of the National Statistical System, and the long-term success of the STATCAP-KM
project. The civil works consists of making available a modern server room to store the data produced by the project and
by INSSED in general. The renovation will also provide space for a critical number of qualified personnel. Additionally, an
extension of the office building is expected to host a conference room and office space. This is a strategic priority to ensure
the sustainability of statistical production.

16.     The AF will fund INSEED’s equipment needs, including tablets for data collection. INSEED plans to modernize its
refurbished office with upgraded desks, meeting rooms equipped with laptops, videoconferencing tools, and other IT
technologies. Additionally, the AF will finance a server room for data storage, connectivity, and security solutions.

Sub-component 2.3: Promote data dissemination, use, and open access

17.     The AF will support dissemination activities to promote the uses of data collected by all national and international
stakeholders, such as workshops and broadcasts in local media programs. These activities include launch and
dissemination of census and surveys outputs, data user satisfaction workshops, and consultative workshops. INSEED
follows an open data access policy as part of its 2011 Statistical Law. The AF will help strengthen INSEED’s capacity to
manage legal aspects of data sharing and address privacy concerns.

18.    Component 3: Project Management will cover the cost of PIU staff and costs related to project implementation
and monitoring and evaluation, operating costs, training for PIU staff, project audit and other expenses required to
complete the AF activities.

19.     The Results framework and Monitoring and Evaluation Plan. The AF will be implemented under the original
project result framework, with revisions to account for AF activities.

 Summary of Screening of Environmental and Social Risks and Impacts
 The environmental and social risk rating has been classified as Moderate and the environmental and social
 performance of the parent project satisfactory for Comoros. Key adverse environmental and social risks and impacts
 relate to ensuring that (i) minor civil works and installation of ICT equipment are managed adequately considering

mitigation hierarchy, particularly in relation to construction waste generation and occupational health and safety
issues; (ii) the replacement, transportation and disposal of old ITC equipment (e-waste) as well new acquired are
undertaken properly; (iii) TA activities including face-to-face trainings, workshops and statistical survey follows
prevention measure against COVID-19; (iv) the Terms of Reference for TA type-2 activities (review or development of
new policies, plans & strategies) have been reviewed and approved by WB to ensure consistency with the ESF; (v) any
statistical guidelines and frameworks established under the project include considerations of digital data protection
and digital security; (vi) there is appropriate stakeholder engagement at the regional and national level with all
stakeholders, including civil society and marginalized groups, in a manner that is understandable and transparent and
explains the benefits and impacts of the activities; (vii) labor conditions, especially with regards to HR restructuring
activities, salary re-adjustments and skills needs assessments, as well as occupational health and safety (minor cuts
and injuries, road safety, etc. during work); (viii) ensuring that inclusion and data protection covers vulnerable and
marginalized groups; and (iv) possible risks of sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment (SEA/SH) at the
work place and during the roll out of surveys/censuses. Capacity of the PIU and respective implementing agencies to
manage environmental and social risk is expected to be limited, however, as the nature of the proposed
environmental and social risks are relatively low to moderate these risks and impacts should be manageable through
known mitigation measures. No land acquisition, economic displacement nor involuntary resettlement is expected and
the construction of INSEED office will occur within the office premises and is free from known encumbrances. The
following standards have been identified as relevant for the project; ESS1, ESS2, ESS3, ESS4, and ESS10. Environmental
and Social risk issues have been addressed in ESF instruments including the a Code of Practices for e-Waste
Management , COVID-19 Contingency Plan, Labor Management Procedures, and well as SEA/SH rIsk mitigation
measures already developed under parent project; and Environmental and Social Commitment Plan, as well as the
Stakeholder Engagement Plan have been updated to consider AF activities, and a Check list ESMP is finalized for the
rehabilitation works. The Grievance mechanism is operational but should be adapted on the scope of project activities
to ensure accessibility. An Environmental and social specialist is in place to support the implementation of these
measures.


E. Implementation

Institutional and Implementation Arrangements

20.      The project institutional and implementation arrangements remain unchanged, with emphasis on the following
improvements. The Project Implementation Unit within INSEED will remain responsible for the implementation of all
activities financed by the project. As the RGPH5 is co-funded from other sources, INSEED will have a census management
unit that will coordinate the technical activities of the census and manage counterpart funds. The census management
unit will work in close collaboration with the PIU, and the project’s PIU will execute Bank funded activities and ensure
compliance with all World Bank procedures. INSEED will ensure the operationalization of its Board of Administration
and officially adopt its oversight role in the implementation of activities.
