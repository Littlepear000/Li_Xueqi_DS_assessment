    I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Project background
1. This Restructuring Paper seeks Division Director’s approval to restructure the Project with the extension of the eligibility
disbursement date of the Recipient Executed Single-Donor Trust Fund to Support the SOLEIL Project (TF074372) by 2 months
- from April 30th, 2026 to June 30th, 2026 - in order to allow completion of activities included under this TF. This constitutes
the third restructuring of the Project. The Project was approved in February 2020 and became effective in September 2020.
Following a restructuring in June 2023, the project cost was reduced from US$100 million to US$63 million, including the
cancellation of the mini grid activity. A first AF approved in February 2026 focused on scaling up results-based connections,
strengthening sustainability through improved connectivity, and extending the closing date to March 1, 2028. A second AF,
which included the Recipient Executed Single-Donor Trust Fund to Support the SOLEIL Project (TF074372), was approved in
March 2026.
2. Project Description. The SOLEIL-Nyakiriza Project is designed to provide energy services to promote human capital
development, enhance productivity of rural enterprises, and reach poor and vulnerable populations, including women. The
Project focuses on: (i) electrifying up to 354 schools and 362 health centers with modular solar PV systems and implementing a
multi-layered maintenance approach; (ii) providing 65,000 off-grid solar products to poor and vulnerable households; (iii)
distributing 300,000 clean and efficient cookstoves to households and schools; and (iv) strengthening the enabling environment
through improved policies, regulations, private sector participation, and capacity building for off-grid solar energy and clean
cooking solutions.
3. The PDO is to expand access to energy services for households, enterprises, schools, and health centers in rural areas of
Burundi and will be achieved through three components: (i) Energy Services for Schools and Health Service Centers (Original
cost US$27 million, Updated cost US$28.23 million); (ii) Energy Services for Households (Original cost US$17 million, Updated
cost US$17.5 million); and (iii) Technical Assistance, Capacity Building and Project Implementation Support (Original cost US$19
million, no change). The PDO remains highly relevant to the country and is in line with the World Bank’s mission, the M300
initiative and SDG7 to achieve universal access to electricity by 2030.
B. Implementation status
4. The Project is implemented by a Project Implementation Unit within the Ministry of Mining Resources, Energy, Industry
and Trade (Ministère des Ressources Minières, Energetiques, de l’Industrie et du Commerce or “MRMEICT”1), on behalf of the
Republic of Burundi. The line Ministry’s main role is to lead national energy policy design, oversee project implementation, and
monitor sector results.
5. Progress towards achievement of the PDO and overall implementation are rated ‘Moderately Satisfactory’. The Project
implementation regained momentum due to strategic actions, including the 2023 and 2026 restructurings, the appointment of
a new Project coordinator, and the signing of key contracts including the RBF. Progress toward achieving the PDO is advancing
steadily, with already 240 000 people connected to electricity. Electrification of health facilities is now essentially complete,
with 361 out of 362 health centers electrified and fully operational, while school installations are actively underway (45),
contributing to the end target of 353 schools equipped with standardized modular solar PV systems. Under the private sector
component, and as of February 2026, grants and RBF mechanisms to 51 companies are operational, with US$8.75 million
committed (53 percent of the total allocation) and with US$4 million disbursed supporting increased household connections
toward the target of 402,000 people gaining access to electricity (240 000 people reached so far) and 1.8 million people
accessing clean cooking solutions (1.3 million people reached).
6. Under Component 1, the electrification of health centers has been nearly completed, with all installations functional and
only one site pending replacement. School electrification is progressing, with initial batches already completed and a rapid
scale-up underway, although some delays related to equipment delivery and logistics may affect the timeline, with completion

expected towards mid-2026. The technical approach continues to emphasize innovative, modular, and scalable solar PV systems
that can be expanded as demand grows. Under the school subcomponent, significant progress has been made in constructing
kitchen shelters and installing improved institutional cookstoves, with half of them already commissioned. The maintenance
strategy is being operationalized through multiple complementary layers: integrating maintenance payments into the health
PBF (in preparation), rehabilitating and equipping district-level maintenance workshops (completed), providing a year-long
training program for biomedical and solar PV technicians (ongoing), establishing a national maintenance center (in preparation),
and developing a centralized remote monitoring platform (proposed under this additional financing). All related agreements
and documentation are being put in place. This work is firmly anchored in the energy–health–education nexus, ensuring
sustainability and long-term service delivery.
7. Under Component 2, the project established the Solar Energy and Cooking Fund (FESEC) managed by an independent
fund manager Bamboo Capital Partners. The fund provides start-up, growth, and RBF grants to private companies to supply
off-grid solar PV products and clean cookstoves, while also ensuring product quality and after-sales monitoring. To promote
sustainability and local ownership, targeted support has been provided to women entrepreneurs. As of March 2026, 53 percent
of the total US$16.5 million allocation has been committed, with US$4 million disbursed. To date, the fund has supported 25
solar companies, reaching 40 000 households and facilitating the sale of 94,017 products. In the clean cooking sector, 30
contracts have been signed, with start-up and growth grants disbursed, resulting in the sale of 218,320 improved cookstoves.
Overall, 12 women-led companies have been supported. In addition, the Private Sector Technical Assistance Facility was created
to provide technical assistance to beneficiaries and other stakeholders such as microfinance institutions (MFI), further
strengthening the enabling environment. Recent learnings also highlight the critical role of consumer financing in overcoming
affordability barriers for rural households, with FESEC beneficiaries leveraging Village Savings and Loan Associations,
cooperatives, and MFI to expand access to affordable energy solutions in rural Burundi.
8. Component 3 has been reinforcing the policy, institutional, and technical foundations for a sustainable and resilient
energy sector in Burundi, with a focus on decentralized energy solutions. Under this component, the project strengthens
technical assistance and capacity-building in Burundi’s energy sector. It supports analytical work, advisory services, and
institutional development to advance the World Bank’s engagement and help the GoB achieve its M300 targets through the
National Energy Compact. Key activities include: (i) least-cost power system planning and investment coordination through the
Energy Sector Working group; (ii) promotion of distributed renewable energy and clean cooking solutions, including off grid and
clean cooking quality standards, national e-waste strategy, national strategy for clean cooking and revised energy policy; (iii)
sector reform and institutional strengthening, such as the revision of implementation decrees from the revised electricity law;
and (iv) human capital development through a new Renewable Energy Master’s Program launching in 2025–2026 at the
University of Burundi (with 30% of female students).
9. The Financial Management (FM) is rated ‘Moderately Satisfactory’ while Procurement remains “Moderately
Satisfactory”. The restructuring will retain the existing financial management arrangements under the parent project, with the
PIU within MRMEICT demonstrating satisfactory FM performance through timely reporting and consistently unqualified audit
opinions; staffing stability will be ensured and reinforced as needed to manage the additional workload under the restructuring.
The PCU will maintain dedicated accounting records for each source of funds, and consolidated IFRs will continue to be prepared
on a quarterly basis. The external auditor, whose ToRs will be updated to reflect the scope of the restructuring, will audit the
combined financial statements annually. The overall FM risk for the restructuring is assessed as Substantial, consistent with the
parent project, subject to continued strengthening of contract management and timely updating of financial records in line with
ongoing supervision recommendations. There are no outstanding external audit reports, and the most recent external audit
opinion was unqualified. On procurement, while contract management capacity constraints remain, implementation has
improved significantly, with most contracts completed or under implementation and only four activities remaining. To mitigate
residual risks, strengthened, risk-based contract management is being implemented, supported by the recruitment of a Senior
Procurement Consultant to oversee high value contracts.

10. The environmental and social risks are rated as Moderate and the E&S compliance performance for the Project is
currently ‘Moderately Satisfactory’. All required E&S instruments have been prepared, adopted and disclosed in-country and
on the World Bank external website. These include Environmental and Social Management Framework, Stakeholder
Engagement Plan, Labor Management Procedures, and Environmental and Social Commitment Plan. E&S assessment and
management. The AF will build on the existing Environmental and Social Management tools for the original financing of the
Project which guides subproject screening, risk categorization, and ESMP preparation, review, and disclosure.
11. All legal covenants are complied with. There are no remaining covenants.
12. Rationale for the restructuring. The processing of the GIZ financing - including the approval process and the fund transfer
between GIZ and IDA - has taken longer than initially anticipated. As a result of these delays, additional time is required to
ensure the proper implementation of planned activities. The restructuring aims at extending the grant closing date from April
30th, 2026 to June 30th, 2026. The World Bank team will work closely with the PIU to implement activities defined under the
RETF given the extended timeframe. The World Bank team will support the PIU in making sure to submit and process all pending
payments within the reduced grace period of 4 months (instead of 6 months as initially planned). It should be noted that the
grant end disbursement date remains unchanged and is set for October 30, 2026.
13. Without this restructuring, the implementation of the planned activities will not be possible before the end of the current
grant closing date.


  II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES


14. This restructuring will extend the grant closing date of the Recipient Executed Single-Donor Trust Fund to Support the
SOLEIL Project (TF074372) by 2 months, from April 30th 2026, to June 30th, 2026 without affecting the grant end disbursement
date that is set for October 30, 2026.
15. The PDO remains relevant and unchanged. The PDO is well aligned with the GoB’s objectives set in the Energy National
Compact.
16. The extension of the grant closing date will allow the completion of the activities defined under this TF. It includes the
strengthening of connectivity to remotely track solar asset performance in public facilities electrified under the Project, with
716 Public facilities with solar systems connected to the remote monitoring platform and 400 public facilities provided with
internet connectivity. In addition, it includes the expansion of access via targeted RBF in surrounding communities to an
additional 3,000 households.
17. Institutional arrangements remain the same. The World Bank will closely work with the PIU to speed up the
implementation of these activities and ensure they are completed within the extended timeframe.
18. The extension of the grant closing date implies a reduced grace period between the grant closing date and grant-end
disbursement date from 6 months to 4 months. DFI as well as CTR provided their non objection regarding this reduction. The
World Bank team will work closely with the PIU to make sure all disbursements and payments are made within this reduced
grace period.
