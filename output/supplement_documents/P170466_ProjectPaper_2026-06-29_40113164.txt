I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING
1.
The Investing in Forests and Protected Areas for Climate-Smart Development (IFPA-CD) Project was prepared
to support the forest, conservation, and tourism sectors in the country. Its Project Development Objective (PDO) is to
(i) improve sustainable management of forests and protected areas (PA) in target landscapes and (ii) increase benefits to
communities from forests in target landscapes. The project aims to improve management and economic productivity of
forest ecosystems. It combines investments in forest management in both state- and community-managed lands and
focuses on improving the management of forests, increasing revenues for sustaining forests, and supporting resilient
livelihoods. The project has a strong job-creation element, both through direct activity support and indirectly through
planned investments in wood processing and tourism facilities. The project was approved by the Board of the International
Development Association (IDA) on April 23, 2020 and became effective on August 18, 2021. The project was formally
launched in March 2022, as the restrictions related to the novel coronavirus (COVID-19) were starting to ease. The project
is implemented by the Ministry of Water and Environment (MWE) and Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA), working in close
coordination with the Ministry of Tourism, Wildlife and Antiquities and the Office of the Prime Minister (OPM) - the latter
two agencies having general government mandates on tourism and refugee hosting, respectively. Uganda Timber
Growers’ Association (UTGA) is an implementing agency for a small set of activities related to production forestry.
2.
The project was restructured twice. The first restructuring was approved in December 2024 to refine the PDO
and better define the project’s geographic scope.1 The second restructuring was approved in November 2025 to (i) remove
the National Forestry Authority (NFA) as a project implementation agency following its dissolution as part of the
government process of agencies’ rationalization, transferring ex-NFA activities to MWE; (ii) adjust the scope of activities
to enable more efficient use of project resources within the remaining project period (including related revisions of the
results framework and disbursement categories); (iii) further refine the definition of the project target landscapes; and
(iv) mitigate risks associated with project support for infrastructure and equipment for PA management. Four
disbursement conditions were introduced as part of the November 2025 restructuring – of these, three have been
complied with to date: related to the signing of an implementation agreement between MWE and UTGA,2 related to the
adoption of standard operating procedures (SOPs) for law enforcement by UWA,3 and related to MWE having in place
satisfactory fiduciary and staff provisions for implementation of activities previously under NFA.4
3.
The project has four components. The project’s structure responds to the need to invest in development of the
forestry and tourism sectors (with Component 2 supporting economic investments in these sectors). Component 1 focuses
on improving PA management practices, while Component 3 targets the improved landscape management in refugee
hosting areas (RHA). Component 4 supports project management and monitoring. Activities under Subcomponent 1.4 and
Component 3 are fully financed with grant resources, through a blend of an IDA grant and a grant from the IDA18 Regional
Sub-Window for Refugees and Host Communities (RSW).
4.
Implementation progress (IP). Commencement of implementation of project activities was slow due to the
delayed effectiveness, related to lengthy internal approval processes by the Government of Uganda (GoU) and COVID-19related restrictions. Effective implementation started with the project launch in March 2022. Project performance had
been in a satisfactory range until early 2025, as reported in the Implementation Status and Results Reports. In view of
continued implementation delays, both the PDO and IP ratings were downgraded to Moderately Unsatisfactory in May
2025. The ratings were further downgraded to Unsatisfactory for PDO and IP by the most recent mission in May-June
2026. Activities implemented by UWA and NFA have made substantial progress. However, the progress achieved by the

II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES
6.
The GoU requested partial cancellation of project funds, in the total amount of SDR30.7 million,5 or US$42.1
million6 equivalent (close to 30 percent of the current commitment) in a letter dated May 4, 2026, received by the Bank
on May 6, 2026. This cancellation is processed through a separate cancellation notice dated June 24, 2026. Cancellation is
effective as of May 6, 2026, the date of receipt of the GoU cancellation request by the Bank. Annex 2 includes the revised
disbursement categories’ allocations, for the record. After the cancellation, total revised IDA commitment is
SDR77,287,877, including SDR43,908,824 under the IDA credit and 33,379,053 SDR under the IDA grant. This is equivalent
to US$106.1 million of IDA financing.
7.
This third project restructuring is proposed to adjust the project scope consistent with the cancellation of funds,
by narrowing project scope. This restructuring was discussed with the GoU counterparts during the February-March 2026
implementation support mission, as recorded in the mission aide memoire, and requested in a letter dated May 4, 2026.
8.
The current overall risk rating of the project is High and is not expected to change with the restructuring. This is
due to a number of existing risks that are assessed as having high likelihood of occurring and high impact in case they
occur, specifically sector strategies and institutional capacity for implementation and sustainability (due to the ongoing
merger of NFA into MWE, including absorption of the former NFA staff working in field positions), fiduciary (due to high
procurement risks), and environmental and social (E&S) and stakeholder risks (due to the environmental and social
sensitivity of the project areas). The existing mitigation measures, including close supervision, reviews, and appropriate
mitigation measures as outlined in the project E&S instruments, will be maintained until the project closing.

IV. DETAILED CHANGE(S)
Summary Description
Operation Abstract: Summary Description of Proposed Operation
The project supports a landscape approach to improve management and economic productivity of forest ecosystems
and combines investments focusing on improving the management of forests and increasing livelihoods of the largely
rural population, as well as making targeted investments in refugee-hosting areas. Target landscapes include selected
protected areas (national parks, wildlife reserves, central forest reserves) and the areas within 10 kilometer radius of
their boundary, located in the Albertine Rift and the West Nile Region, as well as 19 districts in the refugee hosting
areas. Project components are briefly summarized as follows: • Component 1 focuses on improving management of
government-managed forest and wildlife protected areas to ensure they can continue to generate revenues and
provide important environmental services; • Component 2 aims to increase revenues and jobs from these forest and
wildlife protected areas through targeted investments in tourism and productive forest; • Component 3 encourages
establishment of greater tree cover in refugee-hosting landscapes outside protected areas, supporting sustainable
forest management and landscape resilience on private and customary land. This component is wholly funded by the
IDA 18 Refugee Sub Window and IDA grant, supporting project activities in 19 districts in the refugee hosting areas.

These districts have been selected because they are located in the project target areas, and they host refugee
communities or are within a 5-kilometer radius of the refugee settlements. In order to ensure appropriate coverage
and landscape contiguity, the project works in clusters of districts, based on the boundaries set as of July 1, 2010. This
allows the project to benefit from the technical capacity of local government staff in the original districts, while
securing continuity in cases of creation of new districts. Component 4 supports overall project management. The
project is implemented by the Ministry of Water and Environment and the Uganda Wildlife Authority, with the Uganda
Timber Growers’ Association leading implementation of a small set of production forestry activities. Activities related
to tourism are coordinated with the Ministry of Tourism, Wildlife and Antiquities and activities in the refugee hosting
areas are coordinated with the Office of the Prime Minister.

Annex 3. Project Implementation Progress by Component
1.
Component 1: Improved management of protected areas. Under Component 1, the project has so far financed
(i) improvement of infrastructure and equipment for PA management, including maintenance of nine kilometers (km) of
forest roads by NFA, demarcation of 404 km of forest boundaries in twelve Central Forest Reserves (CFR),9 and purchase
of equipment for the NFA and UWA (field vehicles, motorcycles, field equipment (binoculars and radio equipment) and
equipment for the National Forest Monitoring System); (ii) investments aimed at the reduction of human-wildlife conflict
through community-supported boundary trenching and boardwalk barriers (32 km in total), construction of 89.8 km of
electric fences in Queen Elizabeth National Park (NP) (QENP) and Murchison Falls NP (MFNP), supporting farmers around
Bwindi Impenetrable NP to plant 200 acres of tea as animal deterrent, and reconstruction of 14.2 km of a buffalo wall in
Mgahinga Gorilla NP (MGNP), in close collaboration with local communities; (iii) removal of invasive vegetation by UWA
within PAs across over 4,618 hectares (ha); (iv) strengthening of community involvement in PA management through
support to the establishment of 19 Collaborative Forest Management (CFM) groups in 13 CFRs, 81 new collaborative
resource management (CRM) agreements under UWA, and support for park-adjacent communities through provision of
energy-saving stoves (over 4,400 built), rainwater harvesting systems with tanks (over 1,300 distributed and installed),
and livelihoods training for members of the CFM and CRM groups on beekeeping, craft-making, and tour guiding, with
special attention to Batwa communities (consistent with the project VMGPs) around Echuya CFR and Bwindi Impenetrable,
Semuliki, and Mgahinga Gorilla NPs, reaching over 200 Batwa beneficiaries; (v) restoration of degraded forest habitats
through enrichment and restoration planting of close to 8,000 ha in CFRs;10 and (vi) increased forest protection in RHAs
for CFRs and WRs located in proximity to refugee settlements, including improved management, forest restoration, and
invasive species removal. UWA had initiated civil works related to construction of staff outposts and accommodation in
all 10 project-supported PAs following the lifting of a related disbursement condition in February 2026.
2.
Component 2: Increased revenues and jobs from forests and wildlife protected areas. Under Subcomponent
2.1, IFPA-CD includes investments in tourism facilities to increase revenues and job opportunities from forests and wildlife
PAs, including, under MWE (ex-NFA), chimpanzee habituation in Kalinzu and Budongo CFRs to expand ecotourism
opportunities, opening and maintenance of close to 100 km of tourism trails in Budongo, Kalinzu, and Echuya CFRs,
installation of tourism information sign posts, and development of an online tourism central reservations system for CFRs.
UWA has opened 85 km of access roads to six new concession sites in QENP and MFNP, aimed at catalyzing private sector
investment. Construction of five visitor information centers under UWA11 is ongoing since September 2025. Other civil
works are ongoing for additional tourism infrastructure in NPs and WRs aimed at diversifying the tourism product offering
(these include tourist boardwalks (in MGNP and Semuliki NP), a picnic site (in Bwindi Impenetrable NP), climbing ladders
(in MGNP), stream crossings (in MFNP), and tourist gates and bird hides (in a number of UWA-managed PAs)). Under
Subcomponent 2.2, the project aims to support investments in productive forestry, although progress has been limited
to technical capacity building through support to NFC, development of a policy report on harvesting revenues from
plantations for future investments in production forestry, and the recently initiated support to UTGA. Support to the NFC
has enabled the development and approval of new wood processing courses at certificate and diploma levels, aimed at
strengthening the pipeline of skilled professionals needed to support Uganda’s forestry sector and forest-based
enterprises. The project is also initiating support to setting up of a wood processing training hub at NFC, including works

3.
Component 3: Improved landscape management in RHAs. Uganda hosts close to 2 million refugees12, many of
whom are settled near ecologically sensitive areas. This has intensified pressure on natural resources, leading to
deforestation, land degradation, and increased competition for wood fuel and other forest products. Under
Subcomponent 3.1, the project supports development of agroforestry systems on household plots in host communities,
through the engagement of a technical service provider (TSP). This activity is ongoing in all 19 target districts in RHAs, with
over 94,000 farmers reached to date with services and inputs (including training and extension services) and over 21,000
ha of agroforestry interventions supported. MWE has also engaged a TSP to support the establishment of small-scale
commercial woodlots by small-scale farmers on private land in the same districts; this activity was initiated in October
2025 and is ongoing, with over 3,100 ha of woodlots planted to date by the participating farmers. An assessment of
potential investments in community forests in Hoima, Kikuube, and Kiryandongo districts was completed. Additionally,
MWE is managing activities under Subcomponent 3.2 related to the sourcing (from the local tree farmers) and distribution
of firewood to persons with specific needs (PSN) (the most vulnerable individuals as defined by the OPM and United
Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) in the refugee settlements in the project landscape (24 settlements in
total). To date, over 115,000 PSNs in over 80,000 households have benefitted from provision of firewood. MWE's five
field-based firewood monitoring officers support and supervise this activity, in close cooperation with the OPM, district
forest officers, and the UNHCR. Purchase of firewood has so far benefited close to 180 tree farmers who are supplying
sustainably sourced plantation-grown eucalyptus of specified dimensions and moisture content, creating local economic
opportunities and jobs. This activity responds to the refugees’ emergency response needs; it has progressed well but not
to the degree planned. This component also provides operational support to the district forest offices and OPM for projectrelated supervision activities. The activities introduced with the November 2025 restructuring related to support to
planting a boundary buffer strip around selected refugee settlements and training for the community conservation staff
of UWA on fuel-efficient stove technologies have not yet been initiated.
4.
Component 4. Project management and monitoring has supported the implementing agencies in undertaking
regular project management and monitoring, as well as various due diligence activities related to financial management,
procurement, and E&S risk management, including roll-out and operation of the grievance redress mechanism and
revision of various E&S instruments as informed by project implementation.
