 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The project became effective on April 30, 2021. Progress for the project as a whole includes:
           Each state has constituted its State Project Steering Committee (SPSC).
           The states will endorse their respective annual work plans by September 30, 2021 following Bank feedback.
           NPCU and SPIUs will share the updated procurement plans with the Bank by September 30, 2021 for review and clearance, and
            subsequently obtain endorsement by their respective approving agencies by September 30, 2021.
  Information for each component is highlighted below, including both achievements and next steps.
  Component 1. Creating safe and accessible learning spaces:
  Sub-Component 1.1 (new school construction)
           Achievements include - (i) the project has finalized the selection of 400 eligible Junior Secondary Schools (JSSs) and 285 eligible Senior
            Secondary Schools (SSSs) for the construction of classroom blocks in existing Primary Schools (to upgrade to JSS), JSSs (to expand

          and/or upgrade to SSS) and SSSs(to expand), (ii) SPIUs presented their work plans for the sub-component - all the work plans were
          detailed and of reasonable quality after working closely with the WB team; Kano’s workplan was chosen as the best example for the other
          states to follow as they refine their plans, and (iii) the architect consultant deployed by NPCU prepared space standards for the
          construction of classrooms. However, the NPCU consultant is yet to prepare type designs alternativestandard architectural design
          options for various building blocks to be constructed under the project, site plans on modular basis, and obtain inputs on state-specific
          cost estimates on materials and labor – all of which are critical for the states and communities to initiate the design and procurement
          process, and subsequently the construction activities. M any of the preparatory activities such as training of Community Project
          Management Committees (CPMCs) (responsible for managing construction activities) and preparation/approval of design and bid
          documents have not progressed due to lack of project funds.
         Teacher recruitment and deployment plans which are requirements for disbursements under component 1, have been prepared and
          approved in all six participating states that need to do it. Three states have included the required cost in their state budgets. This is a
          requirement for disbursements.
         Next steps: SPIUs will develop and implement a detailed timeline for the next three months on preparatory activities (establishment,
          registration, training of CPMCs, preparation and approval of Environmental and Social Management Plans (ESMPs), drawings, cost
          estimates, bid documents, contract award) so that school construction activities can commence as planned, (b) NPCU will ensure that
          their architect consultant finalizes the standard design with due consultations with States for various building blocks to be constructed
          under AGILE by October 15, 2021, (c) SPIUs work closely with their state SUBEBs to ensure that their DPP staff engineers are available
          to provide technical support to schools and communities in the pre-design activities (e.g. topographical survey) and the design and
          procurement processes after the consultant at NPCU finalizes the standard designs, and (d) SPIUs deploy E&S consultant(s) immediately
          to ensure that the construction design and implementation phases comply with the project’s environmental and social requirements.
  Sub-Component 1.2 (school renovation)
         Achievements include - (i) the states have selected more than 3,700 secondary schools as the beneficiaries of small School Improvement
          Grants (SIGs) grants under this sub-component (WASH, learning-teaching materials, furniture, safe space and general safety/security
          measures such as school fences) but they have not finalized the list of eligible schools for large SIG grants for infrastructure/classroom
          expansion, (ii) all eligible schools have functional SBMCs, and have opened bank accounts for AGILE related activities, (iii) Kebbi state
          has trained all SBMCs using its own funds, (IV) SPIUs have initiated the preparation of SIPs – Kaduna state’s SIP template has been
          chosen as the example for other states to follow, (v) the states are using approved SIG Manual to carry out activities under the sub-
          component, and (vi) NPCU’s school renovation consultant is providing technical support to SPIUs on the design aspects of classroom
          spacing, windows, fencing, toilets, lighting and safe spaces.
         Next steps: (a) By October 15, 2021 SPIUs will complete the training of SBMCs which are responsible for managing SIG grants and
          school renovation activities, (b) by October 30, 2021 the states will verify and endorse the SIPs prepared by SBMCs, (c) by November 15,
          2021, SPIUs will disburse the first round of SIG grants to SBMCs accounts, (d) by December 30, 2021, SPIU’s E&S consultant(s) will
          submit their initial monitoring report on E&S compliance, and (e) the SBMCs will pay special attention to security measures and include
          these in the SIPs appropriately.
  Component 2. Fostering an enabling environment for girls
  Sub-component 2.1. Promoting social and behavior change through traditional rulers, campaigns, and advocacy
  NPCU and SPIUs will share their draft Terms of Reference (ToRs) for civil society organizations and /media partners or consultants with the Bank
  by September 30, 2021.
  Subcomponent 2.2. Providing girls with critical life skills and digital literacy skills
         The work plan for this sub-component includes mapping of existing activities and interventions by all partners in the areas of life skills and
          digital skills in the participating states. The mission discussed technical details on the mode of teaching-learning, including the possibility
          of using remote learning platforms as appropriate, and mapping what the states have developed during the COVID-19 pandemic in order
          to build on that. Draft ToRs for digital skills have already been developed and a preliminary list of schools has been shared by the states.
          The Gender Innovation Lab team presented the conceptual design and proposed methodology on the impact evaluation of life skills and
          digital skills component of this project and invited the states to participate in the randomized control trial (RCT)-based IE.
         By September 30, 2021, the SPIUs will share draft ToR(s) on the technical partner agencies for life skill and digital skills, an advanced
          draft of the latter is available already. By September 15, 2021, the SPIUs will finalize the list of schools that would host life and digital skills
          teaching-learning programs.
  Subcomponent 2.3. Providing financial incentives to the poorest households
         The sub-component provides conditional financial incentives (cash transfer or CCTs) to eligible girls enrolled in secondary education in
          project participating schools. While the states have sensitized the communities on the sub-component, the mission noted information gaps
          on both the technical design and the implementation arrangements.
         By October 30, 2021, the NPCU and SPIUs will finalize the technical design and implementation aspects of the financial incentives
          scheme so that eligible girl students can receive the first round of cash transfers within the calendar year 2021.

  Component 3. System Strengthening and M&E
       The mission noted that the project needs to significantly strengthen its system capacity at NPCU, SPIU and school level to implement
        multiple project interventions. During the mission, NPCU presented the current M&E staffing arrangements, and M&E work plan and an
        overall M&E framework for the project. The WB team provided comprehensive feedback. By September 30, 2021, NPCU and SPIUs will
        prepare a system strengthening work plan for Bank’s review
       By October 30, 2021, NPCU in collaboration with SPIUs, will prepare a detailed multi-level M&E strategy for the project and that would
        include the role of TPM, GRM, Iterative Beneficiary Monitoring, and the use of digital technologies and remote monitoring. The strategy will
        consider explicitly the participation of CSOs/NGOs to enhance accountability, transparency, and community involvement. By November
        30, 2021, the TPM partner agency would be deployed by the project.
