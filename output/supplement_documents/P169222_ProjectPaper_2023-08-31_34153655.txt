 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Project Status

1. The Sao Tome and Principe Girls’ Empowerment and Quality Education for All Project (PEREQT, per the acronym in
   Portuguese) was approved by the Board of Directors of the World Bank (WB or the ‘Bank’) on December 11, 2020,
   with financing of US$15 million provided by the International Development Association (IDA). An additional grant
   of US$2.2 million from the Global Partnership for Education Trust Fund (TF) was approved at the same time, for a total
   project financing of US$17.2 million. The project was declared effective on April 15, 2021. The IDA closing date is
   December 31, 2025, and the GPE closing date is August 31, 2023. As of August 10, 2023, the disbursement rates were
   25 percent and 43 percent for the IDA and GPE portions respectively.

2. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to equip girls with life skills and improve student learning outcomes
   for all. The PEREQT has five components: Component 1 (US$5.65 million) aims to empower girls through the
   acquisition of life skills and the promotion of a safe school environment; Component 2 (US$7.97 million) aims to tackle
   learning poverty; Component 3 (US$1.14 million) seeks to reach the most vulnerable with an early warning system
   and targeted programs; Component 4 (US$2.44 million) aims to support project management, monitoring and
   evaluation; and Component 5 is a zero-dollar Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC) to facilitate an
   emergency response, if required. Further details on activities can be found in Annex 1.

3. The Project Development Objective (PDO) and Implementation Performance (IP) ratings, after a challenging period
   during COVID-19 while activities started up, have improved to achieve ratings of Moderately Satisfactory on both
   dimensions. During the life of the project, the PEREQT has suffered delays due to the public health emergency,
   difficulties in recruiting the requisite staff for the Project Implementation Unit (PIU), and changes in counterparts due
   to changes in the government. However, activities have progressed well, with all indicators rated as Moderately
   Satisfactory or above, with the exception of Monitoring and Evaluation.

4. Overall, the PEREQT has made good progress towards achieving its PDO over the last 6 months. With regards to
   Component 1, the girls and boys’ clubs are now operational and on track to meet the PDO targets by the end of the
   project. Nearly a third of total expected beneficiaries have already been participating in the clubs (460 out of 1,500).
   The infrastructure activities relating to making schools more girl-friendly have also progressed well, with construction
   activities on two lots of schools poised to begin in the coming months. For Component 2, a series of learning
   assessments have already been rolled out, including both national and regional assessments, allowing Sao Tome and
   Principe to compare itself to several comparator countries in the sub-region. The training of preschool teachers is also
   well underway, and on track to achieve its indicators. After a slow start due to substantial deliberations on the terms
   of reference, the work on structured lesson plans and teacher policies is now proceeding. The extra time preparing
   the TORs will have been worth it, as it serves to further guarantee a certain level of ownership of the activities at the
   departmental level. However, activities under Component 3 have not progressed, due to limitations in school data
   management capacities on the part of the government, and the need to prioritize activities under project
   implementation. Finally, Component 4 has been implementing well, but is showing signs of being underfinanced, as
   the costs relating to the Project Management and Fiduciary Agency (AFAP, per the acronym in Portuguese) operations
   have been higher than those anticipated when the project was negotiated.

B. Rationale for Restructuring

5. As per the recommendations during the Mid-Term Review (MTR), this restructuring serves to consolidate project

   activities that have showed good results and eliminate those that have had less momentum under implementation.
   This restructuring would, first and foremost, formalize reallocations across components to account for increases in
   component costs, especially with regards to WASH infrastructure activities to make schools more girl-friendly. Second,
   it would eliminate the activities under Component 3 where implementation never got off the ground, due to school
   data management capacity limitations within the ministry. The component initially envisioned introducing three new
   programs: i) an early warning system to identify students at high risk of school dropout or failure, ii) an adaptive
   learning program for at-risk students, and iii) remedial and accelerated learning courses. The objectives of these
   additional programs will be achieved through activities under Components 1 and 2. In addition, the Results Framework
   (RF) will be revised to include new indicators and complementing existing indicators to give a better sense of both
   scale and quality of the results achieved. Finally, given modest delays in implementation, the closing date of the GPE-
   financed trust fund will be extended to allow sufficient time for the implementation of the revised project activities
   and disbursement of remaining funds.

II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

C. Proposed Changes

6. This paper seeks management approval for a Level 2 restructuring with the following proposed changes:

   (a) Reallocation of costs between components and disbursement categories: The PEREQT project will eliminate
       Component 3 and reallocate funds to Component 1.2, to cover shortfalls due to inflation-related increases in costs.
       Funds will also be reallocated toward Component 4 to cover additional investments in the country’s Education
       Management Information System, and to cover management cost overruns by AFAP.

                (b) Modification to the Results Framework (RF): In line with MTR recommendations, the RF will be revised mainly to
                    clarify methodologies and protocols for data collection and add some complementary intermediate indicators, as
                    well as adjust targets.

                        1. Revisions for clarification of methodologies and protocols: The third PDO indicator has been revised to
                           “Completion rate in secondary education” since this seeks to measure the same ultimate outcome as the
                           reductions in dropout that the previous formulation contained. The MTR recommended using the
                           completion rate since this is a better, more stable indicator for capturing quality education and success
                           for keeping girls in school than reductions in dropout, which vary widely from year to year in Sao Tome
                           and Principe. Otherwise, indicators have been updated based on new information, such as those relating
                           to the Programme for the Analysis of Education Systems (PASEC), where baseline data are now available.
                        2. New indicators: Two new indicators have been added to the RF to give a more complete picture of
                           activities relating to girls and boys’ clubs, to track the number of schools that are being adapted to
                           children living with disabilities, and to track the number of schools participating in the girl-friendly school
                           program beyond just the infrastructure upgrades. These indicators are, respectively: “Number of children
                           participating in girls and boys’ clubs”, “Schools with infrastructure adapted to children living with
                           disabilities”, and “Number of schools benefiting from girl-friendly programming”.
                        3. Adjust targets: In addition, the targets for some of the indicators have been adjusted to reflect modest
                           delays in project implementation, due to slower than expected preparatory activities, COVID-19-related
                           delays, and a change in government. Indicators relating to Component 3 have been deleted. None of the
                           changes imply significant changes to the number or type of beneficiaries.

                (c) Extension of Project closing date for the accompanying GPE grant (TFB3318): The GPE grant closing date was
                    originally scheduled for August 31, 2023, about two and a half years after the Board date (December 11, 2020).

                ANNEX 1. DETAILED IMPLEMENTATION PROGRESS – ACTIVITIES AND DISBURSEMENTS

1. Despite a slow start, the PEREQT project implementation has accelerated over the last six months. Progress of core
   activities under each sub-component is presented below.

       Component 1: Empowering Girls through the Acquisition of Life Skills and the Promotion of a Safe
        School Environment (IDA US$5.15 million, GPE US$0.50 million equivalent)

        Subcomponent 1.1: Imparting life skills and sexual and reproductive health (SRH) education

2. Activities under Subcomponent 1.1 are moving swiftly. Implemented by UNFPA, the girls and boys’ clubs have been
   established in nearly half of the secondary schools throughout the country. The curriculum was developed and
   adapted to the socio-cultural context of STP, with changes introduced early on based on lessons from implementation.
   Baseline data have been collected that will serve to measure the impact that the activities are having on the life skills
   of girls and boys.

        Subcomponent 1.2: Fostering a safer school environment

3. Tendering of the infrastructure activities is now underway, after some delays in the process of completing the
   preparatory activities for the school rehabilitation. The school designs have been submitted, reviewed, and
   approved. This includes safeguard management plans for each of the sites, as well as the scope of work for the
   technical supervision of the rehabilitation. The tendering process for the 15 schools is now underway, with contracts
   in the process of being negotiated and signed for the first two lots. The works are scheduled to start in September
   2023.


       Component 2: Tackling Learning Poverty (IDA US$7.27 million, GPE US$0.70 million equivalent)

        Subcomponent 2.1: Teacher professional development and provision of TLMs

4. The firm that will conduct the training of preschool teachers was hired and the training of trainers was conducted.
   An assessment of infrastructure needs for related distance learning activities and the purchase of ICT equipment was
   also carried out. The list of teachers was updated with the technical teams of all preschools. The list of playful and
   pedagogical materials is under development.

5. The process to select the firm that will develop TLMs is ongoing. Firms interested in developing structured lessons
   plans for teachers and students in grades 1 and 2 have been shortlisted. The process is proceeding with the request

        Subcomponent 2.3: Classroom observation and Learning assessment systems

7. The classroom observations and learning assessments have shown the most progress of all project activities. The
   TEACH classroom observation tool has been carried out, giving the country its first comprehensive snapshot of the
   quality of the teaching and learning process in STP. The country also participated in PASEC in 2022, the regional
   learning assessment of students in grades 2 and 6, and has begun activities for participating in PASEC 2024. Finally,
   the country is carrying out national learning assessments for students in basic and secondary education – Avaliação
   Aferida do Ensino Básico (AALEB) and Avaliação Aferida do Ensino Secundário (AALES) - under the project, thanks to
   substantial technical support provided by the Center for Public Policy and Education Assessment, which is ensuring
   that the learning assessments in basic and secondary school meet the highest international standards.

       Component 3: Reaching the Most Vulnerable (IDA US$0.39 million, GPE US$0.75 million equivalent)

8. Due to a changing context around COVID-19, these activities have not been implemented. The early warning system
   that was to be developed will be replaced by the newly gathered data through the learning assessments and classroom
   observation tool, serving as a monitoring tool for the quality of learning. Rather than developing remedial assistance,
   given how widespread the need for support is, the MECC is focusing on introducing structured lesson plans for all of
   the students, as the country seeks to bounce back from the shock of school closures during COVID-19 pandemic.
