I. PROGRAM STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Program Status
   Program Background
   1. This Project Paper seeks the approval of a restructuring of the Senegal Agriculture and Livestock Competitiveness
   Program for Results (PCAE - P164967, Cr.6616) to make upward revisions to the end of program target for a
   Disbursement Linked Indicators (DLI) as well as associated Disbursement Linked Results (DLR) under the “improved
   crop and livestock productivity and resilience Results Area of the Program. This would be the second restructuring and
   is based on a request by the Government of Senegal (GoS) dated November 10, 2023, to accommodate revisions made
   on the result framework during the Mid-Term Review (MTR) . PCAE was approved by the Board on May 12, 2020, and
   became effective on September 2, 2020. The Program Development Objective (PrDO) is to enhance productivity and
   market access of priority commodity value chains and livestock, in the extended groundnut basin and agro-pastoral
   areas. A first restructuring of the Program was approved on October 19, 2022, and consisted of adding US$20 million
   in International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) resources as co-financing to the Program and closing
   emerging incentive gaps that had the potential to undermine achievement of some DLIs.
   Program Implementation Status
   2. The Program MTR completed on Ma74, 2023 and the last implementation support mission held in January 2024
   indicates that implementation is on track. The Independent Verification Agent (IVA) has conducted three rounds of
   verification confirming achievement of several DLRs. The disbursement rate stands at 53 percent for IDA (US$81.56
   million disbursed out of 150 million) and 38 percent for IFAD (US$7.34 million disbursed out of 20 million) as of May
   22, 2024. To-date, achievement for most PrDO indicator targets is also on track with:
       (a) groundnut yields currently standing at 1.38 tons/ha against the target of 1.1 tons/ha expected by this time
           into implementation, and a final target of 1.5 tons/ha;
       (b) maize yields of 3.36 tons/ha in 2022/2023 against a target of 2.8 tons/ha expected by this time into
           implementation, and a final target of 3 tons/ha;
       (c) millet yields of 1.34 tons/ha against a target of 1.30 tons/ha expected by this time into implementation, and
           a final target of 1.5 tons/ha;
       (d) 319,786 tons in groundnut exports in 2021 compared to a target of 230,000 tons (139 percent achievement);
           and 105,458 tons exported as of 7 May 2024 against a target of 255,000 tons - 41 percent achievement and
           lower than targeted export in 2022/2023, mainly due to overall reductions in production and the attendant
           export restrictions imposed by government;
       (e) number of farmers reached with agricultural assets or services estimated at 200,334 compared to a target of
           225,000 expected this time into implementation (89 percent achievement) and a final target of 300,000. A
           total of 77,555 women have so far been reached with agricultural assets or services (39 percent of
           beneficiaries) compared to a target of 67,500 women expected to be reached this time into implementation
           and an end of program target of 90,000 women.
   3. With regards to the DLIs, the annual targets for 2021, 2022 or/and 2023 for seven out of ten DLIs of the Program
   have been achieved as per Table 1 below.

B. Rationale for Restructuring
       6. The MTR confirmed the relevance of the PrDO and the structure of the Program as well its alignment with the
       new Senegal Food Sovereignty Strategy. The three result areas remain relevant as well as the PrDO indicators and
       their targets. Therefore, there will be no change to the design of the Program, PrDO, Program Action Plan (PAP), or
       implementation arrangements. In retrospect, the government recognized the difficulty in measuring “the vaccination
       coverage rate” as a percentage (DLI4) mainly because of the lack of data on national livestock numbers/actual size of
       the national herd which would be required in computing such a percentage. Consequently, while important progress
       is being made in increasing the number of ruminants vaccinated, currently, this achievement cannot be satisfactorily
       reported on to allow for payments against the associated DLI. The proposed restructuring would address this
       challenge by allowing for reporting on the absolute number of vaccinated animals rather than the vaccination rate
       which has to be computed as a percentage.

                small ruminants plague” with annual targets set to 5,500,000 and 6,000,000 respectively in 2024 and 20251.
