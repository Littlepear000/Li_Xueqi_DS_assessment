I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

     A.        Proposed Restructuring
            1. This is the second restructuring of the Dominican Republic Resilient Agriculture and Integrated Water
               Resources Management Project (P163260), Loan No. 89120-DO (the “Project”), and responds to the request
               of the Government of the Dominican Republic (Letter MH-2025-005190, dated February 19, 2025). The
               proposed Level II Restructuring seeks the approval of the Division Director (DD) to modify the Project as follows:
               i) revision of activities and reduction of scope, ii) modification the results framework, and iii) update the
               disbursement estimates. These proposed changes are based on the findings of the Mid-Term Review conducted
               in September 2024, aiming to enhance and align project implementation with the achievement of development
               objectives. As a result of the restructuring, there will be no changes in the PDO, project components, financial
               management (FM) and procurement arrangements, project risks, environmental and social category, or in the
               loan amount.

      B.       Project Background and Status
            2. The Project was approved by the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) on
               December 13, 2018, with a loan agreement signed on January 28, 2020, for an amount of US$80 million. The
               signing of the Loan Agreement, which took 13 months, was delayed primarily because the loan was not
               included in the Borrower’s budget for the calendar year 2019, which only occurred at the end of 2019. When
               the Borrower was preparing to submit the package to Congress for approval, the global COVID-19 pandemic
               was declared, and the subsequent legislative sessions prioritized the COVID-19 response. In June 2020, the
               Borrower submitted a request to extend the effectiveness deadline beyond 18 months after Board approval,
               which was denied by the Bank. As a result, the Loan Agreement was terminated on July 26, 2020.

            3. The first restructuring of the Project represents the reinstatement of the Loan Agreement. On February 24,
               2021, the Dominican Republic, under a new administration, requested the reinstatement of the loan and the
               retroactive extension of the effectiveness deadline. The request was approved by the Regional Vice President
               on July 8, 2021, leading to the first restructuring of the Project, which also extended the original closing date
               to October 2, 2026 (cumulative 27 months from the original closing date). From the date of the Board approval
               to the declaration of effectiveness on January 11, 2022, 3 years and 1 month had elapsed. During this period,
               project implementation had not begun. Since the declaration of effectiveness, a span of 3 years and 2 months
               has passed, during which the Project has disbursed US$20.8 million, executed to date US$6.7 million (8.39
               percent of the total loan), and committed US$13.5 million.

            4. The latest Implementation Status and Results Report (ISR) was filed in March 2025. The Progress towards
               achievement of PDO and Overall Implementation are rated Moderately Unsatisfactory, as the Project is not on
               track to achieve its objectives by the Project closing date (October 2, 2026). The key issues that have negatively
               impacted project implementation are: (i) the staffing of the Project Implementation Unit (PIU), which took
               about 12 months; (ii) the resulting staffing shortages, which significantly delayed critical bidding processes,
               such as the design of hydraulic and sanitation infrastructure and the contract with the Food and Agriculture
               Organization (FAO) for technical assistance on agricultural investments; and (iii) the gap between project design
               and implementation, which led to a more than 35% increase in costs, affecting planned procurement processes.

            5. Additionally, the Project design includes an implementation framework with an integrated and multisectoral
               approach, grounded in interinstitutional collaboration, which further exacerbates coordination challenges.
               This framework involves four institutions: the Ministry of Agriculture (MA), the Ministry of Environment and
               Natural Resources (MMARN), the National Institute of Potable Water (INAPA), and the National Institute of

         Hydraulic Resources (INDRHI). The INDRHI hosts the Project Implementation Unit (PIU), responsible for
         management. The innovative framework has led to lengthy staff establishment and hiring processes, limiting
         the PIU's operational capacity.

     6. The Project Development Objective remains relevant and aims to contribute to improved sustainable
        landscape management and access to water supply and sanitation services in targeted river basins. The
        Project comprises four components: 1. Sustainable Productive Management of Agroecosystems (US$15.1
        million); 2. Improvement of Resilience and Management of Hydraulic Infrastructures (US$29 million); 3.
        Pollution Reduction through the Improvement of Water Supply and Sanitation Services in Priority Areas
        (US$24.8 million); and 4. Capacity Building for the Management and Governance of Natural Resources and
        Project Management (US$10.9 million). Under Component 1, progress is being made on the preparation of
        agricultural productive subprojects for cocoa, coffee, livestock, and rice. Additionally, critical procurement
        processes are underway, including the design of infrastructure for dam rehabilitation and public irrigation
        canals (Component 2), as well as the construction of sewerage and sanitation plants and associated
        environmental and social (E&S) studies (Component 3).


     7. Fiduciary ratings and status: Financial Management (FM) arrangements are currently rated as Moderately
        Satisfactory (MS). Expenses have started to be documented; the last Interim Financial Report (IFR) was
        delivered on time, and the 2024 audit is about to start. At this time, there are no overdue audits or audit reports
        which are not satisfactory to the Bank. Procurement performance is currently rated as Moderately Satisfactory
        (MS). The Project has improved procurement processes, advancing in contracts for sewerage system feasibility
        studies and dam safety designs. Agreements have been made to update the procurement plan and refine the
        market approach.

     8. Environmental and Social (E&S) performance of the Project is rated MU. The rating of OP 4.37 (Safety of
        Dams) is currently MU, and despite contracting a Dam Safety Expert Panel and delivering equipment to the
        dam management department, no progress has been made on essential safety studies for several dams.
        However, the PIU and partner institutions have environmental and social staff who have updated and published
        relevant instruments, including the Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMP) and
        Resettlement Action Plan (RAP), following Bank approval. With the PIU and partner institutions now fully
        staffed with E&S personnel and overall capacity strengthened, progress on critical activities is expected to
        accelerate.

C.      Rationale for Restructuring
     9. During the Mid-term Review conducted in September 2024, the Borrower and World Bank collaboratively
        assessed that a Project restructuring was needed to adjust its scope and extend the closing date by 27
        months. Since the current closing date is still 18 months away, the restructuring will be executed in two phases.
        The first phase will revise project activities, reduce the scope of work, and modify the results framework. A
        second restructuring to extend the closing date will be processed once the Project achieves critical milestones,
        including: (i) completion of key studies and the design of works under Component 2; and (ii) finalization of
        procurement packages for the execution of works under Components 2 and 3, ready for launch.

     10. The proposed restructuring will not alter the Project’s Development Objective, the components, or the
         institutional arrangements set out in the Legal Agreement. However, given the remaining timeframe as well
         as the impacts of inflation, some activities were adjusted, prompting the need to update the results framework
         accordingly. The main adjustments to the Components agreed upon during the MTR include:

             •    Under Component 1: The entire component will be executed.

                     •    For Component 4: The entire component will be executed.

             11. Actions toward the achievement of the PDO and the improvement of PIU capacity. The comprehensive and
                 multisectoral approach of the Project, its Development Objective, investments, and impact remain relevant to
                 the Borrower. The Project Implementation Coordination Unit (Unidad de Coordinación de Implementación de
                 Proyecto, UCIP) has made numerous efforts to achieve its full managerial, administrative, and operational
                 capacity, as evidenced at the time of the Mid-Term Review. These efforts include (i) completing the planned
                 staffing, (ii) simplifying the internal process of INDRHI to reduce the approval time for bureaucratic procedures,
                 (iii) making progress in the preparation of bidding processes, and (iv) implementing a management consultancy
                 to support the UCIP, approved after the Mid-Term Review. Due to this, the Project has made progress in the
                 delivery of goods and the preparation of bidding packages for the studies and designs of the works. The studies
                 and design of works for the rehabilitation of Mijo dam is under implementation.

    II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES


             12. The restructuring of the project includes the following changes:
                      i. Revision of scope and activities. Activities under Components 2 and 3 were revised and prioritized.
                         The irrigation canals, the rehabilitation of irrigation canals in Mijo, Ms. Bogaert, Villa Vásquez, Mao
                         Gurabo, and Luis Bogaert would be prioritized, with preference given to improving the critical sections
                         (first kilometers) and replacing control structures (Comp.2), and the construction of the wastewater
                         treatment and sanitary sewer of Esperanza (Comp. 3) would be dropped. The resources allocated for
                         the adjusted and dropped activities (approximately USD10.8 million) would not be cancelled but
                         reallocated within components 2 and 3 to cover the financial gap created by inflation to complement
                         the prioritized infrastructure projects.
                     ii. Disbursements Estimates. The disbursement estimate has been revised and can be found in Section
                         IV “Detailed Changes”: Disbursement Estimates. (Table – datasheet).
                    iii. Results Framework. To better reflect the scope of the revised activities, the review of the results
                         framework includes: (i) adjustments to the final targets of two PDO indicators, as part of drinking water
                         investments were carried out with government funds due to the time elapsed between the Board
                         approval and the declaration of effectiveness, and one intermediate indicator, (ii) replace one indicator
                         for two others to better reflect the result of the activity, (iii) incorporation of new intermediate
                         indicators to capture the effort in providing technical assistance activities to improve the capacities of
                         farmers/organizations. The revised results framework indicators are presented in Table 1.
