    I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

1.      Background. The Socio-Economic Resilience Strengthening Project (SERSP) is one of three operations financed by
the International Development Association (IDA) 18 Risk Mitigation Regime (RMR) to support Tajikistan’s Resilience
Strengthening Program, which aims to address risks associated with fragility, conflict, and violence.1 SERSP was approved
on June 20, 2019, and after a delay in ratification, became effective on July 8, 2020. The project is implemented by the
National Social Investment Fund of Tajikistan (NSIFT) with an IDA grant of US$37 million.

2.      Project development objective and component description. The project development objective (PDO) is to
strengthen participatory local governance, improve the quality of local infrastructure, both in targeted communities, and
increase extracurricular or livelihoods opportunities for youth. SERSP seeks to achieve these objectives through financing
community-driven investments, and providing youth inclusive services and livelihoods opportunities. SERSP is structured
around the following three components:
    i.  Component 1: Inclusive Local Development Initiatives (US$22.6 million IDA) provides subgrants to jamoats
        (subdistricts) to finance local-level, climate-resilient, socioeconomic infrastructure that addresses communities’
        priorities, including those of women and youth, identified through a participatory village needs assessment and
        decision-making process. It also finances Technical Assistance for the technical design of the subprojects (SPs). In
        addition, the component aims to strengthen the capacity of jamoat administrations and community-level
        institutions for participatory local development, disaster risk management, and operations and maintenance of
        project investments. The mobilization of communities to participate in the decision-making process, and the
        capacity-building activities are implemented by Training and Facilitating Partners (TFPs) contracted by NSIFT. This
        component also provides resources to refurbish youth facilities operated by the Ministry of Labor, Migration and
        Employment and the Ministry of Education and Science, which will be used to deliver Component 2 activities.
   ii.  Component 2: Youth Inclusion and Livelihoods (US$12.5 million IDA) provides youth-inclusive services (i.e. trainings
        and psychosocial support services) and livelihoods activities (including tools and equipment) to disadvantaged and
        inactive young men and women ages 16–30 to increase their overall resilience to risks of exclusion, inactivity, and
        disaffection. Component 2 activities are implemented by Implementing Partners (IPs) contracted by NSIFT.
  iii.  Component 3: Project Management and Monitoring and Evaluation (US$1.9 million IDA) finances the costs of the
        implementing agency, NSIFT, for project coordination and supervision, procurement, financial management,
        annual audits, monitoring and evaluation, communications activities, and the management of the beneficiary
        feedback mechanism and the management information system.

3.      Geographic coverage. The project operates in four districts in the Khatlon region (Kulyob, Panj, Hamadoni, and
Farkhor) and in three districts in GBAO (Vanj, Ishkoshim, and Shugnan). The target districts were selected for having a
shared border with Afghanistan, a relatively large population, a combination of rural and urban areas, and limited coverage
by other donor interventions.

implementation progress are currently rated as Moderately Satisfactory, mostly due to delays with project ratification and

5.     Implementation progress description. SERSP’s implementation progress by project component is summarized
below:
    i. Component 1: Inclusive Local Development Initiatives: The social mobilization process for the first batch of 22
       jamoats (Cycles 1 and 2) in GBAO and Khatlon resulted in the prioritization of 93 SPs; of these, 17 have been
       completed and most of the remaining SPs will be completed by December 15, 2023.2 The social mobilization
       process for Cycles 1 and 2 of the second batch of 22 jamoats resulted in the prioritization of 96 SPs. NSIFT will
       complete most of the SPs by December 15, 2023 and the remaining SPs by June 30, 2024. To date, community
       mobilization activities have met the project’s 50 percent targets for the participation of youth and women.
       Communities have expressed high levels of satisfaction with the project’s decision-making processes in the
       project’s scorecards. NSIFT completed the renovation and procured the furniture and equipment for 17 youth
       facilities, which the IPs are utilizing for delivering the youth-inclusive services.
   ii. Component 2: Youth Inclusion and Livelihoods: UNICEF and the World Bank developed training modules for
       Subcomponent 2a that the Ministry of Education and Science reviewed and approved for use in SERSP in October
       2022. The IP launched the delivery of the digital literacy and skills, mind masters (critical thinking), upshift (social
       entrepreneurship), psychosocial support services, and gender-based violence modules (renamed “Healthy
       Lifestyles”) between October 2022 and January 2023. To date, NSIFT has delivered the training to 4,293 youth. To
       account for delays in Subcomponent 2a and a lengthy 12-month initial procurement process for the
       subcomponent’s IP, NSIFT simplified the design of the youth livelihoods activities under Subcomponent 2b and
       restarted the procurement of an IP. NSIFT completed the procurement of the IP for Subcomponent 2b in
       December 2022 and launched the outreach for the livelihoods activities in March 2023.
  iii. Component 3: Project Management and Monitoring and Evaluation: The development of the project’s monitoring
       and evaluation activities are underway. The baseline survey was completed in September 2021 and reporting on
       project implementation and results is acceptable.

6.      Project ratings and audit. The financial management and procurement aspects of the project are rated Satisfactory.
Project management, and monitoring and evaluation are rated as Moderately Satisfactory. Based on the latest financial
management review by the World Bank, the Borrower is in compliance with the financial management requirements and the
project does not have overdue financial audit reports.

as Moderately Satisfactory. The environmental risk rating is Moderate, while the social risk rating remains Substantial, as the
SPs are being implemented in intrinsically diverse regions, which are exposed to common conflict and fragility risks that may
affect the project outcomes. The overall E&S risk rating remains Substantial. NSIFT has prepared and disclosed all required
E&S framework instruments, including the E&S Management Framework. NSIFT is also preparing and disclosing Environmental
and Social Management Plans or Environmental and Social Impact Assessments for every SP. The high volume of SPs requires

8.       Proposed restructuring. The proposed project restructuring was prepared in response to the letter from the
Government of Tajikistan dated March 14, 2023 requesting (i) reallocation of US$4.7 million to Subcomponent 1a and US$0.2
million to Subcomponent 1b to expand the subcomponents to one district in Khatlon and one in GBAO. The reallocation will
involve (i) moving US$4.7 million from disbursement category one to category two; (ii) revising an end target for an
intermediate-level indicator that is tied to Subcomponent 2b (iii) extending the project’s closing date by 18 months from June
30, 2024 to December 31, 2025; and (iv) extending the end target dates of the results indicators to align them with the
proposed closing date. In addition, the proposed restructuring seeks to (v) remove a legal covenant for the establishment of
a Project Steering Committee (SC).

9.       Requested restructuring. The World Bank team seeks a proposed project restructuring to accommodate the request
from the client to reallocate US$4.9 million to expand Subcomponents 1a and 1b to one district in Khatlon and one in GBAO
to allow full utilization of the project’s IDA funds. To enable NSIFT to complete the implementation of activities in these two
districts, the proposed restructuring also seeks an 18-month extension of the project’s closing date. The restructuring will
contribute to achievement of the project's development objective to strengthen participatory local governance and improve
the quality of local infrastructure. This is the first restructuring. The restructuring does not require any change in the original
PDO. The proposed reallocation increases the net present value (NPV) and economic internal rate of return (EIRR) for
Subcomponent 2b and does not significantly impact the economic and financial analysis for Component 1. The key project
stakeholders are fully committed to the objectives of the restructuring. The team has agreed on the revised project
implementation plan with NSIFT to ensure that all project activities are completed by May 31, 2025. Despite initial delays, the
progress recently made in project implementation, especially under Component 1, demonstrates the strong commitment of
the client and continued high strategic relevance of the project.

    II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

10.      The proposed restructuring envisages: (i) reallocation of funds between disbursement categories and components
to expand Subcomponents 1a and 1b to two additional districts; (ii) revision of an end target for an intermediate-level
results indicator to reflect the slightly revised Subcomponent 2b scope; (iii) extension of the project’s closing date; (iv)
extension of the results indicators’ end target dates to align them with the proposed closing date; and (v) removal of a
legal covenant. The changes are specified below in more detail. 3

11.    Expansion of Subcomponents 1a and 1b. The proposed restructuring will expand Subcomponents 1a (Subgrants
for Local Development Initiatives) and 1b (Community and Youth Mobilization, Social Accountability, and Local

the proposed restructuring. First, because of the procurement delays with selecting an IP for Subcomponent 2b, the
project would only be able to deliver livelihoods tools and equipment to 2,400 youth, rather than the original target of
3,500 youth, in the proposed extension period.5 Second, there are significant savings of US$1.9 million from consultancy

Subcomponents 1a and 1b and high levels of beneficiary satisfaction with the process and outcomes justifies reallocating
savings and funds from Subcomponent 2b to Subcomponents 1a and 1b. NSIFT will be able to implement one cycle of
community mobilization and subgrants within the project time frame.6

12.     Operational procedures for the proposed expansion of Subcomponents 1a and 1b. NSIFT selected the two
additional districts using the same criteria that were applied to the existing seven districts: (i) a shared border with
Afghanistan; (ii) a relatively large population size; (iii) a combination of rural and urban areas; and (iv) limited coverage by
other donor interventions. NSIFT will also apply the same operational procedures (i.e. social mobilization process, jamoat
grant distribution formula, and negative list for SPs) for the implementation of activities in the two new districts with one
exception. Given the limited time remaining in the project, NSIFT and the TFPs will conduct one cycle of community
mobilization and SP investment rather than two cycles. To ensure the rapid roll out and implementation of these activities,
the social mobilization and capacity-building activities will be implemented by the current TFPs contracted by NSIFT. The
implementation arrangements will remain the same as well: NSIFT will continue to be the implementing agency, managing
the project through a dedicated project management team, including staff based in the project’s regional offices in Khorog
(GBAO) and Kulob (Khatlon).

13.     ESF Requirments. NSIFT has prepared and disclosed all required E&S framework instruments, including the
Environmental and Social Management Framework for SERSP. NSIFT prepares and discloses site-specific environmental
and social management plans on an ongoing basis. NSIFT will strengthen management and monitoring of construction-
related waste. The original project’s E&S analysis covered both GBAO and Khatlon. Since the two proposed districts are
within the target provinces and their socioeconomic conditions are similar to the existing project sites, the E&S analysis
conducted under the original project is sufficient.

14.     Revised results indicators. The reduced scope of Subcomponent 2b will mean that the project will not be able to
achieve the end target for the following intermediate-level results indicator: “number of direct project beneficiaries who
receive livelihoods tools and equipment”. The proposed restructuring will reduce the end target for this results indicator
from 3,500 to 2,400 beneficiaries. Given the delays with the Subcomponent implementation, this new target remains
meaningful. It will enable NSIFT to focus on the quality of implementation and pay careful attention to the selection and
support services to beneficiaries of the tools and equipment. The proposed restructuring will also align the results
indicators’ end target dates with the proposed closing date of December 31, 2025.

15.     Revised economic and financial analysis. The economic and financial analysis for the youth livelihoods
subcomponent (2b) for the original project allocation was calculated based on an expected allocation of US$9.0 million.
Applying the 10 percent social cost of capital, the NPV was estimated at US$6.0 million, with an EIRR of 32 percent. With
a revised allocation of US$4.7 for the subcomponent and a decrease of approximately one-third of the number of
beneficiaries who will receive livelihoods tools and equipment, the revised NPV is estimated at US$2.9 million with an
EIRR of 45 percent.

16.     Extension of the closing date. In order to complete the implementation of subcomponent 1a and 2b activities in
the two new proposed districts, NSIFT requires an extension of 18 months in the project’s closing date. The extension will
not require any additional funds for project management related costs.

17.      Changed legal covenant. The project’s financing agreement included the establishment of a national-level SC to
oversee the three projects comprising the Tajikistan Resilience Strengthening Program within three months following
project effectiveness. The SC’s purpose was to provide strategic guidance, coordination, implementation support, and
interlinkages between the individual projects that form part of the World Bank’s Resilience Strengthening Program and
the relevant Government of Tajikistan’s strategic policy priorities and reforms. To date, the Government of Tajikistan has
not established the SC and its absence has not impeded project implementation. The project management teams and
ministries responsible for implementing the RMR projects have established coordination mechanisms at the working level.
Thus, the proposed restructuring would drop the legal covenant for the SC establishment.
