    I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

1.      Uganda is experiencing one of the highest rates of forest loss globally, with forest cover reducing from 15.8
percent in 2000 to 11 percent of the country’s land area in 2023.1 Despite their vital role in tourism and natural resource-
based livelihoods, forests are under threat due to weak implementation of laws, limited infrastructure, and the spread of
invasive species, which pose long-term ecological and economic risks. The tourism sector recorded a 25.9 percent surge
in earnings in 2024 and now accounts for 3.2 percent of the gross domestic product and 7.2 percent of total employment.2
Tourism is now the top foreign exchange earner for the country, having overtaken the traditional coffee sector.3 While
increasingly important for the economy, tourism remains reliant on a narrow set of offerings—primarily gorilla tracking,
game drives, and rafting—highlighting the need for more diverse and community-supported tourism products that
leverage Uganda’s unique assets. For both forest conservation and tourism development, the greater public investment,
strategic planning, and community involvement are essential to ensure sustainable use of natural resources and improved
economic outcomes.
2.       Background: The Investing in Forests and Protected Areas for Climate-Smart Development (IFPA-CD) Project
was prepared to support the forest, conservation, and tourism sectors in the country. Its Project Development Objective
(PDO) is to (a) improve sustainable management of forests and protected areas (PA) in target landscapes and (b) increase
benefits to communities from forests in target landscapes. The project supports a landscape approach to improve
management and economic productivity of forest ecosystems. It combines investments in forest management in both
state- and community-managed lands and focuses on improving the management of forests, increasing revenues for
sustaining forests, and supporting resilient livelihoods. The project has a strong job-creation element, both through direct
activity support and indirectly through planned investments in wood processing and tourism facilities. The project was
approved by the Board of the International Development Association on April 23, 2020 and became effective on August
18, 2021. The project was formally launched in March 2022, as the restrictions related to the novel coronavirus (COVID-
19) were starting to ease. The first project restructuring was approved in December 2024 to finetune the PDO and better
define the project’s geographic scope.
3.      The project has four components. The project’s structure responds to the need to invest in development of the
forest and tourism sectors (with Component 2 supporting economic investments in these sectors). Component 1 focuses
on improving protected area management practices, while Component 3 targets improved landscape management in
refugee hosting areas (RHA). Component 4 supports project management and monitoring. Activities under Subcomponent
1.4 and Component 3 are financed through a blend of International Development Association (IDA) grant and grant
resources from the IDA18 Regional Sub-Window for Refugees and Host Communities (RSW). The Ministry of Water and
Environment (MWE), National Forestry Authority (NFA), and Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) are the original
implementing agencies for the project, working in close coordination with the Ministry of Tourism, Wildlife and Antiquities
and the Office of the Prime Minister (OPM)—the latter two agencies having general government mandates on tourism
and refugee hosting, respectively.
4.       Implementation progress. Commencement of implementation of project activities was slow due to the delayed
effectiveness, related to lengthy internal approval processes by the Government of Uganda (GoU) and COVID-19-related
restrictions. Effective implementation started with the project launch in March 2022. Project performance had been in
the satisfactory range until early 2025, as reported in the Implementation Status and Results Reports since the start of the
project. In view of continued implementation delays, both the development objective (DO) and implementation progress

    II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

5.        The proposed restructuring of the project is related to adjustments in the scope of activities to allow more efficient
utilization of project resources within the remaining project period, related changes in the results framework, further fine-
tuning of the definition of the project target landscapes, and mitigation of assessed risks related to project support to
infrastructure and equipment for PA management. In order to continue support for the core PA management functions
(infrastructure and equipment for PA management), there is a requirement to complete institutional assessments with
regard to UWA’s and MWE’s law enforcement functions and update their standard operating procedures (SOP). In view
of the dissolution of NFA under the National Forestry and Tree Planting (Amendment) Act 2025 and absorption of its
functions into MWE, the GOU has informed the Bank that activities previously implemented by NFA will be undertaken by
MWE. Key changes are summarized below.
6.       This restructuring seeks to remove the NFA as an implementing agency and replace it with MWE, since the
Recipient has informed the Bank of the mainstreaming of NFA functions into MWE as a department under its Forest Sector
Support Department. Under this restructuring, the activities initially planned under NFA will be moved to MWE. Given
that the mainstreaming is not yet complete and there is insufficient information to determine MWE’s capacity to take on
these activities, implementation of those activities is being paused. A disbursement condition is being introduced for these
activities, pending an assessment of MWE having adequate institutional capacity to take on the activities formerly under
the NFA.
7.      Tightening the definition of the project area (Target Landscapes) to limit this to the selected PAs (CFRs, NPs, and
WRs as listed in Annex 1), and an area within a 10 km radius of each of these (for activities related to PA management), in
addition to the Refugee Hosting Areas as already defined in the legal agreement.
8.       Changes to components and costs. Activity level changes (expansion or reduction of scope) have been introduced
further to discussion with the GoU as part of the implementation support mission undertaken in August-September 2025,
and described below, to ensure project implementation remains well aligned towards achievement of the PDO. These
activity level changes result in a revision of certain project outputs. Some activities within components are dropped due
to the lack of time that prevents adequate completion within the remaining project duration, in the absence of a project
extension. The retained activities are fully consistent with the current World Bank Group Country Partnership Framework
(FY16–21 and extended).6 Project-supported activities are also aligned with a number of policy recommendations for the
forestry and nature-based tourism sectors as identified in the Uganda Country Climate and Development Report. Changes
to component costs are summarized below:

good international practice. Additionally, the restructuring aims to increase support under the project for local community
participation and benefits to communities adjacent to forest and wildlife protected areas, including the Batwa.

33.      ES risk management documents (Environmental and Social Impact Assessments (ESIA)/Project Briefs/ESMPs) have
been approved and implemented for various subprojects, with some ESMPs currently being finalized. The project maintains
an operational grievance redress system with multiple levels and entry points, which is well-known and frequently used by
local communities and refugees. Complaints are being processed and documented, and the GoU is working to improve
record-keeping and consolidation. Protocols for incident management are also in place, and reportable incidents have been
communicated to the Bank and processed under ESIRT. The GoU has actively engaged with local communities, refugees and
VMGPs, with particular attention to the Batwa, in keeping with the Stakeholder Engagement Plan (SEP) and the VMGPs. The
VMGPs are being updated as part of a continuous process of stakeholder engagement and, in particular, incorporate
additional Batwa feedback and enhance benefit-sharing and participation in project implementation. The GoU has also taken
steps to increase community participation in CFM and CRM arrangements, which are key for mitigation and benefit sharing,
including through fee waivers and awareness raising. The restructuring is expected to reinforce and expand these initiatives
by expanding the funding available to them for the outstanding period of project implementation. Ongoing actions to be
supported by the restructuring will also help reinforce the borrower's capacity to address ES issues, such as UWA and MWE
institutional assessments, update of operational standard procedures and operational agreements with law-enforcement
partners. The borrower is also finalizing a data sharing agreement with UNHCR for the Feedback, Referral, and Resolution
Mechanism (FRRM) to streamline and improve grievance management in refugee areas. E&S training (covering stakeholder
engagement, Environmental and Social Framework requirements into procurement and grievances/incident management)
will be provided to the project implementing teams and local grievance committees, among others.

Identify key stakeholders and describe the mechanism for consultation and disclosure on E&S policies, with an emphasis of

34.      The project has identified and engaged stakeholders in accordance with the SEP and VMGPs, as well as various
provisions in different ES risk management documents, such as Process Framework or Environmental and Social
Management Framework /ESIA/ESMPs. Key stakeholders encompass individuals, groups, and local communities who may be
affected by the project - either positively or negatively, directly or indirectly, including groups involved in collaborative forest
management (CFM), community resource management (CRM), and ecotourism. Special attention has been given to those
who are disadvantaged or vulnerable, including communities adjacent to PAs, the Batwa, refugees, and refugee-host
communities. In addition to potentially affected people, the project has engaged other parties who may influence project
outcomes. These include government institutions with relevant mandates, non-governmental organizations (NGO) and civil
society organizations (CSO), the UNHCR, private sector actors in the wood value chain (e.g., UTGA), tourism sector
representatives, development partners, and research organizations (e.g., Nyabyeya FC). Consultations have been guided by
the action plan outlined in the SEP, with tailored arrangements for the Batwa community set out in the VGMF and VMGPs.
The Batwa have been involved in the development of key E&S instruments, including particularly the Vulnerable and
Marginalized Groups Framework (VMGF), as well as in the preparation and the ongoing updating of site-specific VGMPs. To
facilitate meaningful participation, a summary of the VMGF was prepared and translated into four local languages spoken by
the Batwa (Rufumbira, Kiswahili, Lwamba, and Runyankole). Multiple phases of consultations have been conducted with the
Batwa communities, NGOs, CSOs, and district local governments, among others, using local languages to ensure accessibility
and inclusivity. Methods of engagements have included community meetings, focus group discussions and telephone
interviews, among others. Existing participatory forest management mechanisms, such as those implemented by the NFA
(now MWE) and UWA through CFM and CRM initiatives, have also been important channels for engaging local communities.
These mechanisms are open to all resource users, promoting broad participation in project implementation. Recent
stakeholder engagement activities have included training and sensitization sessions for representatives from the MWE, UWA,
contractors, and project districts. All consultations and other forms of engagement are documented.
Procurement:
35.     The project’s procurement risk rating has been predominantly high due to protracted procurement processes for
high value procurements, especially at MWE. The delays in implementation were caused by delayed initiation of
requirements, lengthy evaluation processes exacerbated by long turnaround periods for reviewed reports and allegations
of fraud of corruption in major consultancy procurements at MWE and at NFA for one goods activity. During the August-
September 2025 mission it was noted that most of the ongoing procurements had progressed to advanced stages, but in
some cases the contract periods in the bidding documents are no longer achievable in the remaining project life. However,
compression of the contract period presents challenges in terms of the scope of deliverables that are achievable.
36.      The current procurement arrangements will remain the same in MWE. The impact of the ongoing process of
rationalization of government entities in Uganda is expected to disrupt procurement processing in NFA which was
mainstreamed in MWE from August 1,2025. Relevant assessments will be undertaken when Government’s
communication on the changes is received.
37.     MWE will undertake all procurements (goods and services) for the benefit of UTGA. All technical specifications
and terms of reference will be prepared by UTGA, under guidance from MWE procurement team, and the UTGA team will
participate in the procurement evaluation. All goods will be transferred to UTGA upon purchase and entered into their
fixed assets register.
