I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Introduction

 1. This restructuring paper seeks the approval of the Country Director on a Level 2 Restructuring of the National Urban
    Cadaster and Municipal Support Project (P162278), based on the official request from the Government of Peru
    sent to the World Bank (WB) on May 20, 2024, to consider the changes agreed upon during the Mid-Term Review
    (MTR) carried out from June to December 2023. This is the First Restructuring of the Project, which entails the
    following change: Extend the project closing date by 17 months, that is, until July 31, 2026.

B. Background and Status

 2. The project's total financing is US$ 80.90 million, of which US$ 50.00 million corresponds to the International Bank
    for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) Loan and US$ 30.90 million from the Government of Peru (National
    Counterpart). The project was approved by the Board of Executive Directors of the World Bank Group on January
    10, 2020, effective as of August 19, 2020, and closing on February 28, 2025.

 3. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to improve the coverage of urban cadaster services in selected
    municipalities to enhance local government capacities for revenue generation and urban management. This PDO is
    being achieved through the following components: Component 1: Strengthening Municipal Systems, Services and
    Capacities to Generate and Maintain Urban Cadasters in Project Municipalities (US$ 69.1 million, of which US$44.7
    million is Bank-financed and US$ 24.4 is National Counterpart-financed), Component 2: Strengthening of the
    National Institutional Framework (US$ 6.3 million, of which US$ 5.3 million is Bank-financed and US$ 1.0 is National
    Counterpart-financed), and Component 3: Project Management (US$5.5 million, of which 100% is National
    Counterpart-financed); totaling US$ 80.90 million (US$ 50.00 million corresponds to the Loan financed by the World
    Bank and US$ 30.90 million corresponds to the National Counterpart finance).

C. Project status

 4. PDO is rated as Moderately Unsatisfactory (MU), considering the lack of progress in the PDO-Level indicators with
    the actual closing date, and the Implementation Progress is rated Moderately Satisfactory (MS). In addition, the
    Project is rated Satisfactory in compliance with social and environmental safeguards, Moderately Satisfactory in
    financial management, Satisfactory in project management, and Satisfactory in procurement. The Project
    continues to make progress despite the impacts and delays derived from the COVID-19 pandemic and social-political
    events, which as of May 2024, is consistent with evidence-based results, such as: (i) The signing of two contracts for
    the cadastral survey of areas 1 and 2, comprising 13 of 22 project municipalities (59% project coverage area), located
    in the provinces of Piura (four districts), Lambayeque (five districts), and Lima (four districts), with a target of 661,924
    cadastral units, for a total amount that corresponds to 26% of the Loan. The second stage (areas 3, 4, and 5:
    achievable only if the project closing date is extended) covers nine districts of Lima (41% project coverage area),
    with a target of 988,507 cadastral units. The budget for areas 3, 4, and 5 corresponds to 59% of the Loan, based on
    average estimates of Documents containing the technical specifications (Estudios Definitivos, in Spanish) and Market
    Studies; (ii) The supervision and quality control firms for areas 1 and 2 are already contracted; (iii) The production
    of the modules of the urban cadastral information system, which will enable the collection and processing of

     cadastral information, is already developed (in a trial phase) in-house by a team of consultants (Mesa ágil, in
     Spanish), (iv) The Government's MTR report was sent to the Bank in January 2024; (v) Loan disbursements have
     increased from 2% to 11% as of May 2024 (see details below); (vi) All project municipalities included in cadastral
     areas 1 and 2 are being provided with direct technical assistance for institutional strengthening by the Project
     according to the Cadaster Implementation Plan; (vii) Several activities that contribute to achieving each indicator
     target are underway; and (viii) Implementation arrangements are working well, with the Project under the
     leadership of the Agency for the Formalization of Informal Property (Órgano de Formalización de la Propiedad
     Informal, COFOPRI in Spanish) and based on close coordination with implementing agencies, including the Ministry
     of Economy and Finance (Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, MEF) and the Ministry of Housing, Construction and
     Sanitation (Ministerio de Vivienda, Construcción y Saneamiento, MVCS). Notwithstanding, to better provide support
     to the project and ensure the future sustainability of the urban cadasters, MVCS and COFOPRI must effectively
     undertake the responsibilities on urban cadastral matters established in Legislative Decree No. 1365 and distribute
     them through their organizational structures.

 5. There are no overdue audits for the Project; external audits have been received and considered acceptable to the
    Bank as of the date of this Restructuring Paper. Disbursements have increased from US$ 0.89 million (as of February
    2023) to US$ 5.48 million as of May 2024 (11% of the US$ 50 million Loan). As of May 20, 2024, the Project Actual
    Costs (actual paid) are US$ 2,201,660.60 (4% of the US$ 50 million Loan). The Government plans to request
    disbursements to reach US$ 19.22 million by December 2024 (38% of the US$ 50 million Loan).

 6. In December 2023, the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) team was completed.

 7. The presidential elections held in 2021, the Municipal elections of 2022, and the presidential succession in 2022 did
    not imply risks to the PDO but caused some delays in project implementation. The project risks were re-evaluated
    in the MTR, remaining with the same ratings. Therefore, the project's overall residual risk rating remains moderate,
    with the PDO aligned to be achieved with this restructuring proposal.

D. Rationale for Restructuring

 8. According to the Mid-Term Review, the PDO would be achievable only if the closing date is extended. The rationale
    for project extension is explained by a delay of almost 24 months in Project activities. According to the Project
    Appraisal Document (PAD), it was planned that the cadastral surveys would take 24 months, starting in February
    2022 and ending approximately in February 2024 (which was now the new start date for these activities). Therefore,
    the remaining time until the actual closing date would be insufficient to complete the cadastral surveys (core project
    activities) following the implementation strategy proposed by the PIU to achieve the project targets, which includes
    two sequential phases, the first of which would culminate in March 2025 (areas 1 and 2) and the second in July 2026
    (areas 3, 4, and 5).

 9. This is the First Restructuring of the Project, which entails the following change: Extend the project closing date by
    17 months, that is, until July 31, 2026.

 10. The Financial reports and audit requirements, funds flow, and disbursement arrangements for the Loan remain
     unchanged. Therefore, no changes are expected in the Project's Disbursement and Financial Information Letter.

       11. The PDO is of great importance for the country's strategic goals, as it is aligned with the Government Strategic
           National Development Plan to 2050 and with the World Bank Group's Country Partnership Framework for Peru (CPF)
           for FY17-FY21, which was revised in 2019, as well with the CPF prepared for FY23-27. The relevance of promoting
           the exchange of cadastral information between the national and local governments is maintained, as well as the
           Project's alignment with national and sectoral public policies. Therefore, the MTR corroborated that the PDO
           remains relevant. Consequently, through this Project's Restructuring, the World Bank will continue supporting the
           implementation of the urban cadaster and building a sustainable cadaster and land administration system in Peru.



  II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

       12. This paper seeks Management approval to carry out a Level 2 Restructuring reflecting the following change: Extend
           the project closing date by 17 months.

       13. To comply with the changes described in this Restructuring Paper, the Government of Peru proposes an extension
           of the project closing date to July 31, 2026, sent to the World Bank through an official Project Restructuring Request
           Letter on May 20, 2024.
