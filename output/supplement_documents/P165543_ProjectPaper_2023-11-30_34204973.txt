 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING


A. Introduction

1.       This Project Paper seeks approval of the Country Director to make changes to the Institutional Strengthening
for Improved Village Service Delivery (P165543), IBRD Loan No. 8941-ID and to the project’s legal document. The
restructuring will: (i) adjust project components and activities to improve project effectiveness and strengthen the capacity
of sub-national governments; (ii) change selected results framework indicators and targets; (iii) simplify the
implementation arrangements to reflect the current institutional arrangements and adjusted project activities; and (iv)
reallocate amounts among disbursement categories. The restructuring does not seek any modification to the closing date.
This is the project’s second restructuring, the first one was approved in June 2022.


B. Project Status

2.       Background. The World Bank loan of US$ 300 for Institutional Strengthening for Improved Village Service Delivery
project was approved on June 26, 2019, and became effective on Feb 19, 2020. The closing date of the loan is December
31, 2024. The Project’s development objective is “To strengthen institutional capacity for improved quality of spending in
participating villages”. The project initially comprised four components, Component 1: Strengthening village institutions;
Component 2: Promoting Participatory Village Development; Component 3: Village Performance Awards; and Component
4: National Coordination, Monitoring, and Policy. Component 3 was cancelled in the first restructuring. Project
implementation spans across four ministries, with Ministry of Home Affairs (MoHA) as the Executing Agency and Ministry
of Villages and Disadvantaged Areas (MoV), the National Planning Agency (Bappenas), and the Coordinating Ministry of
Human Development and Cultural Affairs (Kemenko PMK) as implementing agencies. The Ministry of Finance (MoF) was
removed as implementing agency during the first restructuring. A mid-term review was completed in March 2023.

3.       First restructuring. The first restructuring in 2022 (i) modified the results framework to include revision of targets
for select indicators, (ii) dropped Component 3: Village Performance Awards, as the Government will implement and
finance it through their own mechanism, separate from the project intervention, (iii) changed selected scope of activities
in Components 1,2 and 4, (iv) effected a partial cancellation in an amount of US$49.98 million, (v) updated the
implementation arrangements to add the Directorate of Regional Administration, as a new Project Implementing Unit
(PIU), and (vi) adjusted costs between components and the disbursement categories.

4.     Overview of Implementation Progress. Since project approval, a series of factors have contributed to
implementation delays. One of the most notable reasons was the delay caused by the Covid-19 pandemic, which impeded
the implementation of village level activities. The CPMU went through several changes in personnel reducing the
ownership of the project and overall project coordination.

5.      As a result, disbursements were low and only picked up in 2023. By 2022, only 10% of the loan was disbursed. By
end of 2023, total disbursement is at 44%. Progress towards achieving the PDO has remained moderately unsatisfactory,
but implementation progress, procurement, project management and financial management were upgraded from
moderately unsatisfactory to moderately satisfactory in June 2023. The project’s mid-term review (MTR) in March 2023
concluded that while the project remained relevant, a significant implementation acceleration, revamp of the results
framework and improved project management was needed to achieve the PDO by end of 2024. Activities in 2024 will
include the development and implementation of an exit strategy to ensure sustainability of the project. This will be mainly
achieved through institutionalization of the results, outputs and outcomes and institutional strengthening of district
governments (part of the institutional arrangements). All components will include exit strategy activities.


6.       The project is in compliance with legal covenants, as well as the Bank fiduciary and environmental and social
requirements. There is no overdue audit report and interim financial report as of to date. There is also no overdue refund
of ineligible expenditures as the FY2020 and FY2021 ineligible expenditures have been fully resolved. As for the FY 2022
audit, the report was timely received with clean (unqualified) opinion towards the project financial report. Some ineligible
expenditures are identified and have been partially refunded. The project is following up on the refund of remaining
FY2022 Ineligible Expense (IE) balance totaling USD 27,339.21.


7.      The implementation status of the project components is summarized below.


8.       Component 1: Strengthening village institutions (this component has four sub-components). As part of the
project’s implementation acceleration efforts, a first half of face-to-face village institutions trainings will be completed by
end of November 2023, covering 131,610 participants from 33,458 villages across 33 provinces. The second half of training
implementation is scheduled for first semester of 2024, and the tender process for this package is scheduled to begin in
December 2023. The other main procurement packages, the development and operationalization of the Learning
Management System (LMS) (platform development and content development) will be completed by end of November
2023 and implementation is scheduled to completed within the remaining project time frame. Activities relating to the
village financial management system (SISKEUDES), village profile (PRODESKEL) and village financial audit and oversight
system (SISWASKEUDES) are significantly delayed and are only partially under implementation. Procurement of key
packages have not yet started. Added with the last restructuring, the activity related to piloting approaches to strengthen
sub-district institutions for improved coordination, supervision and monitoring of service delivery are delayed,
procurement of this package has only commenced. The Regional Management Consultants (RMC) and National
Management Consultants (NMC) are on board and are supporting project implementation under the Central Project
Management Unit (CPMU).
9.       Component 2: Strengthening community capacity for village development (this component has 4 sub-
components). The last ISR upgraded the rating of Component 2 from MU to MS as the PIU (MOV) managed to implement
activities largely in accordance with the approved annual work plan. However, most of the component 2 activities are not
aligned with the indicators in the result framework. The indicators of the results framework need to be modified to better
capture the activities and outputs under implementation, so they contribute to the achievement of the PDO. Development
of training modules and implementation of face-to-face training for 10,633 village facilitators have been completed. The
development of a Learning Management System for village facilitators is under implementation and a digital marketplace
platform is under development. A study on regulatory harmonization has been completed. Another key activity under this

component is the piloting of inclusion, social accountability scorecards pilots, which will be completed by 2024. The smart
village and village academy packages (akademi desa) are completed.

10.     Component 3: National Coordination, Monitoring, and Policy (this component has 3 sub-components). A
baseline study was completed by Bappenas for component 1, but other project activities are delayed. The integrated village
data system, planned under Bappenas, cannot be operationalized as data governance issues hinder data exchange and
integration between ministries and within Bappenas. The InfoDesa activity was integrated with the Village Data System.
Bappenas is not using loan proceeds for the activities but utilize Rupiah murni.


11.      Challenges in the project implementation. Since the last restructuring, project implementation has progressed
significantly but the delays up to 2023 have had altered the initial design and adjustments to components and activities
are needed. The Mid-Term review, completed in March 2023 found that there is a misalignment between planned and
existing activities with the PDO and results framework. The Executing Agency has assigned a special oversight function to
oversee and support implementation of the acceleration plan. To ensure the agreed acceleration plan can be implemented
and the project could immediately show the intended achievements, a second restructuring is needed.


12.       Environment and Social Safeguards. There are no changes to the project objective and main activities, and the
restructuring is mainly for accelerating and optimizing project implementation. Therefore, the safeguards policies
triggered as set out in the current Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMF, updated in April 2022) are
still relevant. No amendment to the ESMF is required for this restructuring.

C. Rationale for Restructuring.

13.     It is proposed to: (i) adjust project components and activities to improve project effectiveness and strengthen the
capacity of sub-national governments; (ii) change selected results framework indicators and targets; (iii) simplify the
implementation arrangements to reflect the current institutional arrangements and adjusted project activities; and (iv)
reallocate amounts among disbursement categories as indicated below.

        (i) Adjustment of project components and activities:
             (A) to improve project effectiveness. This will be done by rationalizing the scope of selected activities to
            adjust to the project’s remaining time frame. Several key activities cannot be completed anymore under the
            remaining time frame, such as the Grand Design of the PRODESKEL. Other activities need to be rationalized so
            they can be completed within the remaining time frame. The face-to-face training for village institutions was
            not explicitly included in the original project design.
            (B) To Strengthen the capacity of sub-national governments to ensure project sustainability. To ensure
            sustainability of the project’s interventions post-closing date, the Government of Indonesia is proposing to
            add activities by institutionalizing the results at national and sub-national levels for 2024.
        (ii) Improving the likelihood of achieving the PDO. Rationalizations and modifications to project activities to
              address bottlenecks in implementation; and modification of the intermediate results indicators and the targets
              in the Results Framework to streamline monitoring and evaluation.
        (iii) Simplification of institutional arrangements to enhance project effectiveness. The function of component
              three will shift to strengthening the SC’s operational oversight and guidance through the establishment of a
              SC secretariat. It will focus on ensuring continued accelerated project implementation and PDO achievement.

        (iv) Reallocation of loan categories. The amount of US$ 570,000 will be reallocated from disbursement category
             (1) to category (3) for the purpose of strengthening the operational effectiveness of the SC’s secretariat.




 II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

Proposed Changes


14.      Component 1: Strengthening Village Institutions. This component will be renamed “Strengthening Village
Institutions” to reflect the broader scope of this component, which now include the village community institutions that
contribute to the improvement of the institutional strengthening system for village government, such as the Village
Council (BPD) and the Village Women Organization (PKK).


15.      Sub-Component 1A: Strengthening village institutions capacity building systems. The scope of this sub-
component now integrates social and environmental aspects. Development of new systems for village institution capacity
building that integrate social and environmental aspects – and awareness of climate change, their impacts on village
livelihoods – and strengthen inclusion and participation, consisting of: (i) face-to-face trainings and digital learning
platforms to integrate content and deliver cost-effective and cost-efficient capacity building support to village institutions;
(ii) expanded content, modules and tools (e.g. animation videos, e-learning, blended learning, distance learning), as well
as technical manuals, guidelines, modules and supporting instruments to bolster administrative capacity; (iii) performance
evaluation tools to review the capacity building program; and (iv) development and integration of such additional modules
into the capacity building program, systems and training rollout, and transitioning into the digitally enabled LMS.
Previously the project focused on capacity of village governments, but it is now proposed to expand to cover village
institutions generally.
16.     Sub-Component 1B: Rollout of institutional strengthening systems. This sub-component now includes provision
of technical assistance, training, capacity building, and development of technical guidelines and relevant implementing
regulations to rollout and institutionalize the revised institutional strengthening systems (including LMSs) through
subnational governments, including for: (i) the adoption and transition of these systems; (ii) improved coordination and
oversight; (iii) alignment with subnational programs on capacity building; and (iv) supervision of regional development by
regional governments.
17.       Sub-Component 1C: Improvement of oversight and data management. This sub-component drops the activity
to develop a robust and comprehensive village financial management and fiscal intelligence tool. It is now limiting its
scope to cover upgrading, strengthening and integration of MOHA’s (i) village financial management system (SISKEUDES);
and (ii) village financial audit and oversight system (SISWASKEUDES).
18.     Sub-Component 1D: Frontline service delivery. Improvement of frontline service delivery in participating village
governments including piloting of approaches to strengthen sub-district institutions for improved coordination,
supervision and monitoring of service delivery. This new activity was not yet sufficiently defined in the last restructuring.

19.  Component 2: Promoting Participatory Village Development. This component will be renamed to “Strengthening
community capacity for village development” to reflect the focused of community capacity under this component.
20.      Sub-Component 2A: Facilitator Capacity Strengthening. The changes to this sub-component include adding
activities related to social cohesion and climate resilience and integrating the development of a digital marketplace
platform, previously under sub-component 2C. The overall scope and objective of this sub-component remains the same,
which is “strengthening the capacity of facilitators” through: (i) development of training modules related to social
accountability, social cohesion, community empowerment, resilience and participatory planning, (ii) integration of these
modules into the facilitator capacity building program; (iii) development and roll out of a digitally enabled Learning
Management System (LMS) to transition the training program to a platform-based approach .
21.     Sub-Component 2B: Community Capacity and Social Accountability System. This component will be renamed
to “Community capacity, social accountability and approaches” to strengthen community capacity and social
accountability and approaches to better support community participation and inclusion in decision-making and to ensure
the contestability of different forums for deliberation.
22.     Sub-Component 2C: Digital innovation and community learning. The activities remain largely as approved. The
change to this sub-component includes the development and testing of the digital marketplace platform (P2KTD),
previously under sub-component 2A. The activities under this sub-component are the development of digital technology-
based innovations, consisting of: (i) improvement of community learning systems under the MOV’s Akademi Desa 4.0
platform; and (ii) capacity building of villages to optimize the use of digital platforms promoting “Smart Villages”; and (iii)
integration of the development and testing of the digital marketplace platform (P2KTD), previously under sub-component
2A.
23.      Sub-Component 2D: Technical support and program management. This sub-component will reallocate all
activities related to the social accountability and participatory planning systems and provision of support for facilitator
management to sub-component 2B. Remaining activities in this sub-component include the support for development and
implementation of exit strategy and village fund budget prioritization activities to achieve enhanced quality of spending.


Component 3: National Coordination, Monitoring, and Policy.
24.     Component 3: National Coordination, Monitoring, and Policy. Sub-components 3a and 3c are proposed to be
dropped. The remaining sub-component 3b “National Coordination and Strategy” will be kept with its focus shifted toward
strengthening the capacity of Bappenas and PMK in effectively running the steering committee, including project and
relevant policy coordination, studies and assessments to inform future implementation of village law.

27.      Changes to the Results Framework. The initial results framework needs a complete overhaul for the following
reasons: (i) The proposed indicator progress measures were largely to be conceived through tools to be developed under
this project. Given the significant delays, these tools are not available to date, meaning that progress for most indicators
cannot be tracked. (ii) The activities implemented under the project are poorly aligned with the initial indicators, thus the
outputs and intermediate outcomes achieved under the project are not captured. (iii) The initial targets required
calibration to take the implementation delays into account and to adjust to realistic, achievable targets. The newly
proposed indicators and targets aim to better align with the PDO, data is derived from GoI own systems, and targets are
adjusted and aligned with GoI’s KPI’s for village law implementation and increased capacity of village apparatus. The
revised results framework is provided as an annex to this Restructuring Paper with an explanation of the proposed changes
below.
     (a) Deletion of Original PDO indicator 1: “Village institutional capacity to execute core functions strengthened
         (percentage)”.
     (b) Deletion of Original PDO indicator 2: “Beneficiaries feel that village investments meet their needs” (by gender)
         (Percentage).
     (c) New PDO indicator 1: “Increase in village governments submitting village budget realization report (APBDes)
         (Percentage).” The new indicator data source will use the government’s own Siskeudes, with a baseline of 45%
         based on the realized village budget report (APBDes) and an end target of 70%.
     (d) New PDO indicator 2: “Increase in villages with an increase in "village development status" (Indeks Desa
         Membangun/ IDM)(percentage).,” MOV launched the IDM through Minister Regulation No 2/2016. The IDM has
         since been published annually and used to assess village development status. The proposed target for P3PD is
         aligned with the government’s Strategic Planning Document of MOV (Rencana Strategis/ Renstra) 2020-2024. End
         target is that 35% of P3PD participating villages increase their development status (IDM).

28.     Under Component 1, the intermediate results indicator 1 has been simplified from “LMS platform for needs-
based capacity building for village government apparats and institutions established and operational (Text Y/N)” to “LMS
platform for village governments and institutions operational (Y/N)” with an end target of yes.

29.      Under Component 1, Intermediate results indicator 2 has been modified from “Villages where key village apparat
and BPD members completed core training modules” (percentage) to “Villages where governments and institutions
complete basic capacity building training (, percentage).” The end target is 70 percent of the total 66,496 participating
villages in P3PD locations, to be verified through the LMS platform and activity reports.

30.      Under Component 1, a new intermediate results indicator 3 has been added: “Number of villages where
women's organization (PKK) have been trained,” (number) with an end target of 6,000 villages, to be verified through
activity reports. This indicator replaced the previous indicator of “Villages with women representatives in the village
council/ BPD” which has been marked for deletion.

to villages,” and “Kecamatan system for monitoring and coordinating service delivery operational.” This indicator will be

established and accessible.”

inclusion policy for village development exists (Y/N).” (text). This indicator combined two previous indicators of “Women

target for this indicator has been achieved in 2022.
