 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING


The implementation of the Resilient and Inclusive Housing Project in Colombia continues to make significant progress. Efforts
to overcome obstacles have led to accelerated procurement processes and the execution of key activities. The total funds
committed to the project have increased to USD 46.67 million (IBRD USD 33.47 million and grants USD 10.2 million), including
USD 16.4 million disbursed in December 2024.
Regarding Subcomponent 1.1, 1,488 housing improvements were finished (12% of target) and an additional 5,490 are in
progress (55% accumulated of target). Additionally, 200 rent subsidies for migrants have been allocated in Bogota, and the
MVCT signed collaboration agreements with additional municipalities to allocate around 1,300 subsidies in 2025. Regarding
Subcomponent 1.2, four neighborhood improvement interventions and ten parks are in contractual and pre-contractual
processes. Component 2 hasn’t recorded significant progress. To date, only one technical assistance has been carried out
through an individual consultancy contract to structure sustainability and resilience strategies to be implemented in the housing
improvements of the "Cambia Mi Casa" Program.
The project’s procurement performance is Moderately Satisfactory. It is noteworthy that the procurement dynamics have
substantially improved. The general status of the PP represents 179 activities with a total value of US$ 61 million. Signed
contracts: 65% and ongoing and pending processes 35%. It is expected that in the first quarter of 2025, the ongoing and pending
processes will be awarded and signed, potentially resulting in approximately 42 contracts valued at USD 24 million with only
12% remaining to be contracted. The project has outlined the current bottlenecks and risks in procurement management, as
well as proposed mitigation actions and strategies, which will undergo regular monitoring. It is crucial to note that financial and
budgetary constraints remain the most significant risk that could impede the progress of procurement processes and the
execution of related contracts. As well as some significant challenges that have been identified with the implementing agent for
MIB and EP.
The financial management (FM) performance rating for the project has been assessed as Moderately Unsatisfactory. This
rating is attributed to a number of factors: Firstly, the project's planning and budgeting processes have encountered significant
challenges, leading to delays in both project implementation and disbursements. Secondly, there have been delays in the
preparation and submission of Unaudited Interim Financial Reports (UIFRs) and audits, which have adversely affected
compliance with financial reporting agreements with the Bank. Lastly, the project has struggled to justify or submit Withdrawal
Applications (WAs) due to difficulties in adhering to the pari-passu rule for documenting expenditures. These issues have
persisted since the project's inception in September 2021 and are largely due to the low execution of trust funds. In January
2025, the MVCT took steps to strengthen the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) by hiring two additional FM support consultants.
To further facilitate the execution of trust funds proceeds (GCFF funds), the MVCT incorporated grant proceeds through APC
amounting to USD 2.8 million in 2024 and included USD 2.7 million in the Annual Budget Act 2025. Additionally, in early January
2025, the MVCT requested APC for a TF budget allocation of USD 2.9 million for FY25. The MVCT anticipates making the first
withdrawal documentation by the end of April 2025, for approximately USD 1 million on a pari-passu basis.
Financial reporting and audits. In April 2025, the project successfully reported its audited financial statements as of December
31, 2023. The audit report was prepared and presented in accordance with the agreed-upon arrangements, and the auditors
issued an unmodified opinion (clean opinion). The Borrower has no outstanding audit reports. UIFRs were submitted to the
Bank until June 30, 2024, and they were acceptable; the UIFRs as of December 31, 2024, is expected to be submitted on April
30, 2025.
FM and disbursement arrangements. As a result of the restructuring, there are no changes in the financial management (FM)
arrangements that require modifications to the legal agreements; furthermore, the proposed changes do not entail the addition
of new implementing entities. However, one adjustment is required to be included in the disbursement letter (DFIL), specifically
related to the new Customized SOE annex for documenting the new eligible expenditure: House Improvement Subsidies

(Communitarian or Associate modalities) benefiting Eligible Households. The specific adjustments to the UIFRs and audits, which
will include details of the financing for the two new modalities, will be documented in the Project Operational Manual (POM).
The project’s E&S performance is Satisfactory, with housing improvement works being undertaken under ESS requirements
and the implementation of the GM. The ESCP report shows progress in the consolidation of the PIU, the incorporation of E&S
requirements in the terms of reference and SDO, the approval of contractors’ environmental and social management
instruments (C-ESMP and C-LMP), submission of periodic reports, and the implementation of the project's socialization and
dissemination strategy. The project hasn’t experienced significant incidents or accidents, and the questions, complaints and
claims received through the grievance mechanism have been addressed in a timely manner.

During the MTR held in June 2024, the MVCT mentioned the interest in pursuing the restructuring of the project to continue
improving its efficiency. The World Bank officially received the request from the MHCP in December 2024. The proposed
changes are intended to facilitate housing improvement interventions thus contributing to achieve the goal established by the
GoC of improving 400,000 housing units between 2022-2026, to reduce qualitative housing deficit that today affects over 3.9
million households (22.1%), and the Multidimensional Poverty Index. The restructuring involves three changes: i) the relocation
of 50% of the resources (USD 6.73 million) from Component 2 (Institutional Strengthening to Improve Homes and
Neighborhoods) to Subcomponent 1.1 (Supporting Subsidy Schemes to Improve Housing Quality), ii) the inclusion of an
additional eligible expenditure to enable two additional implementation modalities for housing improvement interventions
under Subcomponent 1.1, and iii) the adjustment of the end target for the indicator “Households benefited from home
improvement interventions” considering methodological changes.

By dedicating additional resources to Subcomponent 1.1, change i emphasizes the importance of housing improvement
interventions as the project’s main component directly impacting households' living conditions. The reallocation of resources
also responds to the recent improvements in the implementation of the Subcomponent, which will expedite the execution of
the project and. It is also aims concentrate efforts in achieving the GoC ambitious housing improvement goals and contribute
to reduce the housing deficit and the Multidimensional Poverty Index. Additionally, there has been very low demand from the
PIU and the MVCT to execute Component, with only one technical assistance finished to date and three planned for 2025.

The objective of change ii is to include two additional implementation modalities to expedite housing improvement
interventions by reducing the intermediation of the MVCT and Fonvivienda. Currently, the MVCT manages the whole process
from the calls to subnational governments to present eligible households, to the verification of the households’ and houses'
conditions, the supervision of construction works, and the allocation of subsidies. The new “Associate” and “Communitarian”
modalities reduce the intermediation of the MVCT by granting different counterparts the possibility of leading the same process
and requesting the grant of the subsidy for each household at the end, once the MVCT verifies the fulfillment of the household’s
and houses' conditions and the completion of construction works. To ensure the integrity of the project, eligible counterparts
must develop the process according to the World Bank’s ESF, ESS, the ESCP, and the Anticorruption Guidelines. Eligible
counterparts for the “Associate” modalities are territorial entities and/or other public entities. Eligible counterparts for the
“Communitarian” modalities are members of the civil society, including persons, social organizations, non-governmental
organizations, and community organizations. To enable this change, it is also necessary to include “housing improvement
subsidies” as an additional eligible expenditure.

Change iii refers to the adjustment of the end target for the indicator “Households benefited from home improvement
interventions”. The adjustment takes into consideration methodological changes introduced by the GoC in the 2022-2026

Change ii: Inclusion of an additional eligible expenditure to enable additional implementation mechanisms for housing
improvement interventions under Subcomponent 1.1

Decree 1077 of 2015 establishes the regulatory framework for the housing improvement program in both urban and rural
areas. In addition, the Decree 0413 of 2025 includes different implementation modalities with the objective of scaling up and
speeding up the implementation of the program. In these new modalities, members of the community and legal entities are
involved in executing housing improvement works, giving social and community organizations a leading role in habitat
solutions, and strengthening organizational dynamics and local economies in the territories. In both cases, the MVCT will

                                            Official Use Only                                                              Page 3
          The World Bank
          Colombia: Resilient and Inclusive Housing Project (P172535)


transfer the amount of the subsidy to the “Associate” or “Communitarian” developer once the housing improvement process
has concluded, it has been verified by the designated supervisor and FONVIVIENDA, and accepted by the eligible household.

As stated in Resolutions 0782 of 2023 and 0488 of 2024, in the “Communitarian” and “Associate” modalities, eligible
counterparts are enabled to participate in executing housing improvements in rural and/or urban areas, with FONVIVIENDA
allocating housing improvement subsidies once the executed works have been verified and certified, and after meeting the
subsidy access requirements. Therefore, subsidies allocated through the “Associate” and “Communitarian” modalities must
fulfill the requirements and regulations established in Decree 1077 of 2015 and Decree 0413 of 2025, including the beneficiary
selection criteria and the subsidy amount, among others.

To ensure the integrity of the project, eligible counterparts under the “Communitarian” and “Associate” modalities must
develop the process according to the World Bank’s ESF, ESS, the ESCP, and the Anticorruption Guidelines. Eligible developers
for the “Associate” modalities are territorial entities and/or other public entities . Eligible developers for the “Communitarian”
modalities are members of the civil society, including persons, social organizations, non-governmental organizations, and
community organizations.

The Resilient and Inclusive Housing Project currently finances works carried out by Fonvivienda under the Procurement
regulations. With the Associate and Communitarian modalities described above the Project will finance house improvement
subsidies. Therefore, the Borrower requested the inclusion of housing improvement subsidies as an additional expenditure
category.

 Summary of Performance of E&S Risk Compliance

 The project’s E&S performance is Satisfactory, with housing improvement works being undertaken under ESS requirements
 and the implementation of the GM. The ESCP report shows progress in the consolidation of the PIU, the incorporation of E&S
 requirements in the terms of reference and SDO, the approval of contractors’ environmental and social management
 instruments (C-ESMP and C-LMP), submission of periodic reports, and the implementation of the project's engagement and
 dissemination strategy. The project has not experienced significant incidents or accidents, and the questions, complaints and
 claims received through the GM have been addressed in a timely manner.



 Describe any E&S issues and impacts associated with the change in project design. Identify and describe any potential
 large scale, significant and/or irreversible impacts.

 The proposed changes do not carry E&S issues and impacts respect to changes i and iii, the changes do not have any
 impact on the project design as they concern budget relocation and the adjustment of end targets. Change ii includes
 two additional implementation mechanisms which require updating the E&S assessment to ensure potential risks and
 impacts will be adequately managed by the eligible implementing agents in compliance with the project’s ESS as set in
 the updated ESCP.

 The incorporation of the “Associate” and “Communitarian” modalities under change ii requires the revision of the social
 risks and impacts to update and adapt the mitigation measures in light of the specific contracting and operational
 arrangements, and capacity of the new implementing agents. There are two main concerns relating to issues and
 impacts. Firstly, issues relating to safety, security and the risk of gender-based violence, covered under ESS4, will be
 assessed as part of the World Bank’s due diligence to: (i) ensure the sufficiency of the project ESMF, Community Health
 and Safety Plan, GM, worker code of conduct and other relevant environmental and social tools to identify and manage
 these risks; (ii) understand the application of these tools to date and as necessary, adjust them and inform training
 measures, capacity requirements and additional guidelines to strengthen risk management.

 The inclusion of these modalities may result in impacts related to habitability, livelihoods, health, and safety that housing
 improvements may generate for beneficiary residents during their development. To mitigate these risks, the project will
 include appropriate measures during the duration of the improvements to manage cases where it is necessary to
 temporarily and voluntarily relocate beneficiaries due to their vulnerability, whenever previously implemented
 mitigation measures are insufficient to avoid this impact. The MVCT will establish through administrative and/or legally

binding documents (i.e., agreements with municipalities, contracts, calls, allocation resolutions, agreements, among
others), the necessary mechanisms internally and with municipalities, executors and other relevant to ensure the
allocation of resources to guarantee the proper implementation of the above-mentioned measures.

The environmental and OHS risks and impacts of the Project will continue to be moderate, since the new implementation
mechanisms consider the inclusion of aspects aligned with Colombian regulations and the ESS. In this sense, the
environmental mitigation measures and OHS controls established in the ESCP, ESMF and other E&S instruments are
sufficient to manage the identified risks and impacts. However, as it corresponds to an additional eligible expense, which
no longer considers the execution of the work as part of the financing, the respective adjustments must be made to the
E&S instruments.

Consequently, it will be essential to consider and manage environmental and OHS risks, as well as social risks, under a
preventive approach in the call and diagnosis phases, and to carry out the corresponding verification in the phase of
receipt to satisfaction of the housing improvements. In this sense, it will be required to carry out an adequate
identification and evaluation of the capacity and previous performance of the executors of the new implementation
mechanisms; and define the corresponding training measures (ESF, E&S regulatory requirements, etc.) for the selected
implementing agents.

Describe any potential indirect and/or long-term impacts due to anticipated future activities in the project areas.

No indirect and/or long-term environmental and social impacts are identified in relation to future activities in the project
areas. The location and E&S characteristics evaluated for the project areas persist. It has not been confirmed whether
new municipalities will be included, or whether the new housing improvement mechanisms will be implemented in the
municipalities already characterized in the ESMF. In any case, the corresponding reservation will be included in the E&S
instruments to ensure the prior evaluation and E&S characterization of new municipalities, if required.

Describe any project alternatives (if relevant) considered to help avoid or minimize adverse impacts.

The new implementation mechanisms to expedite housing improvement interventions (“Associate” and “Communitarian”
modalities) will include social and environmental aspects aligned with the Colombian legislation and the ESF considered
for the selection of sites and potential beneficiaries, the delivery of improvements, and the permanent monitoring by
the MVCT's program teams and supervision in the territory. In this new scenario, the E&S aspects will be incorporated
through the following elements: (a) E&S requirements in circulars and annexes to the calls (and transfer to the resolution
for assigning quotas); (b) guide to applying E&S aspects as part of the capacity building process; (c) verification of
compliance with E&S aspects in the certificate of existence of the improvement and document of delivery to satisfaction;
(d) strengthening capacities of the PIU, MVCT and implementing agencies through training, technical assistance, etc.;
and (e) monitoring visits to selected samples by MVCT. To ensure compliance with E&S aspects, it must be ensured that
the supervision in the territory and also the generation of certificates includes the participation of E&S professionals;
and ensure that not only at specific moments, but throughout implementation, there are relevant measures for the
fulfillment of key activities such as socialization and adequate management of the grievance mechanism under the new
implementation mechanisms.

The Project’s ESCP will be modified to include the environmental and social risk and impact management measures that
have been established for the new implementation mechanisms to expedite housing improvement interventions. The ESCP
should include as a commitment the updating of the project's ESMF, in line with the E&S requirements for the new

   Describe measures taken by the borrower to address E&S policy issues. Provide an assessment of borrower capacity to
   plan and implement the measure described.

   The borrower capacity does not vary in relation to the previous assessment. However, the World Bank team has pointed
   out the importance of strengthening the borrower´s territorial presence to ensure adequate management, including the
   articulation with and supervision of the new potential implementing agencies under the “Associate” and “Communitarian”
   modalities. Additionally, the project will ensure that there are robust measures in place to ensure sufficient identification
   and screening of the new implementing entities capacity and past performance so that related risks can be minimized.
   There may be a need to adapt project instruments to ensure enforceability and capacity building in relation to the
   conformance of CSO activities with ESF/project legal requirements. This will be reflected in the updates made to the
   relevant E&S management instruments.

   Identify key stakeholders and describe the mechanism for consultation and disclosure on E&S policies, with an
   emphasis of potentially affected people.
   The project's stakeholders include low-income households with housing shortages, migrant population households
   requiring support to rent housing, or households in neighborhood environments with weak social infrastructure or lack
   of collective facilities that require interventions to improve their quality of life. Other stakeholders refer to territorial
   entities in charge of supporting vulnerable households, including the migrant population and leading entities in housing
   improvement activities. The potential implementing agencies under the “Associate” and “Communitarian” modalities (see
   Section I) will be incorporated into the SEP as interested parties and measures will be reviewed to guarantee compliance
   with ESS1 and ESS10; consequently, the socialization and dissemination methodologies will be updated to reflect their
   needs and interests, and measures will be included to ensure adequate articulation of the GM between the MVCT and
