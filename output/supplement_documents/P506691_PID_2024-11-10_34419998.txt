 B. Introduction and Context

 Country Context
1.       The Interim Government led by Nobel Laureate Dr. Muhammad Yunus assumed authority on August 8, 2024,
after former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina resigned amidst a student-led mass uprising. Bangladesh faces several
significant challenges, including sustaining growth generating employment; reforming the financial sector; enhancing
governance, transparency and accountability of public institutions; and improving the business environment. The Interim
Government intends to address these pressing issues through substantial governance reforms with a sense of urgency.
Bangladesh aspires to develop a public sector that is open and responsive, including a citizen-centric system that fully
leverages technology to ensure that all enjoy equal rights and opportunities.
2.      Real Gross Domestic Product (GDP) growth is estimated to have decelerated to 5.2 percent in FY24. Inflation
remained elevated, averaging 9.7 percent in FY24, driven by rising food inflation. The fiscal deficit narrowed slightly to 4.5
percent of GDP in FY24 compared to 4.6 percent in FY23. Tax revenues remain among the lowest in the world, estimated
at 7.4 percent of GDP in FY24. Expenditure is estimated to have increased modestly to 13.0 percent of GDP, driven by
current expenditure. Capital expenditure growth remained moderate as a result of slow implementation of development
projects.
3.       The Balance of Payment (BoP) deficit narrowed in FY24, led by a narrowing of the current account deficit. A
decline in merchandise imports due to import suppression measures and an improvement in remittance inflows helped
the current account deficit to narrow to US$6.5 billion in FY24 from US$11.6 billion in FY23. The financial account surplus
narrowed to US$4.5 billion in FY24, driven by a decline in net trade credits. Overall, the FY24 BoP deficit stood at US$4.3,
compared to US$8.2 billion in FY23. In the first two months of FY25 (July-August), the current account balance turned
into a surplus, driven by sharp increase in remittances. In May 2024, Bangladesh Bank adopted a crawling peg exchange
rate system and devalued the exchange rate to help restore external balance and stabilize international reserves which
had experienced sustained depletion. FX reserves declined by US$1.7 billion since June 2024 to reach US$19.9 billion (3.3
months of import coverage) as of October 16, 2024. International reserves can deplete further due to payments due for
fuel and fertilizer imports.
4.       Real GDP growth is projected to decline to 4.0 percent in FY25 but could range between 3.2 and 5.2 percent.
The wide range of the growth projection reflects significant uncertainties regarding the economic disruptions caused by
the popular protests and political turmoil. The economic activities and the law-and-order situation have been gradually
improving since the transition to the Interim Government. However, remaining uncertainties regarding the impact of the
political turmoil are expected to keep investment and industrial growth subdued in the short term. Elevated inflation rose
further to 11.4 percent in July 2024 due to supply chain disruptions following the political turmoil. Monetary policy has
remained tight, as the policy rate increased by 375 basis points in the last two years. Vulnerabilities in the financial sector
have worsened. Persistent inflation and subdued global growth prospects will weigh on growth in the near term as well.
Recent floods are expected to moderate agriculture growth. The growth rate is expected to increase gradually in

subsequent years, benefiting from critical financial sector reforms, increased revenue mobilization, improved business
climate, and trade.
5.       Structural reforms are needed to accelerate growth over the medium term. To become an upper-middle-income
country (UMIC), priorities include building an effective and efficient public sector, supportive business environment,
diversifying exports, increasing human capital, building efficient infrastructure, deepening the financial sector, and
attracting private investment. These reforms would strengthen Bangladesh’s international competitiveness as it prepares
for graduation from Least Developed Country (LDC) status in 2026, which will reduce concessional financing and
preferential market access for its exports. Bangladesh has a low risk of overall and external debt distress according to the
June 2024 joint International Monetary Fund (IMF)-World Bank Debt Sustainability Assessment (DSA). Bangladesh is
currently not subject to Debt Limits Conditionality under the Sustainable Development Finance Policy (SDFP).
6.       Bangladesh faces a high level of vulnerability to the effects of climate change. The Global Climate Risk Index
ranks it as the seventh most affected country between 2000-2019, with high susceptibility to extreme weather events
such as cyclones, floods, and storm surges. According to the Bangladesh Country Climate and Development Report (CCDR),
recurring flooding in Bangladesh affects a greater share of the population than any other natural hazard, impacting more
than 1 million people annually. Once every three to five years, up to two-thirds of the country is inundated by floods.
More recently, a major flood in the southeastern region of Bangladesh impacted over 3 million people in August
2024.Addressing these climate risks will support sustainable economic development and prevent vulnerable populations
from being left behind.


 Sectoral and Institutional Context


7.       Bangladesh’s public sector capacity has weakened over the past decades. According to the World Governance
Indicators (WGI), Bangladesh’s government effectiveness and accountability have deteriorated in the past two decades
compared to peers such as Bhutan and Vietnam. It indicates weaknesses in the quality of public services, the civil service,
as well as the ability to formulate and implement policies, which are critical for reaching UMIC status. Indeed, to become
a UMIC, Bangladesh will require a transparent, accountable and citizen-centric public sector able to mobilize adequate
domestic resource and effectively deliver public services.
8.       Over the past two decades, Bangladesh has made significant efforts in the modernization of its National
Statistical System (NSS) but still faces challenges to fully leverage the potential of data for policymaking. The NSS covers
Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS) and all other administrative entities producing statistics, all of which face at various
degree infrastructural, institutional, and human resource capacity challenges constraints. Beyond the BBS, the NSS has
found it increasingly challenging to meet the rising demands of its users. Notable among the challenges are: (i) a lack of
effective coordination and collaboration between elements of the NSS; (ii) inadequate physical infrastructure, especially
at the subnational level; (iii) a shortage of skilled human resource capacity, particularly at the Division and District (Zila)
levels, in data processing, management, storage, dissemination, and specialized skills in data science, big data, and
geospatial data; (iv) the lack of a common format and platforms for collecting, organizing and sharing administrative data,
including environmental, health, labor, trade and agricultural statistics; (v) the absence of proper mechanisms to ensure
the quality of data provided by government ministries and agencies; (vi) insufficient financial resources and technical
capacity for administrative data collection, compilation, processing, and dissemination, particularly concerning
environmental and climatic data; and (v) the absence of designated focal point officers in the data-providing government
agencies. The project aims to address all these challenges and improve data use and transparency in decision-making.
9.    Bangladesh’s revenue mobilization is extremely low and inadequate for its development financing needs. As
documented in the forthcoming Bangladesh Public Finance Review (PFR), Bangladesh revenue-to-GDP ratio declined from

9.1 percent in FY12 to just 8.5 percent in FY24. The tax-to-GDP ratio stagnated between 7 to 8 percent of GDP during this
period, reaching 7.4 percent in FY24, one of the lowest in the world and significantly below its peers, including the average
of other South Asian countries (19.4 percent average from 2017-2022), structural peers (16.1 percent), and aspirational
peers (20.5 percent). The main challenges include: (i) a heavy reliance on trade-related taxes; (ii) a value-added tax (VAT)
system characterized by numerous exemptions and truncated rates that reduce collection without being pro-poor; and
(iii) significant exemptions, deductions, rebates, and tax holidays. The challenges have been identified in several policy
documents, including in the annual government budgets. However, revenue generation has continued to remain low
because of the political economy challenge in reaching a consensus on concrete policy reforms. The proposed operation
will support the implementation of reforms to increase revenue mobilization, including the rationalization of tax
expenditures, the expansion of the taxpayer base by reducing compliance gaps, and the modernization of the tax
administration to expand taxpayer services.
10.      There are significant inefficiencies in public investment management (PIM). 1 The implementation rate of capital
expenditure stood at a mere 77 percent in FY22. There are unclear prioritization rules for project selection. Key
stakeholders such as industry experts and citizens are not consulted in the design and formulation of the long- and
medium-term planning frameworks, including the Five Years Plans (FYP), which often results in unrealistic forecasts and
unmet citizen needs. The government lacks a strong mechanism to ensure that ongoing projects continue to receive
adequate financing, and an overall accountability and transparency institutional framework to ensure that projects are
citizen-centric, prepared and delivered on time, and yield the expected results. These significant gaps in planning and
project preparation at the upstream level have negative cascading effects on project implementation. To address these
limitations, the Project will develop and leverage digital tools, and invest in IT systems and digital competencies of PIM
personnel to improve the efficiency and effectiveness in the use of public funds and strengthen accountability through
external scrutiny and transparency of the public investment budget. Increasing the efficiency of Bangladesh’s capex
spending to the level of the most efficient countries with similar levels of public investment per capita would be equivalent
to increasing Bangladesh’s capex spending by 54 percent.2
11.     Strengthening public procurement will be central to improving public investment efficiency and effectiveness
and service delivery. Bangladesh made significant progress over two decades to systematically reform the public
procurement system. However, there are areas that still need improvement, including contract management (about 70
percent of civil works contracts are not completed on time); lack of incentives for sustainable public procurement (SPP)
that aims to achieve value for money on the life-cycle of goods and services procured; delays in bid evaluation and
approval of contracts; and inadequate integration of the procurement system with the budget and accounting system.
The consolidation of procurement responsibilities and authorities under the Bangladesh Public Procurement Authority
(BPPA) needs to be fully operationalized and complemented with critical steps that will be supported by the Project to:
(a) strengthen project preparation and procurement planning; (b) complete the missing electronic-Government
Procurement (e-GP) functionalities, for example, services (consultancy), international competitive bidding (ICB) for large
works using government and donor funds to ensure the increased use of the e-GP; (c) make mandatory use of e-GP; (d)
disclose the beneficial ownership of public procurement contracts; (e) remove +/- 10 percentage price cap in the national
open tender method; (f) remove the lottery system in the limited tender method; (g) integrate the e-GP with the
Integrated Budget and Accounting System (IBAS++), and upstream with the budget and downstream with the payment
module; (h) professionalize public procurement and enhance capacity of public sector organizations; and (i) implement
SPP and green public procurement (GPP) in key infrastructure agencies.

12.      The Interim Government is emphasizing the strengthening of oversight and accountability of public sector
institutions for improved service delivery to its citizens. The Government has developed significant digital Public Financial
Management (PFM) capabilities, including automation of critical PFM processes. However, achievements remain
fragmented, and strengthening oversight and accountability of public sector institutions will be critical to ensure better
service delivery. The Supreme Audit Institution (SAI) is an important actor in a country’s accountability chain. Its external
oversight role is established by the Constitution. The Office of the Comptroller and Auditor General (OCAG) needs to be
enhanced for greater efficiency and effectiveness through the technological transformation of audit systems across public
institutions and support for specialized audit capacity. The oversight and accountability systems of strategically important
ministries and departments need to be strengthened for growth-sustaining and socially impactful public investment
programs. Their institutional capacity could also be developed by building specialized audit capacity in identified areas,
including the climate finance program, the environment, procurement, emergency operations and large infrastructure
projects. The Project will address these challenges by supporting: (i) an audit in the digital era; (ii) the enhancement of the
overall PFM capacity; (iii) the improvement of budget and expenditure management for better service delivery; and (iii)
the integration of cross-cutting elements of PFM for enhanced budget performance.
 Relationship to CPF

13. The proposed Project is fully aligned with the Country Partnership Framework (CPF) for FY 2023-27, currently under
implementation. Building on the country’s development progress, the CPF seeks to help Bangladesh transform itself into
a more competitive, resilient, inclusive, and greener economy as it aspires to become an upper-middle-income country.
The proposed project will support Objective 3: “Improved effectiveness of public institutions to deliver better services to
citizens and businesses.�? The CPF also proposes leveraging digital tools to enhance transparency and accountability in
Bangladesh, and the proposed project will directly contribute to these efforts.

 C. Proposed Development Objective(s)
 The project development objective is to enhance access to reliable public statistics, increase tax compliance and
 improve efficiency and accountability of public spending.


  Key Results (From PCN)

14. The proposed PDO indicators would consist of:
     • Increase in users trust in public statistics produced by BBS.
     • Increase in tax compliance rates on VAT and Income Tax.
     • Increase in citizen engagement and selectivity in public investment projects.
     • Increase in digital management of public procurement contracts (the 95% target will be specified during the
        project appraisal).
     • Improved timeliness of audit reports.

 D. Concept Description


15.    The project will provide technical and financial support to five critical agencies to implement and institutionalize
urgent transparency and accountability reforms undertaken by the Interim Government. The agencies are BBS; the
National Board of Revenue (NBR); the Planning Division (PD) under the Ministry of Planning; the Bangladesh Public

Procurement Authority (BPPA), and the Office of the Comptroller and Auditor General (OCAG). The Project will have three
key components with a clear line of sight to the policy actions supported through the BEIR DPC.
Component 1: Data for Better Evidence-based Policy and Public Investments
16.      This component complements the transparency reforms supported by the BEIR DPC through the adoption of
digital data systems for statistical capacity enhancement and modernization. It will strengthen the capacity of the BBS to
support evidence-based policymaking and improved service delivery through investments in: (i) modernizing physical and
statistical infrastructure, including data management and information and communication technologies (ICT) systems, to
inform core macroeconomic, fiscal, and social protection policy functions; (ii) improving the transparency and timeliness
of data production to ensure its availability for public and private sectors, civil society, and citizens; and (iii) building public
sector capacity to utilize statistical, administrative and big data to support citizen-centric service delivery.
Component 2: Domestic Revenue Mobilization for Sustainable Public Sector Financing
17.     The Project will finance the design and implementation of business process reforms and relevant change
management activities; provide training; strengthen analytical capacity and policy analysis; manage internal and
external communications; and provide relevant goods and services. The subcomponent will also strengthen the NBR’s
Research and Statistics Wing with the capacity to conduct data collection, management and tax analysis and revenue
forecasting, tax gap and tax expenditure analysis, and identification of compliance risks. It will support the design and
implementation of VAT and tax expenditure rationalization and tax compliance management. The Project will also
strengthen tax administration, focused on digitalization of tax administration and interoperability of the various tax IT
systems. The sub-component will support: (i) Income tax administration, including end-to-end automation of simplified
business processes;(ii) VAT administration, including the development of a SAP competency center within the NBR’s IT
Wing, and the completion of the development of the Integrated VAT Administration System (IVAS) IT system and the
integration of the IVAS with the SAP and Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) Software; (iii) Interoperability among the NBR
IT systems (Automated System for Customs Data (ASYCUDA) World, IVAS and income tax) and with the iBAS++ (Integrated
Budgeting and Accounting System), and other key data systems and (iv) Taxpayer outreach and services, including the
establishment of an automated Taxpayer Call Center. The subcomponent will also support the tax administration
workforce capacity by developing and implementing a capacity development and training plan. In particular, it will
strengthen internal information technology (IT) capacity, including SAP competency and certification programs.
Component 3: Expenditure Efficiency and Transparency for Accountable and Improved Service Delivery
18.      This Project will strengthen institutional capacity for efficient and transparent project planning, preparation,
and selection. First, it will support (i) the development and implementation of a project selection framework and citizens’
feedback mechanisms to streamline, rationalize, and improve public scrutiny of investment projects, including pipeline
and stock; (ii) training of staff in the Planning Commission and across ministries, departments, and agencies (MAD) in
various planning, appraisal, and forecast tools. The Project will improve procurement practices, project implementation,
e-GP scope and coverage and implementation of SPP. This support consists of three interrelated areas (i) E-GP
enhancement, (ii) Public Procurement Professionalization, and (iii) Public Procurement practices and SPP. The project will
also support Strengthening Accountability Institutions for Improved Oversight of Public Expenditures, in particular(i)
Building IT infrastructure and audit digitalization; (ii) Enhancing transparency, accountability and efficiency of public
institutions and high spending sectors, as well as infrastructure investments; (iii) Improving the timeliness of the Annual
Audit, Government Response, Disclosure, Dissemination and Communication Strategy; and (iv) Strengthening the center
of excellence “Financial Management Academy�? (FIMA) and the professionalization of the OCAG personnel (US$1 million).

19.     The project will support the: (i) Modernization of FIMA; (ii) Professional development through training, courses,
training of trainers (ToT) program; (iii) Collaboration framework with the Institute of Chartered Accountants of
Bangladesh, the Institute of Internal Audit, the Anti-Corruption Commission, as well as Civil Society Organizations (CSO)

Component 4: Project Management
20.      A High-Level Oversight Committee co-chaired by the Ministry of Finance and Ministry of Planning will be
established to provide strategic guidance, oversight and coordination between the five Implementing Agencies (IAs)
involved in this project: the BBS, NBR, Planning Division, BBPA and the OCAG. The day-to-day management of the project’s
activities will happen at the IA level. Five Project Directors will be appointed and five separate DPP (Development Project
Proposal) or TPP (Technical Project Proposal) will be prepared to detail the specific activities to be undertaken by each of
the IAs.
