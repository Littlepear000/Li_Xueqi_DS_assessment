 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  Component 1. Creating safe and accessible learning spaces
  Sub-Component 1.1 Creating new learning spaces in secondary schools
        Status: The architectural designs and drawings for all planned construction under this sub-component have been finalized with a
         construction manual. All participating states have prepared bidding documents using construction drawings, the Bill of Quantities, and
         minimum standards specifications. SPIUs are at different stages as some have awarded contracts and sites have been handed over to the
         contractors while some are still reviewing the bids. The actual status of this component and the challenges experienced by the SPIUs will
         be assessed during the upcoming mission.
        Next steps: All procurement processes including evaluation of contracts and awarding of contracts are to be finalized in all participating
         states by the next ISM for both supervision and construction contracts.

  Sub-component 1.2. Renovation and rehabilitation of schools
        Status: School Improvement Manual developed, School-based management committees (SBMCs) have been established in all the
         schools, and school improvement plans (SIPs) have been developed by all schools. School improvement grants (SIGs) have been
         provided to a total of 3,968 schools across the seven participating states and all of the states have completed the implementation of the
         small SIGs. For the Large SIGs, three states (Kaduna, Kebbi, and Borno) have completed the implementation of the large school grants
         while Katsina and Plateau are at 97 and 98 percent completion, respectively. Ekiti and Kano states are currently behind in the
         implementation of the Large SIGs as Ekiti has achieved about 40 percent completion while Kano is yet to commence the activities due to
         political challenge (change of commissioner and project coordinator). Under the third cycle, states are benefitting from special grants
         which are targeted at whole-school renovation. Currently, states are at different stages. Based on available data, over 3,968 schools have
         been renovated; 7,731 classrooms rehabilitated; 11,487 WASH (1,745 new boreholes plus 9,742 new toilets) facilities constructed, and
         198,305 pieces of furniture provided.
        Next steps: (i) Completion of the implementation of large grants in Katsina, Plateau, Ekiti and Kano states. (ii) Commencement of the
         second cycle of Large SIGs in schools in Kano. The achievements reported by the SPIUs under this component will be verified during the
         upcoming mission.
  Component 2: Fostering an enabling environment for girls
  Sub-component 2.1. Promoting social and behavioral change through traditional rulers, campaigns, and advocacy.
        Status: A national communications campaign is being implemented, and communications campaigns have been rolled out across all
         seven states and are in various stages of implementation. Various TV programs, radio shows, jingles, engagement with religious and
         traditional leaders, and various other activities are ongoing to sensitize and create awareness of the importance of girls’ education.
         Preliminary data from the states show that some states have reached up to 4 million persons via Television and Radio programming
         (digital reach). A national workshop was held in February 2023 on social norms messaging, after which the states developed a 6-month
         implementation plan for communication activities guided by the communications framework. Some issues identified during the mission
         was that: (i) information, education, and communication (IEC) activities commenced, and materials produced before the completion of
         audience research; (ii) more emphasis has been placed on IEC products on advocacy; and (iii) there is need for capacity building for state
         government personnel assigned to AGILE state offices.
        Next steps: States are to escalate social norms activities and develop and implement large-scale communications campaigns – an
         adaptation of the National communications campaign Madubbi. In addition, SPIUs will escalate engagement with traditional and religious
         leaders, and NPCUs and SPIUs will increase the use of digital platforms. Lastly, a national-level workshop will be held with the states on
         message development and using marketing approaches for social norms change.
  Subcomponent 2.2.a Providing girls with critical life skills
        Status: Implementation is ongoing in all states with safe spaces established and running in all states. The states are at different
         levels of progress as Kano, Katsina, Kaduna, and Ekiti, are advanced in their implementation and are poised to scale up to additional
         schools for greater coverage. Currently, about 331 schools are implementing surpassing the year-2 target of 240 schools. About 1,147
         facilitators and mentors have been trained to handle the life skills sessions, and there are currently 26,193 girls enrolled.
        Next steps: (i) Provision of necessary support/guidance and clearing of work-plans to enable scale-up across the states (ii) Continuous
         support on implementation and supportive supervision to state teams and schools.
  Subcomponent 2.2.b. Digital skills and remote learning platform
        Status: Digital skills training has commenced in all states. About 628 schools are currently implementing the digital literacy program with
         250,322 students currently enrolled. An average of 63% of the girls enrolled in digital skills training across the states are already
         demonstrating digital literacy. This result exceeds the PDO indicator end target set for this activity.
        Next Steps: (i) A scale-up of training to junior secondary schools and senior secondary schools; (ii) Collaboration with the ICT Bureau and
         ICT Hubs for the hands-on training of female students and; (iii) Continuation of training for teachers.
   Subcomponent 2.3. Providing financial incentives (FI) to the poorest households
        Status: Funds have been disbursed to 189,858 girls who have been registered under cohorts 1 and 2 (transitioning from JSS3 to SSS1
         and JSS1 to JSS2, respectively). This result exceeds the year-2 target for the number of scholarships to be provided under the project. All
         states have also commenced the registration of the girls that will benefit under Cohort 3 and the payments have already started
         in September 2023. The MIS payment and tracking system for financial incentives has been set up at the national level and support is
         being provided to the SPIUs for the replication at the state level. The actual status of this component will be assessed during the upcoming
         mission.
        Next steps: (i) The World Bank and PNCU will continue to provide guidance and support to SPIUs to ensure compliance with the
         developed Financial Incentive Handbook, data collection templates, supervision guidelines, and work plan agreed upon. MIS payment and
         tracking system for financial incentives will be set up at the State Level that will enable the project to track and monitor each beneficiary by
         grade, amount, and progression across grades. (ii) Support states to complete outstanding payments (iii) Provision of support to state
         teams to develop comprehensive client-level data management and reporting systems.
  .Component 3. System Strengthening and M&E
        Status: A Third-Party Monitoring (TPM) firm has been engaged by the national team to verify data on key indicators at the state level and
         identify potential challenges to project implementation. The TPM firm visited all the states and confirmed all the results achieved under the
         last quarter. A report of the field visit has been submitted to the national team. A unified M&E system has been developed at the national
         level to facilitate data collection on all key indicators. State-specific grievance indicators have been defined by the states and are currently
         being tracked. Implementation of the whole school approach is slow across states and the security assessment was completed only in
         Katsina state and is currently being implemented.
        Next steps: The NPCU will continue to work with the consulting firm and the SPIUs to ensure that the unified M&E system is further
         improved and accessible before the mission in November 2023. The TPM firm will commence the verification of the results achieved for
         this current quarter.
