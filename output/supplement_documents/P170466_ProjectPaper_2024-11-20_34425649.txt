    I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

      1. The Investing in Forests and Protected Areas for Climate-Smart Development (IFPA-CD) Project has been under
      implementation for over four years, since it was approved in April 2020 and achieved effectiveness in August 2021.
      The project experienced significant start-up delays due to operational constraints imposed by the novel coronavirus
      (COVID-19) response measures and delays in procurement and environmental and social (E&S) preparatory activities
      (partly due to the restrictions in the operating environment and partly due to lack of familiarity with World Bank
      requirements, as this is the first project approved under the new Environmental and Social Framework (ESF) in
      Uganda). A mid-term review phase 2 mission in September 2024 noted that continued progress has been made in the
      implementation of key project activities, in particular those implemented by the Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) and
      the National Forestry Authority (NFA). Although good progress has also been made with initiating bidding processes
      for various civil works under the Project, no contracts for works have been signed to date.
      2. As discussed during the September 2024 mission and recorded in the mission aide memoire, the Project will be
      restructured to reduce the project scope by removing Ajai Wildlife Reserve (WR). At the same time, the project
      development objective (PDO) will be modified to clarify the scope of the Project Area, and protected areas within the
      project scope will be clearly enumerated in the Financing Agreement. This will require an amendment of the Financing
      Agreement.


    II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

      3. Removal of Ajai WR from project scope. In July 2024, UWA had started advancing the long-planned process of
      resettling a small number of households from inside the boundary of Ajai WR. The resettlement is not caused or linked
      to project activities, but as Ajai WR is one of the project-supported protected areas, the Bank had advised that this
      resettlement should be carried out in compliance with the World Bank ESF, in addition to Government of Uganda
      procedures. Since compliance with World Bank ESF would inevitably involve delays, and the Government of Uganda
      was well-advanced in the resettlement process, including discussions on compensation and timelines with affected
      people, the Government was concerned that delays would frustrate local communities who had expectations
      regarding the timeline of compensation from ongoing consultation with UWA and this could have a chilling effect on
      community relations. In light of that, UWA requested that Ajai WR be removed from the project scope and a
      communication to this effect was received from UWA’s Executive Director on August 15, 2024 and in a letter from the
      Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development dated October 14, 2024. In addition, one of the original
      criteria for inclusion of protected areas in project included “absence of social risks requiring involuntary
      resettlement/land compensation” – as resettlement has now started, Ajai WR no longer meets this original criterion.
      Respective unspent funds (from the Refugee Sub-Window of IDA 18)1 will remain allocated to sub-component 1.4.
      and finance scale up of activities in Katonga WR.

      4. Refinement of the project PDO. In order to clarify the geographic scope of project interventions in protected
      areas, the PDO will change from “to improve sustainable management of forests and protected areas and increase
      benefits to communities from forests in target landscapes” to “(i) improve sustainable management of forests and
      protected areas in target landscapes; and (ii) increase benefits to communities from forests in target landscapes”. In
      the Financing Agreement, the PDO definition will include capitalized use of “target landscapes” and “protected areas”
      as defined terms.
      5. Tightening definitions of the project area in the Financing Agreement. The term “target landscapes” will be
      defined within the Financing Agreement in accordance with project documentation, and the definitions of the specific
      target national parks (NP), WRs, and central forest reserves (CFR) will be enumerated by name in the definitions
      section of the Financing Agreement. Namely: “national parks” will be defined as “Bwindi Impenetrable NP, Kibale NP,
      Murchison Falls NP, Mgahinga Gorilla NP, Queen Elizabeth NP, Rwenzori Mountains NP, and Semuliki NP ; “wildlife
      reserves” will be defined as Toro-Semliki WR; “central forest reserves” will be defined as Budongo CFR, Buhungiro
      CFR, Echuya CFR, Ibambaro CFR, Ihimbo CFR, Itwara CFR, Kakasi CFR, Kalinzu CFR, Kasokwa CFR, Kasyoha-Kitomi CFR,
      Kibego CFR, Kitechura CFR, Matiri CFR, Muhangi CFR, Nkera CFR, North Maramagambo CFR, and South Maramagambo
      CFR; and “Central Forest Reserves and Wildlife Reserves located close to refugee settlements in Refugee Hosting
      Areas” (funded under sub-component 1.4.) will be enumerated in the Project description as Katonga WR, Kabwoya
      WR, Bugoma CFR, Wambabya CFR, Kagombe CFR, Rwensambya CFR, Mt Kei CFR, Otzi East CFR, Otzi West CFR, Era
      CFR, Wati CFR, and Nyakarongo CFR.
      6. Revision of Results Framework. With the removal of Ajai WR from project scope, respective indicators and targets
      in the Results Framework are being revised, as follows: At the PDO level, a sub-indicator for Ajai WR is removed from
      the indicator “Management effectiveness of target protected areas within targeted forest landscapes (using METT
      score) (Number)” (sub-indicator on Ajai WR is dropped); the end of project target for the indicator “Land area under
      sustainable landscape management practices (Hectare(Ha))” and its sub-indicator “Protected areas under effective
      management (with project support) (Hectare(Ha))” are being revised (end of project targets for both are reduced by
      the area of Ajai WR, 16,600 ha).“2

        7. The restructuring proposes to remove the Ajai WR from the project scope with the respective unspent funds
        (under Sub-component 1.4) redirected to finance scale up of activities in Katonga WR. The restructuring therefore
        does not introduce any new activities in new areas but will instead scale up activities in the original scope whose risks
        and impacts have already been identified and analyzed. These activities include community livelihood activities (such
        as beekeeping and wild mushroom growing); removal of invasive species; improvements for basic PA management
        (communication and other equipment, vehicles, ranger posts, and essential infrastructure); improvements for wildfire
        management (fire observation towers and equipment); and boundary demarcation where required and appropriate.
        There are, therefore, no potential large scale, significant and/or irreversible impacts associated with these activities.

    11. The project has a Stakeholder Engagement Plan (SEP) in place, and an interagency stakeholder engagement
    template has been prepared to guide and coordinate stakeholder engagement. The stakeholders has been broadly
    mapped to include ‘people or institutions who have a role in the Project, or could be positively or negatively affected
    by the Project, or who are interested in the Project as described in the SEP and are also being engaged:

      a.        Primary stakeholders being individuals, groups or local communities that may be affected by the Project,
      positively or negatively, and directly or indirectly, including those who are directly affected or are disadvantaged or
      vulnerable. These include the collaborative forest management (CFM) /collaborative resource management (CRM)
      /ecotourism groups, Batwa and Community and landowners in the project area and some are beneficiaries of the
      project.
      b.        Secondary stakeholders being the broader stakeholders who may be able to influence the outcome of the
      Project because of their knowledge about the affected communities or political influence over them or influence
      project decisions and operations. These include government institutions with mandate over environment
      management, social development, tourism, labour, agriculture, etc, non-government agencies and organizations,
      conservation trust funds, United Nations humanitarian agencies, private sector in wood value chains and tourism.
      c.        Tertiary stakeholders being the broader stakeholders who may have interest in project outcomes for the
      benefit of their interests. These include donors and development partners, research and development
      organizations.

    12. The E&S instruments are disclosed on the three agencies websites (Ministry of Water and Environment, NFA
    &UWA) and by the World Bank, communities are also informed about the instruments or aspects in it that is relevant
    to them during community meetings, deliberate efforts were made to disseminate the Vulnerable and Marginalized
    Groups Plans for the Batwa to them in different meetings.
