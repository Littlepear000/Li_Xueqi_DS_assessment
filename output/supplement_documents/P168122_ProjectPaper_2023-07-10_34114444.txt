I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. PROJECT STATUS

  1. The Statistical Innovation and Capacity Building in the Pacific Islands Project (Pacstat-SPC, P168122) is an
     Investment Project Financing (IPF) Operation with the Pacific Community (SPC). The project was approved by
     the Board on February 11, 2020 and became effective on June 9, 2020. Since effectiveness, the project has
     disbursed US$1.13 million (25 percent). The relatively low disbursement can be attributed to a slow start of the
     project activities due to delays in finalizing the Project Operations Manual (POM) and getting started with
     procurement activities.

  2. The project was assessed as having low environmental and social risks during preparation. The project is
     proceeding as planned and the E&S risk remains low, with all of the related documents (Environmental and Social
     Commitment Plan, Stakeholder Engagement Plan, Labor Management Procedures) and structures (Grievance
     Regress Mechanism) in place. No unanticipated risks have been identified throughout implementation.

  3. The project underwent a mid-term review (MTR) in May 2023. The review concluded that the project’s
     implementation progress and progress towards achievement of the development objective are rated Moderately
     Satisfactory (MS). Two PDO indicators and four intermediate indicators have already reached the project end
     target while other indicators are slightly delayed pending successful completion of project activities. No project
     audit reports are pending. A waiver was granted for the period from January 1 to December, 31, 2021 due to low
     project spending. The audited project financial statements for the period January 1 to December 31, 2022 are due
     on September 30, 2023.

B. RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

  4. Based on the findings of the MTR, a Level 2 Restructuring is proposed to improve implementation of the project.
     The proposed elements to be restructured are: i) revising the Financing Agreement to allow Recurrent Technical
     Staff Costs (RTSC) to be an eligible expenditure under Disbursement Category 1; ii) extending the project closing
     date by six months (from June 2025 to December 2025); and iii) revising two intermediate results indicators to
     better suit the project context.

  5. The proposed revision to the Financing Agreement to include RTSC as eligible expenditure under Disbursement
     Category 1 of the budget will allow SPC staff to charge their time towards the project for technical inputs.
     Disbursements remain low compared to progress of technical activities due to higher-than-expected contributions
     of SPC technical staff in lieu of project consultants. The current Financing Agreement limits charging of Recurrent
     Technical Staff Costs to Disbursement Categories 2 and 3. Substantive contributions of SPC technical staff to
     components covered by Disbursement Category 1 were not able to be charged to the project. The proposed
     revision will allow current SPC staff to continue to contribute to project activities and charge their time
     accordingly. SPC-SDD (Statistics for Development Division) is also planning to hire additional SPC staff to contribute
     to core functions, as well as project activities. This will also free up SPC-SDD’s core budget to hire additional staff
     to backfill the business-as-usual duties of those contributing to the Pacstat project.

  6. This revision will increase SPC’s ownership of the project and its outputs, compared to the original consultant-
     heavy model that was envisioned at preparation. The revision will not have negative implications for budget or

      human resources for the project or SPC. The utilization of SPC staff to deliver outputs will improve the
      sustainability of project impacts on the institutional and technical capacity of SPC. It also maintains the distinction
      between Categories 1, 2, and 3 in order to keep the separation between the allocations for technical delivery
      executed by SPC (Category 1), technical delivery executed by sub-grants (Category 3), and project management
      (Category 2). This will effectively ensure that total spending on project management costs stays below 10 percent
      of the total project budget.

  7. The revision to the project closing date is proposed to account for delays related to COVID-19 and a slow start
     of the project initially. COVID-19 delayed the start of project activities, impacting the preparation of the POM as
     well as the implementation of procurement activities. An extension of the project closing date is helpful to enable
     SPC to meet all project targets within the project period. Due to strong progress in project activities to date, it is
     expected that a six-month extension to December 2025 is sufficient to enable completion of all project activities
     including the fourth innovative experiment. It will also ensure that the results framework is on track to be fully
     achieved before the new closing date.

  8. The adjustment of two intermediate indicators in the results framework will better reflect the project context.
     Two intermediate results indicators linked to the innovative experiments do not match the realities of project
     implementation: (a) number of beneficiary NSOs satisfied that their feedback has been reflected in the design of
     experiments; and (b) number of data collection innovation proposals submitted through the annual workplan.
     Both indicators were designed during project preparation assuming that a competitive selection process would
     be used to select the four experiments from a large pool of at least 10 proposals. However, due to the nature of
     the engagement with Pacific member states being much more collaborative and consultative, it was not deemed
     appropriate to solicit proposals that would then be rejected. Instead, preliminary discussions between Pacific
     member states and SPC already filter unfeasible proposals. Hence, the indicators are suggested to be revised by
     only counting the number of proposals that are approved by PSMB, each of which will have gone through the
     consultation process. All other indicators remain relevant and will only have their end target dates extended to
     match the proposed new project closing date in December 2025.

II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

A. SUBSTANTIVE CHANGES PROPOSED

  9. Financing Agreement amendment: Recurrent Technical Staff Costs will be added as an eligible expenditure under
     Disbursement Category 1. All definitions of Recurrent Technical Staff Costs and the methodologies for acquittal
     detailed in the Project Operations Manual will remain the same.

  10. Closing date extension: An extension of six months is proposed to the closing date of the project, from 30 June
      2025 to 31 December 2025.

  11. Results framework amendment: two intermediate results indicators will be revised to better capture the
      innovative experiment process:
          a. The results indicator “number of beneficiary country NSOs satisfied that their feedback has been reflected
              in the design of experiments” will have its end target lowered from 8 to 4, because only four experiments
              will be conducted, and thus only four sets of country consultations.
        The World Bank

           b. The results indicator “number of data collection innovation proposals submitted through the annual
                workplan” will be changed to “number of data collection innovation experiments approved by PSMB” and
                will have its end target lowered from 10 to 4, because only four experiments will be conducted, and thus
                only four pre-selected proposals will be brought forward for PSMB approval after an extensive period of
                consultation.
       The end target for other indicators will be extended to reflect the closing date extension, but no other revisions
       are required. All detailed changes, including adjustments to the intermediate targets for the revised indicators,
       are included in the Results Framework section below.

B. OTHER CHANGES

  12. Disbursement projection update: The disbursement projections will be updated to account for 1) the actual
      disbursements to date, and 2) the extension of the project closing date. The new disbursement projections are in
      line with the Recipient’s implementation schedule for the remainder of the project lifetime.
