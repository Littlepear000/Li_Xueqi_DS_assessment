4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  The progress towards achieving PDO is rated Satisfactory, and implementation progress is rated Satisfactory. As of August
  2024, cumulative disbursement stands at US$18.38 million, representing approximately 75 percent of the total
  commitment of US$25 million. The parent project is on track to complete most of the planned activities. Activities
  completed include the annual budget outlook document, data sets, reports, updated indicators from SIHBS, the
  Compendium of Statistical Concepts, Definitions, and Standards, and the National M&E Framework. Several activities are
  ongoing, but both the client and the task team agreed that these could be completed before August 2025, the original
  project closing date. The project has achieved strong outcomes, the Statistical Performance Indicators (SPI, which is a
  framework of 5 pillars and 22 dimensions to assess the maturity of national statistical systems) of the World Bank have
  indicated an upward trend in outcomes, with the SPI rising from 19.6 in 2019 to 48.4 in 2022. In terms of gender results,
  the number of United Nations Statistics Division (UNSD) core gender indicators that Somalia is reporting has almost
  doubled, from 18 indicators in 2022 to about 34 indicators after the release of the report.
  Implementation of the Somali Integrated Business Establishment Census. The business census exercise was completed
  mid-August, and successfully interviewed 175,217 establishments in 16 major cities across Somalia (including Somaliland
  and Puntland). This activity was implemented by the Somalia National Bureau of Statistics (SNBS) with the support of a
  consultancy firm that managed logistics operations, including handling payment for the field team, transport, and
  security. This is the first-ever such exercise. For the first time, Somalia has a comprehensive picture of its private sector.
  The census data will provide a landscape of economic activities across different areas of the country and an accurate
  sampling frame for future surveys, such as the SNBS’s integrated business enterprise survey and the World Bank B-Ready
  enterprise survey.
  The Business Establishment Census (BEC) aims to establish the Statistical Business Register (SBR), a comprehensive
  database containing detailed information on business entities across Somalia. This database serves as the foundation for
  subsequent phases of business statistics projects, including economic surveys that utilize SBR data to gather insights into
  enterprise costs, revenues, and day-to-day operations. The data collected through the SIBEC will contribute significantly
  to informed decision-making and policy formulation, helping Somalia's government and other stakeholders better
  understand the country's business landscape and economic dynamics. The SIBEC, as phase 1 of the broader business
  statistics project, has made considerable progress, particularly in its preparatory work. A Training of Trainers (ToT)
  session was conducted to equip participants with the necessary skills to train enumerators, covering essential topics such
  as data management, business classification, questionnaire design, and GIS concepts. The SIBEC data collection, covered
  16 major cities across Somalia -began in April 2024 and concluded in August 2024. Approximately 175,217 establishments
  were surveyed, providing a rich dataset for further analysis. Analysis of the SIBEC report is ongoing, and the final report,
  anticipated to be published November 2024, will provide key economic indicators essential for a comprehensive
  understanding of Somalia’s business environment, thus facilitating more effective decision-making and policy
  development.
  As part of phase 2, the Somalia Integrated Business Establishment Survey (SIBES) has also made significant headway, with
  the survey questionnaire drafted and the tabulation plan developed and fieldwork will begin in December 2024.
