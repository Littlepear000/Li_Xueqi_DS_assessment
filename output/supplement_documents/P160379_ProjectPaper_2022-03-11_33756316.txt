 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

1. Background: Innovation in Solar Power and Hybrid Technologies Project (ISHTP) was approved by the World Bank’s
(the Bank) Board on March 29, 2019 and is financed with an IBRD loan (US$150 million), a Clean Technology Fund (CTF)
loan (US$28 million), and a CTF grant (US$22 million) to Solar Energy Corporation of India Limited (SECI), which is the
Borrower/Recipient and the Project Implementing Entity. This is one of the largest CTF grants. The loans are guaranteed
by the Government of India (GoI). SECI is a central sector public utility that is playing a strategic role in implementing the
country’s energy transition agenda.

2. Project Development Objective (PDO): The PDOs are to demonstrate the operational and economic feasibility of utility-
scale innovative renewable energy (RE) technologies and battery energy storage solutions (BESS), and to strengthen
institutional capacity to facilitate scale-up of such technologies on a commercial basis in India.

3. Project Components: The ISHTP is supporting SECI to finance innovative RE and BESS subprojects to demonstrate
applications that can be deployed at a large scale in India’s power system. Furthermore, the project will build SECI’s market
facilitation capacity in a rapidly evolving RE market by monitoring deployment and performance of first subprojects and
disseminating its experience in design, construction, operation, and contractual arrangements to renewable market
stakeholders in India. The project has two components:
              (a) Component A: Investments in Innovative RE Technologies & BESS. The estimated cost of this component
                  is US$398 million, which includes US$150 million from the IBRD loan, US$28 million from the CTF loan,
                  US$20 million from the CTF grant, and US$200 million from SECI. The eligible subproject typologies are:
                        i. Utility-Scale Hybrid Subprojects that combined wind, solar PV and/or BESS technologies (about
                           300 MW targeted)
                       ii. BESS Subprojects integrated with other existing or new RE generation technologies or providing
                           grid services enabling improved use of power (about 40 MWh targeted)
                      iii. Utility-Scale floating solar PV (FSPV) Subprojects - solar PV systems installed on synthetic floating
                           beds anchored to the bottom or on the shore of selected water bodies (about 100 MW targeted)
              (b) Component B: Technical Assistance, Capacity Building, Implementation Support, Monitoring and
                  Dissemination (US$1.81 million equivalent). This component is financed by the CTF grant and will support
                  technical assistance, studies, workshops, training, study tours, and other capacity building and
                  dissemination activities

4. Strategic Importance of ISHTP in India’s Energy Transitions Journey: The project promotes clean energy solutions
contributing to India’s climate change response and is highly relevant for the Energy Transitions agenda of the GoI. The
project will also play the role of a catalyst for developing and demonstrating proof of concept for innovative RE
technologies and in opening up markets for commercial investments by leveraging funding from CTF including a grant
component. ISHTP will finance innovative sub-projects in India’s clean energy market and will directly displace coal
powered generation during peak demand hours.

5. Current Status: The contract for the first sub-project in Chhattisgarh has been placed now leading to a commitment
of about US$63 million of the loan/grant funds (financing 50% of the sub-project cost) which is about 33% of the loan/grant

funds1. Procurement process for the second sub-project in Jharkhand has also been launched. The Loan/Guarantee/Grant
Agreements signing date currently stands on March 31, 2022.

6. Key performance Indicators (KPIs): Given the delays in loan signing and effectiveness, the targets KPIs have also been
revised and the changes are provided in Annex A. Overall, SECI has been able to build on the work done under the Project.
SECI recently launched a 1 GWh battery storage solutions (through auctions) based on the learning experience from
Chhattisgarh subproject. So, the intended objectives of the project are being achieved.

7. Project Ratings: The progress towards the achievement of PDO rating is Moderately Unsatisfactory as the loan is not
yet signed while implementation progress (IP) rating is Moderately Satisfactory given the progress made on the two
subprojects even though impacted by pandemic.

8. Rationale for Restructuring: Due to change in the first subproject (solar-wind hybrid with BESS subproject at Ramagiri,
Andhra Pradesh) after the new state government in the offtaking state declined to buy power from this subproject, SECI
had to work on other subprojects. This has led to delays in the achievement of the target KPIs as well as require an
amendment to a legal covenant reflecting the change in the name of the subproject from Ramagiri subproject to
Chhattisgarh subproject. Hence, the proposed restructuring is required to be done before loan signing to allow an effective
monitoring of the project during the implementation supervision phase.


    II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

9.     Changes in the negotiated legal agreements: The negotiated legal agreements are being revised to replace
Ramagiri subproject with Chhattisgarh subproject wherever applicable.

10.      Change in Results Framework/Key Performance Indicators (KPI):
The changes in the results framework/KPIs are attached as Annex A. Given the delays in the Ramagiri subproject and hence
in loan signing and effectiveness, the results framework/KPI matrix has been adjusted to reflect the current status as well
as the targets. Further, given the delays in the effectiveness of the Project, the reporting period for all the KPIs for year 4
(only) is being revised from March 31, 2023 to December 31, 2023. This will align the milestones targeted under the
subprojects during the remainder of the project period. If March 2023 is retained as the end of the reporting period for
year 4, then there will be no results to be showcased in this year leading to a bunching of all the achievements in the last
year (end-target). There is no change in the reporting period for other years.
