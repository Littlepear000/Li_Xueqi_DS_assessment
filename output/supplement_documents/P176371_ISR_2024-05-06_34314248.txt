 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  As anticipated in the previous ISR of November 2023, the Program made strong progress since the last ISM. The FY disbursement rate increased
  from 0% in November 2023 to 13% during the most recent Implementation Support Mission (ISM), overall disbursement more than doubled from
  US$29 million to US$ 62 million, and recruitment of the two International Verification Agencies (IVAs) was completed. The first DLI verification
  window took place in November 2023 and confirmed achievement of three DLRs. Despite this progress, there remain substantive delays in the
  implementation of project activities among certain Implementing Agencies. Progress by Borrower is as follows:
           The Kenya component continues to face a funding shortfall, after having the budget reduced from KSh 3.3 billion to KSh 1.9 billion amidst
            a general fiscal consolidation. This reduction – equivalent to a 42 percent decline in financing – has affected the activities KNBS is able to
            undertake during this financial year. To address this gap and continue the implementation of priority activities, the National Treasury (NT)
            requested and received an advance which helped in assuring business continuity. Three DLRs under the Kenya component were
            confirmed as achieved during the first DLI verification window in November 2023, for a total amount of EUR4.3 million. Another seven
            DLRs have been completed and are scheduled for verification during the next verification window.
           The Tanzania component has made progress in several results areas but overall Program implementation remains delayed. The delay
            springs from the initial delay linked to the implementation of the Population and Housing Census of late 2022 which absorbed all attention
            and human resources of the NSO. Progress on several DLRs was however made, and the Tanzania component is expected to have 18
            DLRs ready for verification for the July 2024 verification window. Activities in the Program Action Plan are being implemented as
            planned.
           The Rwanda component made good progress since the last ISM. The NSO submitted three DLRs for verification and these are expected
            to be disbursed before the end of the FY. Progress was also made on the implementation of the Program Action Plan (PAP), with the
            number of delayed actions decreasing from 11 during the last ISM to 6 now. Even though the PAP delays are not currently affecting
            implementation of the program, the cybersecurity audit is now 18 months overdue and should be prioritized going forward. Good progress
            was made on the GRM, which is expected to be operational by mid May 2024.
           The EAC component has made progress on various milestones, supporting the regional harmonization of key statistical indicators and
            developing guidelines. Since the last ISM, EAC managed to complete the recruitment of the two International Verification Agencies and
            coordinated the first verification window, which took place in November 2023. Procurement of PIU consultants remains however
            excessively slow. While the ToR for key PIU consultants were cleared by the Bank in December 2022, it is only now, by end March 2024,
            that contracts are being awarded. The slow procurement process constrains project implementation. The EAC component has also been
            without Procurement Specialist since April 2023, when the former Procurement Specialist left. Procurement of a new specialist is in
            progress, while the procurement of a Procurement Officer is almost completed.
           The AUC component remains severely delayed. The AUC component became effective on November 4, 2022, but progress since has
            been slow. The first version of the Procurement Plan was only submitted at the end of June 2023, and none of the legal covenants (which
            relate to staffing the PIU) have been met so far (though they were due within 6 months of effectiveness). Despite the delay, some progress
            has been made since the last ISM: (i) Quarterly progress reports have been submitted, which previously was not the case, (ii) a new
            Program Manager for the project has been appointed, and (ii) PIU positions were advertised and shortlisted candidates have been invited
