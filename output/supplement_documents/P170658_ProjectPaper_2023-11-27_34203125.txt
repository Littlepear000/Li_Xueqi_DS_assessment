I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE

Summary

   1. This is the first restructuring of the Peru Centralized Emergency Response Project, requested by the Government
      of Peru (GoP) on September 26, 2023. The proposed restructuring seeks to extend the Project closing date by 12
      months from January 10, 2024 to January 10, 2025. The 12-month first-phase extension contemplated by this
      restructuring would allow the Project to demonstrate progress towards achieving the Project Development
      Objective (PDO). If the World Bank (WB) is satisfied with the pace of progress, a second-phase extension could be
      considered pursuant to a subsequent restructuring. There are no changes to the PDO, project risk ratings, or
      Project Implementation Unit (PIU).

   2. Continuous changes at the line ministry, Implementing Agency and within the PIU resulted in a lack of
      coordination among agencies and caused delays in the procurement processes. This was compounded by the
      COVID-19 pandemic and changes in the GoP. In June 2022, an unsuccessful bidding process for the construction
      of the 911 Center – the largest contract under the project – substantially affected implementation and
      disbursement pace. As a result, a year without disbursements elapsed and the Project Procurement Strategy for
      Development had to be adjusted. In August 2022, key project ratings were downgraded to Moderately
      Unsatisfactory, and the operation has been in problem status for more than 12 months.

   3. The Project, in the amount of US$36 million, was approved by the WB Board of Executive Directors on April 7,
      2020, and declared effective on August 17, 2020. The PDO is to provide a more efficient response to emergencies,
      urgencies, and associated helpline requests from the population of Lima Metropolitan area and Callao. The Project
      includes the following components: Component 1, Physical infrastructure; Component 2, Integrated platform;
      Component 3, 911 Central response protocols; Component 4, Interconnection of the video cameras of National
      Police to the 911 Platform; Component 5, Use and appropriation of 911 platform; and, Component 6, Project
      supervision and management, and technical studies.

Project Status

   4. The Project is implemented by the National Telecommunications Program (PRONATEL), under the Ministry of
      Transport and Communications (MTC). The Project is currently rated Moderately Unsatisfactory for overall
      progress towards achievement of the PDO and implementation performance (IP), with financial management and
      safeguards rated Satisfactory in the last Implementation Status and Results Report archived on June 26, 2023. As
      of November 23, 2023, the Project has disbursed US$0.65 million (2 percent) and has around 50 percent
      committed in ongoing procurement processes.
   5. Shortcomings in achieving the PDO are due in great part to implementation challenges faced over the past two
      years, which are reflected in the Project's limited disbursement performance. To compensate for these
      implementation delays and restore the project’s potential to meet its objectives, a time-bound Action Plan was
      agreed between the GoP and the WB following the November 2022 mission, including four main critical actions:

   6. During the June 2023 Mid-Term Review, the GoP and the WB reviewed the status of all four items of the Action
      Plan to confirm their achievement. Below is a summary of the implementation progress under each component
      and for the four actions agreed in the Action Plan (see Component 1, 2, 3, and 6):

       a. Component 1: Physical Infrastructure. The contract for the design of the 911 Center was awarded and started
          implementation in July 2023 (Table 1, Action 3). This step is critical to prepare technical specifications for the
          launch of the 911 Center construction. In parallel, PRONATEL plans to launch the bidding processes for (i) the
          supervision of the construction; and (ii) the works for the 911 Center, with a view to contract signature and
          start of the works by August 2024.
       b. Component 2: Integrated 911 platform. The bidding process for the central element of the unified 911
          technology platform (SAGE - Emergency Response and Attention System) was initiated in June 2023
          (estimated contract award in March 2024) (Table 1, Action 4). This is the specialized Computer-aided dispatch
          (CAD) solution that will allow assisted, fluid and coordinated management of the complete emergency cycle,
          from the entry of calls at the 911 Center to their resolution by the various First Response Units (FRU) and Line
          100 (i.e., Gender-based violence hotline). The SAGE will be able to replace, improve or integrate with the
          existing systems of the FRU and Line 100, as appropriate.
       c. Component 3: 911 Central Response Protocols. This activity of defining response protocols for the 911 FRU
          and Line 100 has been under way since January 2023 (Table 1, Action 1) with the contracting of 5 individual
          consultants, 4 local and one international, coordinating the activity. This component was completed in
          October 2023.
       d. Component 4: Interconnection of the video cameras of National Police to the 911 Platform. This component
          will not be fully initiated until the physical infrastructure of the 911 Center is under way. However, the video
          management requirement has been considered for the development of the SAGE, and the interconnection
          with the National Police video system will be tested during the integration phase of the SAGE.
       e. Component 5: Use and appropriation of 911 platform. This component will begin once SAGE is under
          implementation and prior to the completion of the 911 Center. It relates to the training of the FRU and Line
          100 for the appropriation and use of SAGE.
       f. Component 6: Project supervision and management. After numerous changes in the PIU team, with the
          problems and delays that this has caused, a team of professionals has recently been consolidated and
          strengthened and is expected to continue until the end of the Project (Table 1, Action 2). Similarly,
          coordination and supervision mechanisms have been consolidated within PRONATEL and also with the FRU
          and Line 100.

7. Environmental and Social (E&S) Standards. As of November 2023, the Project is compliant with the WB E&S
   standards. The commitments included in the Environmental and Social Commitment Plan are being adequately and
   timely met. The PIU will update and re-disclose the project's E&S Assessment, Environmental and Social Management
   Plan, Stakeholder Engagement Plan and Labor Management Plan once the final technical design of the 911 Center is
   finalized and before its construction. There are no changes to project risk ratings, or E&S performance ratings. The

Rationale for Restructuring

8. Due to the factors discussed in para 2, components 1 and 2 would not be completed by the current closing date.

9. The Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) sent the Bank an official letter on September 26, 2023 requesting the
   proposed first phase 12-month extension of the loan closing date from January 10, 2024 to January 10, 2025. This
   initial extension would allow the Borrower to advance all outstanding activities and contracts, which are also most
   critical to achieving the PDO. The extension would also ensure that the support required for the GoP to implement
   the Centralized Emergency Response System (911) can be maintained, and that Component 5 can be effectively
   implemented. Further, it will enable continued enhancement of project management and implementation capacity of
   the PIU.

10. The requested closing date extension will be complemented by a course of tailored actions included in a detailed
    Action Plan. This Action Plan was prepared and agreed with PRONATEL, to ensure timely completion of the remaining
    Project activities by the extended closing date. The following performance milestones (Table 2) will be evaluated by
    the WB in December 2024 to determine whether an additional extension merits consideration.

       Table 2: Performance Milestones

           Milestones                                                                              Due Date
        1. Sign contract for SAGE implementation (Component 2)                                     March 31, 2024 –

        2. Sign contract for works supervision of 911 Central building                             April 30, 2024
        3.   Award contract for the construction of 911 Center (building).                         June 30, 2024
        4.   Initiate works for the construction of the 911 Center (Component 1, est. 12 months)   August 31, 2024
        5.   Launch Use and appropriation of 911 platform (Component 5)                            November 30, 2024

11. Launching SAGE operations is crucial for progress towards achieving the PDO. Achieving this milestone would reduce
    the risk of further delays. If milestone 4 is achieved, it means that by December 2024, works of the 911 Center will
    have advanced by more than 50 percent. As such, completion could reasonably be expected within 6-9 months and a
    second-phase extension until December 2025 could be considered to allow this output to be completed.

12. Client commitment and capacity. The WB and the client have agreed on the below additional measures to achieve
    the estimated completion dates of the remaining activities:

       a) Government commitment and relevance of PDO. The client, including MTC and MEF, have expressed strong
          interest in completing the Project. The new authorities at MTC and PRONATEL have demonstrated a high level
          of commitment towards implementing the project and have already made notable improvements in project
          management, with the full PIU dedicated team in place. The Project has been highlighted as a priority for the
          The World Bank
          Centralized Emergency Response SystemProject (170658)


            GoP and was explicitly mentioned during the President’s speech to the Congress in July 2023.1 In that regard,
            the PDO remains highly relevant under the new Country Partnership Framework (CPF) for Peru (FY23-27),
            Objective 3 “Enhance Delivery of Public Services”. The Project is also unique for the country as it is building a
            comprehensive, multi-agency 911 response center with different FRU but also unique from a Gender-Based
            Violence (GBV) prevention perspective as it aims to integrate 911 Platform benefits for Line 100 (GBV hotline),
            including accelerating responses in GBV emergencies, reduction in malicious calls, and geolocation feature for
            more efficient response.
         b) Strengthened Implementation Capacity: The new PRONATEL administration is fully dedicated to completing
            the Project and has strengthened the PIU capacity, hiring all personnel as per the Project Operational Manual,
            and is actively recruiting a larger technical team to address various technical bottlenecks.
         c) Intensified WB Implementation Support. The WB is carrying out weekly implementation support reviews to
            proactively address any bottlenecks with the PIU and to provide technical support to accelerate the pace of
            implementation.
         d) Continue with Hands-on Expanded Implementation Support (HEIS). Since mid-2021, the WB has been
            providing HEIS to help advance key procurement processes and accelerate project implementation. This has
            had a significant positive impact, particularly in the preparation phases for the procurement process related
            to the two largest activities noted above and has helped to build critical capacity within the PIU. The HEIS
            advisor’s support has been instrumental in guiding the authorities on how to conduct bidding evaluations,
            write evaluation reports, prepare terms of references, and manage contracts.

13. Legal Covenants. While ten legal covenants have been met, three are pending and under way. One of them is related
    to the approval of the necessary adjustments to the legal framework regarding geolocation requirements that shall
    include the obligation of mobile operators to share real time data and transfer the geolocation data to the 911
    Emergency Response System.

14. Audit Report. (i) There is no outstanding financial audit report; (ii) the auditors issued an unqualified (clean) opinion
    on the financial statements of the project with a paragraph emphasizing the low level of financial and physical
    execution of the project as of December 31, 2022; and, (iii) all audit reports were considered satisfactory to the Bank.

    II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

15. Loan Closing Date. The loan closing date will be extended by 12 months from January 10, 2024 to January 10, 2025. If
    the Project can meet the set milestones (see table 2), an additional extension of 11 months can be considered to allow
    remaining activities to be completed.
