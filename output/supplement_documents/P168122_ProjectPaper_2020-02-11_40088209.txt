I.      BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING

     1. With small populations spread across vast areas, the Pacific Island countries (PICs) face unique challenges in
        maintaining essential statistical systems. The World Bank has 12 PICs members: Papua New Guinea (PNG), Fiji,
        Kiribati, the Marshall Islands, the Federated States of Micronesia, Nauru, Palau, Samoa, the Solomon Islands,
        Tonga, Tuvalu, and Vanuatu. Most national statistical offices (NSOs) in the region have fewer than ten staff
        responsible for producing a wide range of economic, social, and demographic statistics, requiring individual
        professionals to simultaneously manage a wide range of duties across technical and administrative domains.
        These small teams typically lack specialized expertise, leading to quality concerns. Operational limitations also
        constrain the ability of agencies to conduct consistent and regular data collection, with many efforts effectively
        “one-off” episodic tasks that generate incomparable and volatile statistical indicators. The geographic dispersion
        of many PICs further exacerbates these difficulties, requiring extensive logistical coordination to reach remote
        communities.

     2. Given the shared challenges, a common regional approach is needed to strengthen statistics. While
        implementing harmonized methods and exploiting economies of scale have benefits in many parts of the world,
        the approach is particularly crucial in the Pacific context. Countries are small and NSO capacity is thin, limiting the
        opportunities for domestic initiatives. PICs also have many similarities, while being distinct compared to other
        contexts globally. This situation limits the applicability of importing international best practice without careful
        consideration and customization. However, it can provide immense opportunities for rapid adoption and scale-up
        within the Pacific region after local best practices are identified and tested. Considering these conditions, the most
        impactful approach to promoting regional and national data-driven policy is to empower a credible and
        established regional partner to coordinate the agenda.

     3. Before the Pacstat-SPC project (P168122), the region’s NSOs lacked an established coordinating body for quality
        standards and guidelines. The absence of a mechanism to align approaches and a reservoir of specialized
        expertise led to a proliferation of inconsistent statistical measures, undermining comparability between countries
        and even within countries over time. Technical assistance for data collection was haphazard, with key tasks
        outsourced to consultants far removed from the region. Responding to these challenges, the Pacstat-SPC project
        successfully piloted a regional implementation model combining country-level execution of statistical activities
        with regional standards, oversight, and technical assistance. The pooling of expertise and shared services has
        benefited NSOs across the region and at varying levels of capacity, ensuring adherence to a common system
        collectively endorsed through the Pacific Statistics Methods Board (PSMB), a consultative body also supported by
        the project. This approach improved consistency of methods, generated economies of scale, and improved
        compliance with fiduciary, environmental, and social standards.

     4. The project has significantly improved the quality, availability, and standardization of statistics across the
        region. To standardize methods and quality, the project commissioned and published guidance notes for key
        statistical issues (e.g. the Pacific HIES toolkit, collecting food data) for use in PICs. Each was reviewed and approved
        and are now applied in statistical activities across the region. The concentration of specialized technical capacity
        within SPC reduced the lag between data collection and publication, with most countries now producing reports
        within 12 months of the end of fieldwork. The availability of consistent data sets allows the Pacific region to better
        track its overall levels and patterns of poverty, inequality, and employment. These benefits are tracked in the
        results framework, including publication of guidance notes (PDOI 1.1), regionally comparable indicators (PDOI

    HIES reports produced within 12 months of data collection (IRI 2.2).

5. Pacstat-SPC is part of an interconnected Series of Projects (SOP) that is on track to achieve, and in several cases
   exceed, its initial objectives. The SOP includes two country-level operations in Tonga (P171377, “Statistical
   Innovation and Capacity Building in Tonga”) and Kiribati (P171380, “Statistical Innovation and Capacity Building in
   Kiribati”). Pacstat-SPC has directly supported the successful implementation of these companion projects.
   Regionally standardized survey methods and strengthened technical assistance directly contributed to successful
   Household Income and Expenditure Surveys (HIES) in Tonga and Kiribati, resulting in lower per-interview costs,
   improved data quality, and faster publication timelines. Both countries are on track to meet the household survey
   coverage target established in IDA21. The standardization of methods has also supported the Solomon Islands
   “Building Capacity in Statistics and Audit Project” (CAPSA, P180723), which completed a HIES without direct SPC
   technical assistance, as well as the upcoming HIES supported by the PFM II project in Marshall Islands (P510280).
   As of November 2025, funds in the Pacstat-SPC project are nearly fully disbursed, and all PDO and intermediate
   results indicators have been achieved or exceeded. Project ratings reflect strong implementation performance
   and effective project administration. Early challenges with procurement and contract management processes
   have been resolved following SPC leadership’s amendment of internal policies to allow project procurement to be
   undertaken solely through the Bank’s systems. These arrangements are expected to continue through the
   additional year of implementation and to sustain satisfactory procurement ratings going forward.

6. The project is a proving-ground for regionally relevant innovative experiments designed to lower costs, shorten
   production cycles, and raise the quality of Pacific statistical operations. These include: (i) Tuvalu’s Long-Form
   Census (LFC), which tested digitally enabled long-form enumeration and streamlined workflows to produce core
   statistics from a single field operation; (ii) Samoa’s Food-Away-From-Home (FAFH) experiment, which developed
   and field-tested a focused instrument for welfare statistics and price measurement; (iii) a Vanuatu “data science
   for climate change” experiment, integrating emerging data sources such as satellite imagery and analytical
   methods to enhance the statistical system’s responsiveness to climate-linked shocks; and (iv) a pilot for a
   Statistical Population Register in Tuvalu to improve sampling frames and administrative data integration. These
   trials were identified and refined through structured consultations with National Statistics Offices (NSOs) and
   endorsed through the Pacific Statistics Methods Board (PSMB), ensuring technical rigor and relevance to scaling
   across the region. indicators confirm wide consultation, incorporation of country feedback, and progressive
   approval and reporting of the experiments. Collectively, the experiments demonstrate practical pathways to
   reduce per-survey costs (lighter modular instruments), improve coverage and accuracy in hard-to-reach
   geographies (digital capture and remote support), accelerate analysis (standardized syntax and templates), and
   strengthen interoperability with administrative and geospatial data. Their results are already translating into
   faster production of HIES and poverty outputs and more harmonized indicators and microdata across the region,
   laying the groundwork for a more efficient, comparable, and resilient regional statistical system managed through
   SPC’s coordinated support to NSOs.

7. Improved methods have also been applied in countries without statistics IPFs—including Tuvalu, Samoa, Palau,
   and Nauru. The regional endorsement of new methodologies, the production of detailed technical guidance
   notes, and SPC’s improved capacity to provide technical assistance to countries has led to the application of the
   new methods in other PICs. Through the project, SPC has supported the publication of the poverty report in Nauru,

         Palau, Samoa and Tuvalu based on HIES datasets that were collected with improved methods and using a poverty
         measurement methodology that conforms to international best practices. The availability of new and consistent
         statistics on poverty, demographics, labor, and access to services directly benefits the countries in evidence-based
         policymaking and improves the quality of the Bank’s policy engagement and operations portfolio.

    8. The operation and additional financing align strongly with GROW for regional organizations by empowering SPC
       to deliver regional public goods in statistics. The most notable project components contributing to GROW
       objectives include governance and coordination via the Pacific Statistics Methods Board (PSMB), region‑wide
       harmonization and standards for household welfare surveys, pooled technical assistance to NSOs, and a proving-
       ground for innovations that lower costs and improve data quality across countries. These contributions translate
       into comparable, accessible welfare datasets and harmonized microdata that support evidence‑based
       policymaking at both national and regional levels. The project’s support to standardized HIES implementation
       (e.g., in Tonga, Tuvalu, and Kiribati) directly advances the IDA21 objective on welfare survey data coverage, and
       the additional financing sustains this momentum while laying the groundwork for a subsequent Pacstat‑II to
       broaden reach and deepen adoption.

    9. This additional financing (AF) will extend project activities for one year, to sustain high priority activities and
       lay the groundwork for a planned Pacstat-II regional project under preparation for FY27. This AF proposes a
       scale-up of activities to enhance the project’s development impact. The proposal has two objectives: i) extend
       core activities from the ongoing Pacstat-I project for one year while maintaining the development objective, and
       ii) conduct preparatory activities to inform the design of a second phase of Pacstat. Pacstat-II will expand the
       geographic coverage of the SOP by engaging additional IDA countries (early indications of interest have been
       received from Kiribati, Tonga, Tuvalu, Solomon Islands and Vanuatu), while focusing on the next generation of
       efficiency improvements that can be implemented across the region to increase quality, timeliness, and coverage.
       The additional financing will sustain SPC’s increased capacity achieved during the project, which allows SPC to
       continue providing a standard package of technical assistance to WB-member countries in the Pacific1. Additional
       financing will also be used for pilot activities shaping the design of the upcoming project’s planned components.
       The additional financing includes an extension of the project closing date until February 27, 2027.

11. Under Component 1, the additional financing will maintain the activities of the Pacific Statistics Methods Board
    (PSMB) and commission suitability assessments of national administrative data systems for integration to be
    supported in the upcoming project. The project’s support to PSMB has been a success. PSMB meetings occurring
    twice a year as well as the research that feeds into them are vital regional public goods. The meetings are well-
    attended by National Statistics Offices (NSOs) across the region, and the meetings have led to the regional
    endorsement of desk research, experiments, and guidance notes for implementing and analyzing surveys. There
    is strong demand from NSOs and development partners, such as the Australian Bureau of Statistics and Statistics
    New Zealand, to maintain PSMB meetings to coordinate and standardize statistical activities across the region.
    The project includes an additional US$90,000 to maintain the PSMB secretariat function, which includes organizing
    the meetings, setting agendas, and reporting on meeting minutes and recommendations for circulation to NSOs.

12. The additional financing will also support the production of commissioned assessments for PSMB endorsement,
    as well as workshops on those topics. The new commissioned works will cover two topics prioritized for the
    Pacstat-II upcoming regional project: 1) Prospects for integrating administrative data systems into statistical
    systems across the Pacific, and 2) Implementing continuous survey data collection modes in the Pacific. These
    works will be submitted for review at PSMB meetings. Conducting this research under the project and through
    the PSMB mechanism will build regional consensus on the methods that will be implemented in Pacstat-II. As part
    of the work on administrative data, the project will also specifically support work to improve the quality of civil
    registration and vital statistics (CRVS) information. Some promising initiatives in the Pacific include Making Death
    Count, which improves the quality of cause of death information collected by health professionals, as well as the
    OpenCRVS platform currently used by some Pacific countries. The project will finance SPC’s regional supporting
    role in the standardized rollout of these initiatives. The project includes US$150,000 on administrative data
    integration, US$127,000 on CRVS activities, and US$150,000 on survey design models. Finally, regional workshops
    are important to build the knowledge and skills required at the country level to implement the PSMB-
    recommended methods for data collection and analysis. This component will support regional training workshops

    linked to the commissioned work topics (administrative data systems, CRVS, and using continuously collected
    survey data), with an allocation of US$193,000. Results indicators linked to PSMB meetings and commissioned
    works will be updated in accordance with these proposed activities.

13. Under Component 2, additional financing will support ongoing technical assistance to countries and project
    management. The project has increased SPC’s technical assistance capacity on the implementation and analysis
    of household surveys. This additional capacity was the key driver for SPC’s ability to achieve all the results
    indicators related to research output, technical assistance to countries, and overall improvements to the quality
    and availability of statistics in the Pacific. The additional financing will sustain this level of support for an additional
    year, while further expansions to capacity are under preparation for Pacstat-II. Finally, this component covers
    project management costs, which includes a project manager and the required support from SPC’s technical and
    corporate teams as agreed in the Financing Agreement and in accordance with the details specified in the Project
    Operations Manual and based on Annual Work Plans and Budgets approved by the Association. The project will
    allocate US$410,000 to finance the technical staff that provide technical assistance to countries, and US$230,000
    on project management and corporate support. The results indicators for regionally harmonized and accessible
    data and reports will be updated in accordance with the new activities proposed.

14. Additional financing under Component 3 includes a regional pilot on purchasing power parities. The additional
    financing will support improving price data collection to enable more countries in the region to participate in the
    International Comparison Program (ICP), used for the calculation of Purchasing Power Parities (PPP). Currently,
    the only PIC that participates in the ICP is Fiji, with other countries having their PPP calculated based on the prices
    of goods in Fiji. Expanding ICP participation will enable more accurate PPP values to be estimated across the
    Pacific. The new price collections will be implemented by SPC in collaboration with participating countries. This
    will also include a regional workshop to train participating countries in the data collection methods. The project
    will allocate US$150,000 for these activities. A report summarizing the methods and results will be published by
    the close of the project.

   IV. APPRAISAL SUMMARY

A. Economic and Financial (if applicable) Analysis

  16. While the project’s nature does not readily permit a traditional cost-benefit analysis, there is a strong economic
      rationale for investing in statistics. Official statistics are classic public goods. They underpin informed
      decision-making yet typically yield diffuse benefits that markets alone will not supply, warranting public financing.
      Effective planning, policymaking, and M&E require a broad suite of social and economic indicators that the public
      sector is uniquely positioned and incentivized to provide. Public investment in statistics also generates positive
      externalities for governments, citizens, and businesses by enabling evidence-based choices in each domain.
      Consequently, the project’s outcomes produce large, widely distributed social benefits, including more efficient
      resource allocation and improved public information on socioeconomic conditions, while ultimately reducing the
      risks associated with inadequately informed economic policy decisions.

  17. This project strengthens the quality and availability of data needed to monitor jobs and employment, poverty,
      living conditions, and progress toward the Sustainable Development Goals. Comparable, high-quality statistics
      enable analysis of poverty reduction and its drivers. Greater data availability supports regional integration by
      improving cross‑country comparability, providing clearer diagnostics of each country’s socioeconomic situation,
      and deepening understanding of what generates livelihoods and reduces poverty. Better statistics improve the
      targeting of public policies and the allocation of public resources. By making data publicly accessible, the project
      also enhances public accountability, a pathway to better service delivery and more efficient public spending.
      Although these benefits are difficult to quantify, the opportunity costs of poor data are substantial. Without
      reliable information, the risk of misallocating public spending rises, as does the difficulty of assessing whether
      expenditures and policy choices are effective.

B. Technical

  18. The technical design of the project remains appropriate for the Pacific context and adequate to achieve
      development objectives. The AF maintains the objectives of the original project as well as the component
      structure that has successfully achieved those objectives in the first five years of its life. The investment in
      modernization and harmonization of methods through the PSMB, which emphasized consensus and joint
      implementation, has proven effective in the Pacific. Investing in SPC’s capacity to provide technical assistance to
      the NSOs in applying the new methods was also successful. It is expected that a continuation of this component
      structure will continue to achieve results in the additional year of project life.

  19. New activities around administrative data and CRVS data would require clearer safeguards around the
      protection of personal data. The increased scale of personal data that will be managed by SPC in proposed
      administrative data activities escalates the risks around data protection. As such, the Project Operations Manual
      will be revised by SPC to include a Data Protection Annex. This Annex would reference any relevant SPC policies
      and procedures around data protection and add any required provisions around (i) assessing the adequacy of
      national data protection legislations of countries that may be supported, and (ii) offshore and third-party
      processing of this data. The POM revision will be included as a dated Legal Covenant with a target of 30 days after
      effectiveness of the AF.

 20. The AF will sustain activities for improving the production of statistics on gender, disability, and social inclusion
