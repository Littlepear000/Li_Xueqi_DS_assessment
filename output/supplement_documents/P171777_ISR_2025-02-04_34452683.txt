4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  Some important progress has been achieved in this project in recent months:
  Component 1:
     •      The provisional results of the population and housing census were published in June-July 2024, following the
         completion of fieldwork in May 2024. Additional volumes of results should be released within the next 6 months.
     •      The Institute of Statistics (INSTAD) now has a board of directors, appointed by the ministerial decree, that
         meets several times a year. The 5th meeting of the Board of Directors took place recently to adopt the
         provisional budget for 2025. Work on the 3rd National Strategy for Statistical Development has been launched.
     •      INSTAD has been making substantial progress in preparing for the next household budget survey, with a
         boosted sample of migrant and refugee households. The questionnaire was finalized. Recruitment and training of
         enumerators just completed in December 2024, in parallel to the development of the sampling and computer-
         assisted personal interviews (CAPI) program. Fieldwork will start in January 2025.
     • The Economic Census is expected to be carried out over the next 4-8 months. The first drafts of the REGED
         questionnaire have been developed. Sample development is ongoing, drawing on formal lists and the
         cartography from the population census. Preparations on the communication and CAPI program for the
         household budget survey and the Economic Census are coordinated to take advantage of economies of scale.

  Component 2:
  Review of the National Development Plan: The review of that National Development plan, Djibouti “I.C.I.” has
  been undertaken in October 2024. The authorities are currently finalizing the main findings of this review which
  will inform the preparation of the forthcoming National Development Plan 2025-2029.
  Macro/model: The new quasi-accounting macroeconomic forecasting model, supported by AFRISTAT, has been
  successfully completed. However, its launch, initially planned for September 2024, has been delayed due to the
  limited availability of AFRISTAT experts. Similarly, the development of the CGE model, originally expected by Q1
  2025, is also facing delays as the preparation of the Social Accounting Matrix remains pending. The authorities
  have committed to speed up these activities in the first quarter of 2025.
  Medium-term Expenditure Framework: During the May 2024 supervision mission, a roadmap for
  implementing a program budget was proposed and well-received by the authorities. This roadmap outlined key
  preliminary actions through December 2025 and aligned with the newly adopted government public reform
  strategy. However, progress has been limited, primarily due to capacity constraints. To address this, technical
  assistance for developing the Medium-Term Expenditure Framework is scheduled for 2025Q2.
  Public debt management: Staff from the Ministry of Economy received extensive training on debt recording
  and statistics using SYGADE 6 in September 2024, supported by UNCTAD. Additional training on SYGADE 6 is
