I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING


Introduction

1.     This Restructuring Paper seeks Management’s approval for a Level 2 restructuring of the Harmonizing and
Improving Statistics in West Africa Project (HISWA) (P169265), as outlined in Section II below.

A. Project Description and Status

    Project Description

2.       The Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa project was approved by the World Bank Executive
Directors on March 26, 2020, with a total financing amount of US$379.0 million equivalent. The project includes seven
participating countries, namely, Burkina Faso (IDA Credit 65730 - US$30.0 million and IDA Grant D5810 - US$30.0 million),
Cabo Verde (IDA Credit 65740 - US$15.0 million), Cote d’Ivoire (IDA Credit 65750 - US$54.0 million), Ghana (IDA Credit
65760 - US$150.0 million), Liberia (IDA Credit 65770 - US$15.0 million and IDA Grant D5830 - US$15.0 million), Sierra
Leone (IDA Grant D5840 - US$30.0 million) and Togo (IDA Credit 65780 - US$15.0 million and IDA Grant D5850 - US$15.0
million); as well as two regional organizations, namely, the African Union (AU) (IDA Grant D5880 - US$2.50 million) and
the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) (IDA Grant D5820 - US$7.50 million). Following the first and
second Additional Financings (AFs), the total project financing amount has increased to US$394.80 million equivalent.

3.       The first Level 2 project restructuring of the project was approved by the World Bank Regional Integration
Director for Africa and the Middle East on December 20, 2024. The restructuring focused on revising the results
framework by: (i) removing PDO-level Indicator 2—the share of beneficiary countries (National Statistical Offices) scoring
at least 30 out of 50 on the Institutional Capacity Index (ICI)—as it was not implemented across all participating countries
and proved difficult to measure at an aggregate level; and (ii) correcting data entry errors in the aggregate baseline and
end-target values for three other PDO-level indicators.

4.     First and Second AFs: To cover cost overruns affecting key statistical activities under Components 1 and 3, two
AFs were approved in June 2025. These AFs also included a restructuring of the parent project, extending the closing date
from December 31, 2025, to December 31, 2026, for Sierra Leone, Côte d’Ivoire, Ghana, and Liberia.

        • The first AF, approved on June 16, 2025, totaled US$9.60 million (US$6.0 million for Côte d’Ivoire and
        US$3.60 million for Sierra Leone). The Sierra Leone AF prioritized completing the Sierra Leone Living Household
        Survey (SLIHS), modernizing the Consumer Price Index (CPI), updating national accounts, and constructing the
        Statistics Sierra Leone headquarters. Meanwhile, the Côte d’Ivoire AF focused on sustaining and finalizing critical
        activities, including: (i) the Harmonized Household Survey (EHCVM); (ii) CPI modernization; and (iii) improvements
        to economic statistics, particularly national accounts.

        • The second AF, approved on June 30, 2025, totaled US$6.20 million (US$3.0 million for Ghana and US$3.20
        million for Liberia). The Ghana AF addresses cost overruns and supports completion of key statistical activities
        under the HISWA Project, including: (i) the Ghana Living Standards Survey Round 8; (ii) National Accounts rebasing;
        (iii) CPI rebasing; and (iv) the quarterly Labor Force Survey—critical for achieving the Project Development
        Objective (PDO) and closing data gaps in poverty, labor, and economic statistics. The Liberia AF similarly covers
        cost overruns to advance the Household Income and Expenditure Survey (HIES), Labor Force Survey (LFS), CPI
        expansion, improvements in economic statistics, and institutional capacity building.

5.      The Project Development Objective is to strengthen the statistical systems of participating countries and regional
bodies in Africa to harmonize, produce, disseminate, and enhance the use of core economic and social statistics.

6.       The project is designed to address critical data gaps by harmonizing, producing, disseminating, and promoting
the use of essential economic and social statistics. Its objective is to strengthen the statistical capacity of participating
countries, enabling their National Statistical Systems (NSS) to effectively meet user needs and generate more comparable
data to support informed decision-making. In addition, the project advances the modernization of statistical
methodologies, organizational structures, physical infrastructure, and Information and Communication Technology (ICT)
within National Statistics Offices (NSOs) and relevant Ministries, Departments, and Agencies (MDAs), thereby enhancing
their efficiency and effectiveness in fulfilling their mandates.

7.      Structure of the Parent project and the AFs:

        Component 1: Regional Component: Harmonization, Data Collection, Quality Improvement, Dissemination and
        Use of Core Social and Economic Statistics (US$348.21 million equivalent). This component focuses on promoting
        harmonized methodologies, producing core economic and social statistics, strengthening administrative data
        systems, delivering training on methodological innovations, building human resource capacity, and enhancing the
        quality and timeliness of statistical outputs.

        Component 2: Country-Specific Component – Improving or Modernizing Physical and Statistical Infrastructure
        (US$27.79 million equivalent). This component targets the specific constraints and priorities of each participating
        NSO through tailored interventions in physical, technological, and statistical infrastructure. It also includes
        initiatives to strengthen human capacity and improve legal and regulatory frameworks, ensuring that each NSO
        has the foundational capabilities required to effectively implement activities under Component 1.

        Component 3: Project Management and Monitoring and Evaluation (US$18.8 million equivalent). This
        component ensures effective project management and outcome monitoring across participating countries and
        regional organizations. It supports the efficient execution of activities, with NSOs serving as implementing
        agencies responsible for technical oversight and coordination. Each country’s project management team handles
        implementation, reporting, auditing, environmental and social safeguards, and compliance with fiduciary policies.
        Specifically, this component covers costs for project staff, coordination (including steering committee operations),
        general operating expenses, and other essential expenditures.

B. Project Status

8.       The project is currently being implemented across all seven participating countries and two regional
organizations. Official implementation of the parent project began on July 13, 2020, when the Ghana Financing Agreement
became effective. Recent developments include the effectiveness of the Additional Financing Agreements for Sierra Leone
(September 12, 2025) and Côte d’Ivoire (October 1, 2025). The Additional Financing Agreement for Liberia, signed on
August 18, 2025, is still pending effectiveness, while the Ghana Additional Financing Agreement is awaiting client
signature. The ratings for both the Project Development Objective (PDO) and overall implementation progress (IP) are
Satisfactory, reflecting that most project activities are on track to achieve the PDO.

across the participating countries and regional organizations: (i) 99.9 percent for African Union; (ii) 85.4 percent for
ECOWAS; (iii) 89.35 percent in Burkina Faso; (iv) 76.4 percent in Cabo Verde; (v) 94.3 percent in Cote d’Ivoire; (vi) 93.1
percent in Ghana; (vii) 93.5 percent in Liberia; (viii) 99.9 percent in Sierra Leone; and (ix) 78.0 percent in Togo.

12.      Project Implementation Progress by Subcomponents. The HISWA project, including its AFs, is structured into
three main components, each with defined subcomponents and financing allocations. The implementation progress is
tracked at both component and subcomponent levels to reflect country-specific activities and regional coordination
efforts.

        Use of Core Social and Economic Statistics (US$348.21 million)

        Subcomponent 1.1. Support implementation of the SHaSA2 initiative by the African Union, support production
        of harmonized methodologies by the ECOWAS Commission, and improve data access at the regional level
        (US$8.00 million equivalent): This subcomponent has supported the NSOs in developing and adopting

harmonized methodologies aligned with international standards for multitopic living standards household
surveys, national accounts, and key basic statistics, all within a coordinated framework with the regional
organizations, such as AU/STATAFRIC and ECOWAS.

Subcomponent 1.2. Support of Household-Based Surveys at the National Level Using Harmonized
Methodologies (US$168.64 million equivalent): This subcomponent supports the production of core social
statistics in the seven participating countries through population censuses and living standards household surveys,
including multi-topic surveys to measure poverty and labor force surveys to analyze employment barriers. It has
facilitated major surveys and censuses, prioritized the adoption of ECOWAS standards, and aimed to improve data
on gender, disability, and resilience to shocks such as climate change. Additional efforts include regular labor
market analysis, enterprise surveys, and coordination with demographic and health surveys. Ongoing key activities
include the harmonized household survey, demographic and health survey/Multi-Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS),
and the labor force survey system.

Subcomponent 1.3. Support to the Core Set of Economic Statistics at the National Level Using Harmonized
Methodologies (US$88.26 million equivalent): This subcomponent focuses on improving the quality of enterprise
and agricultural statistics to strengthen national accounts and support the adoption of the System of National
Account (SNA) 2008 standard across all participating countries. Key activities include: (i) establishing a robust
enterprise statistics system using surveys and administrative data, clarifying formal and informal sector
definitions, and enhancing data collection methods and coverage; (ii) implementing comprehensive agricultural
statistics systems through censuses and surveys, leveraging international methodologies to close data gaps and
promote climate-smart agriculture; and (iii) modernizing national accounts in collaboration with international
organizations by updating base years, providing software, and delivering technical assistance. These efforts,
backed by substantial funding and technical support, aim to align with international standards and build statistical
capacity in the region. Nearly all activities under this subcomponent have been completed. Critical data sources
such as the business establishment census, agricultural surveys and census, and the producer price index are
either finalized and published or in progress. The rebasing of national accounts has been completed or is underway
in all participating countries.

Subcomponent 1.4. Support the Modernization of the Consumer Price Index (CPI) at the National Level Using
Harmonized Methodologies (US$6.94 million equivalent): This subcomponent focuses on improving the quality
and timeliness of the CPI across participating countries, in collaboration with the International Monetary Fund
(IMF), the West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU), and AFRISTAT. Key actions include updating CPI
methodologies to align with international standards, adopting more recent base years, expanding geographic
coverage beyond major cities, and financing enhanced price data collection often using Computer-Assisted
Personal Interviewing (CAPI). ECOWAS supports software selection and engages both international and regional
price experts. The objective is to deliver a harmonized, integrated national CPI series published monthly on NSO
websites. Progress toward this goal is strong: the transition from paper-based to CAPI-based price data collection
is underway, and CPI continues to be published consistently monthly across all participating countries.

Subcomponent 1.5. Support the Improvement of Administrative Data Sources (US$34.09 million equivalent):
This subcomponent has strengthened the production of administrative statistics through targeted training and
the provision of essential equipment, such as tablets, computers, and laptops. Implementation of these activities
is progressing well across all participating countries.

Subcomponent 1.6. Enhancing the Use of Big Data (US$7.53 million equivalent): This subcomponent has driven
innovation within NSOs by diversifying data sources and establishing innovation centers. These initiatives have

Subcomponent 1.7. Enhanced Data Accessibility and Dissemination (US$4.45 million equivalent): This

analysis using modern technologies and statistical software. Additionally, specialized assistance has been provided
to aligned ministries to build capacity in macroeconomic analysis, fiscal simulation, and policy impact assessment.

Component 2: Country Specific Components (US$27.79 million equivalent): This component aims to rehabilitate
and expand physical infrastructure while modernizing technological systems across all participating countries.
Modernization initiatives include providing computers, statistical software, internet connectivity, and solutions
for data storage and archiving to overcome logistical challenges.

Subcomponent 2.1. Burkina Faso: Modernize Technological and Statistical Infrastructure (US$6.5 million). This
subcomponent has supported the modernization of technological and statistical infrastructure at Institut National
de la Statistique et de la Démographie (INSD) Headquarters, its decentralized directorates, and the DGESS units of
two line-ministries. It addresses critical gaps in technological infrastructure such as internet connectivity,
computers, statistical software and packages, data archiving systems, and generators across INSD and its regional
offices. At headquarters, INSD requires advanced equipment, including robust database software and servers, to
manage and archive multiple datasets effectively. What remains to be done include the establishment of network
connectivity between the Headquarter with the three regional direction of the institute.

Subcomponent 2.2: Cabo Verde: Improving Information and Communications Technology (ICT) Infrastructure
(US$2 million equivalent). This subcomponent has supported the enhancement of ICT infrastructure to enable
data exchange and improve access through the development of a new electronic platform for budget and results
information, with a strong emphasis on administrative data. The project introduced these platforms at both
national and local levels. Key activities include acquiring equipment such as servers and laptops, securing software
licenses, programming data platforms, and strengthening the capacity and functionality of the NSS network. The
project is currently working on operationalization of the platform for public access to data.

Subcomponent 2.3. Côte d’Ivoire: Modernize Technological Infrastructure (US$1.20 million equivalent): This
subcomponent has provided computers, statistical software, and internet connectivity to enable innovations such
as cloud-based data storage. It has also established specialized innovation centers, including a call center, a
cartography center, and a computer center.

        Fiduciary, Environmental, and Social Management

13.      The procurement performance rating is Moderately Satisfactory, and the risk level is Moderate. Overall, the
procurement performance review notes good implementation progress across project-participating countries, the African Union
and ECOWAS. Nonetheless, several challenges exist including missing procurement documents in Systematic Tracking of
Exchanges in Procurement (STEP), and delays in receiving technical specifications and TORs from user departments. Therefore,
there is need for stronger contract management practices, and more collaboration between the technical departments and
procurement unit. As mitigation measures, the World Bank continues to provide training on the use of STEP and arrange related
procurement capacity-building training.

14.      Financial Management (FM) arrangements remain Moderately Satisfactory. The overall FM risk rating is Moderate,
although residual control risks for certain implementing agencies (e.g., AU and Ghana) remain Substantial. Fiduciary compliance
was deemed satisfactory for all the implementing agencies. For example, the unaudited Interim Financial Reports (IFRs) were
submitted on time and found acceptable to World Bank. The last audit reports for the Fiscal Year ending December 31st, 2024,
were submitted to the World Bank on time with an unqualified opinion, except for AU which has submitted its audit reports
with more than two months delay (e.g., audit reports due on June 30th, 2025, were submitted on September 22nd, 2025). FM
team has closely been working with the respective implementing agencies to ensure the timely submission of FY2025 audit
reports.

15.      Environment and Social: The HISWA Project is governed by the World Bank Safeguard Policy. Environmental and Social
Risk Rating: Moderate. Activities are primarily technical assistance, surveys, and minor office renovations in Sierra Leone, with
limited site-specific impacts. Grievance Redress Mechanism (GRM) is operational at regional and country levels; accessible to
project-affected parties. Overall, the Environmental and Social Framework (ESF) performance is on track.

16.     Monitoring and Evaluation Performance: The project has allocated US$18 million to support project management
and monitoring & evaluation (M&E) activities. This allocation covers the implementation of the Results Framework, regular
progress tracking, capacity building for national statistical offices, and coordination with regional bodies to ensure
harmonization and timely reporting. Overall, M&E performance under all project participating countries and regional
organizations (AU and ECOWAS) is satisfactory.

17.      General Risks and Mitigation: The overall risk rating for the project remains Moderate to Substantial with respect
to achieving the Project Development Objective. The proposed restructuring does not change this assessment. However,
residual risks persist in two key areas: Institutional Capacity for Implementation and Sustainability and Environmental and
Social (E&S), as both are currently rated Substantial. To address these risks, the following mitigation measures have been
agreed upon and implemented across participating countries:

    •   Capacity Building on World Bank Operational Policies: Targeted training sessions have strengthened
        understanding and application of World Bank procedures for projects under the HISWA initiative.

    •   Technical Assistance and Implementation Support: Ongoing support has improved procurement processes,
        particularly contract management, ensuring timely and high-quality delivery of project outputs.

         Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa (P169265)



    •   Monitoring and Planning Enhancements: Implementation plans now include quarterly disbursement
        projections to facilitate better monitoring and follow-up.

    •   Environmental and Social Risk Management: ESF-related risks are being managed through structured
        frameworks and stakeholder engagement, with a strong focus on timely reporting and compliance.

C. Rational for Restructuring

18.     During the mid-term review in May 2024 and subsequent implementation support missions, the World Bank
project teams and the Borrowers jointly assessed progress on the HISWA Project. Based on this assessment and a shared
understanding, both parties agreed that extending the project closing date is necessary to accelerate implementation and
address delays caused by a combination of internal and external factors. Following this agreement, the Borrowers
submitted formal requests for: (i) extension of the project closing dates for Burkina Faso, Cabo Verde, Togo, African Union,
and ECOWAS; and (ii) reallocation of funds between categories for the Côte d’Ivoire parent project.

    •   Internal factors: During the initial phase, both the implementing entity and fiduciary agencies needed time to
        build operational capacity, which slowed the execution of planned activities. These constraints were further
        compounded by weaknesses in contract management and oversight, creating procurement bottlenecks. Delays in
        procurement planning and lengthy approval processes also hindered timely implementation. The restructuring
        seeks to address these challenges by adjusting project timelines to enable more effective execution and ensure
        achievement of the PDO.

    •   External factors contributing to delays: Project implementation was significantly hindered by external shocks,
        most notably the COVID-19 pandemic. The pandemic disrupted global supply chains, causing delays in
        procurement and bidding processes for essential goods and services. Delivery timelines for vehicles, computers,
        tablets, interviewer kits, and other data collection equipment were extended well beyond initial estimates.
        Additionally, lockdowns and movement restrictions created logistical challenges for survey implementation,
        requiring major adjustments to field operations and resulting in higher costs and prolonged timelines for
        enumerator training and data collection. Collectively, these disruptions led to substantial delays in project
        execution.

II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

19.       The proposed project changes will include: (i) extending the closing date for (A) Burkina Faso, (B) Togo, (C) Cabo
Verde, (D) ECOWAS, (E) African Union, and (ii) reallocating funds between expenditure categories for Côte d’Ivoire; and
(iii) adjusting the Results Framework end target date to align with the new closing dates for Burkina Faso, Togo, Cabo
Verde, and ECOWAS.

    A. Burkina Faso

amendment is based on a request sent by the Government on May 22, 2025, for a six-month extension of the project
closing date to June 30, 2026, to allow completion of key activities in Burkina Faso. These include: (i) finalizing data
collection for the Harmonized Household Survey; (ii) fully transitioning to Computer-Assisted Personal Interviewing for
modernizing the Consumer Price Index; (iii) advancing improvements in economic statistics, particularly national accounts;
and (iv) supporting institutional reforms and human capital development. The Harmonized Household Survey is being
implemented simultaneously across all WAEMU member states, including Burkina Faso. As shown in the figure below, the

resources to fully complete this activity. In this regard, discussion is ongoing with the Country Management Unit (CMU)
and Regional Integration (RI) to finance data processing, analysis, and dissemination. If complementary resources become

    B. Togo

21.      Request for Amendment to Financing Agreement (IDA Credit-65780 and IDA Grant-D5850): The proposed

objectives, through the implementation and completion of key ongoing activities, including (i) a new household poverty
survey; (ii) cadastral registry; (iii) diaspora mapping; (iv) preparatory activities of the agriculture census; (v) structural
surveys for the GDP rebasing; (vi) semi-annual employment surveys; (vii) improvement in public access to microdata and
administrative data; and (viii) the census of public administration agents.
