                         I.      BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE FOR ADDITIONAL FINANCING

                        A. Introduction

                        1.       This Project Paper seeks the approval of the World Bank Africa West Regional Programs Director for the second
                        Additional Financing (AF) to Ghana component of the Project in the amount of US$19.00 million to support the
                        Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa (HISWA) Project (P169265) in Ghana . The proposed AF includes
                        allocations of US$19.00 million IDA Credits from the Performance Based Allocation (PBA). The proposed AF is intended to
                        address the cost overruns related to the implementation of critical priority activities, particularly the Ghana Living
                        Standards Survey round eight (GLSS8), the Multiple Indicators Cluster Survey (MICS), the Quarterly Labor Force Survey
                        (QLFS) as well as the expansion and rebasing of the Consumer Price Index (CPI), the rebasing of National Accounts,
                        improvement of administrative data and modernization of the physical and Information Technology infrastructure. The
For Official Use Only




                        objective is to close significant data gaps in poverty, labor market and economic statistics and to enhance the production
                        and use of high-quality data, as well as data security and statistical coordination, thereby supporting the achievement of
                        the Project Development Objective (PDO).

                        2.       The parent project was approved by the World Bank Board of Executive Directors on March 26, 2020. The project
                        includes nine recipients with a total financing amount of US$379.00 million - seven participating countries: Burkina Faso
                        (IDA Credit 65730 - US$30.0 million and IDA Grant D5810 - US$30.00 million), Cabo Verde (IDA Credit 65740 - US$15.00

                        17.      The delays and cost overruns encountered under the parent project stemmed from a combination of internal
                        and external factors. Implementation capacity had to be built in both the implementing entity and the fiduciary agencies
                        during the initial stage of project implementation. This slowed the execution of planned activities and contributed to
                        increased operational expenses. Delays in procurement planning, lengthy approval processes, and limited competition in
                        bidding contributed to cost escalations. The lack of robust contract management and oversight further exacerbated these
                        issues, leading to procurement-related bottlenecks and the need for costly adjustments later in implementation.

                        18.     Externally, several major shocks significantly increased project costs. The COVID-19 pandemic severely disrupted
                        global supply chains, delaying procurement and bidding processes, and triggering sharp price increases for essential goods
                        and services. Items such as vehicles, computers, tablets, interviewer kits, and other data collection equipment became
                        substantially more expensive than originally budgeted. The pandemic also introduced logistical challenges for survey
                        implementation. Lockdowns and movement restrictions required major adjustments to fieldwork operations, leading to
                        increased costs for enumerator training, extended data collection periods, vehicle rentals, and additional staff
                        compensation. Collectively, these adjustments substantially raised the overall cost of survey operations.

                        19.      Governments across participating countries increasingly demand more frequent and timely data to monitor the
                        social and economic impacts of COVID-19 on households and businesses. In response, the project supported the rapid
                        design and implementation of multiple rounds of phone-based poverty surveys to address urgent data gaps. While these
                        surveys were more feasible during lockdowns and ensured continuity in data collection, they also introduced new cost
                        drivers. These included expenses related to call centers or mobile service providers, airtime and respondent incentives,
                        enumerator training for phone-based interviews, software customization, and remote data processing systems. In Ghana,
                        many rounds of the COVID-19 Impact Monitoring Survey (CIMS) were conducted, providing critical real-time data to inform
                        government response efforts.

                        20.      In addition, the creation of six additional regions in Ghana, increasing the total from 10 to 16, impacted the
                        design of household and establishment surveys. Given that surveys were expected to produce statistically reliable
                        estimates at the regional level, the sample must include a minimum number of observations in each region. As the number
                        of regions has increased, the survey design had therefore accommodated a larger number of geographic domains, each
                        requiring adequate sample coverage to meet precision standards commonly used by GSS. Indeed, if total sample sizes are
                        not adjusted upward following such administrative changes, the number of observations per region would have declined,
                        leading to higher sampling errors and reduced precision of regional estimates, which affects the reliability of key indicators
                        used for policy dialogue, targeting, and monitoring development outcomes. Consequently, to preserve data quality and
                        comparability over time, surveys conducted after the regional reorganization increased overall sample sizes and costs. For
                        example, the GLSS8 expanded its sample size to 25,521 households to ensure representation across all 16 regions,
                        compared to 14,009 households for GLSS7, which was designed for 10 regions. Similarly, the 2025 Integrated Business
                        Survey increased its sample size to 45,434 establishments, up from 31,152, to achieve representative results within the
                        16 regions. As a result, cost overruns have affected several project components, particularly those related to data
                        collection, production, and dissemination. To address these overruns, the Government of Ghana requested AF of US$30.0
                        million in June 2025. However, given the limited availability of resources toward the end of the IDA20 cycle, only US$3.0
                        million could be allocated under an initial AF, which was strategically focused on supporting the most critical statistical
                        activities initiated under the parent project.

                         IV.     APPRAISAL SUMMARY

                          A. Economic Analysis

                        30.     The economic justification for the project, including the proposed AF for Ghana, remains strong and unchanged
                        from that of the parent project. The analysis covers the entire financing envelope, including the AF, recognizing that the
                        higher costs motivating the AF reflect cost overruns rather than changes in project scope, design, or objectives.

                        31.      The HISWA project addresses fundamental issues in the production and use of official statistics. High‑quality,
                        timely, and comparable economic and social statistics are public goods that are essential for effective policy formulation,
                        fiscal management, service delivery, and private sector decision‑making, yet are typically underprovided without public
                        investment. By strengthening the NSS, the project generates broad and sustained economic benefits for governments, the
                        private sector, researchers, civil society, and development partners.

                        32.     The project supports production, harmonization, dissemination, and use of core economic and social statistics
                        including poverty indicators, consumer price indices, national accounts, labor, and demographic data which are critical
                        inputs for macroeconomic management, poverty reduction strategies, and monitoring development outcomes.

                                                                                                                                            Page 9
                                 The World Bank
                                 Harmonizing and Improving Statistics in West Africa (P169265) – Second Additional Financing for Ghana



                        Improved data quality and comparability across institutions reduce information asymmetries, strengthen accountability,
                        enhance governance, and contribute to more efficient allocation of public resources.

                        33.      The proposed AF for Ghana is economically justified as it safeguards the returns of the parent project by
                        enabling completion of high‑priority activities affected by cost overruns. These cost increases are primarily attributable
                        to inflationary pressures, COVID‑19‑related disruptions, and the scale and complexity of data‑intensive operations, rather
                        than to implementation inefficiencies. Without the AF, unfinished statistical activities would risk undermining data quality
                        and diminishing the economic value of prior investments.

                        34.      Although most PDO indicators have been achieved, the AF‑supported activities are critical to sustaining and
                        deepening results, particularly in the context of Ghana’s recent macroeconomic challenges. Updated poverty estimates
                        strengthened CPI and national accounts, and improved socio‑economic and demographic indicators are essential for
                        assessing the welfare impacts of high inflation, fiscal consolidation, and economic adjustment measures, and for informing
                        policies aimed at protecting vulnerable populations.

                        system‑level reforms with long‑term economic payoffs, including implementation of the National Data Quality Assurance
                        Framework, the Code of Ethics and Practice for the NSS, enhanced statistical coordination, improvements in key
                        administrative data systems, and modernization of physical and ICT infrastructure. These investments improve efficiency,
                        reduce duplication, lower future data production costs, and enhance the sustainability of the NSS.

                        36.     Given the public‑good nature of statistical outputs, a traditional cost‑benefit analysis is not feasible. However,
                        international experience and World Bank practice consistently demonstrate that investments in statistical capacity yield
                        high economic and social returns through improved governance, better‑targeted policies, and more efficient use of public
                        and private resources. The proposed AF therefore represents a cost‑effective intervention to preserve and fully realize
                        the economic benefits of the HISWA project.

                          B. Technical

                        37.     The proposed AF activity is strategically relevant and timely for Ghana. Harmonized social and economic data
                        are essential for effective planning and policy-formulation, particularly for efficient allocation of resources and service
                        delivery. The Ghana Living Standards Survey, the Multiple Indicators Cluster Survey, the Quarterly Labor Force Survey are
                        particularly critical for understanding the evolving socio-economic landscape, especially poverty, infant mortality and
                        sanitation, informality and employment trends, income distribution, and household welfare. The national account and CPI
                        data are critical to better monitor the country's economy, especially in the wake of the recent economic imbalances in
                        Ghana. Without a robust data system and regular high-quality surveys to support data analysis and evidence-based
                        decision-making, much of the potential of data to improve development outcomes and address inequalities will go
                        untapped.

                        Gender

                        50.      Like the parent project, the AF will help address some gender data gaps. By collecting data on sex, age, marital
                        status, and other demographic factors, the Ghana Living Standards Survey as well as the Multiple Indicators Cluster Survey
                        and Quarterly Labor Force Surveys enable a detailed analysis of gender differences. This data can inform policies and
                        resource allocation aimed at promoting gender equality in employment and economic opportunities. The same applies to
                        education levels and literacy, which can highlight gender gaps in access to education. This information is crucial for
                        developing targeted interventions to improve educational outcomes for women and girls at national and district levels. In
                        addition, the project will ensure adequate gender representation in the recruitment of survey enumerators.
