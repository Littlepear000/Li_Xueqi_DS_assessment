    I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING
        1. This paper seeks approval from the Country Director for a Level II restructuring of the Madagascar Economic
           Transformation for Inclusive Growth Project (PIC3), Credit No. 6929-MG, which will cancel an amount of SDR
           15.305 million (equivalent to about US$20 million). This is the first restructuring for this project. The funds
           cancellation is in response to a Government request, which was communicated to the World Bank in a letter
           from the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) received on 1 May 2024.
        2. Background: PIC3 is an Investment Project Financing (IPF) that was approved on June 18, 2021, in the amount of
           US$150 million equivalent, and became effective on November 12, 2021. The project closing date is January 31,
           2027. The PDO is to “increase the growth of Small and Medium Enterprises in Target Regions in Madagascar”.
        3. The project’s target regions are Diana, Atsimo-Andrefana, Anosy, Analanjirofo, Atsinanana, Fitovinany, Vatovavy,
           and Atsimo-Atsinanana. PIC3 consists of five components: (1) Attracting and Retaining Private Investment and
           Removing Key Constraints to Support Economic Recovery from the COVID19 crisis; (2) Removing Constraints to
           Private Investment and Enhancing Local Economic Competitiveness to Support Recovery in Target Regions and
           Target Sectors; (3) Supporting SMEs and Entrepreneurship Recovery and Growth in Target Sectors and Target
           Regions; (4) project implementation; and (5) contingent emergency response component (CERC).
        4. Project performance to date: Progress towards achieving the PDO and implementation progress are both rated
           Moderately Unsatisfactory (MU). Financial Management (FM) is rated Moderately Unsatisfactory, while
           procurement is rated Moderately Satisfactory. There are no pending audits. All legal covenants to date have
           been complied with. Overall risk remains Substantial.
        5. PIC3 has disbursed US$ 30.6 million1 (out of the US$ 150 million budget), representing 22 percent of the total
           budget. The reason for the downgrade to MU after the supervision mission of October 2023 was the low
           disbursement performance that the project had, starting at the end of 2022 and throughout 2023. These
           challenges were mainly caused by the delays in processing the “regularization budgétaire” (a national procedure
           which was a prerequisite for call of funds and withdrawal applications clearance). The delays with the
           regularization budgétaire were due to heavy procedures at the MEF but were also a consequence of important
           delays at the PIC PIU, reflecting a diminished FM capacity at the Project Implementation Unit (PIU). Because of
           these delays, the project was not able to receive funds into its own accounts, which significantly delayed
           payments of suppliers and contractors of goods and services. As a consequence, the project accumulated
           significant arrears and disbursed lower than expected amounts, leading to delays and even temporary
           suspension of the implementation of many committed activities that eventually put the project in problem
           status.
        6. The fiduciary situation has significantly improved since then, albeit FM capacity at the PIU continues to be a
           challenge. Over the past 5 months (January-May 2024), the project has been able to disburse more funds than
           over the previous 2 years. This is due to changes at MEF on the treatment of disbursement requests for externally
           financed projects such as World Bank’s financed projects, which have eliminated many of the delays previously
           experienced. The World Bank supervision team and the PIU also agreed on a list of contracts/activities that have
           been prioritized in terms of implementation. These priority contracts/activities were selected to help improve:
           (i) project financial performance, both commitments but especially disbursement rates; and (ii) project results.
           Progress on these priority activities/contracts are followed weekly during a meeting between World Bank and
           PIU. The World Bank technical team and the PIU have also worked closely with World Bank procurement
           specialist and FM teams to identify and agree on the most efficient procurement methods and to facilitate and

    Progress by Component

   Component 1
7. Business climate: The project has advanced in supporting investment attraction and retention, mainly through
   the support to the Madagascar Investment Promotion Agency (EDBM). This support is framed under a recently
   signed contract between the project and EDBM which focuses on: (i) improving EDBM services in its existing
   regional offices, as well as setting up two new EDBM regional offices (in Fort Dauphin and Manakara); (ii)
   strengthening the institutional capacity of EDBM; (iii) supporting targeted investment promotion activities in
   agribusiness, tourism and ICT; and (iv) supporting the implementation, promotion and development of reforms
   aimed at improving the business environment in general, but more specifically to push for the reforms needed
   at local level (PIC intervention regions).
8. Tourism: The project continues to support the Ministry of Tourism and EDBM to participate in key regional
   investment promotion events (TIFA, AHIF, and FHS). Participation in these events have led to important learnings
   as to how to present the country in terms of an investment destination, and several important interactions with
   major tourism investment groups have been achieved, leading to 7 clear leads for investment opportunities.
9. Agribusiness: The project continues to support sector PPD initiatives (recently on cloves) and the participation
   of key stakeholders at national and international fairs and economic events aimed at connecting the private
   sector and international buyers (Salon du Chocolat in Paris, Biofach in Germany, Trade and Economic Mission to
   Canada, and Buyers mission to Madagascar)
10. Digital: The project has recently launched a consultancy to review the legal and regulatory framework to support
    the development of ecommerce in Madagascar. The project will also support a consultancy to provide an analysis
    and recommendations for legislation to support the development of new enterprises in the country.
11. Financial inclusion: The work for the implementation and operationalization of the SWITCH system at the Central
    Bank has resumed with the signature of the contracts for the key suppliers (technical assistance and equipment
    providers).
12. As part of the support to Microfinance Institutions, selected Micro Finance institutions in PIC regions have
    received equipment such as laptops, servers and vehicles to support digitalization of their operations and the
    expansion of their services, including in underserved areas.


    Component 2
13. Tourism: The project continues to support renewed financial and technical support to regional tourism offices in
    target regions and is preparing expansion of the ongoing Miatrika tourism worker training program to selected
    new regions. The project has launched/is in the process of launching a number of large-scale tourism
    infrastructure investments, including upgrading of access roads to key tourism locations (in Joffreville/Montagne
    d’Ambre, and Sainte Marie) and airport improvements (Diego). Certain new site-level investments are currently
    on hold, pending either updating of safeguards documentation, confirmation of budget availability, and/or
    Government prioritization.
14. Agribusiness: The PIC teams in the new regions (east coast) have started to scale up technical supervision and
    traceability support activities for selected value chains: distribution of “markers” to 4,400 producers is underway

    to support traceability, as well as the establishment of 50 organized markets to facilitate the selling of these
    products. Three agricultural access roads have been prioritized for project intervention in the new regions and
    the related technical studies have been launched. Rehabilitation work is also planned during 2024 for three
    piers/landing structures along the Canal de Pangalanes to facilitate commerce in the region. Finally, the
    rehabilitation work for the Haut Sambirano road started under PIC2.2 is being finalized under PIC3, with works
    expected to be finalized during the second semester of 2024.
15. Digital: Work continues for the preparation and launch of the “Miatrika Digital” program, which will provide
    specialized training for the Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) sector to facilitate employment and further
    investments in the sector. The development of digital infrastructure (communication towers to provide
    connectivity in areas of PIC intervention) has been discussed and an agreement reached that this intervention
    will be implemented by the DECIM project, with close coordination with the PIC project.
16. Local governance: 49 small projects are under implementation (Opération Communale d'Appui Intégré, OCAI
    grants) to improve productive infrastructures in selected communities in priority regions. These projects extend
    the works performed under previous PIC projects to the new regions and help to mobilize additional financial
    contribution from the municipalities for capital investments that facilitate economic activity in the targeted value
    chains at the community level.


    Component 3:
17. Direct support to SMEs and entrepreneurs: The project launched in July 2023 the MIARY Digital, a digital
    incubation program in partnership with the 3 main incubators in Madagascar. 47 entrepreneurs (18 women)
    have been selected for the first cohort and are currently going through a 12-month incubation program with one
    of the 3 incubators selected. In addition to the incubation program, these entrepreneurs are eligible to apply to
    small grants to help them develop their businesses. Evaluation of their proposals for these grants has just been
    completed, and implementation will begin in June 2024. The MBIF Agribusiness Program was launched in
    December 2023 and has been adjusted to take into account feedback received during the first 2 waves of
    projects. The first conventions are expected to be signed in July 2024. The program has been launched across all
    9 regions supported by the project, and it aims to improve the competitiveness of key value chains, by
    strengthening the commercial link between agricultural input providers and agribusinesses that transform
    primary products for export or local markets. The Acceleration of SMEs program is under preparation, while the
    new wave of MIARY Agri and Tourism program will be launched in June 2024, with a structure adjusted to
    consider feedback received during evaluation of the first few cohorts of beneficiaries.
18. Ecosystem upgrading: The pre-incubation program, which will provide basic entrepreneurial training in all
    regions covered by the project, will be launched in July 2024. The project will also provide small grants for
    regional entities that support entrepreneurship initiatives such as incubators, Chambers of Commerce, Regional
    Business Centers, universities, non-governmental organizations, and others. These grants will help them upgrade
    their facilities to provide better services to entrepreneurs.
19. Project impact. Slow disbursements have affected project indicators, but a full review of project impact so far,
    including further adjustments to activities and budgets, changes and eventual deletion and/or addition of
    indicators and their targets will be done following the Mid Term Review mission, as agreed with the Country
    Management Unit and to be carried out during the second semester of 2024.
20. World Bank Scorecard indicator retrofitting. The PIC3 has been selected by OPCS for the World Bank Corporate
    Scorecard Indicator retrofitting exercise. An existing indicator (New jobs created by supported firms in target
