I. PROGRAM STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

Program Status

1.       The Crisis Resilient Social Protection program became effective on March 29, 2021. This is a 4-year, $600
million Program for Results operation with a $ 5 million Technical Assistance component. The project was designed
to respond to gaps identified by the government during the COVID emergency cash transfer response through the
Benazir Income Support Program (BISP). The institutional and operational reforms proposed under the program
were designed to further improve the delivery system, expand coverage to protect human capital investments and
introduce mechanisms to mitigate the risks for the vulnerable population not covered by the regular BISP safety net
programs. The program has three (3) Result Areas (RA)s focusing on; 1) Building a crisis resilient delivery system; 2)
Establishing a Hybrid Social Protection (SP) system for resilience of poor against future crisis; and 3) Protecting
human capital through demand-side education and health initiatives for children.

2.     The program has maintained an overall satisfactory rating in the first year of its operation. There has been
considerable achievement in two of the RAs, with some overachieved. The recent supervision mission noted good
progress towards achievement of the results for the first year and early achievement of those for the second year.
The progress against implementation is as follows:


            (i) Result Area 1: Building a crisis resilient delivery system:
                     i. Sub Result Area A (Innovations to Social Registry): This RA focused on setting up a dynamic
                        update mechanism (MELA) for the National Socio-Economic Registry (NSER) and develop
                        mechanisms for data exchange. The goal of the MELA mechanism is to (i) provide multiple
                        points of entry (physical registration desks, door-to-door surveys, virtual services); (ii)
                        ensure access windows are local; and (iii) ensure data flow is automated. Updated data will
                        be validated and consolidated into NSER, available for use in facilitating eligibility
                        determination for multiple programs. The phase wise development of MELA included
                        completion of the one-time update of the NSER, setting up dynamic update mechanisms
                        for missed out households and subsequently implementing the complete cycle of dynamic
                        update to ensure regular updates. In the first year of operation, the complete MELA design
                        has been developed and backend MIS tested and implemented. The interim mechanism for
                        missed out households is already operational in all Tehsils of Pakistan and the complete
                        MELA module will be functional by the end of September 2022. An API has also been
                        developed for online and secure exchange of data with institutions and programs that rely
                        on NSER for targeting and enrollment of households and families. All expected results under
                        this Sub Result Area are on track and will achieve some of the 3rd year DLI targets a year
                        earlier than planned.
                    ii. Sub Result Area B (Beneficiary-Centric Payment System): There was a need identified
                        during the COVID crisis to enhance the accessibility and expand the choice of payment
                        delivery services for the safety net beneficiaries. The focus was to design a new payment
                        system that can link up with the national payment gateway and provide choice to
                        beneficiaries of multiple payment service providers. The design would also allow the
                        beneficiaries to receive their cash grants from anywhere in the country. Though the results
                        and DLIs associated with this component were not due in the first year of operation,

         The World Bank

                        progress has been made in developing the relevant framework and mechanisms which are
                        under discussion with relevant stakeholders. This component is also on track and there are
                        no anticipated delays.
            (ii) Result Area 2 (Developing Innovative Hybrid Social Protection Scheme): This RA supports a hybrid
                 scheme which blends social assistance with social risk mitigation elements to promote savings for
                 informal sector workers to mitigate against future shocks. This is expected to expand SP coverage
                 through a hybrid SP scheme supporting the Government’s risk mitigation and financial inclusion
                 interventions among informal workers. This is a new concept that was introduced in the program
                 to test new mechanism that could be scaled up if successful, and results are not due in the first year
                 of program implementation. This component does not have a large bearing on the overall safety
                 net reforms envisaged under the program as is a stand alone SP initiative.
            (iii) Result Area 3 (Protecting human capital in the early and primary school years):
                     i. Sub Result Area A (Improved health and nutrition (H&N) in the early years): This initiative
                        supports early investments in H&N by supporting demand-side interventions aimed at
                        mitigating access constraints. The nutrition focused CCT for mother and child incentivizes
                        take up of services provided through the public health system. Support consists of financing
                        for CCTs and establishing sectoral coordination mechanisms for program implementation
                        in federally administered areas. The results under this component have been overachieved
                        including the DLIs for year 3 due to exemplary program expansion. More than 40,000
                        beneficiaries are currently enrolled into the program.
                    ii. Sub Result Area B (Incentivizing school enrollment and retention): This component
                        supports the expansion of WeT and consolidation of efforts to improve coordination with
                        provincial education departments. Expansion includes demand-side incentives for girls to
                        address the schooling gender gap in progressing to secondary education and incentives to
                        complete primary education. It is expected to enhance future resilience of service delivery
                        by enabling access in the aftermath of climate-related shocks, which may restrict physical
                        access of educational facilities. This component has been expanded to all 160 districts
                        across Pakistan with 8.5 million ever enrolled children. The results have been over-achieved
                        with all DLI’s on track for disbursement.


3.       The Program Development Objective and all Results and DLIs of the program continue to be aligned with
the government’s priorities. The RAs were drawn from the experiences of the COVID response and are embedded
in the government’s revised Social Protection Strategy (Ehsaas). The relevance of these initiatives is even more
significant today as the government struggles to respond to the unprecedented floods currently facing the country.


Rationale For Restructuring

4.        The fiscal year 2023 has has been characterized with unpredictable patterns of abnormally heavy
monsoon rains in Pakistan. Flash floods have killed at least 900 people and displaced more than 320,000. Currently,
it is estimated that 4.2 million people have been impacted and more than 670,000 homes have been damaged. The
worst hit provinces are Baluchistan and Sindh, but all provinces have been impacted and a national emergency has
been declared. To provide relief to the affected population the government has announced a cash transfer of Rs
25,000 for families in the affected areas through the Benazir Income Support Program. The government has decided

         The World Bank
         Pakistan Crisis-Resilient Social Protection (CRISP) (P174484)


to apply a two-stage targeting mechanism, where initially the flood affected areas would be geographically targeted
and then the bottom 40 percent of the households within these areas/regions will be identified through the NSER.
The government estimates that $500 million would be required to cover the cost of these cash transfers. The World
Bank is anticipating a contribution of approximately $230 million towards these cash transfers to cover the poorest
40 percent affected households as identified through the NSER. Out of this commitment, $80 million will be
reallocated from the existing DLIs of the CRISP program and a new DLI linked to effective utilization of the systems
to deliver the emergency cash transfers will be included. A formal request for this reallocation has been received
from the Ministry of Economic Affairs vide their letter No. 2(39)WB-III/21 dated August 29, 2022.

5.        The need to reallocate funds towards the emergency cash transfers coupled with the opportunity to try
out the improvement of BISP’s systems supported through the CRISP program, provide a logical rationale for
restructuring of the program. The $80 million will be reallocated from two of the existing DLI’s that are not time
critical and have considerably less impact on the overall adaptive safety net delivery systems. The new DLI has been
designed to provide an opportunity to test out the partially implemented MELA system through which the NSER has
been updated. This will also establish that with the MELA in place, NSER alone is sufficient to be used for any welfare-
based targeting as there will be no need to use additional exclusion filters as was the case during the COVID
emergency response. The new DLI 12 suggests utilization of the NSER that has been updated through the partially
implemented MELA mechanism for identification of the 40 percent of the poorest families in the affected areas.


 II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

6.      The proposed program restructuring includes;

            (i) One DLI (DLI 12) of $80 million has been added that promotes the utilization of the updated NSER
                  for flood emergency cash transfers. This will allow for the testing of the dynamic update mechanism
                  (MELA) in NSER. As the new DLI is testing out the innovations to the targeting registry (NSER), it is
                  logically placed under RA 1: “Building a Crisis Resilient Delivery System” of the program.
            (ii) Two existing DLIs (DLI 2 and DLI 9) have been restructured to draw the $80 million for the new DLIs.
                  $50 million has been reduced from DLI 2 bringing its total amount from $60 million to $10 million.
                  $30 million is reduced from DLI 9, bringing its total amount down from $35 million to $5 million.
                  There is no change in the targets or the timeframe for the DLI delivery. Similarly, there are no
                  changes in the results associated with these DLIs in the results framework.
            (iii) Verification Protocol for all existing DLIs remain the same and verification protocol for the new DLI
                  has been added. The Annex 3 provides the revised verification protocol table.
            (iv) One new result (Result 16) reflecting the new DLI (DLI 12) has been added in the results framework
                  to fully align the framework with the DLIs.



III. SUMMARY OF ENVIRONMENT AND SOCIAL COMPLIANCE

 7.      There is no impact of proposed restructuring on the Bank’s Environment and Social Assessment. The risk
 rating for Environment stands at “Low” and for Social “Moderate”. There are currently no issues with
 implementation and the restructuring will not impact the TA/IPF component.
