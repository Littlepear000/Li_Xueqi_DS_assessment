4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  Overall, at midterm, the project reached momentum in all project activities. Progress toward achieving the PDO rating is
  maintained.
  Component 1. At midterm, over 10,600 beneficiary apprentices completed their apprenticeship training at the National
  Proficiency Level 1 (NPL 1). These beneficiaries are now going through the formal certification process to confirm their
  training completion and receive their certificates. The beneficiaries came from four regions (Greater Accra Region,
  Ashanti Region, Northen Region and Volta Region). The trades for which the beneficiaries were trained are Automotive,
  Construction, Electronics, Cosmetology/Hairdressing and Garment/Dress making. Eighty-five percent of these
  beneficiaries are female. Currently, 5,044 new beneficiaries have been enrolled into the NPL 1 and 2 programs which is
  expected to continue for 8 to12 months. CTVET has established 12 sector skills bodies to align technical education
  delivery with industry demands and has developed 150 CBT packages (in 30 trades) and accredited 183 institutions across
  the country to provide Competency Based Training.
  Component 2.1. At midterm the GEA has now completed all envisaged beneficiary training. During phases 1 and 2
  training, more than 50,800 beneficiaries of which 307 are persons with disabilities received the basic training; over
  16,600 of which 264 are persons with disabilities received the intermediate-level training, and over 9,350 of which 129
  are persons with disabilities received the advanced level training. Parallel training was held in all districts and regions of
  Ghana, with Ashanti, Greater Accra, Central, Eastern and Northern regions being the top five for which beneficiaries came
  from. Coaching and Mentoring activities are ongoing.
  Component 2.2. At midterm CTVET has competitively awarded over 500 grants to private enterprises across Ghana under
  2 rounds of call for proposals. An estimated 12,670 individuals have already benefitted from grants awarded under 1st
  round of call. CTVET opened the 3rd round of calls for proposals to solicit applications for awarding the final batch of
  competitive grants and received over 1,200 applications. Of these, 414 cleared evaluation and submitted full proposals.
  CTVET has reviewed these 414 proposals and will prepare an evaluation report to be submitted to the GSDF Steering
  Committee for review.
  Component 3. At midterm the Ghana Labour Market Information System (GLMIS) has been competed and its
  operationalization is ongoing in Public Employment Centers (PECs). The Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale
  Zusammenarbeit (GIZ) is spearheading a capacity Development programme for staff of the Labour Department to deliver
  effective job connection services at all PECs, working jointly with the project team. A core training team was set up at the
  Labour Department to sustain its capacity development efforts.
  The Labour Department head office building and sixteen (16) were completed, with seven (7) PECs fully functional with
  Information Communication Technology (ICT) equipment, solar facilities and office furniture. The head office building
  intended for Labour Department has been completed, however the MELR has moved in to use it as its head office and
  relocated the head office of the Labour Department to the old ministry building. The old ministry building requires
  renovation on the ground and first floors and the Bank proposes to support this renovation.

  Progress on independent reviews of Youth Employment and Skills Development Programs has been made with three (3)
  firms contracted to review the National Service Authority (NSA), Youth Employment Agency (YEA) and the National Youth
  Authority (NYA) programs, respectively.
  Component 4. The PCU continued actively interacting with and supporting the implementing and partnering agencies in
  executing their activities, participating in various support meetings, and organizing workshops and other events (virtual
  and in-person). The PCU has commissioned a government mid-term review report to inform on the project
  implementation and make recommendations for improvements in project delivery at midterm, which is expected in
  January 2025.
