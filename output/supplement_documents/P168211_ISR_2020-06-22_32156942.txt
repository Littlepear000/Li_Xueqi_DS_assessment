 Program Development Objectives


  Program Development Objective (from Program Appraisal Document)
  The program development objectives are to improve the financial viability, increase the reliability of electricity supply, and strengthen the
  governance of BT.

 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The Program is expected to become effective by end-June 2020 and Barqi Tojik (BT) is on track to meet the targets for disbursement linked
  indicators for the first semester of 2020. Those are related to restructuring of debt between BT and Ministry of Finance as well as adequate
  purchases of electricity to ensure reliable supply.

  Comments
  The Political and Governance Risk is revised to Moderate. At the Program level, the political decision on the priority of BT financial recovery,
  including the measures to be implemented, were made at the highest level (the President of Tajikistan) and the authorities demonstrated full
  commitment. The operation does not present any threat to political stability of the country and the Program is supported across the entire political
  spectrum and is consistent with the development strategy of the country. There are no major corruption or transparency risks related to expenditures
  considering that all expenditures above threshold (around $10,000) are to be approved by the Supervisory Board of BT and the expenditures need


6/22/2020                                                                                                                          Page 6 of 25
            The World Bank                                                                                          Implementation Status & Results Report

  to be consistent with the annual budgets. The corporate governance is in infancy even though there are elements of good corporate governance in
  its charter as well as the charters of the newly-established electricity distribution and transmission companies. However, additional work will be
  required align BT’s corporate governance structure with principles of good corporate governance, which is underway.
  The Macroeconomic Risk remains Substantial. The slow-down of economic activity caused by COVID-19 has been estimated to negatively
  impact the financial standing of BT. The negative impacts may stem from reduction in disposable income of residential consumers, which would, in
  its turn, reduce the collection rate for billed electricity. There are no direct budget subsidies to BT to mitigate this impact, therefore, financial standing
  of BT may deteriorate. Additionally, there will be indirect impacts from depreciation of Tajik somoni (TJS) vs. US$, which would impact the debt
  service costs of BT. Those will not be passed through to end-user tariffs until the tariff adjustment is done as per new electricity tariff methodology
  (2021). The tariff increase planned for early 2020 will still be carried out as per old methodology. Moreover, currently there is no clarity on the
  sources for filling in the additional cash deficit caused by COVID-19.
  The Sector Strategies and Policies Risk is revised to Moderate. The key activities under the Program have been endorsed at the highest level
  and there is low probability of reversal or change. Specifically, the tariff increase trajectory in the current Government Program for Financial
  Recovery of BT for 2019-2025; revisions to subsidiary agreements between BT and MOF to reduce the debt burden of BT; conversion into BT's
  equity of accumulated fines and penalties; introduction of elements of good-practice corporate governance; and some other measures are already
  underway and the Recipient is making overall good progress in all those directions.
  The Risk of Technical Design of the Program is revised to Moderate. The analytical underpinnings and design of the Program are robust and do
  not create material risks for achievement of development objective. The technical design will remain relevant even considering the impacts of
  COVID-19. The Program would require an update to reflect the impacts of COVID-19 on revenue and cost side of BT and revision of targets
  considering the changes in financial situation.
  The Risk of Institutional Capacity for Implementation and Sustainability is revised to Moderate. BT does not have experience in implementing
  such complex operations, however, its technical, economic and financial departments (which will play a role in implementation) are overall
  adequately staffed with experienced professionals. Moreover, BT would hire external consultants to advise on various issues on as-needed
  basis. The team in Anti-Monopoly Committee (AMC), which will be responsible for review of tariff revision requests filed by BT and submission of a
  recommendation to the Government on tariff approval, has just been formed and it does not have experience with the tariff-setting and review
  consistent with the requirements of the new methodology. Capacity-building support will be provided by some development partners, including
  analytical guidance from the Bank. There are no material risks for sustainability of Program results because institutional/legal and regulatory
  mechanisms are being put in place to ensure sustainability of achievements under the Program.
  The Fiduciary Risk remains High. The findings on financial management risks include, but not limited to: (i) limited capacities of the FM/accounting
  staff at BT, in particular at regional level; (ii) weak internal controls, and (iii) lack of good governance practice at BT. The findings on procurement
  risks indicate the following key weaknesses: (i) use of incomplete bidding documents, unclear qualifications and technical requirements, evaluation
  criteria and contract conditions; (ii) lack of contract management mechanism including contract delivery quality and cost controls; and (iii) lack of
  clarity and transparency on matters related to filing of complaints, their processing, and decisions made. The fiduciary risks will be mitigated through
  improved capacity building of BT on accounting and auditing, consistent implementation of good-practice corporate governance principles, and
  improvements to be made to standard procurement documents. Implementation of those mitigation measures is not a straightforward task and thus
  the fiduciary risk remains High and the risk rating will be revisited once the progress is reviewed by the Bank in Dec 2020.
  The Environmental and Social Risk remains Substantial. Activities proposed under Results Area 2 of the PforR Program could lead to
  substantial environmental risks and effects. BT, as the main implementing agency, has limited in-house capacity for environmental due diligence. BT
  developed and adopted corporate level environmental policy in 2006. However, project level environmental management including procedures and
  practices to manage hazardous waste remains weak. The proposal to create power transmission and distribution companies by unbundling of BT
  (responsible for power generation) may further aggravate the project-level environmental management requirements unless new companies adopt
  and implement environmental policy adopted by BT. Environmental risk rating for PforR Program is substantial based on the capacity assessment of
  BT. The environmental risk would be mitigated through adoption of corporate environmental policy and guidelines for carrying out rehabilitation
  works; hiring of additional environmental staff; and training activities. The tariff increases, supported under the Program, would have impacts on
  socially vulnerable consumers and currently there are no mechanisms in place to mitigate those impacts. The Government completed the roll-out of
  Targeted Social Assistance (TSA) program to cover the remaining regions, but there is no additional allocation to mitigate the impact on the poor
  from tariff increases. In parallel, the government counterparts are working on designing lifeline tariff mechanism.
  Stakeholder Risk is revised to Moderate. The tariff increase for 2020 was postponed until early 2021 due to COVID-19 impacts. Therefore, the
  civil society and the private sector are not likely to object to the Program objectives and implementation in 2020. MEWR and BT are currently
  finalizing preparation of the public communication strategy on tariffs and broader energy issues, which, once implementation commences in 2021,
  should help to mitigate the impacts the stakeholder risks stemming from tariff increases.
  Other risk: BT Export Revenue Risk remains Substantial. The BT export revenues after 2022 would to large extent depend on the cash received
  from exports under CASA-1000 project. Specifically, BT is expected to earn about US$140 million from exports of energy under CASA-1000. As of
  now, the realistic commissioning date of CASA-1000 is 2023 compared to 2022 at the time of appraisal of the PforR. Moreover, there is no clarity on
  expansion of exports to Uzbekistan. MEWR has been increasingly more involved to provide the required guidance and support in resolving CASA-
  1000 related issues on Tajikistan part of the project. However, parts of the project implemented by other participating countries are beyond
  Tajikistan's control. MEWR is also closely collaborating with Central Asian counterparts to complete the required activities to enable re-connection of
  Tajikistan with Central Asian Power System network, which should allow to increase exports to Uzbekistan and, potentially, Kazakhstan.
  Other risk: The Risk of Rogun HPP Impact on Financial Standing of BT is revised to Moderate. The main issue would be to ensure that BT (or
  the newly-crated power distribution company) does not purchase more electricity from Rogun HPP than justified from domestic and export demand
  perspective, and the cost of energy from Rogun HPP, which will be supplied to domestic market, is fully reflected in the end-user tariff. Therefore,
  BT’s Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) with Rogun HPP would need to be based on realistic projection of domestic electricity demand as well as
  availability of firm and long-term export commitments. For 2020, the amount of electricity to be purchased by BT from Rogun HPP is consistent with
  the requirements of domestic demand. The PPA between Rogun HPP and BT would be revised in fall of 2020. The new tariff methodology will be

  operationalized in early 2021 and therefore the cost of energy from Rogun HPP will be factored into all tariff increases to take place starting from
  2022.
