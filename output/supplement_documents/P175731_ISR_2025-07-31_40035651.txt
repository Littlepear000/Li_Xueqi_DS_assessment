4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  Overall project implementation has improved since the last ISR, mostly driven by the progress of the Mozambique
  component. Zambia and Madagascar continue to face significant implementation challenges, with disbursement rates
  only at 13%. STP more than doubled its disbursement (now at 35%), SADC continues on track (at 42%), and Malawi and
  Comoros are at the higher end (70% and 97.7%, respectively). 
  The most significant activities implemented this calendar year (since last ISR) include:
  In Mozambique, data collection of the Agricultural Census was completed, with major surveys like the Household Budget
  Survey and Firm Census scheduled to start in July 2025. Comoros successfully finalized several core statistical outputs—
  including the Statistical Business Register, Living Standards Survey, Consumer Price Index (CPI), and National Accounts
  rebasing. Malawi has made significant progress under the data collection component. By July 2025, data collection for all
  key surveys supported by the project had been completed, including the Demographic and Health Survey (DHS), Labor
  Force Survey (LFS), Agricultural Census, Integrated Household Survey, CPI, and Producer Price Index (PPI). The country has
  also initiated the transition of the CPI from paper to CAPI and is beginning to implement a plan for producing quarterly
  National Accounts. In addition, the update of the National Statistical System (NSS) has been finalized, and progress has
  been made on updating the website and upgrading equipment such as laptops and desktops. The project is preparing a
  minor restructuring to align financing categories. Madagascar is advancing with support from AFRISTAT on the economic
  census and may co-finance a social registry census, potentially doubling disbursement. Zambia, though delayed, is
  accelerating with the upcoming economic census and targeted TA support. São Tomé and Príncipe (STP) is progressing
  steadily but faces a financing gap due to cost escalations.
