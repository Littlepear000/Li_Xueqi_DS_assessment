B. Introduction and Context
1.      Background. The Socio-Economic Resilience Strengthening Project (SERSP) is one of three operations financed by
the International Development Association (IDA) 18 Risk Mitigation Regime (RMR) to support Tajikistan’s Resilience
Strengthening Program, which aims to address risks associated with fragility, conflict, and violence. SERSP was approved
June 20, 2019, for an IDA grant of US$37 million, and became effective July 8, 2020. The project is implemented by the
National Social Investment Fund of Tajikistan (NSIFT).
2.      Geographic coverage. The project operates in four districts in the Khatlon region (Kulyob, Panj, Hamadoni, and
Farkhor) and in three districts in GBAO (Vanj, Ishkoshim, and Shugnan). The target districts were selected for having a
shared border with Afghanistan, a relatively large population, a combination of rural and urban areas, and limited coverage
by other donor interventions. Two additional districts, Shamsiddin Shohin in Khatlon province and Darvoz in GBAO
province, were added at restructuring.

3.      Project restructuring. The project was restructured once in May 2023. The restructuring involved: (i) reallocation
of US$4.7 million to Subcomponent 1a and US$0.2 million to Subcomponent 1b to expand the subcomponents’ activities
to two new districts, Shamsiddin Shohin in Khatlon region, and Darvoz on in Gorno-Badakhshan Autonomous Oblast
(GBAO) region ; (ii) revising the target for an intermediate-level indicator related to Subcomponent 2b; (iii) extending the
project’s closing date by 18 months from June 30, 2024 to December 31, 2025; and (iv) aligning the results indicators’ end
targets with the project’s new closing date. In addition, the restructuring removed a legal covenant for the establishment
of a Project Steering Committee (SC).
4.       PDO and implementation progress ratings. The project’s progress towards achievement of the PDO and its
implementation progress are currently rated as Moderately Satisfactory, reflecting the steady and satisfactory progress in
implementing project activities. The project is in its last year of implementation, with US$32.48 million (91%) disbursed and
the remaining US$3.28 million is committed to be disbursed by December 31, 2025, based on current disbursement
schedule. Three of the four PDO results indicators already exceed targets. PDO3 “percentage of youth beneficiaries who
continue their livelihood activities after receiving livelihood tools and equipment�? will only be measured after the related
livelihood support activities have been completed.
5.      Implementation progress of SERSP is summarized below:
a)       Component 1: Inclusive Local Development Initiatives: Social mobilization for the original 7 districts, 44 jamoats in
GBAO and Khatlon resulted in the prioritization of 193 subprojects. Of these, 153 are completed and the remaining 40
subprojects are scheduled to be completed by July 2025. In the two districts (Sh. Shohin and Darvoz) added during the
restructuring, 15 subprojects are under implementation and are expected to be completed by September 2025. Around
75,000 community members (56% women) across 55 jamoats and 507 villages were mobilized, leading to formulation of
Village and Jamoat Development Plans, as well as village-led prioritization of 208 social infrastructure subprojects. Other
donors have supported 53 subprojects that communities identified in village and jamoat development plans, but were not
selected for SERSP funding. Communities have expressed high levels of satisfaction with the project’s decision-making
processes via the project’s social audits. Under sub-component 1c, NSIFT completed the renovation and upgrading of 17
youth facilities, which have been utilized to provide youth-inclusive services under Component 2 and handed the facilities
over to the Ministry of Labor, Migration and Employment and the Ministry of Education and Science.
b)       Component 2: Youth Inclusion and Livelihoods: Under sub-component 2a, targets for all youth trainings have been
met (digital literacy, Mind Masters, Upshift, healthy lifestyle, psychosocial support). Under subcomponent 2b: Youth
Livelihoods, an initial cohort of 1,255 beneficiaries (47% women) received training, prepared basic business plans, and
received tools and equipment, and are carrying out livelihood activities with the support of mentors. Monitoring indicates
that beneficiaries are utilizing equipment and making value chain connections with bigger businesses and creating local
jobs. An additional cohort of 1,145 beneficiaries to receive tools has been identified from youth who attended initial
training, but did not receive equipment, and from youth in the two new districts.
c)       Component 3: Project Management and Monitoring and Evaluation: NSIFT’s SERSP project implementation unit is
adequately managing its coordination and supervision, procurement, financial management, monitoring and evaluation,
and communications activities, including oversight of its beneficiary feedback and grievance redress mechanisms. NSIFT
has established a management information system (MIS) that is regularly updated and tracking project activities and
results. It has also developed a publicly accessible, functional interactive map with information on SERSP subprojects across
the target jamoats, plus information on top priority investments identified by participating jamoats and villages but not yet
funded. The baseline survey was completed in September 2021 and the project endline survey is planned for 2025.
6.       Rationale for Additional Financing. In August 2024, the Ministry of Finance (MoF) submitted a request to the World
Bank for an Additional Financing (AF) to expand SERSP social infrastructure and livelihood opportunities. The proposed AF
will extend the coverage of SERSP’s community-driven investment and livelihoods approach to additional districts and

jamoats in the Rogun Hydropower Plant (HPP)1 affected area and in other disadvantaged rural areas in the country. This
will expand the Government’s successful experience with community-driven development (CDD), as SERSP and the CASA-
1000 Community Support Project (CASA-1000 CSP),2 also implemented by NSIFT, have demonstrated significant results at
the community level.
7.        The AF is designed to complement the Sustainable Financing for Rogun Hydropower Project. First, it will complement
the livelihood restoration activities included in component 3 of the Rogun Project by further enhancing livelihood opportunities
in the project area communities. While the government is providing livelihood restoration support to project-affected persons
(PAPs) who are resettled, livelihood restoration takes time and significant resources. Stakeholders have requested additional
funding and technical support to set up small businesses as well as small local infrastructure investments. The AF will support
Rogun HPP host villages and jamoats and other communities with additional infrastructure investments, livelihood training
and technical support for individuals, groups and small businesses. Second, the Government can use SERSP AF resources to
supplement the limited revenues for Rogun HPP benefit sharing that will be available in the early years of Rogun construction.
The Rogun HPP design provides for the establishment of a Benefit Sharing Program (BSP) which will be financed through a
portion (increasing from 3% to 5% based on achievement of dam construction milestones) of the revenue from the sale of
electricity generated by the HPP. These revenue streams are limited in the initial years, and are expected to scale up
gradually, as all turbines become operational (expected 2033) and the reservoir reaches full supply level (expected 2039). A
third rationale is that the AF project will strengthen the institutional capacity of target jamoats to use a participatory,
community-led approach to plan, budget, and implement local investments and services. This in turn will help them
effectively utilize revenues from future Rogun HPP benefit sharing as well as from other sources. It will also build a strong
sense of ownership on the part of communities for the investments that they select for financing and demonstrate enhanced
responsiveness on the part of government to the prioritized needs of communities.
8.        An additional rationale is the use of the CDD approach for the BSP is one of the top options emerging in the ongoing
process to design the Rogun BSP – although a final decision on the BSP design will not be made until after the AF is approved.
Globally, CDD approaches are used for benefit sharing for large infrastructure projects in multiple countries. This is in part
due to built-in stakeholder engagement and feedback mechanisms that enhance participation, include women, youth and
vulnerable groups, and in turn build local ownership and strengthen risk management. In Tajikistan, the CASA-1000
Community Support Project, also implemented by NSIFT, has successfully used a CDD approach to provide infrastructure and
other services to communities in the CASA-1000 electricity transmission corridor before and during construction with 97% of
beneficiaries reporting that they consider the CSP an effective response to the local impacts of CASA-1000. This CDD
experience is one of the options being considered in the ongoing BSP design process. The design of AF is intended to be
flexible, to be adapted to the BSP approach that will eventually be approved by the Government.
9.       The AF will also align with priority Government objectives for improving the role of public institutions in economic
development, alleviation of social and gender imbalances, and adaptation to climate change, and WB CPF objectives to
enhance people’s knowledge and skills, address regressive gender norms, promote livelihood opportunities for women in
rural settings, enhance public sector transparency and accountability, and ensure affordable, climate resilient energy. Finally,
it will help to establish a platform for other financiers to support, via co-financing or other means, Rogun BSP and place-
based, community-led infrastructure and livelihoods.

10.        The proposed AF will expand SERSP geographic coverage and adjust selected activities to incorporate lessons
learned from SERSP and CASA1000 CSP. Coverage will expand to include Rogun HPP-affected areas and other
disadvantaged jamoats in the country. Key lessons from SERSP and CSP include: (i) the CDD methodology has proven to be
effective in the rural Tajikistan context at mobilizing rural communities to participate in a bottom up and inclusive process
to select and implement local infrastructure investments over 75% of target communities report improved access to social
and economic infrastructure based on CDD mobilization and local investments; (ii) the CDD methodology can provide an
effective instrument in Tajikistan to deliver benefit sharing for large infrastructure projects, as reflected in CSP results on
percentage of beneficiaries who report that CSP is an effective response to the local impacts of the CASA-1000 transmission
line; (iii) initial livelihoods support has delivered expected results, but improvements in selectivity of local livelihood streams,
provision of mentoring and coaching support, and linking components 1 & 2 is needed to better meet strong demand for
local livelihoods and jobs support.

 D. Project Description
13.     Expansion of coverage and allocation. The AF will expand SERSP coverage to include Rogun-affected jamoats (70%
of AF community investments) as well as disadvantaged jamoats (the remaining 30%) in other parts of the country, in
agreement with the Government. In the Rogun-affected area, the AF will cover jamoats in the three districts and two cities
(Faizobad, Nurobad, and Danghara districts, Rogun and Tursunzoda cities) that include resettlement villages under the
second phase of resettlement in the Rogun HPP – 5 jamoats and Darband small town with 8 resettlement villages and an
additional 27 jamoats across the five districts. In addition to the Rogun affected area, two additional districts will be selected
from the two poorest regions of the country (Khatlon and GBAO), and selection of target districts will be based on criteria
such as population, investments per capita, unemployment . These criteria, which will be weighted, will provide a
mechanism to select disadvantaged districts with significant needs, but not exclusively the poorest or most remote. The
selection criteria for these districts, including weighting scores for each criterion, developed by the Government will be
described in POM.

14.      Component 1: Inclusive Local Development Initiatives (US$17.5 million IDA). The AF will provide subgrants to
Rogun-affected and other jamoats to finance local-level, climate-resilient, socio-economic infrastructure that addresses
priorities identified through two cycles of a participatory village needs assessment and decision-making process. It will
strengthen the capacity of jamoat administrations and community-level institutions, including mahalla committees, to
engage with citizens and deliver services efficiently, fairly, and in a way that responds to the needs of citizens, including
youth and women. This component will finance public goods, civil works, community mobilization and capacity building
support for community members and local government staff.
15.       Subcomponent 1a: Subgrants for Local Development Initiatives (US$15.5 million IDA). The AF will build on the
original project design to support selected subprojects to improve local living conditions. Based on a village-level participatory
needs assessment under subcomponent 1b, this subcomponent will support investments in basic, climate-resilient
infrastructure for targeted rural villages and jamoats, such as rehabilitation or construction of kindergartens, schools, local
health clinics, energy generation and distribution, local transportation infrastructure and rehabilitation of village-level water
supply and sanitation systems, on-farm irrigation systems. Eligible subprojects may also include economic infrastructure such
as small-scale processing and storage facilities for horticultural and other products. The types of work may involve new
construction, rehabilitation or maintenance. It may also include investments for nature-based tourism infrastructure or the
restoration of degraded landscapes, involving nature-based solutions such as reforestation with local or drought-resistant
species, nurseries, plantations and related infrastructure. Subprojects will integrate renewable energy and energy efficiency
enhancements where feasible as well as support investments that strengthen resilience against climate-related impacts.
Anticipated climate smart investments are described in the menu of climate adaptation and mitigation measures attached.
Contractors for the infrastructure subprojects will be encouraged to employ local youth and returning migrants with relevant
skills. The updated Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMF) and the Project Operational Manual (POM),
including the subgrants operations manual (SGM), will include a list of ineligible investments.
16.      Subgrant allocation. Subgrants under this Sub-component will only be allocated at the jamoat level. The allocation
will be made per jamoat for social and economic investments and will average about US$300,000. The funding in the
participating districts will depend on the number of jamoats and vary between US$0.60-US$2.7 million. Each jamoat will
receive investment but it will vary up to US$500,000 per jamoat, depending on evaluation and scoring of jamoat proposals.
Jamoat proposals will be scored and ranked based on the following criteria: (i) designated village for hosting Rogun HPP
project-affected people (PAPs) (for the five Rogun-affected districts only); (ii) quality/inclusive participation process of
community members in local development/decision making; (iii) priorities identified by women. Detailed process for
scoring and selecting jamoat subprojects will be defined in the POM. Within jamoats, there will be a process for villages
to apply for single-village subgrants or for multiple villages to apply for larger projects.

17.      Selection of subprojects and implementation. At the jamoat level, village subprojects will be selected based on
selection criteria defined in the POM. There will be two cycles of subgrants in AF project years 1-2 for each participating
jamoat with investments in socio-economic infrastructure, facilities, and services. Prior to the funding of subprojects, each
village will provide evidence that the community mobilization process and decisions regarding the subprojects have been
properly undertaken; that the proposed subprojects contribute to poverty reduction; and that the youth and gender
equity goals have been included in the jamoat development plans. NSIFT will also pilot and seek to scale up a process to
consider climate risks in the jamoat development plans and note in the indicative investment menu which investments
respond to different climate risks. Proposed subprojects, including Operation and Maintenance (O&M) plans, should be
technically viable according to a checklist, ensure donor coordination and alignment, and demonstrate their sustainability.
The SGM will detail the selection criteria, including weighting toward the needs of youth and women living in vulnerable
areas.

18.      Subcomponent 1b: Community and Youth Mobilization, Social Accountability, and Local Governance Capacity
Building (US$2.0 million IDA). This subcomponent will continue to finance mobilization and capacity building activities to
ensure that: (i) communities, particularly women, youth, and the vulnerable, are actively engaged in selecting poverty-
focused project interventions; (ii) village and jamoat committees use participatory, transparent approaches to identify,
implement and monitor local development projects; (iii) village and jamoats improve their capabilities to prepare, raise funds
for, and implement local development plans reflecting community priorities. The subcomponent will support
institutionalization of CDD via an increased focus on strengthening capacity of local government bodies (jamoat
administration) to incorporate participatory and inclusive practices in their own processes. It will mobilize jamoats and
community members to improve: (i) local economic development, including preparation and implementation of the local
development plans and market assessments; (ii) climate resilience by raising awareness of climate change risks, and of
investments that support adaptation and mitigation; (iii) women’s economic empowerment, gender grassroots leade rship
and approaches to combat gender-based violence; (iv) participatory, inclusive, responsive and accountable local planning,
project implementation, and monitoring; and (v) social accountability processes. In addition, if needed this subcomponent
will include awareness raising and outreach to inform communities about the BSP support; and capacity strengthening of
jamoats to potentially manage the future BSP and related communication, depending upon final BSP design.
19.      Mobilization will also identify and support climate ambassadors to raise community awareness of climate impacts
and risks, and to consider investments and livelihoods activities that contribute to local climate mitigation and adaptation
– linked to local planning processes. Climate ambassadors will be mobilized to conduct energy efficiency campaigns, and
to raise awareness of local water and agriculture investments and technology that can enhance resilience to climate risks
(also to be included in the project’s investment menu).
20.       Component 2: Inclusive livelihoods (US$5.0 million IDA). This component is being revised and expanded to reflect
lessons from the parent project and good practices identified from consultations with other livelihood support programs
supported by the World Bank and other development partners in Tajikistan. The key changes in response to lessons include:
(a) expanding the livelihood beneficiaries beyond youth to also include women, Rogun affected communities, returning
migrants and other vulnerable groups, (b) deepening support to livelihood groups supported through the project to enhance
sustainability of enterprises set up by the beneficiaries, (c) continuing and deepening mentoring and coaching support to
beneficiaries, (d) extending support to existing micro and small enterprises to create more economic activity and jobs in the
selected districts and (e) providing enhanced implementation support to NSIFT and selected Implementing Partner(s) to
address capacity gaps in the market and to ensure beneficiaries receive higher quality technical inputs (e.g. trainings,
mentoring, coaching, monitoring of results) through the project. The activities under this component are designed to be
flexible and to complement the Government of Tajikistan’s livelihood restoration support for Rogun-affected communities
as this is detailed and rolled out. These activities will coincide with activities under a grant from the Japan Social Development
Fund (JSDF) to support women’s economic empowerment in Tajikistan. The JSDF grant funded project and the AF have
geographical overlap and are being designed in parallel, so that there is potential for JSDF piloting of alternative livelihood
models, such as self-help-saving groups, to feed into AF implementation.
21.     Subcomponent 2a: Youth inclusive services. The youth trainings under parent project will be completed and will not
be continued under the Additional Financing. Key modules from successful trainings will be integrated in the trainings
provided under component 2b.
22.      Subcomponent 2b: Support to individual and group livelihoods (US$3.5 million IDA). This subcomponent is a
continuation from the parent project with refinements. It will provide new or existing informal entrepreneurs (individuals,
common interest groups) with knowledge, finance, networks and technical support to initiate or expand livelihood activities
in sectors identified through market assessments. This subcomponent will target community members, particularly women
(targeting will be done by using databases of organizations that identified women entrepreneurs in targeted districts), youth,
returning migrants and Rogun affected communities who don’t qualify for more capital-intensive entrepreneurship support.

The AF will identify and provide 7,000 beneficiaries with entrepreneurship and business development trainings. These
beneficiaries will also get hands on support to develop business plans (as individuals or common interest groups). The project
will select and support the top 1,600 applicants with tools and equipment in line with their business plans. Individual
beneficiaries can receive tools and equipment up to US$2,000 and groups up to US$10,000. Following the selection of
business plans, the beneficiaries will also complete additional trainings on topics such as marketing, financial literacy, digital
business tools, climate change and group cohesion. These training programs will also be revised to include successful modules
from component 2a. In addition to the he tools and equipment beneficiaries will also be provided coaching and mentoring
for at least 12 months that will include support with market linkages, registration, financial management, amongst others.
23.       Subcomponent 2c: Support to micro and small enterprises (MSEs) (US$ 1.5 million IDA). This subcomponent is new.
It will support existing and registered MSEs in target districts as business partners to improve local economic opportunities,
create value chain linkages with downstream producers (such as those supported under subcomponent 2b) and generate
new jobs for women, youth, returning migrants and other beneficiaries. This subcomponent will identify and support 100
MSEs through provision of core business expansion trainings. Following completion of these trainings, the MSEs will be invited
to submit a business plan to secure grants from the project. These MSEs will also get hands on support to develop business
plans. The selection of MSEs for receiving grants will be competitive and on a rolling basis. The selection criterion will be
outlined in the POM and will include feasibility of the business plan in the selected sector and value chain, potential to employ
more people, potential to create value chain linkages with smaller vendors and integration of digital and climate tools. This
will enhance the ability of the businesses to conduct a climate risk assessment, enabling proactive identification of
vulnerabilities and the integration of adaptive measures, ultimately strengthening resilience and ensuring sustainable
growth. The project will support approximately 50 business plans from MSEs with individual grants up to $20,000 each that
can finance tools, equipment, nature-based solutions and infrastructure, consultancies and technical assistance needs. The
coaching and mentoring will be tailored and include support with business planning, registration, market linkages, financial
management, support MSEs in accessing credit and loan financing and other topics.
24.     Component 3. Project Management, Monitoring & Evaluation (US$2.5 million IDA). This component will be
expanded to cover new target areas. In addition to supporting overall project management, it will support NSIFT’s alignment
with the BSP adopted by the government if applicable. This component will also support learning between component 2 and
the JSDF on women’s economic empowerment being prepared for Tajikistan in FY 2025-26. This component will also cover
repair and maintenance costs for the sanitary investments of the original project.

Summary of Screening of Environmental and Social Risks and Impacts
The environmental and social risks are rated Moderate and Substantial, respectively, resulting in an overall Substantial'
risk rating. Socially, the project is expected to have positive impacts, particularly for youth and women. Various risks
exist, both external and internal, with key concerns around 'exclusion' due to geographic, socio-economic, and gender
factors. Mitigation measures have been outlined, dependent on the performance of NGOs providing social
intermediation services during implementation. For land acquisition, the project will use existing buildings or unused
public land, with a Resettlement Policy Framework (RPF) in place as a backup. The environmental risk is Moderate as
the impacts are expected to be small to medium scale and the associated environmental impacts are expected to be
temporary, predictable, and easily mitigable. The types of work involve new construction, improvement, upgrading,
