I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING
A. Background
1 The approval of the Division Director is being sought to proceed with a Level Two Restructuring of the Côte d’Ivoire Productive Social Safety Nets System Strengthening Program (PSSN, P175594, IDA credit CI-7109). The
Program for Results (PforR) was approved on May 19, 2022, in the amount of a EUR 180.3 million (US$200 million)
IDA credit (CI-7109) and a US$5 million grant from the Global Financing Facility (GFF), bringing total financing to
US$205 million and with a total Program cost of US$385.5 million, including an Investment Project Financing (IPF)
component of US$12.5 million, of which the IDA-financed portion is EUR 6.5 million. The PforR became effective on December 6, 2022. A first restructuring (RES01184) was approved on February 2, 2026.1 It aimed to better align results with implementation realities and evolving country priorities. This second proposed restructuring was recommended as a conclusion of the June 2026 supervision mission, and has been requested by the Government of Côte d’Ivoire in a letter dated 23 July 2026 to reallocate EUR 3.15 million from a partially achieved Disbursement
Linked Result (DLR) to two remaining DLRs whose formulations are revised and scope or targets adjusted accordingly.
B. Program Status
2 The PSSN PforR is progressing satisfactorily toward achieving the program development objective (PDO), and all implementation ratings have been Satisfactory and Moderately Satisfactory since its inception. Following the
MTR, completed in June 2025, the Program was rated Satisfactory – a rating confirmed by the two following supervision missions of December 2025 and June 2026. Disbursements are on schedule, with rates of 93.6 percent from IDA-71090 and 75.4 percent from TF-B8338 as of June 30, 2026. 2 Out of a total of 31 DLRs, 17 were fully achieved and 1 partially achieved, for a total of EUR 121.68 million. Disbursements include EUR 43.3 million advance, processed January 23, 2023, to be recovered through the achievement of remaining DLRs in 2026. The verification of four DLRs is planned for the third quarter of 2026, while the verification of the other nine DLRs is scheduled for the fourth quarter of 2026, for an additional total of EUR 32.72 million. The IDA-financed IPF component, which provides necessary complementary technical assistance, has disbursed EUR 3.84 out of EUR
6.5 million available (59 percent). This is in line with expectations, as an uptake in evaluations, innovations, and analytical endeavors are expected before the end of the year.
3 Most PDO indicators are progressing well towards their targets, with visible advances along both program objectives. The Program’s six PDO indicators contribute to two objectives and display steady progress:
a. Increasing access of poor and vulnerable households to the safety nets programs, through the flagship
Productive Cash Transfers Program (Programme des Transferts Monetaires Productifs, PTMP) and the non-contributive regime of the universal health coverage (Couverture Maladie Universelle, CMU), and over time to a mechanism covering them in the case of shocks. The main indicator related to the first objective (Beneficiaries of social safety net programs), which represents one of the WBG Outcomes and
WBG Scorecard Target, has already surpassed its end target by 18 percent. Its gender disaggregation is also above target, contributing to the WBG Gender Strategy Targets. The remaining two indicators linked to the first objective (Beneficiaries of RAM-CMU in the regions where social centers pilot takes place and Shock response framework defined and operationalized) are advancing.

C. Rationale for restructuring
6 The proposed restructuring aims to reallocate resources from a partially achieved DLR to other key results.
Specifically, the restructuring reprograms EUR 3.15 million from partially achieved DLR 4.3 (Quarterly payment cycles made on time in Year 4 to all active beneficiaries as stipulated in the PTMP Operations Manual). Treasury-
related liquidity constraints affected the timely delivery of one of the 2025 quarterly transfers to a subset of beneficiaries (Cohorts 7 and 8), resulting in the partial achievement of DLR 4.3 and leaving EUR 3.15 million in unallocated DLR funds. This delay (of about 1.5 months) was beyond the control of the implementing actors and was linked to temporary liquidity constraints during the sensitive pre-election period in October 2025. This was the first such delay since the start of the Program, which continues to show adequate measures to prevent recurrence. The delay was due to exceptional circumstances, and its impact was limited to about 55,000 of the
300,000 beneficiaries. The Program continues to have strong mechanisms in place to ensure regular and timely payments. Rather than leaving these funds undisbursed, the proposed restructuring reallocates them to existing
DLRs to achieve even greater development impact by backing results the Program is already exceeding, and strengthen the Program's trajectory heading into the Additional Financing (AF) preparation period, as described below.
7 Accordingly, the restructuring reallocates undisbursed DLR 4.3 funds to DLI 7 (Enhanced access to economic inclusion activities provided by the PTMP for beneficiaries), where the Program can sustain higher ambitions in the remaining five months of implementation. The reallocation reinforces two complementary directions: (i)
deepening economic resilience through community-based associations, where the Program has shown potential to exceed its targets; and (ii) advancing digital financial inclusion as an innovation track that will inform and be scaled up under the proposed AF.
a. The restructuring proposes allocating part of the reprogrammed resources (EUR 1.8 million) to DLR 7.4 (80 percent of cohort 6 villages and neighborhoods have established at least one savings and loan associations), reflecting the Program's broader goal of deepening financial inclusion and economic

Communautaire, AVEC). The PTMP has demonstrated strong performance in training and supporting beneficiaries to establish these associations: (i) DLR 7.3, which targeted the establishment of at least one

This performance reflects strong community ownership of the approach and confirms its relevance as a mechanism to promote financial inclusion, mutual support, and greater economic resilience at the local level. In light of these results, and based on the Program's demonstrated capacity not only to meet but to exceed its current target, the proposed restructuring would increase the allocation to
DLR 7.4, expected to be verified in September 2026, and revise its target upward from 80 to 90 percent.
b. The restructuring further proposes allocating the remaining reprogrammed resources (EUR 1.35 million) to
DLR 7.5 (A digital financial inclusion pilot is implemented and rigorously evaluated), with increased ambition.
It would extend the result beyond pilot implementation and evaluation towards supporting preparation for its scale-up as an integrated part of the PTMP and a building block for the proposed AF. Digital financial inclusion, leveraging mobile money platforms, is a key next step in deepening beneficiaries' access to capital, complementing the community-based approach supported under DLR 7.4. Since launching the pilot in
March 2026 across 80 localities and 2,000 PTMP beneficiaries, the implementation unit (Bureau de
Coordination des Programmes des Filets Sociaux, BCPFS), in collaboration with Orange Money and Orange
Bank, has been generating operational evidence to inform the design of a more comprehensive package for scale-up during the 2027-2030 period. This would combine digital savings and credit-eligibility pathways with financial and digital literacy support and behavioral interventions to promote active account use. To date, 1,500 beneficiaries have been trained using five new training modules, with field support from the mobile money operator's agents. Quantitative monitoring, complemented by a qualitative mid-line field assessment, will help validate results, assess uptake constraints, refine the intervention, and inform potential partnership arrangements required for scale-up under the proposed AF. In this context, the restructuring proposes to revise DLR 7.5 to increase the allocation and include preparation of a roadmap for scale-up, drawing on lessons learned from pilot implementation, to ensure a well-sequenced transition into the next phase of digital financial inclusion interventions under the AF.
