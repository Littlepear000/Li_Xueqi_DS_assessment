 Implementation Status and Key Decisions


  This ISR is issued following the fifth ISM held on August 1- 12, 2022 and reflects the progress made and results attained since the last ISM in
  January 2022.

  The project implementation during the last six continued to be affected by multiple challenges, including: a) severe and prolonged drought that
  affected three of the six project regions, triggering an emergency response worth US$24.9 million from the project which diverted the attentions of
  all implementing agencies; b) conflicts in Afar, Oromiya, and Benishangul-Gumuz regions continued to interrupt access to 22 project woredas by
  project staff and contractors; and c) material shortages and price inflation affected cement, reinforcement iron bars, and fuel in the rural project
  areas. The material shortages also delayed ongoing contracts and impeded bids for new activities, particularly under Component 1.

  Despite these challenges, the project made progress on implementation of key activities. The Ministry of Irrigation and Lowlands (MILLs) has
  successfully assumed oversight responsibility of the project from the Ministry of Peace (MoP), and all PCUs continued to effectively support the
  project implementation at federal, regional, and woreda levels, with the exception of some conflict affected woredas.

  Key project achievements against the Intermediate result Indicators (IRI) during the reporting period include: a) completion of 27 Public Economic
  Investment (PEI) subprojects bringing the cumulative achievements to 33 subprojects, thus achieving 12.7 percent of the End of Project (EoP)
  target, b): the number of beneficiaries engaged on diversified livelihood activities increased from 3,733 (January 2022) to 24,891, thus achieving
  12.45 percent of EoP target; c) access to rural financial services was provided to additional 29,146 people, bringing the cumulative achievement to
  39,624 people, which is about 79.28 percent of EoP target; d) the number of farmers adopting improved agricultural technologies and practices
  increased from 5,191 in January 2022 to 141,040, exceeding the EoP target by 35.9 percent (to be further verified during the MTR); e)
  establishment of three productive alliances between producers (pastoral and agropastoral communities) and traders and processors to enhance
  commercialization of crop and livestock products, bringing the cumulative achievement to 6 (20 percent of EoP target); f) completion of subprojects
  such as primary school, health posts, water points, veterinary clinics etc., bringing the cumulative achievement to 646 subprojects (92 percent of
  EoP target), of which 555 subprojects are operational; and g) the number of direct project beneficiaries increased by 292,191, bringing the
  cumulative achievement to 1,016,186 (40.65 percent of the EoP target). Female and youth beneficiaries account for 41 and 20 percent respectively.
  In addition, the PCU completed the setup and roll out of the project MIS system, geo-tagged 834 subprojects using the GEMS, established and
  staffed regional PCUs for the newly established Southwest region, and enrolled three new woredas to the project in Benshangul Gumuz region.

  Overall, the implementation progress during the reporting period has been encouraging and the rating is maintained at Moderately Satisfactory.
  However, the mission identified some activities that have not performed as planned. The annual budget utilization rate for EFY14 was only 45
  percent, mainly due to the slow progress under component one.

  The World Bank team and the project coordination unit (PCU) agreed on a number of actions to further speed up implementation of project activities
  for EFY15, including to ; a) develop a clear and feasible action plan to accelerate implementation of all planned activities under Component 1,
  particularly on Subcomponent 1.2 (Public Economic Investment (PEI) subprojects, b) prepare and submit the project restructuring paper to close the
  financing gap of US$125 million, c) finalize preparations for the mid-term review for December 2022 and submit the internal and independent MTR
  reports, d) conduct an internal financial audit of Afar RPCU and provide a status update and supporting documents for the remaining outstanding
  financial audit findings of July 7, 2021, e) develop and rollout a robust national rangeland monitoring system before end of EFY 15, f) promote
  privatization and commercialization of fodder production and management activities for sustainability, g) fill the vacant procurement positions in the
  Afar and Somali RPCUs and prepare an action plan to implement the recommendations from the recent procurement audit findings and h) take
  actions to effectively address to the implementation challenges in the conflict affected three woredas (Dembe, Sedal and Mao-Komo) of
  Benshangul-Gumuz region, where project operation has been suspended for months.

  Progress towards attaining PDO: Despite the multiple challenges encountered since its inception, the project has been progressing well towards
  achieving the PDO. Three of the four PDO indicators have progressed since the last mission. The number of people with improved access to social
  and economic services is now 68.2 percent of the EoP target. The area under sustainable land management practices and farmers reached with
  improved agricultural services and assets are 35 and 25.8 percent respectively.

  However, on May 2020, US$125 million was cancelled from the project following a request from the Government of Ethiopia for a supplementary
  budget support for the Second Development Policy Operation (DPO II) to cope-up with the macroeconomic impact of COVID-19 on Ethiopia.
  Financial resources worth US$250 million for this supplementary DPO were made available by cancelling portions of ongoing IDA financed projects,
  including US$125 million from the LLRP. The project was restructured on May 29th 2020 to reflect the cancellation, but no changes were made to
  the project’s original scope and scales, as the intent was to replenish it through AF by FY24 or early. However, due to the changes in the Bank’s
  safeguard procedures, AF is no longer feasible the the project and hence the project will not be able to achieve its original PDO and Intermediate
  indicators with the existing limited resources.

  Moreover, unless compensatory actions are taken on a timely manner, the implementation delays in the conflict affected woredas will have a
  negative impact towards attaining the PDO. Therefore, the project’s progress towards attaining its PDO is downgraded to Moderately Satisfactory.
  The World Bank team recommended restructuring of the project along with the MTR in December 2022.
