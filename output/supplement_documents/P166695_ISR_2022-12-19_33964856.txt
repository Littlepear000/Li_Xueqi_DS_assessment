 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The Gambia Fiscal Management Development Project (FMDP – P166695) is a US$35 million International Development Association (IDA)
  Grant, which was approved by the Board of Executive Directors on June 4, 2020, became effective on July 28, 2020. In order to respond to
  a number of fiscal management challenges in the Gambia at that time, the Project Development Objective (PDO) sought “to increase fiscal space
  and improve the effectiveness of public resources management.” To achieve this objective, the project is organized around three components,
  including: (i) strengthening revenue collection and public procurement processes; (ii) supporting fiscal management of State-owned Enterprises
  (SOEs) and restructuring plans of telecoms’ SOEs; and (iii) project implementation support and reform coordination. At the time of appraisal, the
  PDO and project components were aligned with the strategic objectives of the Government’s National Development Plan (NDP) and the World Bank
  Group Country Engagement Note for the Republic of The Gambia for FY18-FY21 (CEN n° IDA/R 2018-0139).

  After more than two years since effectiveness in 2020, progress toward achieving the Project Development Objective (PDO) is Moderately
  Unsatisfactory, with a cumulative disbursement rate of only 21 percent. Despite the initial delay pertaining to key activities, Project Implementation is
  currently rated Moderately Satisfactory, as improved progress has been recorded in the last three quarters. All three components have recorded
  Moderately Satisfactory performance, and key milestones have been reached over the period, including the passage of the GPPA Bill by the
  National Assembly on October 27, 2022 and the tendering of the ITAS system acquisition on November 24, 2022. Despite this recent progress,
  annual targets have not been met on any of the three PDO indicators. Likewise, eight out of eleven intermediate results indicators have not met
  their annual targets.

 Overall Comments PDO

  Annual targets have not been met on any of the three PDO indicators. No data is available to measure the Value Added Tax Gross compliance
  ratio (PDO 1); the aggregate annual State-Owned Enterprises report prepared and published by the SOE centralized oversight agency has not
  been prepared as anticipated (PDO 2), and the share of competitive biddings for procuring organizations using electronic Government
  Procurement remains at 0 against a target of 30 percent (PDO 3). In addition to delays in key reform areas, this lack of demonstrable results is
  also attributable to project design flaws, whereby an overly ambitious PDO formulation and the poor results indicator selection do not allow for
  adequate attribution between project activities and intended results.
