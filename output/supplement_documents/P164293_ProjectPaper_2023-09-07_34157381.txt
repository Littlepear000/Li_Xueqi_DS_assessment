    I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

PROJECT STATUS

1.       The Burkina Faso Higher Education Support Project is a five-year operation financed by an IDA grant in the
amount of SDR 48.7 million (US$70 million equivalent). The Project was approved by the World Bank’s Board of Executive
Directors on July 10, 2018 and became effective on December 19, 2018. It constituted the first World Bank support to
the higher education subsector in Burkina Faso, and sought to address key constraints pertaining to access, quality, and
relevance of higher education programs. The Project Development Objective (PDO) is to strengthen higher education
institutions to increase access and deliver quality education in priority subject areas.

2.       Strong progress towards achievement of the PDO. Despite the challenging implementation environment,
particularly following the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic and the political instability in Burkina Faso, the
Government has been able to continue implementing most project-supported activities though at a slower pace than
originally envisaged. Three (3) out of four (4) PDO-level key performance indicators (KPI) have exceeded their expected
end-of-project targets: (i) the number of undergraduate students enrolled in priority subject areas has reached 22,137
students, exceeding the projected end target of 3,891 students (of whom, 31.30 percent represent female students, also
exceeding the projected end target of 25 percent); (ii) the number of teachers1 recruited or trained (CRI) has reached
1,776 teachers trained, exceeding the projected end target of 1,620 teachers. The number of teachers recruited has
reached 748 teachers, also exceeding the projected end target of 75 teachers; and (iii) the number of students benefiting
from direct interventions to enhance learning (CRI) has also reached 203,936 students, far exceeding the projected end
target of 140,000 students (of whom 77,333 are female students, also exceeding the projected end target of 47,600
female beneficiaries. The exception is the increase in the percentage of students who have successfully completed the
first-year program at the higher education institutions (HEIs) supported by the Project, which currently stands at 55
percent against a target of 75 percent.

3.         The Project is comprised of three components. Below is a summary of each component:

4.      Component 1. Enhancing Access to Higher Education: Establishment of the Virtual University of Burkina Faso.
This component supports a cohesive set of interventions that leverage the use of technology to address limited capacity
of current higher education institutes to accommodate the high demand for tertiary education. While the component
has supported key activities that have shown promising results, Component 1 is assessed as Moderately Satisfactory
based on progress to date.

5.       The Project has supported the establishment of an integrated virtual platform (CampusFaso) which is fully
functional. The platform supports improved information and data management and carries out many administrative
functions (student enrolment, orientation, filling administrative forms, archiving of pedagogical materials among
others). The Project also supports an innovative program (One student/One computer) which provided one computer
to each of the 15,000 students via grants as of May 2023. Based on the success of the first phase of the program, a
second phase is currently underway expected to benefit an additional 20,000 students with subsidized computers. In
addition, digital equipment and resources including more than 4,000 e-books were acquired for selected university
libraries.

6.         To diversify and expand access, the Project has also supported the establishment of the Virtual University of

Burkina Faso (VUBF) which, to date, offers virtual training courses to about 1,700 students, below the expected
enrollment number of approximately 8,000 students as originally envisaged. Despite the ongoing challenges in
expanding access, the Government is committed to significantly increase the number of beneficiary students. Up to
10,000 students are expected to be enrolled in the 2023/2024 academic year. Moreover, following the University's
strategic plan and validated business model coupled with the planned increased enrollment of higher education students
in the VUBF, the Government is proceeding with the construction of two out of the three open digital spaces (ODS)
initially planned. Lastly, the component has supported the provision of connectivity to the beneficiary Universities.
Despite significant delays encountered for the past three years, the planned connectivity of HEIs is also on track with the
contract awarded and signed and is expected to benefit twenty-four HEIs with internet connectivity. To date, the four
biggest universities (University Joseph Ki Zerbo, University Nazi Boni, University Norbert Zongo, University Thomas
Sankara) have already started benefitting from internet connectivity.

7.      Component 2. Strengthen higher education institutions and support innovations. This component aims to
improve the quality and increase the relevance of programs provided in HEIs through four subcomponents: (i) the
provision of competitive grants; (ii) development of faculty in HEIs; (iii) strengthening quality assurance in HEIs; and (iv)
strengthening management capacity in HEIs. Based on progress to date, Component 2 is assessed as Satisfactory.

8.      Under subcomponent 2.1, the Project supported the award of funds on a competitive basis to selected
Universities. Along the course of project implementation, the process was transformed from a competitive basis to a
performance-based contract (PBC) modality. To date, a total of ten PBCs (amounting to US$23 million) are being
implemented. The implementation of PBCs is relatively strong and is expected to be completed in 2024. Moreover, under
subcomponent 2.2, the Project supports research activities by providing scholarships to doctoral students. To date, 300
doctoral students benefit from this intervention. Under subcomponent 2.3, eight internal quality assurance units (above
the target of 7) are well established and the development of the national quality assurance strategy and the
establishment of the national higher education qualifications framework have been finalized. Lastly, under
subcomponent 2.4, the Project supports the equipment of research laboratories whose process is well on track and
expected to be delivered and installed by October 2023.

9.       Component 3: Project coordination, management, and implementation activities. This component supported
the coordination and management of Project activities, including those related to M&E, procurement, and FM, as well
as monitoring of activities at the decentralized level. Based on progress to date, Component 3 is assessed as Satisfactory.
The Project Implementation Unit (PIU) is composed of qualified staff who provided the necessary support to carry out
these activities. The PIU has been proactive in building its capacity and actively engaging consultants to support Project
activities and evaluations as needed. As an illustration, the component supported the organization of the National
Conference on Higher Education and Research in Burkina Faso to contribute to the strengthening of Higher Education.

RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

10.     As noted in the Borrower's letter dated August 28, 2023, the proposed Level Two restructuring is to extend the
Closing Date of the Grant for twelve months from September 30, 2023 to September 30, 2024 (see IDA Grant No. D357-
BF, Financing Agreement, Schedule 2, Section III, Sub-section B, paragraph 6). Although the Project is performing well,
the COVID-19 pandemic coupled with the recent political instability have led to some delays in Project implementation
and the PBCs activities are unlikely to be fully implemented by the Project’s current closing date of September 30, 2023.
The proposed restructuring is seized as an opportunity to improve the performance of the Project and to have an impact
on higher education in Burkina Faso, particularly through the continued establishment of PBC conferring increased
autonomy for HEIs, the support for PHD students to increase the critical mass of researchers and professors to fill in

existing gaps in the teaching profession, and the digitization efforts to respond to the increasing demand for higher
education and address the continued challenge of access to quality higher education programs.

11.     Borrower performance. In accordance with paragraph 51 of Section III of the IPF Directive, the performance of
the Borrower remains satisfactory. The last supervision mission for the Project was held on April 24-28, 2023. Project
management is rated Satisfactory in latest ISR. There are no overdue audits for the Project. The restructuring paper
reflects the recommendations made during the mission and subsequent extensive consultations with relevant
stakeholders involved in Project implementation, both in central government and in HEIs, along with a close monitoring
and review of the implementation status of the planned activities. The necessary actions to duly implement the
remaining activities of the Project are identified and agreed upon with the government.

12.      Project closing date extension. The requested 12-month extension of the closing date will provide the Project
with the necessary time to complete all key Project activities and to fully disburse and utilize the remaining Project funds.
Both the Project’s mid-term review (MTR) held in June 20-25, 2022, and the last supervision and implementation support
mission in April 2023 concluded that the PDO remains highly relevant and strongly aligned with the Government’s
priorities for the higher education subsector. The PDO remains achievable, however it will require an additional
implementation time to be granted to make up for the delays encountered to date (including two OP7.30 assessments),
to course-correct and effectively implement all Project-supported activities, and fully disburse Project funds. The World
Bank and the Borrower have agreed on an action plan that the Borrower will carry out to complete all outstanding
activities and to fully disburse the Project’s remaining funds. The cumulative Project age will be 5.9 years once the
restructuring is processed.

13.      The proposed restructuring will allow the full implementation of the following activities: (i) the construction of
the two of the three ODS initially planned; given the insecurity context of the country, the initially planned third ODS
could not be constructed, consequently the number of ODS was reduced from three to two; (ii) the expansion of the
Government’s One Student/One Computer program2; (iii) the rehabilitation of the Central University Library of the
University of Joseph Ki Zerbo (UJKZ); and (iv) the adjustment of PBCs of HEIs to accommodate the additional
implementation period following extension of the Closing Date. Moreover, following the ongoing PBC framework under
implementation, the proposed restructuring allows for the inclusion of new activities that are eligible for financing under
the existing Project description of the Financing Agreement of the Project which aim to remedy existing shortfalls. All
newly introduced activities are expected to be fully implemented before the Project’s revised closing date and include:
(i) acquisition of research equipment for the National Center for Scientific and Technological Research; (ii) acquisition of
equipment to operationalize the two new amphitheaters of the University of Thomas Sankara (with a capacity of 1,000
and 1,500 students) to have them ready for the new academic year 2023/2024; (iii) acquisition and installation of solar
panels3 and boreholes4 at the UJKZ to support the university’s research labs improve research on renewable energy and

 II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

14.      The proposed restructuring includes the following key changes: (i) a 12-month extension of the Project’s closing
date from September 30, 2023 to September 30, 2024; (ii) inclusion of an activity under Component 1 to formally allow
HEIs to offer virtual courses in addition to the Virtual University; (iii) inclusion of new activities under the PBC framework
that is ongoing; (iv) adjustment of the Project’s description for Components 1 and 2 to clarify a number of activities; and
(v) adjustment of the Project’s Results Framework (RF) to clarify the definition of one PDO indicator, to drop one
intermediate results indicator (IRI), introduce two more relevant IRIs, and to ensure proper alignment between the
relevant indicators’ end target dates and the new closing date of the Project. There are no proposed changes to PDO,
PBCs or safeguard category under the Project.

15.      Project closing date extension. To ensure full completion of activities and achievement of the PDO, a 12-month
extension— from September 30, 2023, to September 30, 2024—is proposed. This is the first extension of the closing
date. Under Component 2, the Project is at risk of not completing the execution of the ongoing PBCs with the beneficiary
Universities unless the closing of the Project is extended. All activities financed as part of the PBCs are well underway,
and the last supervision mission recognized the need for additional time for the Universities to implement all the agreed
activities. These activities include, among others, those on the standardization of the school years and the efficacy of
conducting research activities.

16.     Component 1 (expanded activity)—Digitalization support for HEIs to offer virtual courses. To further address
the continued access issue and remedy the shortfalls of the VUBF and the delay in the construction of the ODS, the
Project will expand its digitalization support to enable the respective beneficiary HEIs to offer virtual courses in addition
to face-to-face courses. To do so, the expanded digitalization support will finance the purchase of equipment for HEI,
training of teachers, consulting, non-consulting services. This will help in meeting the PDO by addressing access
constraints such as limited infrastructure, an insufficient number of university professors, and overlapping school years.

17.      Component 2 (expanded activities to be implemented under the ongoing PBC framework). The following
activities will be implemented under the PBCs to remedy existing shortfalls within HEIs: (i) acquisition of equipment to
operationalize the two new amphitheaters of the University of Thomas Sankara to have them ready for the new
academic year 2023/2024; (ii) acquisition and installation of solar panels and boreholes at the UJKZ to support the
university’s research labs improve research on renewable energy and ultimately support the university to reduce its
energy and water consumption bills; (iii) rehabilitation of additional training labs at the UJKZ; and (iv) the construction
of a nursery (Crèche) at the UJKZ for the continued participation of female students who are young mothers. In line with
the PBC framework, these activities will be subject to the approval of the PBC action plans of the beneficiary HEIs.
Moreover, the Project will finance the acquisition of research equipment for the National Center for Scientific and
Technological Research as part of the planned HEI support covered under the Project Financing Agreement.

18.     Change in project description. Under the proposed restructuring, the original description of Component 1 will

be slightly adjusted as agreed during the last supervision and implementation support missions. Specifically, the program
One Student/One Computer which was mainly part of the support to the creation of the UVBF will be presented as a
separate activity. The Government has established through the Project a digital platform whereby students, among other
online services, can apply to acquire a subsidized computer to facilitate the online teaching and learning process.
Students have three options with different pricing to select from and are required to contribute up to 30 percent of the
total cost to acquire the computer. The remaining 70 percent of the cost are covered by the Project as expenditures to
be incurred under Part 1.2 titled 'Support to digital teaching and learning’. Following the COVID-19 pandemic and based
on lessons learned from similar programs in other countries such as Senegal that have designed and implemented similar
schemes, the Program was expanded to benefits students from other HEIs beyond the UV-BF. Given the expansion of
activities related to the support of digital teaching and learning, which will also support the Government's One
Student/One Computer program, Component 1 of the Project will be split into sub-component 1.1 on 'Establishment of
the Virtual University of Burkina Faso' and sub-component 1.2 'Support to digital teaching and learning’. A specific
intermediate result indicator (IRI) will also be included to adequately capture the related Project outputs.

19.     In addition, the Project will support the rehabilitation of the central library of the university Joseph Ki Zerbo of
Ouagadougou as part of improvement of the quality of HEIs. Given the scope of this proposed rehabilitation, it will also
be described as a separate activity under Component 1 for greater visibility and to ensure the effective completion of
the rehabilitation prior to the proposed new closing date. Concerning Component 2, “sub-component 2.1. - Competitive
Grants” will be changed into “Performance Grants” to consider the specificity of HEIs in Burkina Faso. This led to merging
the financing window A and B into a single funding window which correspond to the two types of grants initially
envisaged by the Project. Following this merger, the Project Implementation Manual (PIM) will be updated as part of this
restructuring to streamline the eligibility criteria for the Performance Grants, which is now a single type of grant under
the Project. The updated PIM is expected to be made available prior to three months from the signature date of the
Letter of amendment to the Financing Agreement.

20.     Project’s Results Framework (RF). In the latest ISR dated June 9, 2023, three (3) out of four (4) PDO-level key
performance indicators (KPI) have exceeded their expected end targets and out of seven (7) IRIs measuring the Project’s
overarching outputs, 6 have been achieved or exceeded. The proposed restructuring includes an adjustment to the RF
to achieve the following objectives:
          The IRI pertaining to employer satisfaction with trainees for which the planned satisfaction survey was not
             conducted due to the current insecurity context of Burkina Faso will be dropped given it is not likely that
             trainees will be employed during the life of the Project.
          Two new IRIs will be introduced to measure the Project’s outputs pertaining to the Government’s One
             Student/One Computer program (i.e. number of students who have acquired a subsidized computer with the
             support of the Project) and the research activities supported under the Project (i.e. number of doctoral
             students who have received a scholarship disaggregated by gender).
          Each indicator’s end target achievement date will be revised to reflect and align with the proposed new
             closing date of the Project (September 30, 2024). The Results Framework table sourced from the portal
             highlights that all indicators were revised because the end target date for their achievement was updated to
             coincide with the new closing date of the Project.
