I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING


Summary
        The World Bank
        Digital Rural Transformation Project (P162599)


    1. In response to the request from the Government of Benin (GoB) dated February 12, 2024, an eleven-month closing
       date extension of the IDA Credit 6453-BJ to the Republic of Benin under the Digital Rural Transformation Project
       (the Project) – is proposed, bringing the current closing date of July 31, 2024, to June 30, 2025. The Task Team
       and the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) are confident that project’s key activities can be completed within the
       proposed new timeframe and the project development objectives will be achieved.
    2. This would be the first closing date extension of the Project. As the closing date extension will be less than two
       years cumulatively beyond the original closing date, this is a Level-2 restructuring with Country Director approval.
    3. Furthermore, the proposed restructuring also seeks to update the Project’s result framework to properly reflect
       current and expected level of achievement by the new Project’s closing date. The proposed restructuring also seizes
       this opportunity to retrofit the Results Framework by including the two new digital-related indicators from the
       Corporate Scorecard.
    4. This would be the third Level-2 Restructuring of the Project:
         i.   The first restructuring (May 18, 2020, CD Decision) sought to: (i) cancel EUR 46,296,300 (equivalent to
              US$ 49.95 million, or 52 percent of the original financing amount) from Loan No. 6453-BJ; (ii) adjust the
              disbursement categories as a result of the cancellation.
        ii.   The second restructuring (January 12, 2021, RVP Decision due to change in PDO) aimed to: (i) adjust,
              reduce the scope and streamline the existing components of the project following the cancellation of 52
              percent of the original IDA funding from the first restructuring; (ii) adjust the Project Development
              Objective (PDO) following the restructuring of the components; and (iii) adjust the Project Results
              Framework (RF) as a result of the reduced scope of the existing components.

Project Status

    5. The Project was approved on July 1, 2019, and became effective on December 3, 2019. The initial IDA Credit –
       before the first restructuring of May 2020 – amounted to EUR 89.2 million (US$100.00 million equivalent at the
       date of approval).
    6. Project performance is currently rated Moderately Satisfactory (MS) for both progress toward achievement of the
       PDO (DO) and Implementation Progress (IP). As of April 1, 2024, the Project’s disbursement rate stood at 37
       percent (US$17.56 million). The extension will allow to reach at least 90% disbursement rate by project closure.
       The PIU remains confident that the project will disburse the full amount by the new closing date. Should a
       cancellation of undisbursed amount be needed, the Task Team will anticipate and process the cancellation well
       before the new closing date. Implementation has experienced some delays over the first year of project life,
       primarily due to: (i) delays in creating the Project Steering Committee (PSC) to ensure the overall strategic
       guidance and oversight of the Project; (ii) delays in recruiting the staff of the Project Implementation Unit (PIU);
       (iii) recent international crises (COVID-19 pandemic, Russia's invasion of Ukraine, high prices of goods and
       disrupted supply chains which delayed some procurement activities in addition to local administrative constraints;
       and (iv) turnover of Project Coordinators (with a total of 3 hired since project inception). As of now, the project
       has a stable PSC which meets regularly and provides actionable recommendations to the project, all positions
       within the PIU have been filled and the Project Coordinator has been in place for more than a year (hired in
       February 2023).

     7. The proposed extension would allow additional time for the full completion of the following core activities (with
        financing allocations in US$):

          Component 1 (Digital infrastructure: Extending digital connectivity in targeted rural areas – $30.00m)

            i.      Deployment of rural connectivity sites in selected project areas: This activity has seen significant delays
                    due to the time taken to agree with the government on the specifics of the PPP model and to a lengthy
                    procurement process. The related Request for Bids (RFB) closed in June 2022 and the service orders were
                    delivered to the two selected bidders only in May 2023. The construction work reached a 70% completion
                    rate for the 119 sites planned; the team expects full completion of works by June 2024. Following this,
                    additional time will be needed to ensure proper quality check of the 119 sites, including corrections where
                    required to obtain the full acceptance of works, and to measure initial impact on targeted beneficiaries.1
           ii.      Extension of connectivity and digital solutions in public administration sites (Ministry of Agriculture,
                    Livestock and Fisheries, local municipalities offices, agriculture high schools): These activities were
                    delayed due to administrative hurdles but also several unsuccessful bid processes. However, at the light of
                    recent progresses (all procurement process ongoing, including some contracts signed and start of work in
                    selected sites) all activities should be completed by September 2024.

                 Component 2 (Digital financial inclusion and digital solutions for rural development –$13.00m):

          iii. Development of a shared digital platform for decentralized financial services: The feasibility studies were
               completed and the procurement process for the development of the shared platform for microfinance
               institutions is ongoing and the contract will be signed by May 2024 and the platform deployed by January
               2025 at the latest.
        iv.    Acquisition of Electronic Payment Terminals: The feasibility studies were completed and the RFB for the
               provision of sub-grants to eligible merchants for the acquisition of digital payments equipment was
               launched and the PIU expects the start of activities in May 2024 and completion in January 2025.
         v.    Scale-up of Agritech platforms: The project has successfully deployed three (03) Agritech platforms: (i) an
               agricultural e-advisory platform; (ii) a digital platform for the management of agricultural equipment for
               the benefit of targeted producers; and (iii) a digital platform on farm management and monitoring. However,
               uptake by targeted beneficiaries has been limited. Hence, the PIU signed an MOU with one of the largest
               farmers’ unions (Benin Federation of Farmers Union, FUPRO) to sensitize farmers organizations, enroll
               and train targeted beneficiaries on the use and benefits of these platforms, These activities are expected to
               increase outreach and utilization of the platforms significantly.
     8. The above activities are all essential for the realization of the project PDO. The Project Implementation Unit has
        also committed to streamline its annual workplan to focus only on essential activities.

       9. Regarding the Results Framework (RF), the overall implementation delay as well as further market analytics
           conducted by the team indicate that some indicators are no longer relevant and/or could no longer be achieved
           given the two restructurings, namely the reduction of project loan amount by 52 percent. This adjustment of the
           RF was discussed and officially confirmed with the Government of Benin during past supervision missions2 to
           adequately reflect current project achievements and expected results by project closure.
       10. In addition, the two new digital-related Corporate Scorecard Indicators have been added in the Results Framework,
           namely:
             i.    “people using broadband internet;” and
            ii.    “people using digitally enabled services.”
       11. The proposed extension is in line with the World Bank Policy and Directive on Investment Project Financing as:
           (i) the PDO remains achievable; (ii) the Borrower’s performance remains satisfactory; (iii) the World Bank and
           the Borrower agree on actions that will be undertaken by the Borrower to complete the project by the new proposed
           closing date of June 30, 2025; (iv) the project is not subject to suspension of disbursements; and (v) there are no
           outstanding audit reports and the audits reports are satisfactory to the World Bank.



    II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

       12. The proposed changes pertain to the extension of the closing date from July 31, 2024, to June 30, 2025, and the
           revision of the Results Framework to: (i) adjust the indicators and related targets following the second
           restructuring; and (ii) include the two new digital-related Corporate Scorecard Indicators.
