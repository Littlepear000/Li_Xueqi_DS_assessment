 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Overview. The 5-year Burkina-Faso Higher Education Support Project (US$70 million) was approved by the World Bank’s Board of Executive
  Directors on July 10, 2018 and became effective on December 19, 2018. It constituted the first support from the World Bank to the higher education
  subsector in Burkina Faso, and sought to address key constraints pertaining to access, quality, and relevance of higher education programs. The
  Project Development Objective (PDO) is to strengthen higher education institutions to increase access and deliver quality education in priority
  subject areas. Based on extensive consultations with relevant stakeholders involved in project implementation, both in central government and in
  the higher education institutions (HEIs) themselves, along with a close monitoring and review of the implementation status of the planned activities,
  the project’s overall performance is assessed as satisfactory.
  Below is a summary of the status of each component following the implementation support mission that took place in April 2023:

  Component 1. Enhancing Access to Higher Education: Establishment of the Virtual University of Burkina Faso. This component has
  supported key activities that have shown promising results. An integrated virtual platform (CampusFaso) has been established and is fully
  functional. The platform supports improved information and data management and carries out many administrative functions (student enrolment,
  filling administrative forms, archiving of pedagogical materials among others). The Project also supports an innovative program (One student / One
  computer) which provided one computer to each of the 15,000 students via grants as of May 2023. Based on the success of the first phase of the
  program, a second phase is currently underway expected to benefit an additional 20,000 students with subsidized computers. In addition, digital
  equipment and resources, including more than 4,000 e-books, were acquired for selected University libraries. The component has also supported
  the establishment of the Virtual University of Burkina Faso (VUBF) in 2018 which, to date, offers virtual training courses to about 1,700 students,
  which is below the expected enrollment number of approximately 8,000 students as originally envisaged. Despite the ongoing challenges in
  expanding access, the Government is committed to significantly increase the number of beneficiary students. Up to 10,000 students are expected to
  be enrolled in the new academic year 2023/2024. Moreover, following the University's strategic plan and validated business model coupled with the
  planned increased enrollment of higher education students in the VUBF, the Government is proceeding with the construction of two open digital
  spaces (ODS). Lastly, the component has supported the provision of connectivity to the selected Universities. Despite significant delays
  encountered for the past three (3) years, the planned connectivity of HEIs is also on track with the contract awarded and signed and is expected to
  benefit twenty-four (24) HEIs with internet connection.

  Component 2: Strengthen higher education institutions and support innovations. This component supported the award of funds on a
  competitive basis to selected Universities. Along the course of project implementation, the process was transformed from a competitive basis to a
  performance-based contract (PBC) modality. To date, a total of ten PBCs (amounting to US$23 million) are being implemented. The implementation
  of the performance-based contracts is relatively strong and is expected to be completed in 2024. Moreover, this component supports research
  activities by providing scholarships to doctoral students. Currently, 300 doctoral students benefit from this intervention. Finally, eight internal quality
  assurance units (above the target of 7) are well established, the development of the national quality assurance strategy and the establishment of the
  national higher education qualifications framework have been finalized.

  Component 3: Project coordination, management, and implementation activities. This component supported the coordination and
  management of project activities, including those related to M&E, procurement, and FM, as well as monitoring of activities at the decentralized level.
  The PIU is composed of qualified staff who provided the necessary support to carry out these activities. The PIU has been proactive in building its
  capacity and actively engaging consultants to support project activities and evaluations as needed. As an illustration, the component supported the
  organization of the National Conference on Higher Education and Research in Burkina to contribute to the strengthening of Higher Education.
