 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Project Status
1.       A formal request was submitted by the Government of India—through the Department of Economic Affairs—on
February 14, 2025, seeking partial cancellation of the loan amount due to exchange rate savings. This was followed by a
subsequent request on December 24, 2025, seeking additional loan cancellation, along with proposals to broaden the scope of
the Program and adjust the Disbursement Linked Indicators (DLIs) and Disbursement Linked Results (DLRs) accordingly. After
consultation with the Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MoMSMEs), these requests were agreed upon and
encompass adjustment of loan allocations in line with the cancelled proceeds, expansion of coverage from the initial five
states to twenty-five states—based on competitive evaluation of State Implementation Plans (SIPs)—and the incorporation of
new interventions focused on greening enterprises as well as revised implementation modalities.

2.       The Raising and Accelerating MSME Performance (RAMP) Program was designed to enhance the Micro, Small, and
Medium Enterprise (MSME) support ecosystem in India by prioritizing competitiveness and sustainable growth. As of January
31, 2026, the Program has disbursed US$258.49 million, representing 51.7% of the total loan amount. The current ratings for
both the Project Development Objective (PDO) and Implementation Progress (IP) are Satisfactory. There are no outstanding
financial audit reports, and the Program remains in full compliance with all legal covenants. The PDO, Results Areas, institutional
and implementation frameworks, fiduciary requirements, as well as environmental and social safeguards remain unchanged.
This document reflects the first restructuring of the Program.

B. Rationale for Restructuring
3.      The proposed restructuring is in response to two written requests from the Government of India and includes the
following modifications:
     a) The Program’s coverage will expand from 5 to 25 states/UTs, leveraging the built‑in flexibility of the legal agreement
        and responding to strong nationwide demand. World Bank support will finance selected activities from State
        Implementation Plans (SIPs) approved in the first two submission phases. These SIPs were competitively evaluated
        against the technical criteria set out in the Program Implementation Manual.
     b) The ODR model will shift from a “platform of platforms” approach to a government-owned and operated system that
        links MSMEs with empaneled service providers, including private platforms.
     c) The proposed enhancement to TreDS - using GST filings as deemed buyer acceptance under the TReDS 2.0 model – is
        not feasible; consequently, the Task Force (established under RAMP) has formulated alternative recommendations
        which have informed the revision of the DLI.
     d) The introduction of new greening measures and interventions, along with updated implementation procedures,
        necessitates amendments to the DLIs/DLRs, program descriptions, and verification protocols.
     e) Additionally, resources are being reallocated among DLIs/DLRs to reflect exchange-rate cancellations and to align with
        the Program’s revised priorities.

 II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

4.      Net cancellation of exchange rate savings of US$63.6 million: The exchange rate savings realized from the
difference between the rate at approval and the rates on February 14 and December 24, 2025 are being cancelled,

resulting in a revised loan amount of US$436.4 million while maintaining the debt in INR at INR 37,500,000,000. This
partial cancellation does not affect prior disbursement claims, financial reports, or audits.

5.       Changes in result framework, DLIs/ DLRs, its description, verification procedure: Adjustments to the DLIs and
DLRs, as detailed in the Annex, have been necessitated by cancellations and modifications in Program coverage, design,
and implementation. The overall Program target has increased from 555,000 to 729,000 MSMEs, representing a net
growth of 210,000 MSMEs due to the inclusion of beneficiaries from both ongoing and newly introduced initiatives.
However, targets for two DLIs must be reduced owing to delays in the commencement of certain implementation
activities. For specific details regarding modifications to the DLIs/DLRs, their descriptions, and verification procedures,
please refer to the Annexes.

6.      Prior results accounting of US$5 million disbursement against results achieved under DLR 4.1: Although DLI 4.1
was completed in January 2022, before the Loan Agreement was signed on May 27, 2022, delays in cabinet approval
resulted in the postponement of the signing. By the time the Agreement was executed, MoMSME had already
commenced and successfully concluded the activities under DLI 4.1 as originally scheduled. Accordingly, prior results
financing of US$5 million has been incorporated into the Agreement, with the timeline for DLR 4.1 reclassified from Year
1 to Year 0.

7.       PCE-PCM Tagging/ Maximizing Finance for Development (MFD): The Program mobilizes US$1.11 billion in
private capital over twice the Bank’s US$500 million financing through PCM contributions from two DLIs: about US$1.07
billion under DLI 5.2 (Guarantees to Women MSMEs) from the increased share of women-led enterprises in guarantees
under the revised CGTMSE framework, and US$40 million under DLI 5.3 (Green Investment Guarantees) via a new green
guarantee-product under the GIFT scheme. For PCE, ~US$8.82 billion will be unlocked under DLI 4 (Receivables Financing
via TReDS), enabled by reforms allowing NBFCs to act as factors, TReDS registration with CERSAI, and first-to-file priority
against third parties.
